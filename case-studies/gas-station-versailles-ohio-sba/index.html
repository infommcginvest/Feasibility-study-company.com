Gas Station and Convenience Store Feasibility Study, Versailles, Ohio: An SBA 7(a) Worked Case

Market analysis by Mark Otava · Published October 16, 2026 · Data as of October 2026

Representative worked example. The site, sponsor and project are hypothetical. The market evidence comes from the public sources listed below, with their dates. This page shows how the market analysis is built and how it sets the loan. It is not a client engagement.

Versailles is a Darke County village of 2,715 people [1] with three fuel retailers already on or near Main Street [19]. Land is cheap and demand is steady, but a fourth store's Year 2 income covers the sponsor's $2,747,958 request just 0.62x. The debt the store can carry is $1,481,677, with the sponsor funding 50.9% of cost. Two village approvals come first: a conditional use for the fuel station, and proof that the site sits outside the drinking-water protection zone, where underground petroleum storage is banned [14].

Market Conclusion

A small market with room for one modest store, if the tanks are allowed.

Versailles can support a fourth fuel and convenience store, but only a small one, and only at roughly half equity. The village had 2,715 residents in the 2020 to 2024 survey [1], almost unchanged from 2,692 at the 2020 Census [2]. Growth will not carry this store. What carries it is the village's role as a crossroads and an employment center: three state routes meet in town [12], Midmark keeps its headquarters, innovation hub and largest manufacturing plant here [7], and the surrounding Wayne Township counted 4,491 people in 2020 [6].

Three fuel retailers already serve that market [19]. A new store with modern pumps and a kitchen can take a share, but the share has a ceiling. The model underwrites fuel volume at a little over half of a typical store's and inside sales below an older rural store's. On those terms the store earns $247,467 a year at stabilization. The sponsor asks for $2,747,958 at the start-up minimum of 10% equity, and coverage reaches only 0.62x in the Year 2 test. Restructured, the loan the store supports is $1,481,677, with $1,535,995 of equity, 50.9% of cost.

Cheap land helps but does not decide this case. Land is a small share of cost here, so the debt the store can carry is set by what a four-dispenser store in a small village can earn against the cost of building one. The more pressing question is legal. The village's 2026 zoning code allows a convenience store by right in B-2 but treats a fuel station as a conditional use, and it bans underground petroleum storage inside the drinking-water protection zone [14]. Whether the subject's land sits in that zone was not found. Until the village answers, the lender cannot know whether it is financing a gas station or a convenience store without fuel.

This case completes the small-town end of our gas station and convenience store casebook. Ohio does not yet have a state page on the site; the national rules are on our SBA 7(a) and 504 page.

Stable, older and middle-income

The village's population barely moved between the 2020 Census and the latest survey [1][2], and its median age of 46.0 sits well above Ohio's 39.8 [4][5]. Households are small, 2.15 people on average, and owners occupy 865 of 1,249 homes [1]. The median household income of $70,717 is close to the state's $72,212 [3][5], though the village estimate carries a margin of error of $8,049 [3]. This is a settled, middle-income community. It will not grow a store's volume on its own; it will not shrink it quickly either. For an underwriter that cuts both ways: the downside is limited, and so is the upside.

Daytime demand from industry

The village's daytime population is larger than its census count. Midmark says its headquarters, innovation hub and largest manufacturing facility are in Versailles [7]. No dated headcount for Midmark or any other Versailles employer was found; the county's own employer list gives no date, so the study uses no employment figure, and a dated employer list is a lender condition. The county markets two industrial parks in Versailles inside a Community Reinvestment Area [13]. Workers on shifts buy coffee, breakfast and lunch on the way in and fuel on the way home, which is the trade a pizza-and-breakfast store is built for.

One weekend that changes the town

Poultry Days, now in its 75th year, filled the village on June 12 to 14, 2026 with free admission [8]. The festival served 40,179 chicken dinners in 2025 and more than 1.2 million since 1952 [8][9]; local press describes thousands of visitors but no count was published [10]. That is a peak weekend, not a season, and no monthly fuel volumes exist to measure it. The model carries no festival revenue.

Ohio's store base

Ohio had 5,833 convenience stores at the end of 2025, 38 more than a year earlier [30]. The state is still adding stores slowly, while chains such as Casey's look for small towns without one of their stores [31]. Versailles already has its Casey's, which makes it a less obvious target for another chain entrant and a harder market for an independent.

One related county record exists: a 0.32-acre property next to Little John's sold on February 7, 2019 for $193,500 [17], an improved small site that says more about Main Street commercial values than about the subject's vacant land.

Two more directory names, a garage and drive-through and a "Marathon Oil and Gas" listing on Marker Road, could not be shown to sell retail fuel. In the next ring, Schafer Oil in Russia had 22 GasBuddy reviews in October 2026 [20].

How Casey's arrived

In January 2018 Casey's was negotiating to combine three lots at East Main and Maple for its Versailles store; the county's first Casey's had opened in Ansonia in April 2017, and the company said then that it had no other Darke County stores planned [23]. No fuel or convenience store project for Versailles or Wayne Township appears in the public record for 2025 or 2026; the January 14, 2026 council agenda, which carried the new zoning ordinance and the zoning of 6.782 newly annexed acres, lists none [15]. The subject would be the first new entrant since Casey's.

What a fourth store competes on

Casey's runs the format the subject copies, a branded fuel store with pizza. It earned a 58.3% margin on prepared food and dispensed beverages through the third quarter of fiscal 2026 [35], a level an independent kitchen will not match. The subject's advantages are newer pumps, a site chosen for its own frontage and access, and longer or earlier hours if the sponsor chooses them. Our note on underwriting a c-store franchise covers why the brand agreement and the inside store, not the forecourt, carry the going-concern value.

Site

Business zoning allows the store; fuel needs a permit and a clean water map.

The subject is a hypothetical parcel of about 2 acres with frontage on one of the three state routes that meet in the village, SR 47, SR 121 and SR 185 [12]. Its zoning is an assumption: a business district that permits a convenience store, to be confirmed with the village. The code reading below uses B-2, the business district whose rules the research read in full. The research holds no vacant commercial land sale for the village, and the study takes no listing's asking price for its land. The one recorded commercial sale nearby, the improved Main Street property described in the competitor section [17], prices a building rather than raw land. Land therefore enters the model as an allowance of $75,000 an acre, and an appraisal replaces it before the loan is sized. Land is a small share of this project, so the loan does not turn on that line.

What the zoning code allows

The village adopted a new zoning code by Ordinance 26-01 on February 11, 2026 and revised it by Ordinance 26-36 on July 22, 2026 [14]. In B-2, "Drive-Through Carry Outs and Convenience Stores" are permitted by right under Section 305.2. "Automobile Service Stations" are conditional uses under Section 305.3, and Section 413 makes filling stations conditionally permitted in the business and industrial districts [14]. A fuel station therefore needs a conditional use permit from the Board of Zoning Appeals under Section 441, with a development plan under Section 413.D [14]. Should a rezoning ever be needed, Section 209.4 sets public hearings at least 30 days after notice at both the Planning Commission and Council, each with a 30-day decision window [14].

The filling station standards

Section 413 sets the physical program. A filling station site must have at least 12,000 square feet and 100 feet of frontage [14]. Buildings sit 40 feet from the front lot line and pumps 15 feet, with 20-foot side and 40-foot rear yards [14]. The site needs at least two driveways at least 20 feet apart, with curb cuts no wider than 40 feet; canopy lights must be recessed and deliver at least 1.0 foot-candle at grade; a 6-foot screen is required next to residential districts; and one pole sign is allowed [14]. A two-acre parcel meets all of these comfortably.

The rule that can end the fuel side

Section 424 governs the Drinking Water Source Protection Plan Zone. It prohibits underground storage of petroleum products inside the zone, requires existing and new underground tanks to meet the federal UST rules in 40 CFR 280 and 281, and sends non-residential uses in the zone to Planning Commission site-plan review [14]. A fuel store stores its fuel underground. If the subject sits inside the zone, it cannot sell fuel, and the project falls back to a convenience store without fuel. The research did not find the zone map, which makes the zone determination the first lender condition, ahead of the market questions.

Utilities, access and environment

Utilities are a condition here, not a finding. The study relies on no record of which providers serve a given parcel, so water, sewer, electric and gas service to the site, with tap and connection fees, must be confirmed in writing with the village and the gas utility before the loan is sized. The site's flood zone must likewise be confirmed on FEMA's map before the loan is sized [52]. A parcel identifier, an ODOT traffic count for the subject's route [51] and the nearest interstate exit were not found. Darke County Airport sits two miles southwest on SR 121 [11], a reminder that any canopy or sign height should be checked against airport surfaces. A Phase I environmental site assessment and documented tank testing go into every gas station loan file regardless of loan size [42].

The revenue table is built from gross profit: fuel gallons times margin, and inside sales times margin, split between packaged goods and the kitchen. A pump-price revenue line would be misleading here for a specific reason. Ohio cut its motor fuel tax from 38.5 cents a gallon on gasoline to a hundredth of a cent from October 4, 2026 to January 2, 2027, and the state requires retailers to pass the full saving to customers [25]. The only Versailles prices on record, $2.66 regular at Casey's and at the Marathon station on October 15, 2026 [19], were taken inside that holiday. They are not normal prices, and a revenue model built on them would understate sales by the tax and overstate how cheaply the store can sell. The margin per gallon is unaffected because the tax passes straight through.

Fuel

By Year 3 the pumps sell 2,300 gallons a day, a little over half of the roughly 4,000 that NACS gives as a typical store's daily volume [32]. That is a share of a small village's fuel, not a highway's. The margin is 38.0 cents a gallon, close to the 39.7-cent national gasoline margin for 2024 [32] and well below Casey's 47.8 cents in the quarter to July 31, 2026 [37]. Three sellers in a village price close together, as the matching $2.66 posts show [19]; that is competition without a price war.

Inside store and kitchen

Inside sales reach $1,350,000 in Year 3, below the $1.7 million Yesway's older rural stores sold in 2025 [36]. Food takes 28.5% of the inside total, the national mix for 2025 [33], at a 55.0% margin, a little under Casey's prepared-food result [35]. Packaged goods earn 30.0%, under Yesway's 35.2% blended inside margin [36]. The S&G menu prices above, $3.29 for a breakfast burrito and $2.89 for a 20-ounce soda [22], are the street prices a new kitchen meets.

Gross profit reaches $820,198 in Year 3. Fuel is its largest single line, and the kitchen earns the highest margin per dollar of sales in the store.

Rate: Prime of 7.00% [48] plus the maximum spread SBA allows for this loan size [46]; a lender may price lower. Guaranty: 75% of the loan, Source [45].

The rules are those of SOP 50 10 8.1 with Technical Policy Updates, which governs applications SBA receives from October 1, 2026 [46]. For a start-up the minimum injection is 10%, and coverage on the projections has to reach 1.15x within two years; this case tests Year 2. With real estate above half the proceeds, the loan can amortize over 25 years. Pricing starts from Prime of 7.00% [48] and adds SBA's ceiling spread for a loan of this amount, giving 10.00%; the lender fixes the real rate at pricing. SBA's guaranty on loans of this size is 75.0% [45].

Under the FY2027 fee notice, a rural borrower whose loan stays at or below $700,000 pays no upfront guaranty fee, rural meaning a county that the U.S. Census Bureau has defined as at least 30 percent rural [47]. Darke County's Census rural share was not part of the research, and both the proposed and restructured loans exceed the cap, so the model charges the fee. A sponsor who shrank the project below the cap in a qualifying county would save it.

The state's lending market sets the context for a loan this size. Across Ohio, 135 active lenders made $1.12 billion of 7(a) loans to 2,909 businesses in 2025, an average of about $386,000 a loan, and Huntington led with $291.4 million across 1,453 loans [53]. The restructured loan here, $1,481,677, is several times that average, and a fuel store with tanks and a kitchen is special purpose collateral, so the sponsor should approach lenders with an active gas station book rather than the nearest branch. No 7(a) loan to a Darke County borrower for 2024 to 2026 was found in primary sources.

A fuel store carries its own program points. SBA lists gas stations among special purpose properties [46], which governs how the appraiser values the going concern. SBA treats a fuel contract covered by the Petroleum Marketing Practices Act as a franchise [46], so the brand contract is reviewed like a franchise agreement. Ownership has been limited to U.S. citizens and nationals since March 1, 2026, when SBA also withdrew a short-lived allowance for small foreign stakes [50]. On the study itself, 13 CFR 120.160(b): SBA may require professional appraisals, a survey, or a feasibility study [49]. On a start-up whose fuel use still needs two approvals, the lender will want one.

Ohio has 320 Opportunity Zones designated in 2018, and new designations take effect on January 1, 2027 [44]; whether the subject's tract is or will be in one was not found. Whether the parcel lies in the village's Community Reinvestment Area, which covers the industrial parks [13], was not found either.

As proposed: the sponsor offers the least equity a start-up may put in, with 10.0% equity ($305,329) and $2,747,958 of debt. The structure does not qualify: coverage falls below the floor.

As restructured: 50.9% equity ($1,535,995) and $1,481,677 of debt with $161,568 of annual debt service. Every test is met.

As proposed, the store covers $299,649 of debt service 0.62x in Year 2 and 0.82x in Year 3. Even settled, it does not earn its payments. The shortfall is not a matter of a weak ramp; the stabilized store is simply too small for the loan.

At 50.9% equity the loan falls to $1,481,677 and debt service to $161,568, Year 2 coverage reaches 1.15x and the stabilized years hold above the floor. For a lender that is a modest, well-covered loan against a special purpose property in a stable market, conditional on the zoning answers. For the sponsor it is a much larger check than planned.

One scenario sits outside the model. If the wellhead zone rules out tanks, the project becomes a store without fuel. Sunoco's APLUS franchise disclosure for 2025 put a newly built no-fuel store at $808,950 to $1,973,600 all in, and the building alone for about 3,000 square feet at $375,000 to $1,100,000, as a contractor relays those figures [41]. That is a much cheaper project, but it gives up the fuel line, which is the largest single source of gross profit in this model, and it would compete with three stations that sell both. The study does not run that case; a lender who faces it should ask for a separate test rather than scaling this one.

The three start-up cases in this set fail for three different reasons. In Lockhart, Texas the town grows fast but the land is priced for that growth. In Lake Wales, Florida the highway is busy but national chains hold its customers. Here land is cheap and competition is settled, and the limit is simply the size of the village. The Opelika, Alabama acquisition and the Cartersville, Georgia interstate case show how the same asset tests when a buyer has trailing earnings or an open highway node.

Risks

The Versailles risks, starting with the water map.

  1. The site is in the wellhead zone

    Section 424 bans underground petroleum storage inside the Drinking Water Source Protection Plan Zone [14]. If the parcel is inside it, the fuel business does not exist.

  2. The conditional use is denied or conditioned

    A fuel station is a conditional use in the village's business districts and needs a Board of Zoning Appeals permit and a development plan [14]. Neighbors on Main Street and the existing operators can be heard. No observed approval timeline for the village was found.

  3. Casey's answers

    Casey's can match a newcomer on price and outspend it on food. Its prepared-food margin [35] shows the gap in kitchen economics an independent starts with.

  4. A small, flat market

    The village barely grew from 2020 to the latest survey [1][2]. If Midmark or another large employer cut shifts, the store's daytime trade would fall with no growth to replace it.

  5. Prices after the tax holiday

    When the fuel tax returns after January 2, 2027 [25], pump prices rise by the full tax overnight. Customers who learned new habits during the holiday may shop on price more than before, which tests the margin assumption.

  6. Construction cost

    Tanks and the canopy are steel-heavy, and steel prices rose sharply into 2026 [40]. In a store this small, a cost overrun of a few hundred thousand dollars is a large share of the loan.

Lender Conditions

Conditions a lender should set before commitment.

The conditions below are ordered by how early each can stop the project. Each closes a gap the research left open; related gaps are grouped.

  1. Wellhead protection zone

    A written village determination, with the zone map, that the parcel lies outside the Drinking Water Source Protection Plan Zone, or the Planning Commission's site-plan approval if any part of it lies inside.

  2. Fuel use approval

    Written confirmation of the parcel's zoning district, an approved conditional use permit and development plan for the filling station, with any conditions, plus the village's zoning and permit fees and an observed approval timeline.

  3. Parcel record and value

    The Darke County Auditor parcel identifier, valuation and tax-year 2025 rate abstract; an appraisal of the land, which replaces the land allowance, and of the finished business's going-concern value; and the Community Reinvestment Area and Opportunity Zone status of the tract.

  4. Utilities, traffic, flood and environment

    Written confirmation from the village and the gas utility that water, sewer, electric and gas serve the parcel, with tap fees; an ODOT count for the subject's route with station and year [51]; the site's FEMA flood zone, confirmed on FEMA's map before the loan is sized [52]; a Phase I report, tank system specifications meeting the federal UST rules, and insurance quotes.

  5. Competitor records

    Operator or Auditor confirmation of the S&G Marathon and Little John's, Little John's current brand and address, fueling positions and store size for all three in-village stations, their opening years and their parcel and tax records, and whether the two unverified directory listings sell fuel.

  6. Prices outside the holiday

    A posted-price survey after January 2, 2027, when the standard fuel tax returns, to test the margin assumption against normal prices.

  7. Demand detail

    A dated list of the village's largest employers with headcounts, daytime employment for the village from Census OnTheMap, a primary Census retrieval of the village's survey figures and its 2025 population estimate, monthly seasonality, truck volumes and a Poultry Days attendance count.

  8. Costs and licenses

    Contractor bids or a local construction cost index, village tap and connection fees, and Ohio's license list and fees for fuel, tobacco, lottery and liquor sales.

  9. Program items

    The rate at pricing, Darke County's Census rural percentage, proof that every owner meets the citizenship rule, the brand supply agreement, recent Darke County 7(a) approvals from SBA's loan-level data, the tract's HUBZone status, and USDA Business and Industry eligibility if that program is considered.

  10. First-year reserve

    A funded reserve covering the gap between Year 1 operating income and debt service.

Versailles Questions

What a lender asks about a fourth fuel store in a small Ohio village.

Can the village's zoning stop the fuel side of this project even if the store is allowed?

Yes. The 2026 zoning code permits a convenience store by right in the B-2 district, but an automobile service station is a conditional use that needs a Board of Zoning Appeals permit and a development plan [14]. Separately, the code bans underground storage of petroleum inside the Drinking Water Source Protection Plan Zone [14]. Whether the subject's land lies inside that zone was not found. If it does, the store can open without fuel or not at all.

Why are the October 2026 gas prices in Versailles so low?

Ohio suspended its motor fuel tax from October 4, 2026 to January 2, 2027, cutting it from the standard 38.5 cents a gallon on gasoline and 47 cents on diesel to a token amount, and retailers must pass the whole saving to customers [25]. The $2.66 regular price posted at Casey's and the Marathon station on October 15, 2026 [19] was taken inside that holiday and is not a normal price. That is one reason the model works in margin per gallon, which the holiday does not change.

Three stations already serve 2,715 people. Is there room for a fourth?

Room for a modest one. The village is the center of a farming and manufacturing township, Midmark keeps its headquarters and largest plant in town [7], and Poultry Days fills the streets each June [8]. But the store's volume is underwritten at little over half of a typical store's, and even then it carries only about half its cost in debt. A larger format would not fit the market.

Does Casey's presence make a new store harder to finance?

It sets the bar. Casey's earned a 58.3% margin on prepared food in the first nine months of fiscal 2026 [35] and runs the format this subject copies, pizza plus fuel. Casey's also says about 75% of towns of 20,000 people or fewer have no Casey's [31], which means the chain has many easier targets than a village where it already operates. The subject competes on location and service, not on scale.

What if the site is inside the wellhead protection zone?

Then the project becomes a convenience store without fuel, which the village's B-2 district permits by right [14]. A newly built no-fuel store under one franchise system's 2025 disclosure costs $808,950 to $1,973,600 [41], far less than this project, but it also gives up the fuel margin that pays most of this model's debt. It would need its own feasibility test.

Is a USDA Business and Industry loan an alternative here?

Possibly, but this study cannot say. The research found USDA housing-program eligibility maps for Darke County, not a Business and Industry determination for the site, so the page makes no eligibility claim. A sponsor considering that program should start with a written eligibility determination from USDA Rural Development.
