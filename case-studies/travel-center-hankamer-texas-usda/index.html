Travel Center Feasibility Study at Hankamer, Texas: An I-10 Truck Stop on a USDA B&I Loan

Market analysis by Mark Otava · Published October 16, 2026 · Data as of October 2026

Representative worked example. The site, sponsor and project are hypothetical. The market evidence comes from the public sources listed below, with their dates. This page shows how the market analysis is built and how it sets the loan. It is not a client engagement.

Hankamer sits at the I-10 and SH 61 interchange in Chambers County, a county that grew 23.8% from 2020 to 2025 [5], on the freight corridor between Houston and Beaumont. Truck directories credit the existing travel plaza at the interchange with 10 to 20 truck spaces [16][17], and the chain travel centers are 20 miles west at Baytown. The sponsor's opening request, 15.0% equity, falls short of the B&I equity minimum and covers Year 3 debt service only 1.04x. Restructured at 28.7% equity, the loan reaches the 1.25x floor in Year 3. The guarantee does not create cash flow: the market supports this travel center, but with far less debt than the sponsor asked for.

Market Conclusion

A parking gap on a freight corridor, and a guarantee that does not fill a coverage gap.

The Hankamer interchange can support a new travel center. The evidence for that is the shape of truck parking along this stretch of I-10, not a traffic count, because no TxDOT count at the interchange was found. Between the chain travel centers at Exit 789 in Baytown and the two small truck stops at Exit 829 in Winnie, drivers have an existing Shell travel plaza at the SH 61 interchange that truck directories credit with 10 to 20 parking spaces [16][17], two Stuckey's stores at Exit 819 [10][11], and a pair of TxDOT rest areas that allow truck parking [12]. Love's at Baytown lists 160 truck spaces and TA lists 173 [13][14]. A travel center with a real truck lot at Hankamer would sit in a gap the chains have left.

The finding on the loan is less comfortable. With volumes benchmarked to a comparable independent site and margins benchmarked to a Texas convenience chain's 2025 filing, the project earns $717,343 of net operating income in its third year on a total cost of $7,703,799. That supports a loan of $5,492,808 at the assumed coverage floor, not the $6,573,960 the sponsor asked for. The USDA guarantee lowers the lender's loss if the project fails; it does nothing to raise the project's income, and coverage is measured on income.

Three facts frame the rest of the page. First, the program is assumed. The case research could not confirm which loan program the sponsor would use and took the USDA Business and Industry guarantee as its working assumption; this case adopts it. Second, Hankamer's rural status for B&I was not determined: it is unincorporated, but Chambers County sits inside the Houston metro, and the site must be checked against USDA's population test [33]. Third, the decisive demand data, the TxDOT count and truck share at the interchange, were not found, and the only traffic figure in circulation is a broker's claim of over 60,000 vehicles a day at Exit 814 [1]. Every volume in the model is therefore a benchmark-based assumption, and the first lender condition is to replace it with counts.

The truck stop and travel center hub explains the asset's economics nationally. The Houston metro page covers the region Chambers County belongs to, and the Texas state page covers statewide taxes, fuel rules and hazards.

Demand

Freight first, then a fast-growing county.

A travel center's customer is mostly passing through. Federal Highway Administration's calibrated truck parking model assigns 77% of truck rest parking demand to commercial truck stops and 23% to public rest areas [51], and the drivers who stop at Hankamer are hauling between Houston, Beaumont and Louisiana. That is why this study defines the primary trade area as the I-10 mainline from Exit 810 to Exit 820 rather than a radius of households, and why the missing TxDOT count matters more here than any Census figure.

Local demand is a second, growing layer. Chambers County had 57,594 residents in July 2025, up 23.8% from 46,539 in 2020 [5], while Texas grew 1.25% in the single year 2024 to 2025 [9]. The county's 17,229 households average 2.97 persons [6], its median age is 35.1 [8], and its median household income was $109,804 (plus or minus $10,467) against $78,476 for Texas in the 2020 to 2024 survey, with per capita income of $42,258 against $40,752 [7]. New subdivisions along SH 61 south of I-10 were reported only in marketing material, which this study does not rely on; a lender should confirm their size and pace from the county's plat filings.

SH 61 also carries recreation traffic south toward Anahuac and the coast. Texas Gatorfest in Anahuac expects about 10,000 visitors in September, timed to the alligator season [21], and the national wildlife refuge near Anahuac, renamed the Jocelyn Nungaray refuge on July 24, 2025 [22], is a seasonal draw for hunting and birding. Visitor counts and monthly fuel seasonality were not found. These are useful for car traffic and the store, not for diesel.

The fuel market is volatile. EIA put the 2025 retail diesel price at $3.66 a gallon and forecast $5.19 for 2026 and $4.49 for 2027 [52]. Margins, not prices, pay the debt, but high pump prices squeeze fleet buyers and push them toward discount programs at the chains. The largest operator's results show the pressure: Pilot's 2025 pre-tax earnings fell to $190 million from $614 million on revenue of $42.2 billion, down from $46.9 billion [49].

Country Boys' Country Store and Travel Plaza, a Shell station on the northeast side of the SH 61 interchange, has no website of its own; one truck directory lists 20 truck parking spaces and 4 diesel lanes, and another lists 10 or more spaces, a conflict the lender should resolve on site [16][17]. DJ's Country Store, an Exxon on SH 61 south of I-10, appears in directories with a deli [18]; its fuel positions and parking were not found. Both are a lender condition: confirm the operator, fuel brand, diesel lanes and truck spaces, and pull the appraisal district record. These two are the subject's direct competitors for local car fuel and store sales.

The next ring east is small. JP's Truck Stop at Exit 829 in Winnie lists 15 parking spaces [19], and a second stop shares the exit; neither has an operator page. To the west, a Flying J at Exit 789 could not be confirmed because its page refused automated access.

The chains are expanding parking, not abandoning it. Love's planned $700 million for 20 new stores, 35 remodels and 1,500 more truck spaces in 2026, on a network of more than 50,000 [50]. Buc-ee's has scheduled a groundbreaking on November 9, 2026 for a 74,000 square foot store in Lafayette, Louisiana, about 150 miles east, with about 120 fuel pumps and nearly 700 parking spaces at a cost of $82 million including about $12.5 million of public infrastructure, opening in 2028 [20]. That store competes for car travelers on the corridor, not for trucks.

No travel center permitted or under construction in the Exit 789 to 829 ring was found for 2025 or 2026, but county permit logs and Commissioners Court agendas were not searched item by item, which is a condition. Land listings show that others see the same opportunity: a 6.82-acre tract at Exit 814 marketed for a truck stop or travel plaza sold on June 5, 2026 for a disclosed $482,001 to $552,000 against a $668,428 list price after 92 days [1].

Site

A county site with no zoning and a flood rule.

The hypothetical travel center sits on about 10 acres with access from the I-10 frontage road in the Hankamer segment of the corridor, the stretch from Exit 810 to Exit 820 around the SH 61 interchange, in unincorporated Chambers County [4]. No particular tract is assumed: the site's position, its ramp access and its frontage are conditions. The land cost in the model, $800,000, is an assumption at $80,000 an acre, set inside the land evidence the research holds for the area. At the low end, two SH 61 tracts set back from the interstate are listed at 16.27 acres for $995,000, or $61,156 an acre [2], and 73.62 acres for $2,576,700 with 1,035 feet of frontage [3]. At the high end, 6.82 acres of I-10 frontage at Exit 814, marketed for a truck stop, sold on June 5, 2026 for a disclosed $482,001 to $552,000 after listing at $668,428 [1]. Interstate frontage, which a truck lot needs, belongs toward the top of that range. A lender should test the land price against an appraisal and a signed contract before it accepts the cost.

Unincorporated Chambers County has no zoning, so a travel center needs no rezoning wherever the site falls in the corridor [1]; the lender confirms that no deed restriction on the chosen tract bars the use. Approvals are ministerial instead: a drainage review with a $1,000 deposit, commercial building permits of $150 to $450 per structure by size, plat review at $200 and construction plan review at $400, fire-code review at $100 per 10,000 square feet, $150 per fuel tank, a $300 flammable liquids permit per building, a $460 commercial septic permit if the site is not sewered, and food permits of $150 to $250 by risk class; work without a permit doubles every fee [23]. The county publishes no approval timeline, which is a condition.

The flood rule matters more than the fees. Chambers County requires all new construction to sit at least 24 inches above base flood elevation regardless of flood zone, and revised its floodplain rules in June 2025 [24]. The site's FEMA zone must be confirmed on FEMA's map before the loan is sized. Raising a store pad, a fuel canopy area and a heavy-duty truck lot two feet above base flood elevation across a 10-acre site on the coastal prairie is a material cost, and the model's site line is an assumption until an engineer prices it.

Utilities are not confirmed for the site. The county's fee schedule names the Trinity Bay Conservation District for water and sewer releases and charges a $25 utility release fee per site for water or sewer and for electric service [23]; the district's tap and impact fees, the electric provider and natural gas service were not found. The Exit 814 comparable also advertised public water and sewer [1], and the 73.62-acre tract lists an 8-inch waterline and 4-inch force main nearby [3].

Five taxing units levy on land in this part of the county. The model adds the 2025 county and conservation district rates, the 2024 school district rate, which the research assumes was unchanged for 2025, and the 2024 hospital and navigation district rates, which are the latest found [25][26][27], and applies the sum to land and improvements at cost, because Texas appraises at full market value [28]. The county's rate has fallen each year since 2021 [25], but the official 2025 rate sheet, the 2025 hospital district rate and the primary 2025 school district adoption were not found. Hankamer collects only state sales tax and the county health services tax [29], a small advantage for the store.

There is no TxDOT count for the interchange. TxDOT's public ramp count layer, updated January 6, 2025, is the source a lender should read for ramp volumes [30], together with the department's vehicle classification counts for truck share [31].

The revenue table carries gross profit, not sales. Fuel sales dollars swing with the pump price, but the margin per gallon is what pays the loan, so diesel and gasoline enter the model as gallons times cents per gallon, and the store as sales times its margin. Total gross profit in Year 3 is $2,153,000.

Volumes. No gallons for any Hankamer station were found, and no operator in the trade area publishes them. The benchmark is an independent travel center offered for sale in September 2025, which reported 3.4 million annual gallons, 2.6 million of them diesel, and $2.5 million of inside sales [45]. That is a broker's unverified listing for a site in another state, and it is the only volume record in the research. The subject ramps to the benchmark's diesel volume, 2,600,000 gallons by Year 3, and to its gasoline and store levels. The case for reaching it is the parking gap described above; the case against is that the benchmark is an established site and the subject is new, while a Shell plaza at the SH 61 interchange already has diesel lanes.

Margins. The study uses 40.0 cents a gallon for diesel, 36.0 cents for gasoline and 33.0% for the store. The anchor is a Texas convenience chain's March 2026 registration statement, which reported fiscal 2025 margins of 43.1 cents on diesel, 39.2 cents on gasoline and 35.2% on merchandise [44]. NACS reported that fuel margins averaged over 40 cents a gallon across the industry in 2025 [43]. Each input sits below its benchmark, because the subject is new, faces two branded stations at the SH 61 interchange, and competes for fleet diesel with chain travel centers that sell on discount programs.

What is not in the model. The model charges nothing for truck parking, treating the lot as the draw for diesel. Showers, scales, laundry and any truck service are left out for lack of evidence on their revenue; they would add income, and a lender who sees the operator's plan can add them.

No operating ratios for travel centers are published; the asset class's public evidence is the large chains' consolidated results. The model therefore builds costs line by line. Payroll starts at $640,000 for a store, kitchen and fuel desk staffed around the clock, and grows with 3.0% a year. For scale, the Texas chain reported salaries and benefits of $200.9 million across 448 stores in fiscal 2025 [44], and Pilot attributed its 2025 earnings decline partly to employee compensation, insurance and maintenance [49].

Card fees are the line most new operators underestimate. NACS put card fees at 8.4 cents a gallon in 2023 [53], and the model applies that to every gallon, diesel and gasoline alike; at the underwritten volume the fees are the second-largest expense. Utilities, repairs, supplies and marketing start at $200,000; convenience store operating expenses rose 23.3% between 2021 and 2025 [43]. Insurance starts at $110,000, a placeholder: Chambers County is on the Gulf coast, wind and flood premiums were not found, and a quote is a condition.

Property tax is applied to land, building, site and fuel system at cost at the combined rate of the five taxing units [25][26][27]. The USDA annual retention fee is not an expense line here; it is folded into the loan's all-in rate, so it declines as the loan amortizes, as it does in practice. Net operating income is $717,343 in Year 3, a margin of 33.3% of gross profit.

Total project cost is $7,703,799, including the USDA initial guarantee fee, which the model finances. Every line is an assumption, land included, which is priced at $80,000 an acre inside the area's land evidence [1][2]. The public cost record for travel centers is thin and comes from chain projects: $10 million for an 11,365 square foot urban travel center approved in February 2026 [46], and $18 million for a chain travel center in Illinois with more than 80 truck spaces and 55 full-time jobs, announced in November 2024 [47]. A Texas convenience chain budgets $10.0 million to $12.0 million all-in, land included, for a 5,800 square foot store with 27 dispensers on 3.7 acres [44]. The subject's improvement budget is below those, consistent with an independent operator building a smaller store and a paved truck lot rather than a chain format.

Two pressures push the other way. Inputs to nonresidential construction rose 8.9% in the year to August 2026 [48], and the county's 24-inch freeboard rule [24] raises the cost of every pad on the site. The lender should require a contractor's bid and an engineer's flood and drainage estimate before it relies on the budget, and should expect the bid to land above the assumption rather than below it.

A B&I loan is a single conventional loan from an approved lender with a partial USDA guarantee. For fiscal 2026 the guarantee is 85% for loans under $5 million and 80% for loans of $5 million to $25 million, the program maximum; the initial guarantee fee is 3.0% of the guaranteed amount and the annual retention fee 0.55%, and a guarantee issued before construction is complete costs a further 0.50% [32]. This model assumes a construction-to-permanent loan guaranteed before completion, so it adds that 0.50% to the initial fee. USDA announced the move to 85% for smaller projects on March 27, 2026 [33]. Both the sponsor's request and the restructured loan exceed $5 million, so the model applies 80%. A lender that trimmed the restructured loan below $5 million would gain the higher guarantee, at the cost of more sponsor equity.

USDA sets no rate. Rates are negotiated between lender and borrower, fixed, variable or a mix, and a variable rate must track a published base rate the Agency accepts [35]. The model's all-in rate of 9.44% is an assumption: Prime of 7.00% on October 9, 2026 [54] plus two points, plus the retention fee on the guaranteed share. The term may run to the justified useful life of the project or its collateral, at most 40 years [35]; the model uses 25 years, blending long-lived buildings with shorter-lived fuel equipment.

The feasibility study is mandatory here, which sets B&I apart from SBA lending. Under 7 CFR 5001.306(a)(3)(i), a guaranteed loan of more than $1,000,000 to a new business requires a study by an independent qualified consultant acceptable to the Agency; at $1,000,000 or less the Agency may still require one [34]. The projections in the application must run at least two years at full capacity or stable operations, with assumptions listed and a pro forma closing balance sheet [34], and the lender's credit evaluation must include its own written evaluation of the study [36]. By contrast, 13 CFR 120.160(b): SBA may require professional appraisals, a survey, or a feasibility study [56]. The note on USDA's five feasibility components shows how the study maps to the Agency's review, and the USDA Rural Development practice page describes how we scope these studies.

The program itself carries risk. B&I obligations reached $2,111,702,000 in fiscal 2025, up from $1,828,800,000, and Texas took $289.2 million, 13.7% of the national total [38]. Fiscal 2026 program levels conflict: $1,428,899,083 in USDA's own table and $1,750,000,000 in the enacted language as CRS reports it [38][39]. The fiscal 2027 budget request proposes no B&I program level at all [38]. Participation has narrowed: loans fell from 309 in fiscal 2015 to 176 in fiscal 2024 while the average loan rose from $2.75 million to $8.1 million [40], and on May 12, 2026 USDA removed ten lenders whose portfolios held about $620 million of delinquent loans, about 47% of Rural Development's delinquencies, leaving over 750 approved lenders [41]. USDA's loan-level Lender Lens data, published January 19, 2026, is where a sponsor finds the lenders still active in Texas [42].

Two siblings on this site use the same guarantee: the Bastrop County RV park case applies it to a campground, and the Texas meat processing case to a plant. The Terrell truck stop case finances the same asset class on I-20 with SBA 504, where gas stations sit on SBA's special purpose list [55]; reading the two together shows how differently the programs treat equity and coverage.

As proposed: the sponsor leans on the USDA guarantee to keep its own cash low, with 15.0% equity ($1,160,111) and $6,573,960 of debt. The structure does not qualify: the equity injection is below the program minimum; coverage falls below the floor.

As restructured: 28.7% equity ($2,210,990) and $5,492,808 of debt with $573,139 of annual debt service. Every test is met.

USDA's rules reviewed for this study set no single coverage ratio for B&I; the lender's credit policy governs, and the Agency's eligibility determination asks whether repayment is reasonably assured. The model tests an assumed 1.25x floor in Year 3, the first year of stable operations, which satisfies the two-year projection requirement [34]. The equity minimum of 25.0% of project cost is the model's reading of 7 CFR 5001.105(d) for a new business whose guarantee issues before construction is complete [37]; the lender confirms the measure at application.

As proposed, the sponsor puts in $1,160,111, 15.0% of cost, and borrows $6,573,960, expecting the guarantee to carry the rest. The structure fails both tests: equity is below the minimum, and annual debt service of $685,950 is covered only 1.04x in Year 3.

As restructured, the loan is sized to the floor. Raising equity to the minimum is not enough, so the engine raises it to the smallest share that meets 1.25x in Year 3: 28.7%, or $2,210,990. The loan becomes $5,492,808 with debt service of $573,139, and coverage is 1.25x in Year 3 and 1.27x in Year 5.

That is a feasible project and an expensive one for the sponsor. A lender should read the determination as a statement about debt capacity: the projected cash flow carries $5,492,808 of debt on $7,703,799 of cost. A sponsor who cannot fund the equity should shrink the cost, starting with the store and truck lot, the largest line, before asking the guarantee to do more.

Risk Factors

What could go wrong at this interchange.

  • Volume without a count. Every gallon in the model is benchmarked; the only traffic figure is a broker's claim for a nearby tract [1]. If truck share on this segment is lower than the benchmark site's, the cushion disappears.
  • Interchange competition. Country Boys' Shell plaza already has diesel lanes and truck parking at the SH 61 interchange [16], and an Exxon store sits on SH 61 [18]. Their response to a new entrant, on price or on parking, is unknown.
  • Chain discounting. Love's, TA and a Flying J at Exit 789 sell fleet diesel under national programs, and Love's is adding 1,500 truck spaces in 2026 [50]. The subject competes for the same fleets with no network.
  • Flood and storms. The county's freeboard rule [24] raises costs, wind and flood premiums were not found, and a Gulf coast storm can close an interstate fuel stop for days.
  • Environmental liability. Underground tanks are the asset's main loss channel; the county permits tanks [23], but state tank registration and a Phase I assessment were not found.
  • Program funding. The fiscal 2027 request would end new B&I lending [38], and the lender base has narrowed [40][41]. A delay in Texas allocations could stall the project after design costs are spent.
  • Rural eligibility. If USDA finds the site ineligible under the population test [33], the program assumption fails and the project needs a different structure.
  • Fuel price swings. EIA's forecast of $5.19 diesel in 2026 and $4.49 in 2027 [52] shows how fast pump prices move; high prices squeeze fleet buyers, and falling prices can compress retail margins in a price war.

The special purpose property note explains why a fuel-and-store building is hard collateral to resell, which is the bank's exposure on the unguaranteed fifth of this loan.

Hankamer Travel Center Questions

What lenders ask about this Hankamer travel center.

Will USDA want a feasibility study for a travel center at Hankamer?

It has to have one. Two facts trigger the rule: the borrower is a new business, and the guaranteed loan is far above $1,000,000, the size above which 7 CFR 5001.306(a)(3)(i) requires a study by an independent qualified consultant acceptable to the Agency for a new business [34]. Here the study's hardest job is the truck count, because every gallon in the model stays a benchmark until TxDOT's figures for the corridor are in the file. SBA lending works differently: 13 CFR 120.160(b): SBA may require professional appraisals, a survey, or a feasibility study [56].

Is Hankamer a rural area for B&I purposes?

That has not been determined. For B&I, a rural area is one not in a city or town of more than 50,000 people, judged at the project site [33]. Hankamer is unincorporated, but Chambers County sits inside the Houston metro, so the site must be checked on USDA's eligibility map before application. If it qualifies, an unincorporated location earns 5 priority points [34].

Why does the model use an 80% guarantee rather than 85%?

Because of the loan size. For fiscal 2026, USDA guarantees 85% of B&I loans under $5 million and 80% of loans from $5 million to $25 million, with a 3.0% initial fee and a 0.55% annual retention fee in both tiers [32]. The sponsor's request and the restructured loan both sit in the upper tier.

Who competes for diesel and truck parking at Hankamer?

Two branded fuel stops at the SH 61 interchange, a Shell travel plaza that truck directories credit with 10 to 20 truck spaces and an Exxon store, neither confirmable on an operator website [16][17][18]. Two Stuckey's stores sit at Exit 819, TxDOT rest areas at mile markers 814 and 815 allow truck parking [12], and the large chains are at Exit 789 in Baytown, where Love's lists 160 truck spaces and TA 173 [13][14].

What traffic count supports the diesel volume in the model?

None from TxDOT yet. A broker listing for a comparable tract cites over 60,000 vehicles a day at Exit 814 [1], with no station or year. The diesel volume is benchmarked to an independent travel center's reported sales instead [45], and the TxDOT count and truck share are the first lender condition.

Could the B&I program itself disappear before this loan closes?

It is a real risk to timing. USDA's fiscal 2027 budget request proposes a B&I program level of zero [38], although fiscal 2025 obligations were a record $2,111,702,000. The enacted fiscal 2027 level was not found, so the lender should confirm funding availability with the Texas state office before the sponsor spends on design.
