Hill Country Lodging and Retreat Feasibility Study, Johnson City, Texas: An SBA 7(a) Worked Case

Market analysis by Mark Otava · Published October 16, 2026 · Data as of October 2026

Representative worked example. The site, sponsor and project are hypothetical. The market evidence comes from the public sources listed below, with their dates. This page shows how the market analysis is built and how it sets the loan. It is not a client engagement.

West of Johnson City, the US 290 wine corridor sells weekends: Carter Creek's lowest Saturday price in mid-October 2026 was $293 against $120 on Wednesday [18]. A small cabin retreat can earn that weekend rate, but its loan is tested on the weekdays too. At the sponsor's 10.0% start-up minimum the retreat covers debt 0.53x in Year 2. The market supports a $2,008,718 7(a) loan with 57.5% equity, which reaches 1.15x.

Study at a Glance

A small cabin retreat on the wine corridor, tested on its weekdays.

Market Conclusion

Strong weekends, thin weekdays, expensive land.

The Johnson City trade area supports a small retreat, but not a 90% loan. Demand is real and priced high on weekends: for the week of October 12, 2026, Carter Creek's lowest nightly prices ran $137 on Monday, $129 on Tuesday, $120 on Wednesday and $144 on Thursday, then $263 on Friday and $293 on Saturday [18]. Friday and Saturday more than double midweek. The resort's most direct guest review on Tripadvisor is titled "Don't stay on weekdays!" [20]. That is the market in one line: a weekend wine-trail destination that has not yet built a weekday business.

The organized supply is small. Four operators run 160 keys: Carter Creek's 78 villas, the Best Western's 53 rooms, the Presidio's 14 rooms and Walden's 15 safari tents [16][21][27][25]. No new lodging was found under construction in the trade area. The 2026 change was a repositioning, when a Houston boot company bought the 12-acre wine castle property and reopened its 14-room hotel as the Presidio in June 2026 [28][27]. The real supply wave is in the next ring, around Fredericksburg and Lake Travis, and it is luxury.

A retreat's case rests on filling midweek nights with groups: company offsites, wedding parties and small conferences that book the whole property. The model builds that business slowly. On that basis the retreat earns $242,293 of net operating income in Year 2 and $311,065 in Year 3.

The sponsor asked for the start-up minimum: 10.0% equity and a $4,312,059 7(a) loan. Year 2 coverage on that loan is 0.53x. The loan the market supports is $2,008,718, with 57.5% equity ($2,717,677), which reaches 1.15x in Year 2 and 1.47x in Year 3. The gap is driven by three facts: corridor land is dear, with the one improved corridor ask in the research at about $123,000 an acre [6], small cabins cost more per guest than hotel rooms, and the SBA test falls in Year 2, before a new retreat's group calendar has filled.

Demand

Wine-trail weekends replaced a federal park that stopped drawing.

Johnson City's demand base changed in two years. The town's historic anchor was the Lyndon B. Johnson National Historical Park. Visits there fell from 119,278 in 2024 to just over 38,000 in 2025, a drop of nearly 70%, according to NPS data reported by the Houston Chronicle, and the Texas White House has been closed to tours since August 2, 2018 [31][32]. A lodging plan built on park visitors would be built on a number that no longer exists.

The wine corridor took its place. Fredericksburg, west along US 290, counts more than 75 wineries, vineyards and tasting rooms [27]. The Hill Country's luxury wave confirms the demand: Forbes listed a 210-room Kimpton in Fredericksburg targeting late 2027, a Waldorf Astoria with 60 rooms, 42 resort villas and 50 residences on 106 acres, a 41-room boutique hotel approved at a Fredericksburg winery, and Canyon Ranch's 141-room resort near Lake Travis taking stays from October 15, 2026 [27]. Developers do not commit that capital to a region without weekend demand.

The local economy is small, affluent and older. Blanco County had 13,581 residents on July 1, 2025, up 19.4% from 11,378 in April 2020, against 8.8% growth for Texas [1]. Median household income was $92,425 in the 2020 to 2024 survey, 17.8% above the Texas median of $78,476 [1][3]. The median age was 51.5 against 35.6 for the state [2]. The county has 709.25 square miles of land and 16.0 people per square mile [1]; the 2020 Census counted it as 0.00% urban [5]. Johnson City itself had 1,627 residents in 2020 and about 2,200 by the city's 2026 estimate, and the city calls itself "mainly a tourist center" [7]. Its net taxable value rose from $155.19 million in 2017 to $433.00 million in 2026 [7].

Local residents do not fill cabins, so these figures matter for staffing and for the event business, not for room nights. The county had 5.62 thousand employed residents in 2024, led by construction (809), professional and technical services (595) and manufacturing (553) [4]. Housekeeping and event staff will be hard to hire in a county that size, which is why the model holds payroll as a fixed cost.

Blanco County lies just west of the Austin metro, not inside it. Its guests come from Austin, San Antonio and Houston, and its competitors include Austin-area retreats. Our Austin metro page covers the region the guests drive from, and the Texas market page places the Hill Country in the state picture.

Site

Corridor land: one benchmark ask, no zoning, no utility records.

The hypothetical site is about 12 acres of rural land on the US 290 wine corridor near Johnson City. The site is hypothetical, and so are its size, its jurisdiction, its utilities and its land cost: each is an assumption. The research holds one corridor land ask the study can use as a benchmark: 26 acres with a 2,280 square foot building built in 2019 and more than 500 feet of frontage, asking $3,198,000 when posted December 10, 2024 [6], about $123,000 an acre with the building included. One improved ask is not a range, so the land line is an allowance of $95,000 an acre, set below that ask because the subject buys raw land with no building, well or drive. A smaller tract often prices higher per acre than a large one, so the appraisal may come in above the model as easily as below it; until a contract and an appraisal exist, land is the least certain cost in the budget.

Zoning. The study assumes the site lies outside the city limits, where the city's zoning schedule does not apply unless the land is annexed; that assumption is a lender condition, and annexed land is temporarily zoned single-family [8]. The city rewrote its zoning in full with Ordinance 25-0402 on April 15, 2025 [8]. If the site is in the city, highway frontage falls in the Highway Commercial Corridor district, which allows 3 stories or 42 feet, 80% lot coverage on a minimum 6,000 square foot lot, and requires a 30-foot buffer and 8-foot fence next to residential land [8]. Conditional uses go through the Planning and Zoning Commission and City Council; a three-fourths council vote (4 of 5) is needed if the commission recommends denial or a 20% protest is filed, and a conditional use permit lapses if the use does not start within one year [8]. Whether lodging is permitted by right was not found; the schedule of uses was not retrieved. The city held joint hearings on August 18, 2026 on a downtown conditional use amendment and a rezoning request whose use was not stated [14].

Utilities. Service is an assumption too. No water or sewer provider was found for any corridor parcel, so the model budgets a well, a septic system and an electric line extension, the service rural acreage off a city system usually needs, and each one is a lender condition. The Blanco-Pedernales Groundwater Conservation District levies $0.016176 per $100 [7], and a lodging well will need its permit. Pedernales Electric Cooperative, the city's largest taxpayer at $40,267,365 of assessed value in 2025, is headquartered in Johnson City [7]; the provider for the parcel was not confirmed. Gas service was not found.

Traffic. Johnson City sits at the junction of US 281 and US 290, with no interstate in the county [7]. TxDOT's public count layer was updated January 6, 2025 [35], but no count for the site's segment was extracted.

Flood. The Hill Country flooded on July 4 to 5, 2025, with outages and local flooding across the cooperative's area [33]. On July 16, 2026 the Pedernales reached its highest level since 1979, with a forecast crest near 21 feet at Johnson City and flow of 89,000 cubic feet per second [34]. The site's FEMA zone was not found. Any parcel with creek or river exposure needs a flood determination and an insurance quote before the lender commits.

Taxes. The 2025 rates per $100 are $0.332300 for the city, $0.375232 for Blanco County, $0.860800 for Johnson City ISD, $0.100000 for the North Blanco ESD and $0.016176 for the groundwater district, $1.684508 in all inside the city [7]. The city held its rate at $0.3323 for 2026-27 [7]. The appraisal district's last published out-of-city combined rate was 1.347471 for 2024 [13]; the 2025 out-of-city total was not published in the record, so the model applies the 2025 county, ISD, ESD and groundwater rates. Guests pay the state's 6% hotel tax on rooms of $15 a day or more, including meeting and banquet rooms [11], plus the city's 7% inside the city, which also taxes cleaning and pet fees [9], or Blanco County's 7% outside the city and its extraterritorial jurisdiction [10]. Combined state and local hotel tax cannot exceed 17% [12], although the city's budget table adds its columns to 20.00% [7]; the table sums rates that do not all apply to one property. The city lists sales tax components of 6.25% state, 1.50% city, 1.00% ESD, 0.50% library district and 0.50% county against a stated total of 8.25% [7]. The listed components exceed the total, so at least one does not apply everywhere; the lender's accountant should confirm the rate for the site.

Cabins. 14 cabins rent at $283 in Year 3 at 56.0% occupancy. No ADR or occupancy is published for the trade area, so both are assumptions. The rate sits inside the observed price band: Carter Creek's lowest prices ran $120 to $293 for the week of October 12, 2026 before its resort fee [18], Walden showed $208 to $329 and up [23][24], and glamping operators nationally reported a 2025 ADR of $338 for cabins and tiny homes [37]. The occupancy stays just below AirDNA's 2026 forecast of 57.4% for U.S. short-term rentals [36], because weekdays are weak here. The model blends weekends and weekdays into one rate and one occupancy; a lender should ask the sponsor for the split, because the weekday half carries the risk.

Events. Retreats, weddings and group bookings are the weekday plan. The model counts 32 events in Year 3 at $6,365 each for venue, coordination and catering margin. No event count, festival calendar or venue rate was found, so both are assumptions and both are conditions. The test is simple: the sponsor should show signed or letter-of-intent bookings for the first year before closing.

Other. Pet fees, bike or kayak rentals and retail add 3.0% of cabin revenue. Under the city's ordinance the hotel tax applies to cleaning and pet fees as well as rooms [9], so the model treats them as revenue before tax, like room rent.

Glamping operators in a 2025 survey reported average gross revenue of $538,618 and operating costs of $292,917 across an average of 13.5 structures [37]. The subject's revenue per cabin is higher because of the event line and the corridor's rates; its costs are higher too, as the next section shows.

Operating costs are $726,743 in Year 3, leaving $311,065 of net operating income, a 30.0% margin. That is lower than the glamping survey's self-reported average and close to what a small full-time lodging business carries once a manager, housekeepers and an event coordinator are on payroll.

Payroll is $240,000 in Year 1, fixed and growing 3.0% a year, because a retreat needs a manager and cleaning staff whether the cabins are full or not. Laundry and guest supplies are $22 per occupied night. Booking commissions and marketing run at 10.0% of cabin revenue; a retreat that books most groups directly can beat that, and one that depends on online agencies will not. Direct event costs are 30.0% of event revenue. Utilities, repairs and grounds, administration and a replacement reserve run as shares of revenue; hot tubs, decks and furnishings wear quickly on small cabins.

Insurance starts at $36,000 and grows 5.0% a year. That is an allowance; the site's flood and wildfire conditions were not found, and the 2025 and 2026 Pedernales floods make a quote essential. Property tax applies the 2025 county, school, ESD and groundwater district rates to land and improvements at cost, growing 2.0% a year.

No management fee is charged, because the plan is owner-operated. That is a credit choice as much as a cost one: SBA's operating procedure bars passive businesses and directs lenders to review management agreements outside a listed franchise to decide whether they make the borrower passive [48]. A retreat run by its owner with its own staff is an operating business. Cabins handed to a vacation rental manager on a percentage-of-revenue fee may not be.

Total project cost is $4,726,395, or $337,600 per cabin. Land, at the study's allowance, is the largest single line after the cabins. Each cabin is carried at $120,000 built and set. Census put the 2025 South average for new single-family homes at $155.62 per square foot before land [38], and NAHB put the 2024 West South Central custom-home median at $138 [39]; a small cabin with a full bath, kitchenette, deck and hot tub costs more per square foot than a house, because its expensive rooms take up most of its floor area.

The lodge is carried at $520,000 and site work at $480,000, both allowances pending an engineer's estimate. Rate: Assumption: Prime was 7.00% on October 9, 2026 [43]. SBA caps the spread over Prime by loan size [41]; 2025 7(a) loans to bed-and-breakfasts averaged 9.49% [45]. We carry Prime plus 2.5 points. The lender sets the rate at pricing. Guaranty: 75% of the loan, Source [44].

Under SOP 50 10 8.1 with Technical Policy Updates, effective October 1, 2026, a 7(a) start-up must inject at least 10% equity and reach coverage of 1.15x, on the basis that historical and/or projected, with 1:1 global coverage; start-ups reach 1.15x within two years [41]. The model therefore tests Year 2. The loan cannot exceed $5 million [41]; the sponsor's request was below that, so the cap did not bind. SBA guarantees 75% of a loan between $350,001 and $5 million [44]. Because real estate is most of the project, the loan runs 25 years.

The rate is an assumption at 9.50%. Prime was 7.00% on October 9, 2026 [43], and SBA caps the spread a lender may charge over Prime for a loan of this size [41]. Bed-and-breakfast 7(a) loans averaged 9.49% across 26 loans and $35.1 million in 2025 [45]. The model carries Prime plus 2.5 points, and the lender sets the rate at pricing. The fiscal 2027 upfront guaranty fee applies to the guaranteed share under the notice in force [42].

The lodging rule. A lodging borrower must show more than 50% of the prior year's revenue from transients who stay 30 days or less at a time; a start-up shows it in projections [41]. A retreat whose guests stay two or three nights passes easily, and its projection should say so in a line of its own. The test bites only if the sponsor later leases cabins by the month to workers or long-stay tenants.

Ownership. Since March 1, 2026, every direct and indirect owner and every SBA-required guarantor must be a U.S. citizen or national; Policy Notice 5000-876441 rescinded the earlier notice that had allowed a small foreign-owned share, and lawful permanent residents no longer qualify [47]. The sponsor's ownership documents should be checked before any credit work.

Why not a 504. A 504 would split the project between a bank lien and a fixed-rate debenture. For a new business in a lodging property, which SBA treats as special purpose, the 504 contribution is 20% [41]. The 504's lower blended rate would lift coverage, but the restructured equity here is far above either program's minimum, so the binding constraint is the market, not the program. Our SBA 7(a) and 504 practice page compares the two. On the study itself: 13 CFR 120.160(b): SBA may require professional appraisals, a survey, or a feasibility study [46]. For a start-up lodging project with no history, the lender will usually want one.

As proposed: the sponsor proposes a single 25-year 7(a) loan at the start-up minimum, with 10.0% equity ($479,118) and $4,312,059 of debt. The structure does not qualify: coverage falls below the floor.

As restructured: 57.5% equity ($2,717,677) and $2,008,718 of debt with $210,601 of annual debt service. Every test is met.

As proposed, the sponsor put in 10.0% ($479,118) and asked for $4,312,059. The equity meets the start-up rule and the loan is within the 7(a) limit, but coverage is 0.53x in Year 2 and 0.68x in Year 3. The retreat cannot carry that loan.

As restructured, the loan falls to $2,008,718 with annual debt service of $210,601, and equity rises to 57.5% ($2,717,677). Coverage is 1.15x in Year 2, 1.47x in Year 3 and 1.64x in Year 5.

The Year 3 coverage shows that a stabilized retreat could carry more debt than the Year 2 test allows. The sponsor's options follow from that. A lower land basis, set by appraisal or by buying a smaller tract, cuts cost directly. A phased build of fewer cabins first lowers the loan and lets the event business prove itself before the second phase. Pre-sold group bookings for Year 1 and Year 2, documented before closing, would let the lender accept a faster ramp. Cash equity is the last of these, not the first.

The Townsend, Tennessee cabin resort case tests a larger cabin project near a national park through SBA 504 and reaches a similar conclusion for a different reason, a disagreement between data vendors. The Bastrop hotel case runs a Texas lodging project through 504 and finds its constraint in a hotel pipeline rather than in weekday demand.

The rate risk is real. The next-ring luxury openings will discount to fill their first seasons, and a guest choosing between a new resort with a spa and a cabin at a similar weekend price will often choose the resort. The retreat's defense is the whole-property group booking, which the large resorts cannot match.

Risks

What could break this retreat's projection.

  1. Weekday demand never arrives

    The corridor's pricing shows weekends selling and weekdays not [18][20]. If retreat groups do not book, the model's occupancy and event lines both fall short.

  2. Flood

    The Pedernales reached its highest level since 1979 in July 2026 [34], a year after the July 2025 floods [33]. A site with creek exposure can lose a season or its insurance.

  3. Luxury competition

    Canyon Ranch opened to stays in October 2026, Mirasol Springs targets 2027 and a Kimpton targets late 2027 [27][29]. New resorts compete for the same corporate retreat and the same weekend couple.

  4. Land cost

    The land line is an allowance bounded by a single improved corridor ask [6], not a sale. A smaller tract can price higher per acre, so the appraisal could land above the model as easily as below it.

  5. Labor

    A county of 13,581 people with 5.62 thousand employed residents [1][4] is a thin labor pool for housekeeping and events. Wages may have to exceed the model's payroll to staff the property.

  6. Passive-business drift

    If the owner hands operations to a vacation rental manager, the loan's eligibility comes into question [48].

  7. Water and septic

    A lodging well and septic system on a dry Hill Country tract need groundwater district and state permits, and capacity limits the cabin count.

Lender Conditions

Conditions before commitment.

The research listed 18 open items. They reduce to 13 conditions, below.

  1. Land. A purchase contract for the specific acreage, an appraisal with comparable corridor sales that replaces the land allowance, a survey and the appraisal district's parcel record.
  2. Jurisdiction and zoning. City-limit and extraterritorial jurisdiction status; if in the city, the zoning district and whether lodging is allowed by right or by conditional use under Ordinance 25-0402; the expected approval timeline.
  3. Flood. A FEMA flood zone determination and elevation information for every structure site.
  4. Water, septic and power. A well permit path with the groundwater district, a septic design and permit, the electric provider's line extension estimate, and gas or propane service.
  5. Traffic and access. The TxDOT station count and year on US 290 at the site, and a driveway permit path.
  6. Insurance. A bound quote for wind, flood and wildfire.
  7. Costs. A contractor's estimate for the cabins, lodge and site work, replacing the model's allowances, and any impact or connection fees from the city's fee schedule.
  8. Taxes. The 2025 and 2026 out-of-city combined property tax rate from the appraisal district, and the sales tax rate that applies at the site.
  9. Demand data. The city's hotel occupancy tax reports for 2021 to 2024, any wine-trail visitor or festival attendance data, and a short-term rental market report for the trade area with weekday and weekend splits.
  10. Competitors. Opening years and appraisal records for Carter Creek, the Best Western and Walden; operator rates for Carter Creek, Walden and the Presidio; sizes and rates for the Ladybird Inn, Hes' Country Store Bed and Breakfast, Flint & Flowers, Texas Glampgrounds and The Retreat on the Hill; the count of whole-home rentals; the property behind H K Hospitality LLC.
  11. Pipeline. City lodging permits for 2025 to 2026 and the use proposed in the August 2026 rezoning request.
  12. Events. Letters of intent or deposits for first-year retreats and weddings supporting the event count.
  13. Program. Citizenship documentation for every owner and guarantor; an owner-operator plan with no passive management agreement; the rate set at pricing; SBA loan-level data for Blanco County lenders from the public file [50].

Two items stay as context: HUBZone and Opportunity Zone status for Blanco County tracts were not verified, and no construction cost index for the county exists. Johnson City Retreat Questions

Questions lenders ask about this Hill Country retreat case.

Is Johnson City part of the Austin market?

Not formally. Blanco County lies just west of the Austin metro, in the Hill Country, and the 2020 Census counted it as entirely rural [5]. Its guests come from Austin, San Antonio and beyond, but its hotel tax, zoning and appraisal are local, and Johnson City had about 2,200 residents by the city's 2026 estimate [7].

Why did the study use SBA 7(a) instead of 504?

The research assumed 7(a), and this case adopts it. A 504 would lower the blended rate, but for a new business in a lodging property it asks for more equity at the start, and the binding problem here is Year 2 coverage, which a 504 would not remove. The case should be rerun as a 504 if the sponsor prefers fixed-rate debt.

What happened to the LBJ park as a demand driver?

It collapsed. Visits to Lyndon B. Johnson National Historical Park fell from 119,278 in 2024 to just over 38,000 in 2025, a drop of nearly 70%, as reported from NPS data [31]. The Texas White House has been closed to tours since August 2, 2018 [32]. The retreat's demand comes from the wine corridor, weddings and corporate groups, not the park.

Can the sponsor hire a short-term rental manager to run the cabins?

Carefully. SBA bars passive businesses, and its operating procedure tells lenders to review management agreements outside a listed franchise brand to see whether they make the borrower a passive owner [48]. An owner-operated retreat with its own staff passes that review more easily than cabins handed to a vacation rental manager.

How much does flood risk matter here?

A great deal for any site near the Pedernales or its creeks. The river reached its highest level since 1979 on July 16, 2026, with a forecast crest near 21 feet at Johnson City and flow of 89,000 cubic feet per second [34]. The site's FEMA zone was not found, so a flood determination and an insurance quote are conditions before commitment.

Does the luxury pipeline in Fredericksburg help or hurt?

Both. A Kimpton with 210 rooms, a Waldorf Astoria with 60 rooms and 42 villas, and an Auberge inn of about 71 rooms raise the region's profile and its rates [27][29]. They also compete for the same drive-in weekend guest and the same corporate retreat. A small retreat wins on price and privacy, not on amenities.

Planning a Hill Country lodging project? Test the weekdays first.

We separate weekend and midweek demand, price the land and the flood risk, and size the loan to the year the lender actually tests.
