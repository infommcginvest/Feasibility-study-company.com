RV Park and Campground Feasibility Study, Bastrop County, Texas: A USDA B&I Worked Case

Market analysis by Mark Otava · Published July 13, 2026 · Updated October 16, 2026 · Data as of October 2026

Representative worked example. The site, sponsor and project are hypothetical. The market evidence comes from the public sources listed below, with their dates. This page shows how the market analysis is built and how it sets the loan. It is not a client engagement.

Around Bastrop, RV sites are housing as much as they are vacation stays: the town's long-stay parks post monthly rents of $550 to $800 plus electric, and one lists 49 of its 66 spaces as seasonal [19][25][21]. That demand is deep, but USDA guarantees resort campgrounds, not residential trailer parks [50], so the plan keeps most of its rent from nightly and weekly guests. At the sponsor's 20.0% equity the park covers debt 1.11x in Year 3; at 28.7% equity and a $2,719,820 guaranteed loan it reaches 1.25x.

Market Conclusion

Long-stay demand is the strength. It is also the constraint.

Bastrop's RV market is a long-stay market with a leisure season on top. The evidence for long stays is in the operators' own price lists: every park that posts rates offers a monthly rent, clustered from $550 to $800 a month plus metered electric [19][25][29][31][32]. Basin RV Resort, a Good Sam park on SH 71, lists 49 of its 66 spaces as seasonal [21]. Behind that demand stands the Starlink campus, where SpaceX told the Commissioners Court in May 2026 that it had more than 1,500 employees in Bastrop [41], and a housing market in which the median gross rent was $1,443 and the median owner-occupied home was valued at $339,600 in the 2020 to 2024 survey [2]. For a contractor on a two-year assignment, a $700 pad is cheap housing.

The leisure layer is real but seasonal. Bastrop State Park drew 133,102 visitors in 2025, and the parks department says the park often reaches capacity, with a busy season in spring, summer and fall and prescribed burns from October to March that can close parts of it [37][35]. Buescher State Park, the next park down SH 71, drew 53,588 [38].

The financing turns on which of these two demands the park serves. USDA's Business and Industry program lists resort trailer parks and campgrounds as eligible tourist facilities and excludes residential trailer parks [50]. A park that becomes workforce housing drifts toward the excluded category. The plan therefore holds 26 of 64 RV sites for monthly stays and earns most of its rent from nightly and weekly sites and 6 cabins. That choice costs income: the monthly section is the easiest to fill.

On that plan the park earns $308,247 of net operating income in Year 3. The sponsor proposed 20.0% equity and $3,059,783 of guaranteed debt, which covers Year 3 debt service only 1.11x. Restructured to 28.7% equity and a $2,719,820 loan, coverage reaches 1.25x in Year 3 and 1.38x in Year 5. The market supports the park. It supports less debt than the sponsor asked for, because a new park's transient sites take three years to fill and the county has already approved a much larger competitor.

Demand

Three flows: workforce stays, state park weekends and in-migration.

Workforce stays. The Starlink and SpaceX complex on FM 1209 is the county's largest source of long-stay demand. In August 2026 SpaceX filed a $7 million office renovation at the site with the state, with work to finish in fall 2027 [40]. The same reporting described a $280 million semiconductor research project backed by a $17.3 million state grant and expected to bring more than 400 jobs [40]. In May 2026 the county backed SpaceX as an Enterprise Project for an $855 million Starlink kit expansion [41]. Construction crews for these projects, and for the data centers and warehouses that follow them, need housing by the week and month. RV parks are where many of them stay.

Leisure. Bastrop State Park is the anchor. Its reservation system books 78 units: 27 full-hookup, 17 electric and 20 water-only campsites, 12 cabins, a barracks and a group hall, priced in peak season at $25 for full hookup, $20 for electric, $15 for water only, $110 to $200 for cabins and $850 a night for the barracks [36]. Group lodging is the state's business here; the subject has no group building, and the model earns nothing from groups. That inventory is small and cheap and it fills, which pushes overflow to private parks nearby. The leisure season runs spring through fall, and winter burns reduce park access [35]. Nationally, more than 52 million households camped in 2025 and spent $66 billion locally, but RVs accounted for 47% of trips, the lowest share in KOA's series [69].

In-migration. The county had 118,908 residents on July 1, 2025, 22.3% more than in April 2020 [1]. People moving to a county faster than it builds housing use RV sites as a bridge. That demand is real but it is monthly, so it adds to the same pressure on the tenure mix.

The county's lodging tax shows how small the overnight leisure market outside the city is. Bastrop County collected $106,357.61 of hotel occupancy tax from properties outside city limits in fiscal 2025, against $421,700.95 inside them, for $528,058.56 in all [16]. RV stays under 30 days are taxable lodging; monthly stays are not [15]. A park outside the city, as the model assumes, would sit in the smaller of those two pools.

Three facts stand out. First, the monthly price band is tight. Five operators post monthly rents between $550 and $800 [19][25][29][31][32], and most add metered electric and a deposit; Basin asks a $100 deposit and the Pines $250 [19][25]. A new park cannot price far above that band without better pads, and the model's $743 monthly rent in Year 3 sits inside it. Second, nightly prices are low against national figures. ARVC's 2023 survey found a median of $55 midweek and $58 on weekends for full-hookup sites [58]; Bastrop's posted nightly rates run from $35 at the newest park to $70 for a pull-through at the Pines [32][25]. Third, apart from the B J Mayes Road park, none of the confirmed parks reports a recent opening, so the subject would be the newest product with the largest pads in the set.

Two more names lack an operator page. Colorado River RV Park on FM 969 appears only on a booking platform, which lists $140 a week for a full-hookup site in 2026 [39]. Prickly Pear RV Park on 3 Oaks Lane is named only in local news. Both stay out of the table, and their size and rates are a lender condition. The county appraisal district's records for the whole set were not retrieved; they would give acreage and assessed value per site, the best local evidence of land value for the subject.

The Cedar Creek approval is the material supply risk. Its 200 stands and 40 cabins are several times the subject's program, and a park of that size will price to fill. If it opens first, it sets the market's monthly rent and absorbs the leisure overflow from the state park. If it opens second, it arrives in the subject's ramp years. No other 2025 to 2026 RV permits were found in the county record; that search is a lender condition.

Site

An unconfirmed tract under the county's RV park rules.

The hypothetical site is about 10 acres in the SH 71 and SH 21 trade area around Bastrop; no particular tract is assumed. Its jurisdiction is a condition to confirm, because it sets both the approval path and the tax bill. The study assumes a site outside city limits, in unincorporated Bastrop County. There the county has no general zoning power, so there is no zoning district, and the binding approval is the county's [3]. A site inside the city or its extraterritorial jurisdiction would face a different path and a different tax rate, and the lender should not rely on this section for one. Road access is a condition too: the county rules set the frontage a park needs by the class of road it fronts (below).

The approval path. Bastrop County treats an RV park as a subdivision under Chapter 232 of the Local Government Code [3]. The developer holds a required pre-application meeting, files an application and an Infrastructure Development Plan, and the county reviews the file for completeness within 10 business days. A 30-day review follows, then a Commissioners Court vote; a plan not rejected within the review period is approved [3]. Utilities may not serve the park until the county issues a Certificate of Compliance [3]. A development permit expires if construction is not finished within three years, and a septic permit expires one year after its authorization to construct [4].

Design standards that drive cost. The rules require at least 40 feet of deeded frontage on a county road (60 on a state road), a hardened pad of 12 by 40 feet for each RV space, 20 feet between units with slide-outs, setbacks of 25 feet from the right-of-way, 15 from property lines and 10 from internal roads, two-way roads at least 20 feet wide and underground electric [3]. A service building is required for every 50 spaces, plus one fixture set for each 30 more [3]. Where there are no hydrants, a park must store 2,500 gallons of fire water below 50 spaces and 5,000 gallons at 50 or more; where hydrants exist they must deliver 250 gallons a minute for two hours from a main of six inches or more [3]. These standards are why the model's site cost sits above the older survey ranges.

Sewer and water. A park producing 5,000 gallons a day or less needs a county septic permit; above that, it needs state approval under the Water Code [3]. A park of this size with a bathhouse and laundry may approach that line, so the engineering decides whether the subject needs a county or a state permit. Water, sewer and electric providers for the hypothetical tract are not known and become a lender condition; gas service was not found anywhere in the research.

Endangered species. Parts of the county fall under the Lost Pines Habitat Conservation Plan for the Houston toad. Participation costs a $120 application fee and a $54 recording fee, with mitigation of $1,500 for half an acre or $3,000 for an acre of disturbance; direct impact over an acre needs a federal incidental take permit [5]. Whether the tract lies in toad habitat is a condition.

Traffic. County roads are not on TxDOT's count system, and the site's access road is not known. The nearest counts are on SH 71 and SH 21 in TxDOT's public layer, updated January 6, 2025 [18]; no station count was extracted. For an RV park the relevant traffic is the turn from the state highway onto the site's access road, which a site visit should check for rig clearance.

Taxes. The county adopted $0.4554 per $100 for fiscal 2026-27 [71], up from $0.42870 for 2025-26 and $0.40275 for 2024-25 [10], so the county levy has risen two years running and the model's 2.0% yearly growth in taxable value is not the only way the bill climbs. Bastrop ISD's 2025-26 rate is $1.0679 [11]. Emergency services districts No. 2 and No. 3 each adopted $0.10 for 2025 [12][13]; which district covers the tract is a condition. The model assumes a site outside city limits, where there is no city levy; a site inside the city would add one. Property is assessed at 100% of market value [14]. Guests staying under 30 days pay the state's 6% and the county's 7% hotel tax outside the city; inside the city or its extraterritorial jurisdiction the city's 7% applies instead of the county's, plus a 0.75% county share [15][17]. All county lodging properties have filed online through Localgov since October 2, 2023 [15].

Fire. The 2011 fire burned 34,068 acres of the Lost Pines [42]. Wildfire drives insurance cost and availability for a park among pines, and the model's insurance line is an allowance until a quote is bound.

The revenue build separates the park by tenure, because tenure decides both price and program eligibility. Total revenue is $645,305 in Year 1 and $883,507 in Year 3.

Monthly sites. 26 of the 64 RV sites are held for monthly stays at $743 a month in Year 3, before metered electric, with occupancy of 93.0%. The rent sits in the upper half of the posted band because the subject's pads are new and sized for large rigs. Rent grows about 3% a year, the rate Newmark's 2026 survey uses for RV parks [61]. Electric is billed to the tenant at cost, as the local parks do, so it appears in neither revenue nor expense.

Transient sites. The other RV sites rent by the night and week at a blended $55.00 in Year 3, reflecting weekly discounts. Occupancy ramps to 60.0%. ARVC's 2023 survey put full-hookup occupancy at 68% in the months parks were open [58], but the national transient business has weakened since. Equity LifeStyle's core seasonal and transient rent fell from $126.7 million to $115.1 million in 2025 [62]. Sun Communities' RV same-property NOI fell 1.4% in 2025 [63]. Campspot reported transient demand up 6% in January 2026 and 3.6% in February, then negative from April as gasoline crossed $4.00 a gallon [64]. The model's transient ramp stops short of ARVC's 2023 figure for those reasons.

Cabins. Six cabins rent at $133 in Year 3 at 55.0% occupancy. The state park's cabins run $110 to $200 a night [36] and Basin rents a poolside apartment at $130 plus tax with a two-night minimum [20]. Cabins are the line that brings families and state park overflow, and they are the most transient revenue in the plan.

Store and services. A camp store, laundry, propane and a few storage spaces add 6.0% of rent. Bellamont's $95 a month for RV storage shows the local price for that service [31].

Monthly rent stays the smaller share of the park's rent in every year of the projection. That share is the number the lender should watch every year, because the program's line between a resort campground and a residential park is drawn by use, not by intention.

Payroll is fixed at $200,000 in Year 1 and grows 3.0% a year. Total project cost is $3,814,615, or $54,494 for each RV site or cabin. Site development is carried at $25,000 per RV site. ARVC's 2023 survey of new parks found 60% of respondents spending $15,000 or more per full-hookup site and 20% spending $30,000 or more [58]. A state filing for a 176-site resort in Wichita Falls estimated $3.5 million of construction [59]. Construction inputs rose 8.9% in the year to August 2026 [60], and Bastrop County's pad, road and fire-water standards push the cost toward the upper bands.

Land is the weakest number in the build. The research holds no closed land sale and no assessed land value for the trade area, so land is carried at $50,000 an acre as a labelled placeholder, and the appraisal is a condition. The cabins, the office and bathhouse building, the contingency, soft costs and furnishings are assumptions, each stated in the inputs table. B&I money may pay for working capital, which a 504 debenture does not, and that is why the park's pre-opening costs are counted in the project here.

The USDA guarantee fee of 3.5% of the guaranteed amount is added to project cost and shown as a use [47]: the 3.0% initial fee plus 0.50% because the guarantee issues before construction is complete. For comparison, Streamside Parks announced in February 2026 that it paid $97 million for seven established resorts with 1,562 RV sites, about $62,100 per site [70]. Stabilized parks set a value ceiling that a new park's cost should not exceed by much.

The B&I guarantee for fiscal 2026 is 85% for loans under $5,000,000 and 80% from $5,000,000 to the $25,000,000 maximum, with an initial fee of 3.0% of the guaranteed amount, a further 0.50% when the loan note guarantee issues before construction is complete, as it does for this park, and an annual retention fee of 0.55% [47]. USDA raised the small-loan guarantee from 80% to 85% and announced it on March 27, 2026 [44]. A reduced-fee category carries a 1.0% fee and 0.50% retention but only an 80% guarantee [47]; the park does not need it.

The rate is negotiated between the lender and the borrower and may be fixed, variable or a mix [48]. The model carries 7.75%, which is Prime of 7.00% on October 9, 2026 [66] plus 0.75 points; broker-reported conventional RV park closings in 2026 ran 6.74% to 7.16% [67]. The term may run to the useful life of the project or collateral, up to 40 years [48]. RV pads, roads and pedestals do not last 40 years, so the model amortizes over 25.

Two parameters are assumptions, not rules. The equity test uses 25.0% of project cost. The research reads 7 CFR 5001.105 as requiring a new business to hold at least 20% to 25% tangible balance sheet equity or 25% of project cost [51]; the lender's credit memo must confirm the figure. The coverage floor is 1.25x in Year 3, because USDA sets no single ratio for B&I and the lender's credit policy governs. The regulation asks for projections through at least two years at full capacity or stable operations [49], which is why the test year is the first stabilized year.

The feasibility study. USDA requires a feasibility study by an independent qualified consultant acceptable to the Agency for a B&I guaranteed loan of more than $1,000,000 to a new business (7 CFR 5001.306(a)(3)(i)) [49]. The restructured loan of $2,719,820 to a newly formed park company meets both tests. The study must cover economic, market, technical, financial and management feasibility [50], and the lender's credit analysis must include its own written evaluation of the study [52]. Our note on the five USDA components sets out what each part must prove, and the USDA Rural Development practice page covers the other programs.

Eligibility. The project must sit outside any city or town of more than 50,000 people and its adjacent urbanized area, and the project site governs [43][46]. Bastrop is far below that size and the model assumes a site outside city limits, but the site's location is a condition and the OneRD lookup must be run on the parcel [45]. An unincorporated site or a city under 25,000 earns 5 priority points [49].

Program risk. B&I obligated $2,111,702,000 in fiscal 2025, and Texas took $289.2 million of it, 13.7% of the national total [54]. The fiscal 2026 loan authority is $1,750.0 million in the enacted act [55], while USDA's own table shows $1,428,899,083 [54]; the two have not been reconciled. The fiscal 2027 budget request proposes no B&I program level at all [54], and the enacted outcome was not found in a primary source. On May 12, 2026 USDA revoked the approved-lender status of ten lenders whose portfolios held about $620 million of delinquent loans, about 47% of Rural Development's delinquencies [53]. B&I loan counts fell from 309 in fiscal 2015 to 176 in fiscal 2024 [56]. The sponsor's lender should be one of the remaining approved lenders, active in Texas, and the application should be timed against the appropriation; our USDA deadline calendar tracks what is open.

For contrast, an SBA lender would test the park against SBA's lodging rule, which asks for more than 50% of the prior year's revenue from transients who stay 30 days or less at a time; a start-up shows it in projections [68]. This plan passes that test on paper. A Bastrop park whose monthly section grows with workforce demand could fail it later, which is one reason USDA is the better fit for a park with a real monthly component.

The break-even moves only transient occupancy and holds the monthly section, the cabins and rates constant. In Year 3 the park covers its debt service until transient occupancy falls to 49.4%, a cushion of 17.7%. It meets the 1.25x floor down to 60.0%.

Those are low thresholds compared with ARVC's 68% figure for 2023 [58], which is the point: with a monthly base underneath, the nightly sites do not have to be full to pay the loan. The risk is that the monthly base and the nightly sites fall together, as they would if a Starlink slowdown cut both contractor stays and the weekday traffic that fills nightly pads. Our note on the break-even occupancy trap explains why a single-driver break-even understates that kind of joint risk.

Risks

What could break this park's projection.

  1. The Cedar Creek park

    An approved 200-stand, 40-cabin park on 43.2 acres [6][7] can set both the monthly rent and the cabin rate for the east side of the county. Its opening date is not in the record.

  2. Tenure drift

    Workforce demand will press the park to rent more sites by the month. Past a point, USDA's exclusion of residential trailer parks [50] becomes an eligibility question for the guaranteed loan, and the lender's covenants should set a ceiling on the monthly share.

  3. One employer

    SpaceX's statement of more than 1,500 Bastrop employees [41] is the base of long-stay demand. A pause in its construction programs would cut monthly and weekday nightly demand together.

  4. Transient softness

    The REITs' 2025 transient declines and Campspot's spring 2026 slowdown [62][63][64] are national, but a new park's nightly ramp is the most exposed line in the model.

  5. Wildfire and insurance

    The 2011 fire burned 34,068 acres of the Lost Pines [42]. A park among pines may face high premiums or limited carriers, which the model carries only as an allowance.

  6. Program funding and lender base

    The fiscal 2027 request proposes no B&I program level [54], and ten lenders lost approved status in May 2026 [53]. A delayed appropriation or a lender exit can stall the guarantee after the land is bought.

  7. Septic and county timing

    A park near 5,000 gallons a day can move from a county permit to a state one [3], and permits expire on fixed clocks [4]. Engineering should settle the permit route before closing.

  2. Land value and title. An appraisal with comparable sales of acreage in the trade area, a survey and a title commitment; the model's land price is a placeholder.

Bastrop RV Park Questions

Questions lenders ask about this Bastrop RV park case.

Does USDA require a feasibility study for this RV park?

The park company is newly formed and its guaranteed loan is well over $1,000,000, and that pairing is what makes a study compulsory under 7 CFR 5001.306(a)(3)(i): USDA wants it written by an independent consultant the Agency accepts [49]. In a campground file, the hardest question the study answers is the mix of nightly and monthly guests, since B&I funds resort campgrounds and not residential trailer parks [50]. Once the study is in hand, the lender adds its own written evaluation of it to the credit analysis [52].

Why not fill the park with monthly workforce tenants, since that demand is strongest?

Because the program funds resort trailer parks and campgrounds and excludes residential trailer parks [50]. A park that rents most of its sites by the month to people who live there starts to look residential. The plan holds monthly stays to a minority of the RV sites and earns most of its rent from nightly, weekly and cabin guests.

Would an SBA 7(a) loan be simpler?

Only if the park passes SBA's transient test for lodging businesses [68]. This plan would pass on paper, but a Bastrop park whose monthly section grows to meet workforce demand could fail it in a later year. USDA's 85% guarantee under $5 million also gives the lender more cover on a start-up [47].

How big is the new competition?

Bastrop County's Commissioners Court approved a 43.2-acre park in Cedar Creek with 200 RV stands and 40 cabins on March 9, 2026 [6][7]. A Smithville park with 77 sites received its county development permit in August 2025 [8][9], and a new park on B J Mayes Road opened January 1, 2026 [32]. The approved Cedar Creek project alone is several times the size of this subject.

Is the site eligible as rural?

Probably, but it has not been confirmed. B&I funds projects outside cities of more than 50,000 people and their adjacent urbanized areas, and the site governs [43]. The model assumes a site outside city limits, but where the site sits is itself a condition, and the OneRD eligibility lookup must be run on the exact parcel before application [45].

What is the biggest risk to the projection?

Transient occupancy. The two listed RV REITs reported 2025 declines of about 9% in transient revenue [62][63], and the model needs nightly occupancy to climb for three seasons. The break-even table shows how far that can slip before coverage fails.
