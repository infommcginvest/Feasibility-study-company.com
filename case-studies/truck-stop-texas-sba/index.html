A New Truck Stop at I-20 Exit 498, Terrell, Texas: An SBA 504 Feasibility Case

Market analysis by Mark Otava · Published July 13, 2026 · Data as of October 2026

Representative worked example. The site, sponsor and project are hypothetical. The market evidence comes from the public sources listed below, with their dates. This page shows how the market analysis is built and how it sets the loan. It is not a client engagement.

Terrell's freight base is growing fast, with an Amazon distribution center due in 2027 and speculative warehouses rising on US 80 [27][30], and the I-20 corridor carries a truck stop's customers. A new independent truck stop on Highway Corridor land at Exit 498 earns $837,822 in Year 2 and covers its SBA 504 debt only 0.69x at the 20% equity a new business on special purpose property must bring. No 504 structure clears 1.15x in Year 2: the corridor supports the use, but the interchange's land price and 2026 build costs do not support this loan.

Market Conclusion

The freight is coming. The land at this exit is priced for someone else.

Terrell is where Dallas's eastward growth meets the I-20 freight corridor. The city grew 35.5% between April 2020 and July 2025 [4], Kaufman County was among the ten fastest-growing counties in the country in the year to July 2025 [5], and the industrial pipeline is concrete rather than announced: Amazon has filed for a distribution center of 1,160,399 square feet due in August 2027, and VanTrust is building two speculative logistics buildings at US 80 and Spur 557 [28][30]. Every one of those facts points to more trucks on the roads that feed a truck stop at FM 148, although no current TxDOT count for I-20 at the exit was retrieved.

The supply side is thinner than the demand story suggests, and that is the case for a new site. The one full-service travel center in the corridor, TA Terrell #0233, sits at Exit 503 on Wilson Road with 300 truck spaces and five service bays [8]. At Exit 498 itself, the ONE9 dealer at FM 148 lists five fuel lanes and 12 public parking spaces, and Buc-ee's, the largest fuel retailer at the exit, does not admit 18-wheelers [9][15]. No Love's, Pilot-branded or Flying J truck stop was verified inside Terrell, and no new truck stop was found in the 2025 or 2026 pipeline. A mid-size independent with five diesel lanes and about 80 truck spaces would fill a real gap at the interchange where trucks leave I-20 for FM 148 and US 80.

The case stops on cost, not demand. Interchange land at Exit 498 is priced at the level a Buc-ee's exit commands; the case assumes the sponsor buys about 12.0 acres there at $310,000 an acre, an assumed 2026 asking-price level that is deliberately not tied to any listing, and builds at 2026 prices, for a total project cost of $16,666,199. Under SOP 50 10 8.1, a new truck stop is a new business on special purpose property, so the borrower's contribution is 20%. At that contribution the bank loan and the debenture together cost $1,211,336 a year, and the truck stop earns $837,822 in Year 2: coverage of 0.69x against a 1.15x standard. More equity does not solve it, because in a 504 project extra cash shrinks the debenture and leaves the bank's first lien, half the project, in place; that lien alone needs more coverage than Year 2 provides.

The determination is that the market supports a truck stop at Exit 498 but does not support this loan. The lever is the land: the I-20 tract at Exit 509 that was marketed for a truck stop sold in March 2026 after listing at $1,300,000 for 16.63 acres [23], a fraction of the per-acre price assumed here. A sponsor who can secure interchange land near that basis, or a smaller parcel at Exit 498, has a project worth re-testing. For the asset class itself, see the truck stop and travel center guide; for the metro context, the Dallas-Fort Worth market page and the Texas state page.

Two other cases frame this one. The Hankamer travel center case on I-10 finances a comparable asset with a USDA Business and Industry guarantee in a rural county, where the program and the land both work differently. The Cartersville gas station case looks at an interchange where two national travel centers already serve diesel and concludes that a newcomer should stay out of the truck business; Terrell is the opposite case, where the truck customer is underserved and the obstacle is price.

Demand

Freight generators along Airport Road and US 80, and a city that grew by a third.

Truck-stop demand comes from the interstate, not from the residents of the town that hosts it, so the primary trade area is the I-20 corridor from Exit 498 to Exit 509, about ten miles, plus the local distribution centers that send trucks onto it. FHWA's national model sizes truck parking demand from truck-hours of travel on a highway segment and assigns 77% of it to commercial truck stops, the rest to public rest areas [44]; hours-of-service rules, an 11-hour driving limit after 10 hours off and a 30-minute break after 8 hours of driving, decide where drivers stop [60]. Neither input was available for I-20 at Terrell: the truck share of the corridor's traffic was not found. The demand case therefore rests on generators and growth.

The generators are what make Terrell different from a typical interstate exit. Cross-dock buildings exist to move trailers in and out; the Amazon center and the two VanTrust cross docks will add trucks that start and end their trips within a few miles of Exit 498. The three VanTrust buildings and Amazon's ITX7 alone add millions of square feet of logistics space within a few years [28][30]. Local trucks buy diesel and park between loads, and many of their drivers want food and showers without driving east to TA at Exit 503.

Two discounts apply. First, the Amazon figures conflict: the state filing shows 1,160,399 square feet, the newspaper roughly 1.2 million, and an earlier filing 933,656 [27][28][29]. The study uses none of them as a volume input; they establish that a large facility is coming, not how many trucks it sends. Second, speculative buildings fill on the market's schedule, not the developer's. The model therefore phases diesel volume up over three years and reaches its stabilized level only after both projects are due to finish.

For scale on what a destination fuel stop sells, Buc-ee's representatives said in June 2026 that a typical Buc-ee's travel center does $25 million a year in gross sales and sells 18 million gallons [32]. That store excludes trucks, and its number is a company-wide claim, but it shows how much of the car traffic at Exit 498 is spoken for. Seasonality and events specific to the corridor were not found; the model treats freight demand as steady.

The inner ring is small. Inside Terrell, only TA offers the full program of parking, scales, service and food that a long-haul driver plans a stop around [8]. The ONE9 dealer at FM 148 is a fuel stop with a dozen public spaces [9]. QuikTrip #993 sells truck diesel on State Highway 34 but is a convenience store, not a truck stop [16]. Buc-ee's dominates the car market at the exit with a store that drew an appraised owner-level value of $15,909,580 in 2020, up from $7,961,870 in its opening year, but keeps trucks out [11][12][13][15]. It did not appear on the 2023 list of Terrell's ten largest taxpayers, where tenth place was $23.68 million [14].

TA's posted prices on October 15, 2026, $5.999 for diesel and $3.739 for unleaded [8], set the reference for what a new site would charge. EIA's October outlook forecast a 2026 U.S. retail diesel average of $5.19 a gallon, against $3.66 in 2025 [51], so diesel at Terrell was posted well above both EIA's 2026 forecast average and the 2025 average. High pump prices invite fleets to shop, and a new independent can win fuel business with a modest discount. It cannot win parking: TA's 300 spaces are far more than the program this case builds [8].

Several names in directories could not be confirmed and stay out of the table. A Van Travel Plaza listed at 1102 FM 148, next to the ONE9 dealer, has no operator page and no known status. A "Love's Travel Stop #221, Terrell" appears in a single directory and nowhere on Love's site. Dukes Travel Plaza on I-20 east of Terrell appears only in directories. Each is a lender condition, because an operating truck stop at 1102 FM 148 would change the Exit 498 picture.

The pipeline is quiet. City hearing notices for September and October 2025 and for December 2025 and January 2026 carry no travel-center Specific Use Permit [24][25]. The nearest competing land is the Exit 509 tract marketed for an RV park, truck stop or storage, which sold on March 11, 2026 with no announced use [23]. Outside the trade area, the chains keep building: Love's opened a Big Spring site on I-20 with 105 truck spaces and nine diesel service bays by September 2025 and planned 20 new stores and 1,500 truck spaces for 2026 [35][39]. Any of them could choose Terrell.

Site, Zoning and Utilities

Highway Corridor zoning, a Specific Use Permit and no utility record.

The subject is a hypothetical site of about 12.0 acres at the I-20 and FM 148 interchange, inside Terrell city limits and assumed to lie in the Highway Corridor district, the zoning the city applies along IH 20, US 80 and Spur 557 [1]. The acreage is roughly what a comparable Love's build used of a 30-acre site in Rock Falls, Illinois [40]. The case assumes frontage on FM 148 and access from the I-20 ramps; the actual frontage and ramp configuration are lender conditions. The site, its size and its zoning are assumptions, and no real parcel, owner or listing is represented.

Zoning and entitlement

Terrell's zoning ordinance, as amended by Ordinance No. 3138 on September 30, 2025, lists Use #416, Truck Stop and Fuel Station, with an "S": a Specific Use Permit is required, and the use is permitted by right in no district [1]. The use chart's district column for that "S" could not be resolved from the published PDF, so the city's confirmation that Highway Corridor is one of the districts where the permit is available is the first entitlement condition. The same chart marks some uses "R", prohibited within 700 feet of the IH 20, US 80 and Spur 557 rights-of-way in the Highway Corridor district [1]; whether Use #416 carries that mark was not determined, and it matters for a site on the interstate.

Two recent hearing cycles show the timetable. A Specific Use Permit for a wrecker and salvage use went to the Planning and Zoning Commission on September 25, 2025 and to Council on October 7 [24]. A later notice posted November 25, 2025 scheduled Commission and Council hearings on December 18, 2025 and January 13, 2026 [25]. Commercial permits then go through the city's online portal [26]. Entitlement is a matter of weeks once the application is complete; the risk is the vote, not the calendar, and truck stops draw the noise, air and litter objections FHWA's 2026 handbook documents [60].

Utilities, traffic, flood and environment

The research found no water, sewer, electric or gas provider for land at the interchange and no impact or connection fee schedule. A truck stop with showers, a kitchen and a truck wash is a heavy water and wastewater user, so provider letters with capacity confirmation are conditions. No TxDOT count with a station identifier was found, and the truck share of I-20 traffic was not found. Flood zone: not determined. Environmental record: none at all. Truck stops fall in NAICS 457, and SBA's environmental policy asks for a Phase I report on every such property, whatever the loan amount [55].

Taxes

Kaufman CAD's adopted 2025 rates for property inside Terrell sum to $2.423473 per $100 of value: Kaufman County $0.334478, road and bridge $0.080635, Terrell ISD $1.130500, the City of Terrell $0.764200 and Trinity Valley Community College $0.113660 [33]. An independent rate map shows the same total for the city zone [34]. At that rate property tax is one of the largest fixed costs a new truck stop carries.

Payroll. Card and fleet-card fees. 8.4 cents on every gallon of diesel and gasoline, the NACS card-fee figure for 2023 [46]. Fleet-card programs charge truck stops their own fees, and a site that wants fleet business has to accept them.

Other expenses. Insurance for a 24-hour fuel site, power for the store and a lit truck court, hot water for showers, repairs, tank testing, scale certification and supplies start at $540,000 and grow 3.0% a year.

At stabilization the truck stop keeps 20.3% of revenue (fuel margin, inside sales and services) as NOI, $1,113,311 in Year 3. A cross-check from the largest public owner of travel-center real estate puts that in proportion: the 175 TA and Petro sites leased from Service Properties Trust covered their rent 1.20 times at the end of 2025, on annualized minimum rent of $264,262,000, and one master lease covered only 1.05 times [38]. Those are mature, branded sites averaging far more parking than this one, and their coverage was already thin.

Construction is priced from 2026 projects, not a cost index, because no travel-center cost survey exists. Bank rate: Assumption: Prime from the H.15 release [57] plus 1.25 points for a special-purpose start-up; no published 2026 lender terms for travel centers were found, the 10-year Treasury sat near 5% in September 2026 [59], and the bank sets the rate at pricing. Debenture rate: Source [54].

SBA 504 splits a project three ways. The October 1, 2026 rules (SOP 50 10 8.1 with Technical Policy Updates, in force since October 1, 2026) set the standard split at Third Party Lender 50%, debenture 40%, borrower 10%; debenture 35% at a 15% contribution and 30% at 20%. The borrower's contribution starts at 10%, rises to 15% for a new business or a limited or special purpose property, and to 20% for both [50]. SBA defines a special purpose property as a limited-market property with a unique physical design, special construction materials, or a layout that restricts its utility to the use for which it was built, and gas stations are on its list of examples. A truck stop with tanks, canopies, a scale and a truck court fits the definition, and a business that has not opened is new. The sponsor's proposal of 15.0% reads the rule as if only one surcharge applied, and it fails the equity test.

At 15.0% the proposal also fails the size test. The debenture would be $5,834,631, above the $5 million limit for a project that is not a small manufacturer. At 20.0% the debenture falls to $4,999,860, just inside the limit, and the bank lends $8,333,100.

The debenture rate is 6.97%, the 25-year effective 504 rate from the October 8, 2026 pricing, which folds the ongoing program fees into the rate [54]; the actual rate is set when the debenture is sold. The bank rate is an assumption of 8.25%, Prime of 7.00% plus 1.25 points for a special purpose start-up [57], with the 10-year Treasury near 5% in September 2026 [59]. Both amortize over 25 years. The bank may well ask for a shorter term on a truck stop, which would only raise debt service.

Two more program rules bear on the sponsor. SBA's 504 jobs standard is one job per $95,000 of debenture; the case's staffing of about 26 full-time equivalents falls short of that ratio on a debenture near the limit, so the CDC would have to qualify the project under a community development or public policy goal. And under the ownership rule in force since March 1, 2026, the borrower must be wholly owned by U.S. citizens or nationals living in the United States, a test its guarantors share [37]. How CDCs and banks split the risk on a project like this is set out in our practice note on SBA 7(a) and 504 lending.

As proposed: the sponsor proposes 15% equity, the tier for a new business alone, with 15.0% equity ($2,500,556) and $14,169,817 of debt. The structure does not qualify: the equity injection is below the program minimum; the loan exceeds the program size limit; coverage falls below the floor.

As restructured: 20.0% equity ($3,333,240) and $13,332,959 of debt with $1,211,336 of annual debt service. The project still fails at least one test; the market does not support this loan.

The 504 coverage standard is 1.15x, and a new business has no history, so the engine tests the projection in Year 2. As proposed, at 15.0% equity, the project fails all three tests: the contribution is below the tier, the debenture is above the limit, and Year 2 coverage is 0.65x. As restructured, at 20.0% equity with the debenture brought inside the limit, two tests pass and the third still fails at 0.69x.

The engine then looks for an equity share that would clear the standard and finds none. That is a structural result, not a rounding one. In the 504 split, cash the sponsor adds above the tier reduces the debenture; the bank's first lien stays at half the project. On this project, the bank's annual payment alone is more than Year 2 NOI can cover at the standard, so even a sponsor who paid for the entire debenture portion in cash would not pass. The only paths to a passing structure are a smaller project, a cheaper one, or a store that earns more in its second year than the market evidence supports.

The determination is therefore that the market does not support this loan. That is not the same as saying the market does not support a truck stop. The demand evidence for Exit 498 is strong: a growing city, a growing county, three logistics projects and only one full-service competitor in the corridor. What fails is the basis: interchange land priced at a Buc-ee's exit and a 2026 build cost, financed with half the project in a bank loan that needs coverage from the second year. The lender's answer is to send the sponsor back to the land.

Risk Factors

Risks particular to a truck stop at Exit 498.

  1. Land basis. At the assumed $310,000 an acre, land is the largest single line in the budget, and the true price is unknown until an appraisal and a contract fix it.
  2. Entitlement. A truck stop needs a Specific Use Permit in every district [1], and the 700-foot restriction near IH 20 may apply.
  3. Chain entry. Love's is adding 20 stores and 1,500 truck spaces in 2026 [39]; a chain site at Exit 498 or a neighboring exit would compete on parking, loyalty and price.
  4. TA's scale at Exit 503. 300 truck spaces and five service bays at Exit 503 [8] set the driver's default stop.
  5. Industry margins. Pilot's 2025 pre-tax earnings fell by more than two-thirds [43], and one TA master lease covered rent only 1.05 times [38].
  6. Construction cost. Inputs rose 8.9% and diesel 77.8% in producer prices in a year [45][52]; fixed-price bids are a condition.
  7. Logistics timing. Amazon's center completes in August 2027 and VanTrust's Phase 1 in Q2 2027 [28][30]; delays push the volume the ramp depends on.
  8. Environmental liability. A new site starts clean, but every tank, line and dispenser is an exposure the lender inherits [55].
  9. Special purpose collateral. If the truck stop fails, the buyer pool is other fuel operators, and the land price paid will not come back. The special purpose property risk note covers what lenders recover from failed fuel sites, and why the trade area is the study explains why a corridor, not a town, defines this one.

Terrell Questions

Questions about the Terrell truck stop case.

Why does a new truck stop need 20% equity under SBA 504?

Because two surcharges stack. A business two years old or less adds to the standard 504 contribution, and so does limited or special purpose property, and SBA counts gas stations as special purpose property. A new truck stop is both, which puts it in the top tier of 20% [50][55].

Can a truck stop be built by right on Highway Corridor land in Terrell?

No. Terrell's 2025 zoning ordinance lists Use #416, Truck Stop and Fuel Station, only with a Specific Use Permit, and permits it by right in no district [1]. Recent hearings ran 12 to 26 days from the Planning and Zoning Commission to City Council [24][25].

Is Buc-ee's a competitor for a truck stop?

For cars and retail, yes; for trucks, no. Buc-ee's Terrell opened in June 2015 with about 90 gas pumps and does not admit 18-wheelers [11][15]. It takes the four-wheel trade at Exit 498, which is why the case underwrites modest gasoline volume.

Why not test coverage in Year 3, when the store is stable?

Because a new business has to show it can carry its debt early, and the case engine tests start-ups in Year 2. On this model the truck stop's coverage improves in Year 3 but still stays below the standard at the minimum equity; the lender's problem is not the test year alone.

What would make the project financeable?

A lower land basis or a smaller build. The I-20 Exit 509 tract that was marketed for a truck stop sold in March 2026 after listing at $1,300,000 for 16.63 acres [23], a fraction of the per-acre price the case assumes at Exit 498. Cheaper land, or fewer acres than the case assumes, is the lever.

Does SBA mandate a feasibility study for a truck stop?

No. The rule in 13 CFR 120.160(b) is permissive: SBA may call for a study [58]. A CDC and a bank lending to a new special purpose business usually want one because the borrower has no history to test.

Weighing a truck stop on a growing freight corridor? Test the land price before the plans.

We census every diesel competitor on the corridor, build demand from freight generators and traffic, and size the 504 structure to what the site can earn by its second year.
