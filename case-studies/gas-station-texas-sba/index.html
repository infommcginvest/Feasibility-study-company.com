Gas Station and Convenience Store Feasibility Study, Lockhart, Texas: An SBA 7(a) Worked Case

Market analysis by Mark Otava · Published July 12, 2026 · Updated October 16, 2026 · Data as of October 2026

Representative worked example. The site, sponsor and project are hypothetical. The market evidence comes from the public sources listed below, with their dates. This page shows how the market analysis is built and how it sets the loan. It is not a client engagement.

Lockhart grew 26.2% between April 2020 and July 2025 [1], and a new store with a kitchen on US 183 can reach national-average fuel volume by its third year. Demand is not the constraint; cost is. With highway land carried at an assumed $20 a square foot and a 10.00% start-up rate, the store covers the $4,845,493 loan the sponsor asked for only 0.62x in Year 2. It supports $2,638,826 of 7(a) debt, which means sponsor equity of 53.2%.

Market Conclusion

Lockhart can fill a new store. It cannot pay for the land with debt.

The market answer is yes. Lockhart counted 18,159 residents on July 1, 2025, up from an estimates base of 14,394 in April 2020 [1]. Caldwell County added 2,211 people in the year to July 2025, a 4.2% gain that ranked tenth among all U.S. counties, and 87.2% of that gain came from people moving in [2]. A city adding households at that rate, on the highway that carries most of its retail, can support another fuel and convenience store, and a modern store with a kitchen can take share from an older field.

The financing answer is no, at least not as asked. No closed land sale turned up in the research and there were too few priced commercial tracts in town to build a range, so land is a labelled assumption: about 2.0 acres of US 183 frontage at $20 a square foot, a planning level taken from no single listing. With land at that level, the project costs $5,700,579 before restructuring, and the sponsor asks for a $4,845,493 7(a) loan at 10.00%. Stabilized net operating income of $412,352 covers that debt 0.62x in the Year 2 test and 0.78x in Year 3. The proposal fails by a wide margin, and the failure does not come from weak demand.

Restructured to the coverage the program asks of a start-up, 1.15x within two years [44], the store carries $2,638,826 of debt and needs $2,999,691 of sponsor equity, 53.2% of cost. That is a bankable structure on these numbers, but it is a different deal from the one proposed: either the sponsor brings far more cash, or the land is bought well below the assumed level, or both. The sensitivity table later on this page shows how much a land discount is worth.

Two items sit ahead of the loan question. The city has not been shown to allow gasoline sales by right in the commercial district the site would sit in, and an entrance-corridor overlay adopted in March 2026 would require a Specific Use Permit for any gas station on the US 183 frontage once its map is approved [22]. This case belongs to our gas station and convenience store practice; Caldwell County sits in the Austin metro, and the statewide context is on our Texas page.

Growth that arrives by moving van

The Caldwell County gain is mostly migration [2], and that matters for a fuel store. New households arrive with vehicles and with jobs elsewhere. Lockhart residents average a 28.6-minute trip to work [1], long enough that most commuters leave town on SH 130 toward Austin or on SH 142 toward San Marcos and I-35 [3]. A commuter fills where the trip begins or ends. A store on US 183 inside town sits on the home end of those trips, which is the steadier end: it does not depend on a toll road's through-traffic choosing to exit.

The national trend runs the other way. EIA reports that U.S. gasoline use fell 1% in 2025, to 8.9 million barrels a day, and expects it to keep declining in 2026 and 2027 as fleet fuel economy improves [10]. A typical vehicle used 457 gallons in 2025 and came to a station 54.4 times [9]. In a flat or shrinking national pool, a site's volume grows only if its local vehicle count grows. Lockhart's does, which is the strongest single argument for this store. Our note on gas station demand per capita explains why the per-vehicle decline still leaves room for a well-placed store in a growing town.

A county seat that sells to the county

Lockhart's retail sales of $319,643,000 in 2022 came to $21,412 per resident [1], the profile of a county seat that draws shoppers from the surrounding county. Its largest employers, as the economic development corporation lists them, are the school district, Walmart and H-E-B [4]; the corporation gives no date for its headcounts, so the study uses none of them, and daytime employment becomes a lender condition. Household income is middle-market: a median of $68,400 and a per capita figure of $30,208 [1]. A store selling to this base sells everyday baskets, fountain drinks and fast food, not destination grocery.

Visitors on a calendar, not a curve

The Texas Legislature named Lockhart the Barbecue Capital of Texas in 1999 [6], and the town's visitor draw follows that reputation. The 2026 Texas Monthly BBQ Fest brings more than 35 of the magazine's Top 50 barbecue joints downtown on November 7 and 8 [5]. The Chisholm Trail Round-Up reported 20,000 attendees in June 2025, a promoter's figure [7]. Lockhart State Park recorded 74,469 visits in 2025 and added 188 acres in 2026, figures taken from an encyclopedia entry because the parks department's own count was not retrieved [8]. These are peaks. No source gives monthly fuel volumes, so the model carries no event revenue; visitor traffic is upside, not underwriting.

A well-supplied town

Texas had 16,504 convenience stores at the end of 2025, more than any other state, and the country had one store for every 2,257 residents [11]. Lockhart's directories list 16 fuel sites for 18,159 people, far denser than that national ratio before any through-traffic is counted. The town is not underserved. The case for a new store is not a gap in coverage; it is a newer, larger format taking share from an older field while the vehicle count grows.

The site at 1910 S Colorado, unbranded in the research's directories, showed as Murphy USA in a fuel-price directory checked for this study; Walmart's store page, which would confirm a Murphy USA fuel center, answered that check with a bot challenge, so its brand stays unconfirmed. A Murphy USA would matter, because it is a low-price format. Every one of these becomes a lender condition.

The twelfth is a 7-Eleven at 2410 W San Antonio, on SH 142 toward the toll road. No operator page confirmed its fuel offer for this study, so it is a condition like the others. A national chain on the toll-road side of town competes more for SH 130 commuters than for the in-town trade a US 183 store depends on.

Who sets the price

The H-E-B fuel center is the reference price on US 183. Grocery fuel centers tend to price low to bring shoppers into the store, and that limits how far any nearby station can push its margin. The model reflects it with a fuel margin below national averages (see the revenue build). Posted prices would test this directly; none was found on any operator's site, so a pump-price survey is a lender condition.

New supply in and around town

The nearest new entrant is inside Lockhart: a "Top Stop C-Store" of 7,178 square feet, a 5,740-square-foot store plus 1,500 square feet of lease space, estimated at $1.5 million, with construction expected to begin in November [18]. Its address and state registration number were not found. In Luling, QuikTrip #4041 registered a 5,023-square-foot store with retail gasoline on April 3, 2026, estimated at $619,000, with work planned from July 1, 2026 to February 1, 2027 [17]. A 5.484-acre tract at 1507 N Cesar Chavez Parkway was rezoned to commercial on January 18, 2022 for a convenience store with a gas station and market; no construction has been reported [19].

The regional giants sit on the interstates. Buc-ee's opened a 75,000-square-foot store with 120 fueling positions on I-10 in Luling on June 10, 2024 [16]. Its San Marcos store on I-35, 74,707 square feet at an estimated $47.2 million [20], carries conflicting opening dates in the record: a Buc-ee's representative gave July 27, 2026 [21], while a San Antonio paper reported an August 12, 2026 opening with 128 fueling positions [49]. The planned Boerne store is capped at 100 fueling positions with at least 26 EV chargers [20]. Chargers on that scale are a travel-center amenity, not a reason a commuter stops in Lockhart; our page on EV charging at fuel and convenience sites sets out how a lender underwrites them. These stores pull highway travelers, not Lockhart's daily commuters. Two ring listings in the research, a Pilot on US 183 and a TXB on Lehman Road, could not be placed inside Lockhart's trade area during this study and are left out.

What the field means for the subject

The confirmed competitors are a grocer's fuel center and older branded stations. The subject's advantage is format: a larger store, a kitchen and modern forecourt. That is the ground a branded fuel store with foodservice competes on, and it is also exactly what Top Stop is building. If Top Stop opens first and on US 183, the subject's ramp slows; the sensitivity table shows what a 10% or 20% volume shortfall does to coverage.

Site

Zoning, an overlay on its way, and a traffic count nobody has pulled.

The subject is a hypothetical pad of about two acres with frontage on US 183 inside city limits, in a commercial district that permits a fuel and convenience store. The district is a condition to confirm, not a fact taken from any parcel. The city has placed the use in a commercial district before: the 2022 Cesar Chavez Parkway rezoning noted under new supply moved a tract from Agricultural Open to CHB for a convenience store with a gas station [19].

Zoning: two open questions

The first is whether the site's district lets a convenience store sell gasoline by right. Lockhart's code defines a convenience store as one that may include gasoline sales and separates uses that are "Allowed", meaning by right, from uses that are "Permitted", meaning subject to a Specific Use Permit [23]. The research did not reach the use table for fuel sales in the commercial districts. A written determination from the city is the first zoning condition.

The second question is the entrance-corridor overlay. The city council adopted its text on March 17, 2026. Inside the overlay, gas stations and convenience stores need a Specific Use Permit, truck stops and travel plazas are prohibited, buildings sit behind a 50-foot front setback and a 15-foot landscape buffer is required [22]. The map that would apply the overlay to US 183 properties went to a Planning and Zoning hearing on August 26, 2026, was continued, faces a workshop on October 28, 2026 and returns for hearing on November 18, 2026 [22]. If the subject's frontage is mapped, the store needs a permit regardless of what the district allows, and the setback moves the canopy deeper into a two-acre lot. No typical permit timeline was found.

Utilities

The City of Lockhart supplies water and sewer; electric service comes from either the city or Bluebonnet Electric Cooperative, and which one serves the parcel was not determined [24]. Texas Gas Service supplies natural gas to the school district [32]; parcel service was not confirmed. Non-residential water costs $8.12 per 1,000 gallons and wastewater carries a fixed charge of $25.08 a month, both from November 2025 billing [25]. The city's demand-metered electric tariff adds a purchased power cost approved by Council in October 2007, and the rate page does not show its current value [26], so the model treats utilities as an allowance. Road, water and wastewater impact fees date to Ordinance 02-02 of January 15, 2002 and are collected at building permit; the commercial amounts are not published [27].

Traffic, flood and environment

No TxDOT count station, annual average daily traffic or count year for US 183 at the site was found; the state's count dataset exists [28] and should be queried. The FEMA flood zone, the parcel identifier and any environmental record were not found either. For a fuel site the environmental file is not optional: gas station loans carry a Phase I environmental site assessment regardless of loan size, with tank and line testing documented [42].

This table is a gross-profit table: each line is cents or percent of margin times volume, and the totals are gross profit, not sales. The study chose that form for two reasons. First, no Lockhart operator posts a pump price, so a fuel revenue line would rest on a national figure; EIA forecasts $3.91 a gallon for 2026 after a September 2026 national average of $4.35 [50], a swing that would move revenue without changing what the store earns. Second, fuel was 65.0% of industry sales in 2025 but only 38.8% of gross profit [33]. Margin per gallon and margin on inside sales pay the loan, so the model underwrites those.

Fuel volume

The store ramps over two years to 4,000 gallons a day in Year 3, the national average of about 4,000 gallons a day per store [9]. A new 12-position store on the town's main highway should reach the average in a city growing this fast, but the study does not underwrite above it because 16 listed sites already split the town's demand. For scale, Yesway's older stores sold 1.0 million gallons a year in 2025 and its new large-format stores 3.1 million [35]. The subject sits between the two.

Fuel margin

The model carries 36.0 cents a gallon. That is below the national gasoline margin of 39.7 cents in 2024 [9] and the five-year average of 39.3 cents to September 2026, and well below the 35.1 to 52.6 cents listed operators earned in the second quarter of 2026 [37]. The 2026 figures came after a Middle East supply shock and should not be treated as normal. An H-E-B fuel center on the same highway [15] is a further reason to stay under the averages.

Inside sales and food

Inside sales reach $2,200,000 in Year 3, between Yesway's $1.7 million older store and its $3.1 million new store [35]. The kitchen is what justifies more than an older store's figure. Foodservice is 28.5% of inside sales, the 2025 industry share, and it produced 38.9% of in-store gross profit nationally [33]. The model gives food a 50.0% margin, below the 58.3% prepared-food margin Casey's reported for the first nine months of its 2026 fiscal year [36], and packaged merchandise 32.0%, below Yesway's 35.2% blended inside margin [35]. The inside store is not a profit center on its own: NACS reports that the average in-store transaction lost 7 cents after all expenses in 2025 [51]. It earns its place by sharing fixed costs with the forecourt.

In Year 3 these lines produce $1,342,460 of gross profit.

Rate: Prime of 7.00% [46] plus the maximum spread SBA allows for this loan size [44]; a lender may price lower. Guaranty: 75% of the loan, Source [43].

The loan is tested under SOP 50 10 8.1 with Technical Policy Updates, in force for applications from October 1, 2026 [44]. A start-up puts in at least 10% of project cost, and its projected coverage must reach 1.15x within two years, which is why Year 2 is the test year here. A single 7(a) loan tops out at $5 million; the sponsor's 15.0% equity proposal exists to stay under that line. Because real estate is well over half of the proceeds, the whole loan can run 25 years.

The rate is Prime of 7.00% on the October 9, 2026 H.15 release [46] plus the largest spread SBA allows at this loan size, 10.00% in all. A lender may price lower, and the final rate is set at pricing; a lower rate helps, but even a full point off does not close a gap this wide. SBA guarantees 75.0% of a loan this size [43], and the FY2027 upfront guaranty fee follows the schedule in the program's fee notice [45].

Three program rules bear on a fuel store in particular. Gas stations are on SBA's list of special purpose properties [44]; under 504 a new business on such a property puts in 20%, which is one reason this sponsor chose 7(a). Fuel supply agreements covered by the Petroleum Marketing Practices Act fall within SBA's definition of a franchise [52], so the brand agreement goes into the file. And since March 1, 2026, every owner must be a U.S. citizen or national; the notice that briefly allowed up to 5% foreign ownership was rescinded [48]. Our note on special purpose property risk covers why lenders lean on going-concern value for assets like this, and our SBA 7(a) and 504 practice page sets out the full rule set.

The feasibility study itself is the lender's call. Under 13 CFR 120.160(b): SBA may require professional appraisals, a survey, or a feasibility study [47]; on a ground-up special purpose project with a start-up borrower, most lenders ask for one.

As proposed: the sponsor proposes the smallest round equity share that keeps the 7(a) loan under the program's size limit, with 15.0% equity ($855,087) and $4,845,493 of debt. The structure does not qualify: coverage falls below the floor.

As restructured: 53.2% equity ($2,999,691) and $2,638,826 of debt with $287,748 of annual debt service. Every test is met.

As proposed, coverage is 0.33x in Year 1, 0.62x in the Year 2 test and 0.78x at stabilization. Annual debt service of $528,372 is larger than the store's stabilized income. No reasonable revision of the operating assumptions closes that gap: the store would need far more volume than the national average to carry a loan of that size at this rate.

The engine restructures by raising equity until Year 2 coverage meets the floor. At 53.2% equity, debt service falls to $287,748 and Year 2 coverage reaches 1.15x, rising to 1.43x in Year 3. That is a sound loan on these assumptions. It is also a loan of $2,638,826, a little over half of what the sponsor wanted.

There are three ways to reach a structure like this, and the lender should ask which one the sponsor intends. The sponsor can bring the cash. The sponsor can buy the land below the assumed level, which lowers both cost and debt; the sensitivity table quantifies it. Or the sponsor can find a cheaper site off US 183, which changes the volume case and needs its own study. What does not work is the original request.

Set beside the other fuel cases in this series, Lockhart is the one where the market is strongest and the cost is the obstacle. The Lake Wales, Florida start-up has cheaper land but fails on share, because national chains already hold its highway. The Versailles, Ohio start-up has the cheapest land of all and fails on the size of its village. The Opelika, Alabama case shows the acquisition route, where a buyer is tested on a seller's actual earnings rather than a ramp, and the Cartersville, Georgia case tests an interstate exit. Read together, they show that the land line and the competitive set decide a new store's loan long before the pump count does.

Risks

What could break the Lockhart projection.

  1. The overlay map reaches US 183

    If the November 18, 2026 hearing maps the frontage into the entrance-corridor overlay, a gas station needs a Specific Use Permit and a 50-foot front setback [22]. A denial ends the project; an approval with conditions can shrink the forecourt on a two-acre lot.

  2. The district does not allow fuel by right

    The code distinguishes by-right uses from permitted uses [23], and the entry for gasoline sales in the site's district was not found. A permit requirement adds time and a public hearing to a project whose land cost already strains the loan.

  3. Land costs more than assumed

    The study carries land at $20 a square foot, an assumption rather than a price on record. If the contract comes in higher, the equity requirement rises with it; the sensitivity table shows coverage with land 15% and 30% above the assumption. An appraisal below the contract price also shrinks what a lender will advance against the real estate.

  4. A new store opens first

    Top Stop's 7,178-square-foot project is expected to start construction in November [18]. If it opens first on the same highway, the subject competes for the same new households with a similar format, and its ramp slows.

  5. Margins revert

    Listed operators earned 35.1 to 52.6 cents a gallon in the second quarter of 2026 on a supply shock [37]. The model's 36.0 cents margin already assumes reversion, but a price war led by a grocer's fuel center could take it lower; the break-even table shows the volume equivalent.

  6. Construction prices keep rising

    Steel mill products were up 16.9% year over year in June 2026 [40]. Every dollar of cost overrun at the restructured equity share adds debt the store can only just carry, so the contingency should not be spent on scope.

  7. The environmental file

    The parcel's environmental history was not found. Fuel sites carry a Phase I and tank testing as a matter of course [42]; a recognized environmental condition on land carried at this cost would be expensive to resolve.

Conditions before commitment.

  1. Land price and title

  2. Zoning determination

    A written city determination that convenience stores with gasoline sales are allowed by right in the parcel's district, or an approved Specific Use Permit; confirmation of whether the parcel falls inside the entrance-corridor overlay map after the November 18, 2026 hearing, and the permit timeline if it does.

  3. Traffic count

  4. Flood and environmental

  5. Utility service and fees

    The electric provider for the parcel (city or Bluebonnet), natural gas service, the commercial road, water and wastewater impact fees, and the current purchased power cost.

  6. Competitor confirmation

    Operator or appraisal-district confirmation for the twelve unconfirmed in-town listings, resolution of the three brand conflicts, confirmation of any Murphy USA or Stripes presence, and fueling positions, store size, opening year and appraisal-district records for each competitor.

  7. Pipeline status

    The address and state registration number for Top Stop, the official state record for QuikTrip #4041, and the build status of the 2022 rezoning on Cesar Chavez Parkway.

  8. Demand gaps

    Ring or drive-time demographics, daytime employment, truck volumes, fuel seasonality and the parks department's own visitation count.

  10. Costs and taxes

    A funded reserve for the Year 1 gap between operating income and debt service.

Lockhart Questions

What lenders ask about the Lockhart fuel and convenience case.

If Lockhart is growing this fast, why does the store fail at the sponsor's equity?

Because growth is already in the cost of highway land. No closed land sale was found, so the study carries about two acres of US 183 frontage at an assumed $20 a square foot, a planning level the appraisal must replace. Even at that level, land is the largest line in the budget, and a loan near the 7(a) ceiling at today's start-up rate costs more each year than the store earns once it settles. The market supports the store, not the leverage; the coverage table on this page shows the gap.

Is Lockhart's fuel market saturated?

It is dense, not closed. Directories list 16 fuel sites in a city of 18,159 [1], and only four could be confirmed on an operator's own page during this study [12][13][14][15]. Most of the field is older, smaller stores. A new store with a kitchen can take share, which is why the model reaches the national average of about 4,000 gallons a day [9] by Year 3 and goes no higher.

What could stop the project before any loan question arises?

Zoning. Whether gasoline sales are allowed by right in the site's commercial district was not found, and the entrance-corridor overlay adopted on March 17, 2026 would require a Specific Use Permit for gas stations once its map reaches US 183; the map hearing is set for November 18, 2026 [22]. A lender should not commit before the city answers both.

Why does the model use gross profit lines instead of pump revenue?

No Lockhart station posts a price on its own site, and pump prices swing with crude: EIA put the September 2026 national average at $4.35 a gallon [50]. Fuel is 65.0% of industry sales but 38.8% of gross profit [33]. Cents of margin per gallon and inside margin are what pay the loan, so those are what the model underwrites.

Would an SBA 504 loan fix the coverage problem?

No. A 504 would lower the rate on the real estate share, but a new business on special purpose property puts in 20% [44], and the gap here is income against land cost, not program terms. A cheaper land contract or more sponsor cash closes it; a different program does not.

How much margin of safety does the restructured loan carry?

On the restructured loan, Year 3 fuel volume can fall below the national average for a store before income stops covering debt service; the break-even table gives the exact threshold. The opening year is the exposed one, because income is still building while debt service is fixed, so the lender should require a funded reserve for Year 1.
