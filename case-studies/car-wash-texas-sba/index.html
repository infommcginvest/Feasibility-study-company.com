Express Car Wash Feasibility Study in Seguin, Texas: A New Tunnel on an SBA 504 Loan

Market analysis by Mark Otava · Published July 13, 2026 · Data as of October 2026

Representative worked example. The site, sponsor and project are hypothetical. The market evidence comes from the public sources listed below, with their dates. This page shows how the market analysis is built and how it sets the loan. It is not a client engagement.

Seguin grew 39.3% between April 2020 and July 2025 [1], and a new express tunnel on SH 46 near I-10 would enter a market that already has four operating conveyor washes within about 3 miles. The sponsor's opening request, 15.0% equity, misses the 504 tier for a new business in a special purpose property and covers Year 2 debt service only 1.13x. Restructured at 20.0% equity, coverage reaches 1.20x in Year 2 against the 1.15x floor. The market supports the project, on condition that the lot's flood status and zoning clear.

Market Conclusion

Enough cars for a fifth tunnel, if it earns its members.

Seguin can absorb another express tunnel, but only one priced into the middle of a crowded menu and only on a lot whose flood status is settled. The town is the growth story of the San Antonio to Austin corridor: 41,048 residents in July 2025, up 39.3% from an April 2020 base of 29,460 [1]. The Census estimate a year earlier was 38,789, a single-year gain of 7.4% [2], and the city's economic development corporation counted 27 subdivisions under construction and more than 16,000 dwelling units in the pipeline in 2024 [3]. Car wash demand follows rooftops and commuters, and Seguin is adding both.

The supply side is just as real. Four conveyor washes operate inside the 3-mile trade area this study uses, and two of them, Cosmic Express on SH 46 and Cosmic Express on Eastwood Drive, were announced in 2022 with 130-foot tunnels [11] and had site plans in city review in April 2023 [13]. The nearest one sits on the same SH 46 and I-10 node as the subject. Finish Line Express and Pic-n-Pac Express Wash work the SH 123 Bypass retail strip and publish full price menus [15][16][17]. Nationally, the industry is digesting its own build-out: the chief transformation officer of Zips told the bankruptcy court in February 2025 that upwards of 900 new car wash sites had opened each year for five years [20], and the International Carwash Association described 2026 as a year of recalibration rather than contraction [25].

What decides this loan is not whether Seguin has demand but whether a fifth tunnel can sign enough unlimited members against four incumbents. The study underwrites a membership ramp that stays below the fleet averages of the large operators, a blended member price inside the posted local range, and retail washes priced at Finish Line's second tier. On those inputs the project earns $560,992 of net operating income in Year 3 on $1,454,600 of revenue, and coverage passes the 504 floor in Year 2 only once equity is raised to the tier the program requires.

Three findings carry into the lender conditions. First, no FEMA zone was found for the lot, and the marketing material the research reviewed for the corridor tract flags it as flood plain; no budget or appraisal should be relied on until the panel is pulled. Second, Cosmic Express, the nearest competitor, does not publish its plan prices [10][12], so the price position of the subject against its closest rival is unverified. Third, Year 1 does not cover debt service in the model, which is normal for a ramping tunnel but means the sponsor needs liquidity or an interest reserve beyond the equity.

For readers comparing markets, the express car wash hub sets out the format's economics nationally; this page applies them to one Guadalupe County site. The San Antonio metro page covers the wider region Seguin belongs to, and the Texas state page covers statewide rules and costs.

Demand

Rooftops, commuters and a manufacturing base.

An express wash draws most of its customers from close by. Auto Laundry News's long-running conveyor survey finds about 70% of customers living within 5 miles of the wash [26], which is why this study measures demand at the city level and treats a 3-mile radius as the primary trade area. Radius-level Census tabulations were not available, so the City of Seguin is the reporting geography; a lender should order a drive-time profile as part of its own review.

The household base is middle-income and owner-heavy, which suits a subscription product. Median household income was $64,861 and per capita income $33,633 in the 2020 to 2024 American Community Survey, and 65.2% of homes are owner-occupied [1]. Owners tend to keep cars at home and on driveways, and the mean commute of 24.7 minutes [1] puts most workers on a highway every day. Retail sales in the city reached $1,156,738 thousand in the 2022 Economic Census, or $34,624 per resident [1], which shows Seguin already functions as a shopping destination for a wider area than its own households.

The employment base matters as much as the population. JobsEQ data published by the Seguin EDC count 8,446 manufacturing employees in ZIP areas 78155 and 78123 as of 2024Q1, a location quotient of 4.16 [5]. Caterpillar, Schaeffler, CMC Steel and Tyson Foods are among the city's largest employers [39]. A manufacturing workforce of that size commutes by car, and cars that are driven every day are the base an unlimited plan is sold to. The EDC's employee counts by employer carry no date, so they are not used here; a lender who wants daytime population should order it as a condition.

Growth is recent and steep. Texas Public Radio, reporting the May 2025 Census release, placed Seguin third among the fastest-growing cities in the Austin to San Antonio region and 29th nationally, with growth above 7% in 2024 [4]. Texas Lutheran University adds a student population of about 1,400 [4]. On SH 46, TxDOT counted an average of 33,700 vehicles a day near Clear Springs in 2019 and projected 60,050 by 2048 [6]. That count is a corridor figure, not a count at the site, and the station nearest the subject was not found.

Two sites stay out of the table because no operator page confirms them. A Zips Car Wash on the SH 123 Bypass appears on Yelp as closed in April 2026, with a reviewer saying it was sold to Finish Line [18]; directory addresses suggest it is the same lot Finish Line now operates, but no primary source confirms the sale. The posted menus define the price band the subject has to sit in. Finish Line's top unlimited plan, $29, is below Pic-n-Pac's middle plan, $30, and Finish Line's entry plan, $14, is the cheapest subscription in town [16][17]. Pic-n-Pac bundles a fuel discount with its top wash [17], an advantage a standalone tunnel cannot match. The study's blended member price of $24 a month assumes most members buy a middle plan priced between those two operators. The gap in the census is Cosmic: the closest rival, with the newest equipment and the largest vacuum plaza, does not post prices, and its plan menu is the first thing a lender should pull.

No new car wash permits for 2025 or 2026 were found in city records or local news, and the Planning and Zoning Commission's 2025 agendas, which run from January 14 to December 9, were not reviewed item by item [40]. The two Cosmic sites were the last visible pipeline: the city listed both site plans in review in April 2023 [13]. A lender should treat the pipeline as unconfirmed until those agendas are read.

Site

A commercial lot on SH 46 with one unresolved flag.

The hypothetical project sits on about 2 acres of commercial land fronting SH 46 near the I-10 interchange in Seguin, Guadalupe County. The land cost in the model, $1,298,959, is an assumption: it equals the 2025 asking price the research found for a corridor tract of that size. That listing is not cited, so that no real parcel is identified, and its price was last updated in January 2025. An appraisal and a current land contract set the value, and both are conditions.

The study assumes the lot is zoned C - Commercial, the district the research found for the corridor tract, but the research did not find whether a car wash is allowed by right in that district. Seguin's Unified Development Code provides Specific Use Permits for uses the land use matrix marks for one [9], and Ordinance 2026-029, adopted and published in Supplement 56 on July 23, 2026, adds Conditional Use Permits to the code [8]. A lender should not assume a by-right approval until the matrix is read for car washes in the C district.

Flood exposure is the decisive site issue. No FEMA flood zone or panel number was found for the lot, and the marketing material the research reviewed for the corridor tract flags it as flood plain. If the lot lies in a special flood hazard area, the building pad, tunnel pit and equipment room must be elevated or floodproofed, flood insurance becomes mandatory for the lender, and the appraisal must account for both. The model carries a contingency line for flood work, but the amount is an assumption, not an engineer's number.

Utility providers were not confirmed from a citable source: water, sewer, electric and gas service to the lot are all conditions. A tunnel wash discharges process water and usually reclaims part of it, so sewer capacity and any pretreatment requirement matter more here than for most retail uses.

TxDOT is studying improvements to the I-10 interchange at SH 46 and FM 78, including new frontage-road segments and conversion of frontage roads to one-way operation [7]. One-way frontage roads can change which side of an interchange is easy to reach, so the lender should confirm the subject's access under the proposed design.

Revenue has two lines, and the first one carries the loan. Unlimited plan revenue is average active members times the blended monthly price times twelve. Retail revenue is single washes times an average ticket. Prices are held flat for five years; any increase is upside the model does not count.

Members. The study ramps from 3,200 average active members in the stabilized year, with a lower count in the first two years. Three public benchmarks frame it. Mister Car Wash ended 2025 with about 2.3 million Unlimited Wash Club members across 548 locations, and members produced 76% of its wash sales, up from 74% in 2024 [27]. Zips reported about 625,000 members across more than 260 locations when it filed in February 2025 [19]. A software vendor's panel found that sites with more than 4,000 members convert 11.1% of retail customers to members, against 2.4% at sites under 2,000 [38]. The subject's stabilized count sits between the two chains' fleet averages; a lender can compare it directly by dividing each chain's members by its locations. In this model members produce most of the revenue by Year 3 but a smaller share of wash sales than at Mister, because four incumbents have already signed the town's earliest adopters.

Member price. $24 a month is a blend, not a plan price. Finish Line's plans run from $14 to $29 and Pic-n-Pac's from $20 to $40 [16][17]; most members of a new tunnel take a middle plan, and a blend near the low middle of the combined range leaves room for discounting at opening. Mister's annual report states the value of a member in two ways, more than four times and about seven times the spend of a retail customer, in different sections of the same filing [27]; the inconsistency is a reminder that member economics need the lender's own scrutiny.

Retail washes and ticket. The model assumes retail volume grows slowly while members grow quickly, which matches the industry trend: the same vendor panel reported same-store sales up 5.7% in the third quarter of 2025, with membership revenue up 10% and retail down 3% [38]. The $13 average ticket equals Finish Line's second single-wash tier and sits between Pic-n-Pac's $10 and $15 washes [15][17].

Ramp check. Franchise disclosure data give the only public ramp curve. In calendar 2024 Tommy's Express first-year sites averaged $1,093,264 in gross sales (median $995,079), and older cohorts averaged $1,603,302, $1,876,137, $2,028,912 and $2,204,358; the all-outlet quartiles were $1,541,308, $1,866,787 and $2,265,150 [31]. The model's $1,012,400 in Year 1 and $1,454,600 in Year 3 sit below those cohort averages, the discount for entering a town that already has four tunnels. Mister says a new greenfield takes about three full years to reach average unit volume [27], which is why the model treats Year 3 as stabilized.

No single-site operating survey exists for express tunnels, so the expense ratios come from the one audited multi-site profit and loss statement in the industry. In 2025 Mister Car Wash spent $302.3 million on labor and chemicals, 28.7% of $1,051.7 million of revenue; other store operating expenses were $436.7 million, or 41.5%; and adjusted EBITDA was 32.8% of revenue [28]. Mister's other store costs include rent, which it guided at about $123 million for 2025 [29], so an owner-occupied site should run a lower ratio.

The model uses 29.0% of revenue for labor and chemicals, close to Mister's ratio, and 18.0% for utilities, repairs, card processing, software and marketing, which removes rent from Mister's ratio but keeps a margin for a single site's lack of scale. A management allowance of 4.0% of revenue stands in for owner time; the market price of brand and support in this format is the 4% royalty plus 1% brand fund in the Tommy's Express disclosure [32]. Insurance starts at $45,000 and rises yearly; no insurance quote or market survey was found, and the flood flag makes it a condition.

Property tax is the line most likely to surprise a first-time owner. Applying the 2025 Seguin ISD area rate to land, building and equipment at cost gives the figure in the table, and it does not grow in the model even though the 2026 rate is likely to rise. Net operating income margin reaches 38.6% in Year 3. That is lower than Mister's margin would be with its rent added back, a deliberate cushion for a single owner-operated tunnel without a chain's purchasing power.

Total project cost is $5,857,746. Every line is an assumption: land at the corridor's 2025 asking price, and the rest built from published ranges. The 2025 Tommy's Express disclosure puts construction of its 130-foot model at $2,056,978 to $3,930,750, with the building structure at $803,411 to $853,190, the conveyor at $197,312 to $252,659, wash equipment at $218,505 to $257,130 and soft costs at $107,563 to $582,000 [30]. A Texas contractor quotes tunnel equipment packages at $1.4 million to $2.1 million for 2026 [33], and a state-filed estimate for a 4,696 square foot express wash building in San Antonio was $1.25 million [34]. The model's building and equipment lines sit inside those ranges.

Costs are still moving. Prices for inputs to new nonresidential construction rose 8.9% in the year to August 2026, with steel mill products up 23.4% and aluminum mill shapes up 27.3%, and 55% of contractors surveyed reported a project cancelled, postponed or scaled back in the prior six months [37]. A tunnel is steel and stainless intensive, so the contingency inside the soft-cost line is not generous.

The cost also runs ahead of value. In April 2026 net-leased car wash properties were listed at an average price of $5.11 million with an average asking cap rate of 6.21% across 190 listings, with average net operating income above $318,000 [35], and Mister sold one express site in a sale-leaseback for $5.0 million in the third quarter of 2025 [29]. Texas car wash listings averaged a 6.36% asking cap rate in August 2025 [36]. A finished tunnel's real estate, in other words, trades for less than this project costs, and the gap is the business value the appraisal must support. Because a car wash is special purpose collateral, the appraisal needs an appraiser experienced with the property type, and the lender should expect the business, not the real estate, to carry part of the value.

Bank rate: Assumption: Prime was 7.00% on October 9, 2026 [46]. The bank holds a first lien at half of cost, its lowest-risk position, so the rate is set a quarter point over Prime, far below SBA's cap on the third-party spread. The lender sets the rate at pricing. Debenture rate: Source [42].

An SBA 504 project has three layers. Under SOP 50 10 8.1 with Technical Policy Updates, effective October 1, 2026, the standard split is Third Party Lender 50%, debenture 40%, borrower 10%; debenture 35% at a 15% contribution and 30% at 20% [44]. The borrower's share rises with risk: 10% standard, 15% for a new business or a special purpose property, and 20% for both. Car wash businesses are on SBA's special purpose list, and this sponsor has no operating history, so the 20% tier applies.

The sponsor's proposal of 15.0% reads the rule as if only one test applied. Several published summaries make the same mistake in another form, quoting a 10% equity injection for start-ups; that is the 7(a) start-up rule (10%), and it does not set the 504 contribution.

The debenture is $1,757,324 in the restructured case, well inside the $5 million limit. Its rate is the 25-year effective rate of 6.97% from the October 8, 2026 pricing as posted by a CDC [42]; that rate already includes the ongoing fees, and the model also finances the 0.50% upfront fee for fiscal 2027 [45], so the fee is counted slightly twice, on the conservative side. The annual service fee for fiscal 2027 is 0.203% [45]. Debenture terms are 10, 20 or 25 years, and the bank loan must run at least 7 years for a 10-year debenture, 10 years for a 20- or 25-year debenture [44].

The bank rate of 7.25% is an assumption: Prime was 7.00% on October 9, 2026 [46], the first lien sits at half of cost ahead of the CDC, and SBA caps the third-party rate at 6% over New York Prime. Both rates are set at pricing, which is a condition. Since March 1, 2026 every direct and indirect owner of an SBA borrower must be a U.S. citizen or U.S. national [43], a test the CDC will apply at eligibility.

SBA's car wash lending has been steady rather than booming. WashIndex's reading of SBA's loan-level data counts 504 car wash loans at 111 in fiscal 2021 and 99 in fiscal 2025, with a fiscal 2025 median 504 loan of $1,339K, and a charge-off rate of 4.4% on loans approved from fiscal 2010 onward against 13.7% for 1991 to 2009 approvals [41]. The figures are a data vendor's tabulation, not an SBA publication. Other 504 cases on this site show how the same rules land on different assets: the Bastrop hotel case is another special purpose property, and the Weatherford dealership case shows a 504 project that is not special purpose. The SBA 7(a) and 504 practice page explains how we scope these studies.

As proposed: the sponsor applies the tier for a new business alone and overlooks the special purpose test, with 15.0% equity ($878,882) and $4,980,331 of debt. The structure does not qualify: the equity injection is below the program minimum; coverage falls below the floor.

As restructured: 20.0% equity ($1,171,549) and $4,686,197 of debt with $402,683 of annual debt service. Every test is met.

The 504 coverage floor is 1.15x under the current SOP [44]. Car wash businesses are on SBA's special purpose list [44]. SBA reads 504 coverage on historical EBITDA where a business has history; a start-up has none, so the test falls on the projection, and this study applies it in Year 2, the second full year of operation.

As proposed, the sponsor puts in $878,882, 15.0% of cost, and asks the CDC to fund the gap with a larger debenture. The structure fails twice: the contribution is below the 20% tier for a new business in a special purpose property, and the larger debenture lifts annual debt service to $427,563, which Year 2 income covers only 1.13x.

As restructured, the contribution rises to the tier, 20.0% or $1,171,549. The bank's first lien stays at half of cost; the debenture shrinks to $1,757,324, and debt service falls to $402,683. Coverage is 1.20x in Year 2 and 1.39x in Year 3, against the 1.15x floor. The extra equity does two jobs at once: it meets the program rule and it buys the coverage the projection needs.

The margin over the floor in Year 2 is thin, and the restructure does not change Year 1, where coverage is 0.86x. A CDC will want to see the sponsor's post-closing liquidity cover the Year 1 shortfall, or an interest reserve funded from equity, before it relies on the Year 2 test.

Risk Factors

What could break this tunnel.

  • Flood exposure. An adverse flood zone could add elevation, floodproofing and insurance costs, delay permits under the city's flood ordinance, or reduce the appraised value. Until the FEMA panel is pulled, the budget is provisional.
  • A four-tunnel price ceiling. Finish Line's $14 entry plan and $29 top plan [16] cap what a new entrant can charge. If Cosmic undercuts, the subject's blend falls and the matrix shows how quickly coverage follows.
  • Consolidation and distress. The Zips bankruptcy shows how leverage, not demand, breaks car wash operators [19]. A reported conversion of the former Zips lot to Finish Line [18] means the strongest posted-price operator now runs a site the market already knew.
  • Membership churn and regulation. Recurring billing is the business model, and Mister's filing flags possible regulation of automatically renewing subscriptions as a risk [27].
  • Cost inflation. Steel and aluminum prices rose 23.4% and 27.3% in the year to August 2026 [37]; a tunnel ordered late in a rising market can overrun its budget before the conveyor is set.
  • Appraisal gap. Finished tunnels list at an average of $5.11 million [35], below this project's cost. Special purpose collateral narrows the bank's exit if the business fails.
  • Access changes. TxDOT's interchange study proposes one-way frontage roads [7]; a change in circulation could make the subject harder or easier to reach from I-10.
  • Tax growth. The county proposed a 2026 rate above its no-new-revenue rate as values fell, and the school district called an election [23][24]. The model holds tax flat.

The special purpose property note explains why lenders and CDCs apply higher equity and tighter appraisal standards to assets like this one, and the SOP 50 10 8.1 note covers what changed in the rules on October 1, 2026.

Seguin Car Wash Questions

What lenders ask about this Seguin tunnel.

Why does this car wash need 20% equity under SBA 504 when the sponsor offered 15%?

Two tests stack. Car wash businesses are on SBA's special purpose property list, and the borrower is a new business. Either one alone sets the contribution at 15%; both together set it at 20% under SOP 50 10 8.1 [44]. The 10% start-up injection some summaries quote is a 7(a) rule and does not govern a 504 project.

Is Seguin already saturated with express car washes?

It is contested, not closed. Two Cosmic Express tunnels, Finish Line Express and Pic-n-Pac Express Wash all operate within about 3 miles, and a former Zips site on the SH 123 Bypass is reported closed. Against that, the Census Bureau puts the city at 41,048 residents in July 2025 against a base of 29,460 in April 2020 [1], and the EDC counted more than 16,000 dwelling units in the pipeline in 2024 [3].

What member price does the study assume, and is it supported?

The study assumes a blended average near the middle of the local menus. Finish Line posts unlimited plans at $14, $19, $24 and $29 a month [16], and Pic-n-Pac posts $20, $30 and $40, with touch-free at $30 [17], both read on October 15, 2026. Cosmic Express does not post plan prices online, so its menu is a lender condition.

Does flood risk on the lot stop the loan?

Not by itself, but it stops underwriting until it is resolved. No FEMA zone was found for the lot, and the marketing material the research reviewed for the corridor tract flags it as flood plain; that material is not cited because it identifies a real parcel. The lender needs the FIRM panel, an elevation certificate, a flood insurance quote and the City of Seguin's flood ordinance review before it relies on the cost budget or the appraisal.

Does SBA require a feasibility study for a car wash 504 loan?

No rule makes one automatic. Under 13 CFR 120.160(b): SBA may require professional appraisals, a survey, or a feasibility study [47]. For a start-up in a special purpose property with no operating history, the projection is the only evidence of repayment, so lenders and CDCs commonly ask for an independent market study.

How sensitive is the loan to membership growth?

Very. Unlimited plans carry most of the revenue in the model, and the industry's own data show how much member counts vary by site: a software vendor's panel found retail-to-member conversion of 11.1% at sites with more than 4,000 members against 2.4% at sites under 2,000 [38]. The sensitivity matrix on this page pairs member count with member price for that reason.
