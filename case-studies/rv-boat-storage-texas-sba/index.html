RV and Boat Storage Feasibility Study in Granbury, Texas: Why This SBA 504 Loan Does Not Work

Market analysis by Mark Otava · Published July 13, 2026 · Data as of October 2026

Representative worked example. The site, sponsor and project are hypothetical. The market evidence comes from the public sources listed below, with their dates. This page shows how the market analysis is built and how it sets the loan. It is not a client engagement.

Granbury sits on Lake Granbury at the southwest edge of the Dallas-Fort Worth region and grew 21.0% between April 2020 and July 2025 [1], so boat and RV owners are not the problem. Rents are: the operators who post prices charge $90 to $175 a month for covered space [10][14], and the land assumption, $1,399,000 for about 2.7 acres, is close per acre to what finished storage facilities sell for. The sponsor's 10.0% proposal misses the new-business tier, and even at 15.0% the facility covers Year 2 debt service only 0.01x. The determination is negative: the market does not support this loan as designed.

Market Conclusion

The owners are here. The rents are not.

Granbury has the customers an RV and boat storage facility needs and the competitors to serve them at prices that do not pay for new construction on this land. That is the finding, and it holds at every equity share the 504 structure allows. A lender should decline the proposal as designed and tell the sponsor why, because the reasons point to what a workable project would look like.

The demand side is sound. Granbury had 13,263 residents in July 2025, up 21.0% from an April 2020 base of 10,957, and Hood County had 70,501, up 14.5% [1]. The households are older and better off than the state's: 29.9% of city residents are 65 or older, the median owner-occupied home is worth $315,800, and county median household income is $88,160 against $78,476 for Texas [1]. Those are the owners of motorhomes, fifth wheels and lake boats, and the lake is the reason many of them live here.

The supply side is crowded. This study confirmed 13 operators with RV or boat parking on their own websites in and around Granbury, and found a fourteenth with only a social media page. The Dallas-Fort Worth market as a whole had 130 dedicated facilities on 1,488.91 acres in October 2025, delivered 28.3% of its inventory in the prior three years, and saw parking rents rise only 1.9% in the year to September 2025 against 4.4% nationally [33]. Locally, every size on Park'er Here's rate card showed available on October 15, 2026, and A&E offered covered and uncovered spaces online the same day [10][14].

The price side decides the loan. Posted covered rents run $90 to $175 a month by length at Park'er Here, $128 for a 45-foot space at A&E, and under $150 for Vanguard's largest pull-through spaces [10][11][14]. The land assumption, $1,399,000 for about 2.7 acres, is the 2026 asking price the research found for this corridor, and per acre it is close to the more than $570,000 an acre that completed RV and boat storage facilities sold for nationally in 2025 [33]. The listing is not cited, so that no real parcel is identified. A new facility that pays nearly the finished-asset price for raw land, then builds canopies and paving at 2026 costs and pays Texas property tax on all of it, cannot earn its debt at Granbury rents. The model shows net operating income of $23,438 in Year 3 against annual debt service of $254,256.

The RV and boat storage hub sets out the format's national economics. The Dallas-Fort Worth metro page covers the wider market Granbury belongs to, and the Texas state page covers statewide taxes and rules.

Demand

A lake town of retirees and commuters.

Storage demand comes from owners who cannot or will not keep a boat or RV at home, and it is measured by drive time to home or to the launch ramp rather than by a fixed radius. Radius and drive-time demographics were not available, so the table brackets the market with city, county and state figures. A lender should order a drive-time profile around the lake's residential shoreline.

Three things in the numbers favor the use. The city is small and old, with a median age of 51 against 45.9 for the county [3][4], and its households are small, 2.13 persons each against 2.69 statewide [1]: retirees with time and money for an RV. County income is high, with per capita income of $43,607 against $40,752 for Texas [1]. And visitors spend real money: the city's accommodation and food services sales were $136,781,000 in the 2022 Economic Census [1], and its hotel and motel tax brought in $1,087,725 in fiscal 2023 against $1,132,517 the year before [25].

The employment base is anchored by Comanche Peak, the nuclear plant in neighboring Somervell County, with more than 600 employees and more than 200 permanent contractors at a 2,400-megawatt station [23]; its two units are licensed into 2050 and 2053 [24]. Hood County also faces eight data center proposals covering more than 7,600 acres, and Texas has at least 248 data centers planned, nearly half in unincorporated areas [26]. Those are proposals, not commitments, and the study counts no demand from them.

Tourism is real but it is not storage demand. The city's 2023 financial report shows tourism revenue of $1,715,589 and conference center revenue of $1,036,030, from an 18,000 square foot center next to a hotel [25]. Visitors who tow a boat to the lake for a weekend take it home again; the storage customer is the resident or second-home owner who keeps a boat or RV within reach of the water. The study therefore sizes demand from the resident base and treats visitor spending as evidence of the lake's pull, not as a source of tenants.

The national demand inputs are weakening. The RV Industry Association cut its 2026 wholesale shipment forecast to a median of 314,000 units in its Summer 2026 outlook, 8.2% below the 342,200 shipped in 2025 [36], and shipments through August 2026 were 207,191, down 14.2% [37]. New powerboat retail sales were 214,292 in the twelve months to April 2026, down 7.1% [38]. The installed base still grows each year, and storage demand follows owners rather than new sales, but softer sales mean slower growth in the pool of new customers. RVIA's 2025 owner profile counts 8.1 million RV-owning households, 6.7% of households with vehicles [40], and Texas had 552,138 registered recreational vessels in 2024 [39].

A fourteenth operator, J&B RV Ranch and Boat Storage, offers storage and RV living spots but has only a social media page, so it stays out of the table and is a condition to confirm. Only three operators publish full rates, and their cards agree: covered space rises with length from $90 to $175 a month, and open space rents for $40 to $50. Vanguard's $15 starting price for uncovered spaces and its $1 move-in offer [11] are the clearest sign that operators are competing on price for open capacity rather than rationing space.

The product mix matters for the subject. Most local supply is covered or open parking, the product the subject would build. Enclosed units are thinner, with Dry Dock and Lock's five buildings the main example [12], but no Granbury operator posts an enclosed rate, so a lender cannot underwrite an enclosed premium here from public evidence.

The pipeline is modest but real. Granbury noticed a Specific Use Permit, SUP-25-11, for self-service storage in Heavy Commercial on a 2.17-acre US 377 parcel listed at $1,300,000, heard by the City Council on February 3, 2026, and another, SUP-25-14, for a truck parking lot, heard by the Planning and Zoning Commission on February 9 and the Council on March 3, 2026 [7][8]; neither outcome was found. A 39,575 square foot addition at Store House Storage on FM 4, with an estimated cost of $2,500,000, was registered with the state on October 16, 2025 and scheduled to finish on October 1, 2026 [9]. That addition is climate-controlled self-storage and competes for a different customer. The next ring of counties was not searched.

Site

A fenced corner on US 377 by the airport.

The hypothetical project sits on a 2.7-acre corner parcel on US 377 near Lake Granbury and the city's regional airport, in Hood County. The study assumes fenced, unimproved land with public water and sewer available, and prices it at $1,399,000, the 2026 asking price the research found for a corridor tract of that size. That listing is not cited, so that no real parcel is identified. An appraisal sets the value, and it is a condition.

Zoning is the first open question. The research found the corridor tract marketed only as commercial and industrial land, and the city district was not confirmed. Granbury's use table permits auto, boat, trailer, RV and truck storage by right in the Industrial district, requires a Specific Use Permit in Heavy Commercial, and prohibits it in the lighter commercial districts; self-service or mini-warehouse storage needs a permit in both Heavy Commercial and Industrial [5]. The supplemental standards require a site plan, lighting directed away from homes, no wrecked vehicles and an improved hard surface [5]. If the lot is Heavy Commercial, a permit is required. A permit goes from the Planning and Zoning Commission to the City Council, becomes a zoning amendment and may be time-limited [6]; the one hearing cycle observed in 2026 ran about three weeks between the commission and the council [7].

The flood zone, the electric and gas providers and the water and sewer provider were not found; the city is presumed to serve water and sewer but that is unverified. No TxDOT count at the site was found; the state's public traffic count layer, updated January 6, 2025, is the source a lender should read [32]. TxDOT's US 377 corridor study notes that 84 miles of US 377 are not yet four lanes [27], which matters for towing customers more than for daily traffic.

Revenue is spaces times monthly rent times twelve times occupancy, for covered and open spaces separately. The program is 70 covered and 30 open spaces, about as many as a 2.7-acre lot holds once drive aisles wide enough for motorhomes, a gate, a wash bay and setbacks are laid out. Cushman & Wakefield uses 40 spaces an acre when it estimates supply [34]; the subject is below that because a corner lot loses more land to access.

Rents. Covered space is priced at $135 a month in Year 1, Park'er Here's 50-foot rate and between A&E's 45-foot price and Vanguard's ceiling for its largest pull-through spaces [10][11][14]. A new facility could argue for a premium, but the operators who post prices all show space available, so the study does not assume one. Open space is $50, Park'er Here's outside RV rate and just above A&E's uncovered rate [10][14]. Both grow at 1.9% a year, the Dallas-Fort Worth parking rent change for the year to September 2025 [33].

Context for the rent level. Yardi Matrix put national advertised parking rents at $6.38 per square foot a year in September 2025, up 4.4%, with large 12 by 40 to 12 by 50 foot units at $3.80, and Dallas-Fort Worth at $5.69 [33]. Granbury's posted covered rents translate to well under the metro average for spaces of that size, which is consistent with a small-town market at the edge of the metro and a large local supply. A lender should not import metro rents into a Granbury pro forma.

Occupancy. The ramp averages 85.0% in the stabilized year, reaching a level close to the 8.5% vacancy a 56-acre Florida facility carried in its 2023 loan underwriting [44]. One Florida facility that opened in June 2025 signed 30 leases a month against the 17 it projected [46]; Granbury, where incumbents show open space, is not that market, so the first year is slower.

Revenue in Year 3 is $115,975. That is the whole income of the facility; there is no fuel, food or service line to cross-subsidize it, and the only ancillary items a storage yard usually sells, such as wash bay or dump station use, are too small and too unevidenced to model.

RV and boat storage is cheap to run. A 1,489,149 square foot Florida facility on 56.28 acres carried an expense ratio of 29.2% and a net operating income margin of 70.8% in its 2023 securitization underwriting [44]. The subject has no on-site staff: a third-party manager at 6.0% of revenue, the typical self-storage fee [45], runs it remotely, and a fixed allowance of $30,000 covers insurance, utilities, repairs, software and marketing.

Property tax breaks the pattern. Those benchmark margins belong to large or mature facilities whose assessed value is low relative to their rent roll. A new facility on land bought at the corridor's asking price and improvements at 2026 cost carries a tax bill sized to that cost, and in this model the tax line is the largest expense by a wide margin. That is why the net operating income margin is only 20.2% in Year 3. A successful protest of the assessed value could lower the bill, but even with no tax at all, Year 3 income would cover only a fraction of debt service.

Under SOP 50 10 8.1 with Technical Policy Updates, effective October 1, 2026 [54], the 504 structure is Third Party Lender 50%, debenture 40%, borrower 10%; debenture 35% at a 15% contribution and 30% at 20%. RV and boat storage is not on SBA's special purpose list [54], so the property does not raise the tier. The business does: a borrower with two years or less of operating history must contribute 15%, not the standard 10%. Had the facility also been special purpose, the tier would be 20%.

The sponsor's 10.0% proposal treats the project as an existing business. Restructured to the new-business tier, the debenture is $1,233,809, well inside the $5 million limit, priced at the 25-year effective rate of 6.97% from the October 8, 2026 pricing posted by a CDC [49]. The model also finances the fiscal 2027 upfront fee of 0.50% [55], which the effective rate already reflects, so the fee is counted conservatively; the annual service fee is 0.203% [55]. The bank's first lien is priced at 7.03%, the 10-year fixed rate a broker quoted for self-storage loans on July 27, 2026 [48], with Prime at 7.00% on October 9, 2026 [56]. Both rates are set at pricing.

Two eligibility checks run before any of this. Since March 1, 2026 every direct and indirect owner must be a U.S. citizen or U.S. national with a principal residence in the United States [50]. And no 504 loan to a Hood County borrower in 2024 to 2026 was found; the SBA loan-level file dated June 30, 2026 is where a lender would look for local precedent [51]. Storage itself is a strong credit class when it is underwritten on real rents: the self-storage CMBS delinquency rate was 0.1% in September 2025 against 6.6% for all CMBS [47].

Other 504 cases on this site show the same rules producing workable loans. The Seguin car wash case is a new business in a special purpose property and clears at its tier, and the Weatherford dealership case, also in Dallas-Fort Worth, is a 504 project outside the special purpose list. The SBA 7(a) and 504 practice page describes how we scope these studies.

As proposed: the sponsor applies the standard contribution and overlooks the new-business test, with 10.0% equity ($352,605) and $3,173,447 of debt. The structure does not qualify: the equity injection is below the program minimum; coverage falls below the floor.

As restructured: 15.0% equity ($528,775) and $2,996,394 of debt with $254,256 of annual debt service. The project still fails at least one test; the market does not support this loan.

The 504 coverage floor is 1.15x [54]. SBA measures it on historical EBITDA where a business has history; a new facility has none, so the test falls on Year 2 of the projection.

As proposed, the sponsor contributes $352,605, 10.0% of cost. The structure fails the equity test, because a new business must contribute the higher tier, and it fails coverage by a wide margin: Year 2 income covers debt service 0.01x.

As restructured, the contribution rises to 15.0%, $528,775, and debt service falls to $254,256. The equity test is met. Coverage is still 0.01x in Year 2 and 0.09x in Year 3. The engine then looks for the smallest equity share that would meet the floor and finds none: in a 504 project the bank's first lien stays at half of cost however much the sponsor puts in, and the facility's income does not cover even that first-lien payment. Raising equity shrinks the debenture, but it cannot shrink the bank loan below half of cost inside the program.

A 7(a) loan would not change the arithmetic either. A single bank loan for most of the cost would carry a higher rate than the blended 504 structure, so debt service would rise, not fall. The problem is the ratio of cost to income, and no SBA program changes that ratio.

The determination is therefore negative, and it is not a close call. A lender should not approve this structure, and should not look for one by stretching amortization or lowering the rate. The sensitivity table below shows that no plausible combination of rent and occupancy changes the answer.

The break-even solves for the covered rent at which Year 3 income equals debt service, holding everything else. It finds none: even triple the underwritten covered rent does not cover the restructured debt service, so the table shows only the underwritten rent and the debt service. For comparison, the highest covered rate any Granbury operator posts is $175 a month, for a 65-foot space [10].

A break-even that sits outside the market's price range is the clearest form of the problem the break-even occupancy note describes: occupancy or rent assumptions are not the issue when the cost basis is too high for any reasonable rent to carry.

Occupancy above the stabilized level is not realistic in a market with open space at every posted operator, and a 20% premium on the 50-foot covered rate would put the subject above every posted Granbury price for a space of that length.

What would change the answer is not on either axis. The land basis and the tax bill on new improvements are the inputs that sink the project. A sponsor who could buy a smaller, cheaper industrial tract, or acquire an existing facility at a price its income supports, would face a different calculation.

A workable version would start from the cost side. Land zoned Industrial, where vehicle storage is permitted by right [5], avoids the permit risk and usually trades below highway commercial frontage. Building open, paved spaces first and adding canopies only as occupancy proves the covered rent defers the most expensive line until the market has priced it. And buying an operating facility lets the lender underwrite real rent rolls and real tax bills rather than a projection. Each of those is a different project with its own study; none of them is this one, and the lender should not let the sponsor's land contract drive the structure.

Risk Factors

Why even a cheaper version would carry risk.

  • Local oversupply. Thirteen operators confirmed on their own sites, open space at those that post availability, and a Dallas-Fort Worth market that delivered 28.3% of its inventory in three years [33] mean any new facility competes on price from day one.
  • Price competition. Vanguard advertises uncovered space from $15 and a $1 move-in [11]. Cushman & Wakefield reports numerous examples of occupancy loss from aggressive rate increases at storage facilities [34], so rent growth is not a reliable cure.
  • Softer equipment sales. RV shipments were down 14.2% through August 2026 [37] and new powerboat sales down 7.1% over twelve months [38]; fewer new units slow the growth of the storage customer pool.
  • Land basis. At the corridor's asking price, land alone costs close per acre to what finished facilities sold for in 2025 [33]. A buyer who paid that would struggle to sell the facility at cost.
  • Entitlement. If the parcel is Heavy Commercial, the use needs a Specific Use Permit that may be time-limited and must pass the council [5][6]; a denial or a short term would end the project after land costs are sunk.
  • Tax growth. The city's 2025 rate rose to $0.4200 from $0.3850 [30]. A storage yard has little ability to pass a tax increase through to tenants who can move their boat across town.
  • Weather. Local operators market hail and ice-storm protection [11]; a hail event on unprotected open spaces can drive tenants to covered competitors, and canopies themselves need wind and hail coverage.
  • Speculative demand. Data center proposals covering more than 7,600 acres in Hood County [26] could bring construction workers and RV living demand, but none is committed, and the county faces litigation over them.

The SOP 50 10 8.1 note covers the rule changes that took effect on October 1, 2026, and the Bastrop hotel case shows a Texas 504 project where the market does carry new construction.

Lender Conditions

What a lender would need before reconsidering.

The determination is negative, so these conditions describe what a lender would need before it could look at any revised project on this site. They cover the 13 groups of items the research marked not found and the figures this page dropped because their sources carried no date.

  1. Zoning. Confirm the parcel's district on the city zoning map, and if it is Heavy Commercial, the Specific Use Permit path, timeline, any time limit and the outcome of SUP-25-11 and SUP-25-14.
  2. Parcel records and value. Pull the Hood Central Appraisal District record: owner, parcel number, recorded acreage, 2025 value and tax, and special district rates; order an appraisal of the land, since the land input is an asking price.
  3. Flood and environment. Pull the FEMA flood zone and obtain a Phase I environmental site assessment.
  4. Utilities and fees. Identify the water, sewer, electric and gas providers and any impact or connection fees.
  5. Traffic. Read the TxDOT count, station and year on US 377 at the site.
  6. Trade area. Order drive-time demographics, daytime employment and the missing Texas comparison figures, and obtain Lake Granbury visitation, launch counts, local boat registrations and monthly seasonality.
  7. Competitors. Obtain space counts, posted rates, occupancy and opening years for the eleven operators that do not publish them, confirm J&B RV Ranch and Boat Storage, and search the next ring of counties.
  8. Enclosed rents. If a revised project includes enclosed units, obtain Granbury enclosed rents from operators such as Dry Dock and Lock before underwriting any premium.
  9. Costs and insurance. Obtain a contractor's bid, a construction cost index for the area, insurance quotes for hail, wind and flood, and the license and certificate of occupancy requirements.
  10. Tax assessment. Confirm with the appraisal district how new storage improvements are valued, and the assessment basis.
  11. Program. Confirm owner citizenship, the borrower's age and therefore the equity tier, Hood County 504 lending history from the FOIA file, the CDC and third-party lender, and USDA rural, HUBZone and Opportunity Zone status; set rates at pricing.
Granbury Storage Questions

What a lender should ask about this Granbury project.

Is RV and boat storage special purpose property under SBA 504?

No. SBA's special purpose list names car washes, marinas, hotels and others, but not self-storage or RV and boat storage [54]. The equity tier for this project is set by the borrower's age alone: 15% for a new business, 10% for an existing one.

If Granbury has so many boat and RV owners, why does the project fail?

Because the rent the market pays is low relative to what the land and canopies cost. Park'er Here posts a 50-foot covered space at $135 a month and A&E a 45-foot space at $128, checked October 15, 2026 [10][14], while the land assumption, the 2026 asking price the research found for a 2.7-acre corner on US 377, is close per acre to what finished storage facilities sold for in 2025 [33]. Texas property tax on a new facility valued at cost takes a large share of what those rents bring in.

Is there evidence of unmet demand in Granbury?

Not in the posted data. Every size on Park'er Here's rate card showed available on October 15, 2026, and A&E offered covered and uncovered spaces online the same day [10][14]. One operator, Lake Granbury Boat and RV Storage, says spaces are limited [13], and the Dallas-Fort Worth market added more new facilities than any other in the three years to September 2025 [33].

What would make an RV and boat storage loan work here?

A much lower land basis, an existing facility bought at a price its income supports, or a product with materially higher rent than the covered spaces Granbury operators post. Enclosed units are one candidate, but no Granbury enclosed rent was found: Dry Dock and Lock does not post its prices [12], and a lender would need that evidence before underwriting it.

Does the zoning allow RV and boat storage on US 377?

It depends on the district, which was not confirmed. Granbury's use table lists auto, boat, trailer and RV storage as a Specific Use Permit in Heavy Commercial and by right in Industrial, and prohibits it in the lighter commercial districts [5]. The lender must confirm the district on the city's zoning map.

Should the sponsor take this study to an SBA lender anyway?

Only with a different project. 13 CFR 120.160(b): SBA may require professional appraisals, a survey, or a feasibility study [53], and a CDC reviewing this pro forma would reach the same arithmetic. The study's value to the sponsor is that it shows the problem before land is bought.

Weighing RV and boat storage near a Texas lake? Price the land against the rent first.
