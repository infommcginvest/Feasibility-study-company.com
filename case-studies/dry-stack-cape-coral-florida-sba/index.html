Dry-Stack Boat Storage Feasibility Study in Cape Coral, Florida: 240 Racks Tested Against SBA 504

Market analysis by Mark Otava · Published October 10, 2026 · Data as of October 2026

Representative worked example. The site, sponsor and project are hypothetical. The market evidence is real, public and dated October 2026. This page shows how the market analysis is built and how it sets the loan. It is not a client engagement.

Some feasibility studies end with a structure that works. This one ends with a number the market would have to reach first. Cape Coral has the boats, the population growth and a waterfront that sends boaters to the Gulf by way of the Caloosahatchee. What it does not have, on any rate a Southwest Florida operator publishes, is rack rent high enough to pay for a new hurricane-rated building. This case shows how the market analysis gets there and what would change the answer.

The market conclusion

The addressable fleet is large: roughly 34,700 boats in the 16-to-under-40-foot classes in Lee County, against 240 racks, so the project needs well under 1% of the core fleet. But the only rack rates Southwest Florida operators publish sit between about $15 and $22 per foot per month, and most facilities quote only on request. At $22, the building earns $329,438 of net operating income on a $20.5 million cost. Covering debt 1.25 times would take $36 to $40 per foot at the average boat length the county's fleet suggests, roughly double the posted band. Separately, SBA treats a marina as eligible only when more than half its revenue comes from transients staying 30 days or less, and monthly and annual rack contracts do not meet that test as written.

The growth is in the 26-to-under-40-foot class, which earns more per rack but needs taller bays and heavier forklifts. The smaller class has contracted. Lee's pleasure fleet peaked at 50,090 in 2022 and fell to 48,139 in 2024. That timing coincides with Hurricane Ian in September 2022, which suggests lost boats and slow replacement rather than structural decline, though the data do not prove the cause. An older, fast-growing, boat-owning population is a sound demand base. It is not an exceptional one, and it does not by itself set rate.

At the $22 band, the building supports about $3.0 million of debt at 1.25x, roughly 14% of what it costs. Even the premium case covers only 0.57x at 75% leverage and 0.69x at 60%. The gap is replacement cost: about $85,000 per rack all-in, against rents that the region's published operators set for buildings that were paid for long ago. Institutional appetite for the asset class is not in doubt (Blackstone Infrastructure closed a $5.65 billion purchase of the largest marina platform in April 2025 [21]), but platform pricing is not a comparable for a single start-up building, and no Florida single-asset dry-stack sale with a public cap rate was found.

SBA eligibility comes before economics

SOP 50 10 8.1 with Technical Policy Updates (effective October 1, 2026) admits a marina only if more than 50% of its revenue, prior-year or projected for a start-up, comes from transients staying 30 days or less [22]. SBA's 504 eligibility form asks the same question [23]. Rack customers in Southwest Florida contract monthly or annually, so the core storage line will not count as transient. Fuel, store, launch and club revenue might help the mix, but whether club usage counts as transient is not settled in any source reviewed. That is a question for the CDC and SBA in writing, before any further cost is spent. Marinas also sit on SBA's special-purpose list, which raises the minimum equity.

What would change the answer

  1. A lower basis. Buying and repositioning an existing rack building at a price the rates support. At $22 per foot the income carries about $3.0 million of debt, which points to an acquisition, not a new barn.
  2. Rate evidence. Dated, written annual and monthly quotes for 24-, 30- and 36-foot boats from PORT 32, Safe Harbor Cape Harbour, Sweetwater, Snook Bight, Salty Sam's, Moss and Burnt Store, with waitlist status. If a new hurricane-rated building can hold $32 or more at a 34-foot average, the conventional structure comes within reach.
  3. A different program. If the revenue mix cannot clear 50% transient, conventional bank debt at lower leverage.
  4. Storm risk priced in. Hurricane-rated specifications, business-interruption cover and a debt-service reserve sized to a 12- to 24-month outage. One Fort Myers marina reopened in September 2026 after a $15 million rebuild that did not start until early 2025, more than two years after Ian [24].

Other local inputs a lender will ask for

Lee County's manatee protection plan was approved in 2004 for both incorporated and unincorporated areas, and Cape Coral waters carry mapped slow-speed and idle zones [25]. A dry stack's queuing docks and launch well over sovereign submerged lands pay the greater of a per-square-foot base rate ($0.156623 currently) or 6% of gross revenue from the use, with a 30% discount for marinas open to the public on a first-come basis and 10% for designated Clean Marinas [26]. Zoning height limits for dry storage in Cape Coral, flood zone and Coastal Barrier Resources Act status, and 2026 construction bids were not found in public sources and need a zoning verification letter, a flood determination and an estimator's cost.

Questions sponsors ask about this market

What does rack storage cost in Southwest Florida?

Posted rates run from about $15.63 to $22.25 per foot per month. Most Cape Coral and Fort Myers operators quote on request.

Can an SBA 504 loan finance a dry-stack marina?

Only if more than half of revenue, actual or projected, comes from transients staying 30 days or less. A storage business built on monthly and annual contracts does not meet that test on its face. Get a written reading from the CDC before spending on design.

Is there enough demand for 240 more racks?

Probably. The project needs well under 1% of Lee County's core fleet, and the 26-to-under-40-foot class is growing. The constraint is rate, not boats.

Why would a feasibility study recommend against a project?

Because a lender will reach the same answer later, after the sponsor has spent on design and permits. Finding the rate the project needs, early, is the cheaper result.
