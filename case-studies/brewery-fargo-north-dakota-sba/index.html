Brewery and Taproom Feasibility Study Case: Fargo, North Dakota, SBA 504

Market analysis by Mark Otava · Published November 4, 2026 · Data as of November 2026

Representative worked example. The site, sponsor and project are hypothetical. The market evidence comes from the public sources listed below, with their dates. This page shows how the market analysis is built and how it sets the loan. It is not a client engagement.

This brewery feasibility study case tests a production brewery with a taproom in Fargo, where Cass County held 126,659 covered jobs in March 2026 [1]. Demand is not the obstacle. Debt is: the program minimum leaves Year 2 coverage at 0.76x, and the model reaches the floor only when the sponsor puts in 40.7% of cost.

Study at a Glance

A production brewery with a taproom in Fargo, tested against the SBA 504 rules.

Feasibility Study Company prepares brewery feasibility studies for owners, developers and their lenders. A brewery study has four jobs: read the market from dated records, count the competing field, price the build, and test how much debt the project can carry. This page does those four jobs for one hypothetical project, a production brewery with a taproom in Fargo, Cass County, North Dakota, financed with an SBA 504 loan. If you are looking for a brewery feasibility study consultant or a brewery feasibility study company for a Fargo site, have us put the same questions to your project.

The subject is generic: a one-story building of about 10,000 square feet on about 2.0 acres along the I-29 corridor, with about 3,500 square feet for the taproom and the rest given to brewing, storage, packaging and an office. The market figures are dated public records. The engine produces the model figures from an input list near the end of the page, in which each line names a source or says assumption. The project falls under our brewery and distillery practice, the city is in North Dakota, and the program rules are described on our SBA 7(a) and 504 practice page.

Federal rules say that SBA may require a feasibility study on a loan: 13 CFR 120.160(b): SBA may require professional appraisals, a survey, or a feasibility study [7]. Whether yours does is decided file by file, and a lender often asks for one before SBA does.

Market Conclusion

Fargo has the jobs and the visitors for a taproom. The debt, not the demand, sets the loan.

The market read is favorable on the demand side and cautious on the supply side. Cass County held 126,659 covered jobs in March 2026 [1], the Fargo metro labor force stood at 156.3 thousand in September 2026 [2], and the county's real output reached $14,875,063 thousand in chained 2017 dollars in 2024 [9]. Visitors arrive by air in growing numbers: Hector International Airport recorded 590,542 boardings in 2025 [3] and a September 2026 record of 49,464 [10]. Those are the people who fill a taproom on a weeknight and buy a case to take home.

The supply read is thinner than a market brief would like, and we say so. No document in the research for this case names a Fargo brewery, so the competitor census is a lender condition, and the competitors section carries the Census Bureau's own count instead: at least four brewery and distillery establishments in Cass County and at least five in the metro area [5]. The count is a floor, because the Bureau withholds some small cells.

The financing answer comes out of the model. Under the sponsor's 10.0% equity proposal the project costs $5,782,766 and covers Year 2 debt service only 0.76x. The engine resizes the structure: the contribution rises to 40.7% of project cost, or $2,349,971, the bank lends $2,886,942 and the debenture falls to $536,971. On those terms Year 2 coverage is 1.15x against a floor of 1.15x, and the stabilized Year 3 reaches 1.29x. The project can be financed, but not with the thin down payment the sponsor had in mind. Talk to us about testing your own brewery against the same floor.

Jobs and wages

Cass County is the state's employment center. Its 126,659 covered jobs in March 2026 are about three in ten of the 420,702 covered jobs in North Dakota [1], and its average weekly wage of $1,376 sits above the state's $1,333 [1]. A taproom lives on discretionary spending, and a county that pays above the state average and holds a large payroll base is the right place for it. The metro's jobless rate of 2.3% in September 2026 [2] says the same thing from the other side: labor is scarce, which raises the cost of hiring bartenders and brewers. The model carries that cost in its wage inputs.

The trend is flat rather than strong. Statewide nonfarm jobs slipped from 448.7 thousand in August to 447.3 thousand in September 2026 [12], and the twelve-month change was only 0.2% [4]. The Fargo metro supports 155.9 thousand nonfarm jobs [2]. The study therefore does not assume a hiring boom behind the taproom. Statewide leisure and hospitality employment of 41.6 thousand [13] is the closest labor-market series the research holds for the trade the taproom competes in for staff.

Output and housing

Cass County's real GDP of 14,875,063 thousand chained 2017 dollars in 2024 [9] is the county series; the older metro series, 20,535.575 million dollars for 2023, has been discontinued by its publisher and is cited only for scale [14]. The state economy printed $82,349.8 million in current dollars and 65,268.3 million in chained 2017 dollars for 2025 [15][16].

Housing shows household wealth. The median listing price in Cass County was $399,625 in September 2026, with 712 active listings [17][18]. The federal house price index for the Fargo metro stood at 365.43 in the second quarter of 2026 [19], and the statewide index at 539.91 [20]; the two indexes use different base periods and are not compared. Permits show the building cycle: Fargo's monthly count peaked at 281 in July 2025 and fell to 16 in December and 3 in January 2026 [21], a seasonal drop in a cold climate that also tells a builder to plan a winter schedule.

Visitors and travel

Hector International Airport recorded a record 590,542 boardings in 2025 [3]. In June 2026 it set a June record of 50,959 [22], and in September 2026 a September record of 49,464 [10]. Across North Dakota's commercial airports, boardings reached 973,044 in the first nine months of 2026 [10], and the state recorded 1,322,791 for all of 2025 [3]. September 2026 alone brought 104,380 boardings statewide [10], and March 2025 set a state monthly record of 123,180 [23]. Airport traffic is an imperfect proxy for taproom visitors, but it shows that outside spending reaches Fargo in rising volume.

The state bank and credit

North Dakota has a state-owned lender that matters to a borrower here. The Bank of North Dakota reported total assets of $10.7 billion at the end of 2025 and a total return to the state of 4.69% for the year [24]. Whether it takes a participation in a brewery loan is a lender question, not a finding, but a bank that knows the state bank can ask.

Downtown and the apartment base

Downtown Fargo is the district where an urban taproom would compete for foot traffic. An appraisal firm's quarterly survey puts metro apartment vacancy at 6.2% in the second quarter of 2025 and downtown vacancy at just over 16%, after nearly 850 downtown units were built in five years [25]. Vacancy that high downtown cuts two ways: new residents live near the taproom district, but the district has more apartments than renters for now. The subject sits on the I-29 corridor instead, where it can draw from both the downtown crowd and the suburban metro, and the lender should test that choice against a drive-time study.

What the count tells a newcomer

The class holds at least four establishments and 90 paid employees in the county, and at least five establishments and 101 employees in the metro area [5]. Employees outnumber establishments by a wide margin, which says the existing operations are real production businesses with staff, not one-person shops. The rate per ten thousand residents is a small fraction of one establishment in both geographies, shown in the table, which suggests a thinly served market, though a low rate can also mean that a small market has little room. The study does not read it as an opportunity by itself.

The program's own history is thin. The SBA data show 2 504 loans to this class in North Dakota in the ten fiscal years to 2025, totaling $1,126,000 [26]. A lender and a CDC in the state have little local precedent for a brewery, which makes the documentation, the equipment appraisal and the job plan more important.

Distilleries in the same class

The class combines breweries and distilleries, so some of the establishments counted are distilleries that do not compete for the same pint. Part of the lender condition is to separate the two. The condition also asks for each brewery's taproom hours, tap count, distribution footprint and packaging format, none of which the research could find.

Site

An arterial site with truck access: zoning, utilities and wastewater are all still unknown.

The hypothetical site covers about 2.0 acres along the I-29 corridor in Fargo. Its parcel, zoning district, utility territory and flood status have yet to be confirmed. The research could not retrieve a parcel record, a zoning map, a traffic count or a utility tariff, because the city and state agency hosts refused the connection, and the study says so rather than guess.

Zoning, frontage and utilities

A production brewery is an industrial use with a retail use attached, and many cities treat the two differently: the taproom may be allowed by right in a commercial district while the production hall needs an industrial or mixed district. The lender needs a written zoning determination that covers both, the parking requirement for the taproom and any limit on outdoor seating. It also needs the names of the utilities that supply power, gas, water and sewer to the parcel, and the commercial capacity and wastewater discharge charges in force. A brewery uses far more water and discharges stronger wastewater than a retail building of the same size, so the pre-treatment allowance in site work is a real cost that the city's rule can raise or lower.

Traffic and visibility

The research holds no count for the corridor. Traffic counts from a state station, the nearest interchange, the sign rules and the turning movements at the entrance are lender conditions. Retail space in the metro gives a rough view of how location is priced: Goldmark Commercial's survey put the average base retail lease rate at $17.08 per square foot in 2025 [27], and the area around West Acres had a vacancy of 2.9% in mid-January 2026 against 18.3% downtown [27]. A taproom in the strong retail pocket would pay more rent but meet more traffic; the subject owns its building instead and gives up that exposure to avoid the rent.

The commercial market behind that rent is soft in places. Metro retail leasing totaled 119,000 square feet in 2025 and leased space fell 43% from the year before, with about 642,000 square feet available at the end of 2025 [27]. Sales of existing retail property rose 41% over the same year, and the average base office lease rate was $15.12 per square foot [27]. A buyer of an existing building may find better terms than a ground-up build.

Land

The research records no Fargo land sale or listing, so the study neither prints nor cites one. The model carries land at a round $300,000 an acre, and the appraisal and a signed purchase contract take its place. The spread between a prime retail pocket and a corridor site is large, and the lender should order a land comparison before the budget is final.

Property tax

The research holds no Cass County or Fargo mill levy, assessment ratio or abatement schedule. The model carries a planning rate of 1.20% of the cost basis, and the actual levy is a lender condition, along with any tax incentive the city offers for a manufacturer.

Weather

Severe storms, tornadoes and straight-line winds on June 20 and 21, 2025 led to a federal disaster declaration, DR-4888, on September 11, 2025, covering 19 counties including Cass [8]. A production building with a large roof, outdoor tanks and a patio is exposed to wind. Insurance quotes and the structural design should price that in.

Volume

The model reaches 3,200 barrels in Year 3, the stabilized level, after a smaller first year. Volume is the driver because every other line moves with it. No Fargo brewer publishes output in the research, so the ramp is an assumption, set so that a newcomer reaches a busy but not saturated taproom in its second year. The test is the number of regulars the taproom can build, and that is a demand study, not a public record.

Channel mix and price

A brewery earns more on a pint poured at its own bar than on a barrel sold through a distributor. The model starts with 40.0% of barrels over the bar, falling as wholesale grows, at $1,400 a barrel on premises and $400 a barrel wholesale, net of the distributor's margin. The pint price behind the taproom figure is an assumption. Events, merchandise and visiting vendors add 8.0% of taproom beer sales.

Revenue grows from $1,720,800 in Year 1 to $2,703,360 in Year 3 and $3,125,760 in Year 5. The mix matters more than the total: a year in which distributors take less than expected leaves the taproom to carry the loan, and the sensitivity table below tests exactly that.

A 504 project has three funding layers. The rules in force from October 1, 2026 (SOP 50 10 8.1 with Technical Policy Updates) [6] set the split as Third Party Lender 50%, debenture 40%, borrower 10%; debenture 35% at a 15% contribution and 30% at 20% [6]. The borrower's minimum rises with risk: 10% in the standard case, 15% for a new business or special purpose property, and 20% for both [6].

A brewery is a new business here, and the study treats the building as special purpose, because the SBA's example list of special purpose property names wineries and describes a limited-market layout; whether a lender treats a brewhouse the same way is a lender condition, and it moves the minimum between 15% and 20%. At the sponsor's 10.0% the proposal fails the equity test and the coverage test, even though the debenture of $2,313,106 would fit under the manufacturer limit of $5.5 million [6]. The ordinary limit is $5 million [6].

The manufacturer limit is relevant because a brewery's primary activity is making beer. It applies to a small manufacturer, and the lender must document the business's size and its production activity. The taproom does not defeat the claim, but a lender will want to see that production is the primary activity and that the taproom does not outgrow it. The same classification affects fees: the FY2027 notice waives the 504 fees for both 504 fees waived for the same three categories, including 504 refinance loans [28], which include manufacturers. The model does not take the waiver and charges the standard upfront fee of 0.50% [28], so a waiver would be an upside.

Job creation is a second manufacturer rule. A 504 debenture is measured against the jobs the project creates or retains: $95,000 of debenture per job in the standard case and $150,000 for manufacturers and energy projects, under Federal Register 2025-19072 (90 FR 47117), 504 loans approved on or after October 1, 2025 [6]. Because the restructured debenture is small, the job count the project must show is modest compared with the production and taproom payroll in the model, and the lender should still ask for a payroll plan with start dates.

The debenture rate of 6.75% and the bank rate of 7.50% are assumptions. Prime was 7.00% in the Federal Reserve's H.15 release [29], and the bank rate adds a half point for a start-up production business. The program's maturities are 10, 20 or 25 years [6]. The owner-occupancy test asks the operating borrower to occupy 60% of a new building [7], which a brewery that fills its own building meets easily.

The regulation leaves the decision to the file: 13 CFR 120.160(b): SBA may require professional appraisals, a survey, or a feasibility study [7].

As proposed, at 10.0% equity, the project falls short of the 20.0% contribution a new business on special purpose property needs, and Year 2 coverage is 0.76x, below the floor.

As restructured, the engine lifts the contribution to the program tier and then looks for the lowest share at which Year 2 coverage reaches the floor. Risks

What could break the Fargo projection.

The risks below follow from the numbers above.

  1. The taproom does not fill

    The model's second year depends on a taproom busy enough to sell 40.0% of the early barrels. The metro adds jobs slowly, with a twelve-month statewide change of only 0.2% [4], and the Census count of at least four county establishments in the class [5] means a newcomer fights for regulars. A late ramp fails the Year 2 test.

  2. Distributors take less than modelled

    Wholesale carries the rest of the volume. If a distributor drops the line, the taproom must carry the debt alone, and the sensitivity table shows how little room there is. The program has funded the class only twice in the state in ten years [26], so the CDC and bank have little local precedent.

  3. Labor is scarce

    A metro jobless rate of 2.3% [2] and about 41.6 thousand leisure and hospitality jobs statewide [13] mean wages for bartenders and brewers may rise faster than the model's inflation input.

  4. The district weakens

    Downtown retail vacancy stood at 18.3% in January 2026 and leased retail space fell 43% in 2025 [27], and downtown apartment vacancy was just over 16% [25]. A taproom that counts on downtown foot traffic could find less of it. The subject's corridor location reduces the exposure but does not remove it.

  5. Costs run high

    Land, building and equipment are assumptions, and an overrun lands on a contribution that is already large. Building permits drop sharply in winter, with 3 in January 2026 [21], so a late start can push the opening past the planned ramp.

  6. Weather and wastewater

    A June 2025 storm brought a federal declaration for Cass County [8], and an unresolved wastewater charge can add recurring cost. Both are lender conditions.

  7. Rates and the loan market

    The bank and debenture rates are assumptions around a Prime rate of 7.00% [29]. A higher rate at closing lowers coverage, and the restructured case has little spare cushion.

Lender Conditions

Conditions before commitment.

Open questions the public record left for the lender to close are listed below.

  1. Competitor census

    A roster of competitors, each paired with its own website or county assessor record, separating breweries from distilleries, with taproom hours, tap counts, distribution footprint and packaging. The Census count is a floor of at least four county establishments [5], and no research document names the field.

  2. Site, title and zoning

    The county assessor's parcel number, a zoning letter that covers production and a taproom together, the parking requirement, the appraisal, the survey and the sale history of the land.

  3. Utilities, wastewater and taxes

    The utilities that serve the parcel, the capacity and wastewater discharge charges in force, any pre-treatment requirement, the commercial mill levy and any manufacturer tax incentive.

  4. Traffic, flood and environmental

    Daily traffic from a state count station near the corridor, the FEMA flood map panel, a Phase I environmental report, and a wind-load review in light of the 2025 declaration [8].

  5. Licences and taxes on beer

    Federal brewer's notice and permit, state licences for production, wholesale and on-premises sale, and the federal and state excise rates, which are an assumption in the model.

  6. Volume, price and distribution

    Signed distribution letters, a taproom price list, pint and barrel volumes from comparable operators, and a demand study with drive times, since the research holds no brewery sales data.

  7. Costs and equipment

    Contractor bids, vendor quotes for the brewhouse, tanks and packaging line, an appraisal of equipment for the lender's collateral, and quotes for property, liability and liquor liability insurance.

  8. Program items

    Confirmation of the special purpose classification, the manufacturer classification and size, the job plan against the per-job figure, the CDC and bank terms at pricing, owner eligibility documents under the SOP [6], and the NAICS code used for any fee waiver [28].

  9. Sponsor capital

    Proof of the $2,349,971 contribution in the restructured case, its source and seasoning, and a plan for the additional working capital if the ramp is slow.

Fargo Questions

What lenders ask about the Fargo brewery case.

Is there demand for a new brewery taproom in Fargo?

The base is solid. In March 2026 Cass County had 126,659 covered jobs and a weekly wage averaging $1,376 [1], the metro jobless rate was 2.3% in September 2026 [2], and Hector International Airport recorded 590,542 boardings in 2025 [3]. Statewide job growth was only 0.2% over twelve months [4], so demand depends on winning regulars, not on market growth.

How many breweries are there in Fargo?

The research for this case names none, so the study does not either. The Census Bureau's County Business Patterns show at least four brewery and distillery establishments in Cass County and at least five in the metro area [5]. A named census with each brewery's own website is a lender condition.

Why is the equity this brewery needs so far above the program minimum?

The minimum sets a lowest contribution, and coverage sets a second test. A new business on special purpose property puts in at least 20% of project cost [6], and projected coverage in its second year must also reach 1.15x. Second-year earnings are small against the cost of a brewhouse and a building, so the engine kept adding equity until the second test was met.

Does the manufacturer limit apply to a brewery with a taproom?

It can. A brewery's primary activity is production, and the program sets a higher debenture limit for small manufacturers, $5.5 million against $5 million [6]. The lender decides whether the taproom is incidental to production and whether the borrower is a small manufacturer.

Is a feasibility study mandatory for a brewery loan?

Not as a blanket rule: the regulation reads 13 CFR 120.160(b): SBA may require professional appraisals, a survey, or a feasibility study [7], and the research note on that wording sets out what "may" does and does not mean. A lender financing a ground-up brewery for a first-time owner is likely to ask for one, and Feasibility Study Company prepares such studies for owners, developers and their lenders.

Which unknowns about the site could end the project early?

Three the research could not retrieve: whether zoning allows production and a taproom on one parcel, what the city charges for wastewater discharge, and which utilities serve the site. A 2025 storm declaration for Cass County [8] also moves the wind design and insurance quotes to the front of the list.
