Limited-Service Hotel Feasibility Study, Bastrop, Texas: An SBA 504 Worked Case

Market analysis by Mark Otava · Published July 13, 2026 · Updated October 16, 2026 · Data as of October 2026

Representative worked example. The site, sponsor and project are hypothetical. The market evidence comes from the public sources listed below, with their dates. This page shows how the market analysis is built and how it sets the loan. It is not a client engagement.

Bastrop has the demand for another branded hotel: a city that has grown 38.2% since 2020 and a Starlink campus with more than 1,000 workers on the SH 71 corridor [1][39]. It also has 228 extended-stay keys that opened in 2025 and four more branded hotels due between December 2026 and July 2028 [23][15][20]. At the sponsor's 15.0% equity the hotel covers debt 0.82x in Year 2. The market supports the loan at 39.1% equity and $8,457,529 of debt, which reaches 1.15x.

A new hotel on SH 71, tested against a crowded delivery calendar.

Market Conclusion

Demand is there. The calendar is the problem.

Bastrop can absorb another branded limited-service hotel, but not at the leverage the sponsor requested. The city had 13,383 residents on July 1, 2025, against 12,720 a year earlier, and has grown 38.2% since April 2020 [1]. The county reached 118,908 [1]. The Starlink campus on FM 1209 employs more than 1,000 people, and permit filings describe a 1.1 million square foot factory with up to 500 more jobs [39]. When Everhome opened its 114 suites on February 18, 2025, the opening was tied to demand from SpaceX and The Boring Co. [24][23]. On the night of Wednesday, July 22, 2026, Choice's own booking page showed that hotel sold out [25].

The same evidence carries the warning. Branded supply in the city jumped in 2025: Everhome and Home2 Suites added 228 extended-stay keys between February and July [23][36]. Four more branded hotels are in the state record or under construction, with completion dates from December 25, 2026 to July 3, 2028 [15][18][19][20]. A hotel that closes its loan in 2027 opens into the middle of that wave. Its first two years are the years in which the new Marriott and IHG product competes for the same Starlink contractors, wedding blocks and SH 71 travelers.

The model therefore ramps occupancy slowly and prices rooms below the national average. On that basis the hotel earns $840,985 of net operating income in Year 2 and $961,134 in Year 3. The sponsor asked for a 504 structure with 15.0% equity. That fails twice: the equity tier for a new business in a special purpose property is 20%, and coverage in the Year 2 test comes to 0.82x. The structure that clears the 1.15x floor carries 39.1% equity ($5,430,039), a $6,943,784 bank first lien and a $1,513,745 debenture, for $8,457,529 of debt. Coverage then reaches 1.15x in Year 2 and 1.31x in Year 3.

That is a financeable hotel, but it is a different deal from the one proposed. Three facts would move the answer, and each is a lender condition below: an STR trend report for the named competitive set, a land price set by appraisal rather than by assumption, and the adopted city tax rate. A trend report that shows the set running above our occupancy and rate would release equity. One that shows the 2025 openings diluting the older hotels would not.

Demand

Four demand streams, none of them measured in room nights.

Hotel demand in Bastrop comes from four sources, and the public record measures none of them in room nights. That is the central limit of this study. The research found no hotel occupancy tax receipts series, no state park visitation for the town's park, no hospital volumes and no seasonality data. What it did find are the drivers' sizes and their direction.

Industrial and contractor travel. Starlink is the anchor. Texas awarded Starlink a $17.3 million Semiconductor Innovation Fund grant for its Bastrop manufacturing in March 2025 [40]. Local reporting in September 2025 described an expansion of 1 million square feet over three years, with an $8 million phase starting that month [41]. The July 2026 coverage of the newest hotel filing listed a $440 million data center and a film studio among the other projects drawing workers to town [20]. These are weekday travelers: engineers, vendors and construction crews. They book branded limited-service and extended-stay rooms, and they are the demand that justified Everhome and Home2.

Leisure and events. Bastrop's leisure base is older and concentrated. From 2007 to 2015 the Hyatt Regency Lost Pines produced more than 75% of the city's hotel occupancy tax revenue, and a marketing agreement of about $900,000 a year was funded from that tax [42]. A resort that dominant spills weddings, tournaments and golf groups into the limited-service hotels on SH 71 when it fills. The destination office now markets weekend getaways, business meetings, filming and festivals [20], but the record gives no event attendance.

Highway travel. SH 71 is the town's through route, and Bastrop has no interstate. TxDOT publishes station counts [3], but no count for the subject's segment was extracted, so highway demand is a lender condition rather than a number.

Planned public projects. The city contracted Hotel & Leisure Advisors on December 9, 2025 to study a hotel and convention center [6], and a designer has floated a west-side hospital, hotel and convention concept that it says could reach about $1.6 billion [43]. Both are at the idea stage. Neither belongs in a projection, and a city-sponsored convention hotel would be a competitor, not a feeder.

Two demographic facts shape the product. Median household income in the city was $81,551 and per capita income $41,634 in the 2020 to 2024 survey [2], which supports midscale and upper-midscale rates for leisure guests. The city's accommodation and food services sales were $102,439,000 in the 2022 Economic Census, against retail sales of $97,973 per resident [2]. Bastrop is a regional shopping and services town, which adds weekday traffic from the county's 118,908 residents in 36,868 households [1][2].

The demand mix argues for a transient hotel, not a third extended-stay. Long-stay contractor demand is real, but the two 2025 openings were built for it, and the national extended-stay segment added 4.0% to its supply in 2025, its largest increase in four years [55]. The subject's niche is the guest who stays two to five nights: a visiting engineer, a wedding guest, a tournament family. That guest also keeps the borrower inside SBA's lodging rule, discussed in the loan section.

The pipeline is the decision-relevant table. Only one project has a published key count, the 121-key Fairfield and Staybridge. The Chronicle's account of the 75-acre, roughly $300 million Sendero plan includes a 120-room hotel alongside 782 apartments and more than 250,000 square feet of retail and dining [21]. The four branded buildings range from 63,000 to 99,798 square feet, depending on which report of the Sendero building is right [15][17][18][19][20]; for comparison, the 114-suite Everhome is 57,830 square feet [16]. On building area alone, each project is at least as large as Everhome, and each would enter a town whose older branded hotels run from 42 to 89 keys [33][37].

Three of the four are Marriott flags, two of them extended-stay or suite brands. That matters for the subject's brand choice. A Hampton or Holiday Inn Express already operates in town, and Fairfield arrives twice. The sponsor's franchise application will have to clear the brands' area protection, and the lender should see the radius terms before approval. Our note on franchise versus independent underwriting explains why the flag is part of the credit, not a detail of the pro forma.

The national pipeline points the same way, and at the same product. Lodging Econometrics counted 703,001 rooms in the U.S. construction pipeline at the end of June 2026, 133,216 of them under construction, and forecast 74,820 rooms to open in 2026, supply growth of about 1.3% [69]. The upper midscale and upscale chain scales, where select-service brands sit, held 214,027 and 159,252 of those pipeline rooms [70], and the Austin metro carries one of the deepest local pipelines, covered on the Austin market page. Bastrop's own wave, 228 extended-stay keys in 2025 and four more branded hotels by mid-2028, runs far ahead of that national pace. A new select-service hotel here competes for brand approval and for guests in the most crowded part of the pipeline.

The county appraisal district's search is a form, not an indexed record, so parcel numbers, tax years, assessed values and land areas for the set were not retrieved. That becomes a lender condition, because the assessed values of the 2025 openings are the best local evidence of what a new hotel is worth for tax.

Site

A highway pad whose zoning, utilities and traffic count are still open.

The hypothetical site is a 3-acre pad with SH 71 frontage inside the City of Bastrop; no particular tract is assumed. Hotel pads in town run small: the Sendero hotel site under contract in March 2025 was 2.5 acres [22]. Land is carried at $200,000 an acre, an assumption. The research found no recorded sale of SH 71 frontage and one land value on the corridor that this study can use: a 2.47-acre mixed-use parcel on East SH 71, marketed from March 14, 2023 and no longer advertised, carried a total appraisal-district assessment of $503,737, a little over $200,000 an acre [66]. The appraisal district assesses at 100% of market value [7], so the model rounds that level. One assessment is thin evidence: an appraisal with comparable sales sets the value, and it is a lender condition.

Zoning. Bastrop repealed its B3 Code in 2026 and adopted the Bastrop Development Code (Ordinance 2026-06) with a comprehensive zoning map amendment (Ordinance 2026-07) [4]. Any earlier reading of where hotels are allowed has to be checked against the new code. Under Ordinance 2023-16, if the planning and zoning commission denies a zoning change, council approval needs a supermajority [4]. Ordinance 2025-02 capped impervious cover at 50% in the P3 place type, 60% in P4 and 65% in P5 Core [5], a limit that drives parking layout on a 3-acre pad. The pad's district, and whether a hotel is permitted by right in it, are to be confirmed.

Traffic. SH 71 is a TxDOT-maintained state highway. TxDOT's public count layer was updated January 6, 2025 [3], but no station count for the frontage was extracted. The listing for the East SH 71 parcel quotes a daily count with no station and no year [66]; it is not evidence. The lender should pull the nearest TxDOT station and year.

Utilities. Water, sewer, electric and gas providers for the pad were not found. Hotels need sewer capacity and fire flow that a highway pad does not always have, so will-serve letters belong in the conditions.

Flood and fire. The FEMA flood zone was not determined. The city imposed a development moratorium from August 14, 2018 to May 21, 2019 over flooding and drainage [64], and the 2011 Bastrop County Complex fire burned in the county's pine forest [65]. Both belong in the insurance quote and the survey.

Taxes on the property. Bastrop County adopted $0.42870 per $100 for tax year 2025, above its no-new-revenue rate of $0.40151, made up of $0.30678 for maintenance and operations, $0.04371 for debt and $0.07821 for roads and bridges [10]. For fiscal 2026-27 the county raised the rate 6.22% to $0.4554 [11]. Bastrop ISD adopted $1.0679 for 2025-26 ($0.6669 for operations, $0.4010 for debt) [12]. Emergency services district rates for 2025 were $0.10 in ESD No. 3 and a $0.103878 voter-approval rate in ESD No. 2 [14][13]; which district covers the pad was not found. The city's adopted 2025 rate could not be confirmed from a primary record: the city's tax page links only the 2024-25 ordinance [7]. Property is assessed at 100% of market value as of January 1 [7]. The model uses the county's 2026-27 rate, the ISD rate and a labelled placeholder for the city.

Lodging tax. Guests pay 13.75% inside the city or its extraterritorial jurisdiction: 6% state, 7% city and 0.75% county; outside the city the total is 13% [7][9]. The city tax applies to rooms of $2.00 a day or more, gives the hotel a 1% collection allowance, and exempts guests who stay 30 consecutive days or more and state and federal employees on official business [8]. The tax is collected for the government and is not hotel revenue.

Opportunity Zones. Bastrop County has three Opportunity Zone tracts from the 2018 round: 48021950200, 48021950600 and 48021950801 [44]. Texas's next-round designations take effect January 1, 2027 [45]. Whether the pad sits in one was not found; it could change the sponsor's equity source, not the loan.

Revenue is almost all rooms. The model multiplies 80 keys by 365 nights, by occupancy and by average daily rate, and adds a small line for meeting room rental, vending, pet fees and guest laundry at 2.5% of rooms revenue. Total revenue is $2,478,204 in Year 1 and $3,056,452 in Year 3.

Occupancy opens below the national level in Year 1, passes it in Year 2 and reaches 69.0% in Year 3, a point higher from Year 4 (the inputs table lists each year). U.S. hotels ran 62.3% in 2025, the first full-year decline since 2020 [49], and in August 2026 CoStar and Tourism Economics raised their 2026 forecast to 63.1% [67]. The stabilized level is set against the hotels the subject resembles, not the all-hotel average. Apple Hospitality's comparable portfolio of upscale, rooms-focused hotels ran 71.1% occupancy and $111.36 RevPAR in the first quarter of 2025 [56], and a hotel portfolio KBRA rated in December 2024 ran 74.5% over the twelve months to September 2024, at 101.3% of its competitive sets' occupancy [68]. Those are seasoned hotels holding their fair share; the subject is new and opens into a four-hotel pipeline, so it stabilizes a point or two below the Apple portfolio. It stabilizes above the national average for two local reasons: the weekday base around the Starlink campus is growing [39], and midweek compression is already visible, with Everhome sold out on a Wednesday in July 2026 [25]. Extended-stay hotels ran 77.8% in August 2026 against 65.5% for comparable hotels [54], which shows how much of Bastrop's contractor demand the two 2025 openings can hold. CoStar expects 2026 growth in midscale and economy RevPAR to come mainly from demand rather than rate [50], which supports a ramp built on occupancy.

Average daily rate reaches $148 in Year 3. No ADR for Bastrop is published. The aggregator prices found in the research carry no stay date and are asking rates, so they are not used. The model sits below the 2025 U.S. ADR of $160.54 [49] and the $156.56 select-service ADR in Apple Hospitality's first quarter 2025 portfolio [56], and above the $116.08 extended-stay average for the fourth quarter of 2025 [53]. That places the subject where a new upper-midscale hotel in a small, fast-growing town should sit.

Two checks matter more than any single input. First, the lender should compare the Year 3 RevPAR this implies with the trailing twelve months of the Hampton and Holiday Inn Express, which a trend report will give. Second, the Everhome sold-out night shows midweek compression in July 2026 [25]; a trend report will show whether that is a pattern or a single night. Our note on why the STR report is not the analysis explains how the trend report and this projection should be read together.

Operating costs total $2,095,318 in Year 3 and leave net operating income of $961,134, a 31.4% margin after the replacement reserve. CBRE's 2025 sample converted 22.8% of revenue to EBITDA across all hotel types, down from 23.3% in 2024 [48]. A rooms-only hotel with no restaurant should do better than that blend. Apple Hospitality's comparable rooms-focused hotels earned an adjusted hotel EBITDA margin of 32.3% in the first quarter of 2025 [56]; the model's Year 3 margin, after its replacement reserve, sits a little below that seasoned portfolio.

Payroll is the largest cost and is held as a fixed amount that grows 3% a year. Non-union U.S. hotels ran labor at 33.5% of revenue in 2025 [57], a ratio weighted to hotels with food and beverage. Holding payroll fixed is deliberate: when occupancy falls, a hotel cannot cut its front desk and night audit in proportion, and a percentage-of-revenue payroll would flatter the break-even.

Franchise costs run at 10.8% of rooms revenue, the average total franchise cost in HVS's 2020 guide [52]. That guide is six years old; the lender should replace it with the chosen brand's disclosure document. Rooms costs cover linen, supplies, breakfast and online travel agency commissions. Undistributed costs cover utilities, maintenance, sales and administration. A management fee of 3.0% of revenue is carried even if the owner manages, so the projection pays for management.

Property tax applies the county's 2026-27 rate, the ISD rate and a placeholder for the city levy to land and building cost, on the 100% market value basis. The line grows 2% a year. CBRE found midscale hotels' property taxes rose 12.3% in 2025 while their gross operating profit fell 5.7% [48], which is why the tax line is not left flat. Insurance is an allowance pending a quote that prices the county's wildfire history. The reserve sets aside 4.0% of revenue for room renovations and brand improvement plans.

Total project cost is $13,887,569, or $173,595 per key. HVS put the 2025 median for limited-service and midscale extended-stay hotels at $170,000 to $197,000 per room, all-in, with construction costs still rising nationally [47]. The local filings are lower but incomplete: the 114-suite Everhome registered an estimated construction value of $13.0 million for a 57,830 square foot, four-story wood-frame building [16], and the Courtyard registered $10,000,000 for 63,603 square feet [15]. Owner estimates filed with the state exclude furniture, design, financing and land, so the model carries building and site work at $125,000 per key, above those filings. On the value side, U.S. hotels that sold for more than $10 million in 2025 averaged $211,000 per room [71]. Those trades lean to larger hotels in bigger markets, so they cap rather than support this hotel's value; a new limited-service hotel in a town of this size should not be underwritten to appraise above its cost.

The contingency is 10.0% of hard cost, the low end of the 10% to 15% that hotel construction lenders require [51]. Soft costs and construction interest are an allowance. Furniture, fixtures and equipment are carried at $16,000 per key; HVS's full-service median is $36,752 [58], and a rooms-only hotel needs about half.

Two items sit outside the 504 project. Pre-opening payroll, training and a working capital reserve are not fixed assets, and 504 proceeds fund fixed assets. The sponsor must show cash for them in addition to the equity in the table. The SBA fee on the debenture is added to project cost and shown as a use, so the sponsor's equity share carries part of it.

Bank rate: Assumption: Prime was 7.00% on October 9, 2026 [62]. Broker-reported 2026 all-in rates for hotel loans run 6.35% to 7.25% [51]; we take the top of that range for a new-build hotel with no history. The lender sets the rate at pricing. Debenture rate: Source [59].

The 504 program splits a project three ways. Under SOP 50 10 8.1 with Technical Policy Updates, effective October 1, 2026, the standard split is Third Party Lender 50%, debenture 40%, borrower 10%; debenture 35% at a 15% contribution and 30% at 20% [60]. The borrower's share rises with risk. It is 10% for an established business in a general-purpose building, 15% for a new business or a special purpose property, and 20% when both apply [60]. Hotels are on SBA's special purpose property list, and a borrower formed to build and run a new hotel is a new business. The sponsor's 15.0% treats the hotel as one or the other, not both. Our note on special purpose property risk explains why the program asks more of single-use buildings.

The debenture is capped at $5 million for a non-manufacturer [60]. At the sponsor's 15.0%, the debenture came to $4,866,516, under the limit by a small margin. A slightly larger hotel would have hit it. Combined 7(a) and 504 exposure can now reach $10 million [60], which gives room for a later renovation loan but does not change this test. The upfront 504 fee for fiscal 2027 is 0.50% of the debenture, financed [61].

Coverage for a 504 is 1.15x [60]. A new hotel has no history, so the test runs on projections, and this case tests Year 2. The bank rate is an assumption at 7.25%, set from Prime of 7.00% on October 9, 2026 [62] and broker-reported 2026 hotel pricing. The debenture rate is the 25-year effective rate of 6.97% from the October 8, 2026 pricing [59]. Both are set at pricing, and both are conditions below.

Two eligibility rules come before the credit analysis. First, lodging borrowers must earn more than 50% of the prior year's revenue from transients who stay 30 days or less at a time; a start-up shows it in projections [60]. The subject's transient positioning meets that by design, and its projection should show it. Second, since March 1, 2026, all direct and indirect owners and SBA-required guarantors must be U.S. citizens or U.S. nationals with a principal U.S. residence, and entity owners must be organized in the United States (Procedural Notice 5000-876626) [46]. If any owner is a lawful permanent resident rather than a citizen or national, the borrower is ineligible under the current rule, so the check comes first.

On the study itself: 13 CFR 120.160(b): SBA may require professional appraisals, a survey, or a feasibility study [63]. Lenders and CDCs often ask for one on a ground-up hotel. Our note on the SBA feasibility myth separates the rule from the practice, and the SBA 7(a) and 504 practice page describes what our studies cover for both programs.

The break-even holds rate constant and lets occupancy fall, and it keeps payroll fixed. In practice a hotel facing new competition loses rate before it loses occupancy, because the new hotels discount to fill. Risks

What could break this hotel's projection.

  1. Opening into the pipeline

    The Courtyard is scheduled to finish December 25, 2026, the Sendero dual-brand in early 2027, the Residence Inn in November 2027 and the Fairfield and Staybridge on July 3, 2028 [15][18][19][20]. The subject's ramp years coincide with all four. The key counts of three of them are unknown, so the supply shock cannot yet be sized.

  2. One employer drives weekday demand

    Starlink and SpaceX are the reason two hotels opened in 2025 [24]. A pause in expansion, a shift of work to another campus or a slowdown in data center construction would hit the weekday base that the model counts on.

  3. Rate erosion from new product

    New hotels open with introductory rates and loyalty promotions. Midscale hotels lost 5.7% of gross operating profit in 2025 while their property taxes rose 12.3% [48]. A rate war in a small market reaches coverage faster than an occupancy dip.

  4. Site entitlement under a new code

    The 2026 development code and zoning map replaced the old code [4]. If the pad needs a zoning change and the commission recommends denial, council approval needs a supermajority. Entitlement delay pushes the opening further into the pipeline.

  5. Flood, drainage and wildfire

    The 2018 to 2019 drainage moratorium and the 2011 county fire [64][65] point to insurance cost and availability risk that the model carries only as an allowance.

  6. Ownership eligibility

    The March 1, 2026 citizenship rule makes any owner who is not a U.S. citizen or national an eligibility failure [46]. Hotel ownership groups should be checked before the credit work starts.

  7. Tax growth

    The county's rate rose 6.22% for 2026-27 [11], and the city's adopted rate is unconfirmed. A new hotel is assessed near cost, so tax is a large, fixed line.

Bastrop Hotel Questions

Questions lenders ask about this Bastrop hotel case.

Why does a new hotel in Bastrop need more SBA 504 equity than an established business would?

Two separate tests stack. The borrower is a new business, and a hotel is a special purpose property, so the 504 contribution steps from the 10% standard to 20%. The sponsor in this case asked for the 15% tier, which covers only one of the two conditions. The SOP sets the tiers [60].

Is the Bastrop hotel market overbuilt?

Not on demand, which is growing with the city and the Starlink campus, but the timing is crowded. Everhome and Home2 added 228 extended-stay keys in 2025, and Courtyard, a Fairfield and TownePlace dual-brand, a Residence Inn and a Fairfield and Staybridge are registered or under construction for delivery from December 2026 to July 2028 [23][15][18][19][20]. A hotel financed now opens into that wave, which is why the model ramps occupancy slowly.

Why is the subject a transient hotel and not another extended-stay?

The 2025 openings already serve long-stay contractor demand. SBA's lodging rule asks for more than 50% of the prior year's revenue from transients who stay 30 days or less at a time; a start-up shows it in projections [60], and Bastrop's hotel tax exempts guests who stay 30 consecutive days or more [8]. A Bastrop hotel that drifted toward monthly contractor stays would weaken both its eligibility and its tax filings.

What ADR and occupancy did the study use, and where do they come from?

No published ADR or occupancy exists for Bastrop, and the aggregator prices in the research carry no stay date. Both are labelled assumptions. Occupancy opens at 60% in Year 1, under the 62.3% U.S. figure for 2025 [49], because the pipeline opens around the hotel; it reaches 66% in Year 2, 69% in Year 3 and 70% from Year 4, a point or two below the 71.1% that Apple Hospitality's rooms-focused hotels ran in early 2025 [56]. The rate starts at $138 and stays under the 2025 U.S. ADR of $160.54 in every year [49]. The first lender condition is an STR trend report for the named competitive set.

Could the sponsor use a 7(a) loan instead of a 504?

A 7(a) start-up loan needs only 10% equity, but its rate is variable and the coverage problem is the same: the hotel's Year 2 income supports about the same amount of debt under either program. The 504 keeps a fixed debenture rate near 7% for 25 years [59], which matters for a hotel whose income takes three years to stabilize.

Does SBA require a feasibility study for this hotel?

The rule is 13 CFR 120.160(b): SBA may require professional appraisals, a survey, or a feasibility study [63]. Lenders often ask for one on a ground-up hotel. Here the study's job is narrower and more useful: it sets how much debt the market supports while the pipeline opens.
