Cabin Resort Feasibility Study Outside Townsend, Tennessee: 11 A-Frames on an SBA 504 Loan

Market analysis by Mark Otava · Published October 10, 2026 · Data as of October 2026

Representative worked example. The site, sponsor and project are hypothetical. The market evidence is real, public and dated October 2026. This page shows how the market analysis is built and how it sets the loan. It is not a client engagement.

Townsend sells itself as the quieter side of the Great Smoky Mountains: the road into Cades Cove, far from the attraction strip in Pigeon Forge. For a cabin resort that positioning cuts both ways. Guests pay for the calm, but they pay less per night than they do twenty miles east, and the two data vendors that track Townsend's rental market disagree on almost everything except the rate. This case tests whether 11 new two-bedroom A-frames with private hot tubs, on about 9 acres in unincorporated Blount County a few miles outside Townsend, can carry SBA 504 debt.

Market conclusionThe market supports the product at upper-middle performance. A 75-cabin resort opening in 2027 and a 14-point disagreement between data vendors mean the loan should be sized to median-plus RevPAR, not to the top quartile.

The market conclusion

Demand in Blount County is growing in dollars even while Smokies park visits fall, because spending is shifting toward overnight and fly-in visitors who need beds. That is the right tailwind for cabins. But Townsend's rental market tops out around 53% occupancy on the most generous data source, it is sharply seasonal, and 75 new resort cabins break ground five minutes from the Cades Cove entrance this autumn. At a $117 RevPAR, between the two vendors' market averages, the resort earns $211,956 of net operating income. That covers a standard 20%-equity 504 structure only 0.84 times. Sized to 1.25x, the market supports about $1.97 million of debt, and the sponsor carries about 46% of the cost.

The park figures need care. Recreation visits fell 8.3% in 2024 and 5.4% in 2025, to the lowest total since 2018. Part of the 2024 drop came from a new National Park Service counting model; the park's spokesperson said the decline "was not due to a decrease in actual visitation" [27]. The 43-day federal shutdown in late 2025 did not close the park, because state, local and nonprofit funding kept it open. Early 2026 shows recovery. Meanwhile Blount County visitor spending rose for a third straight year and air traffic set records. The demand base is stable to soft in visits and rising in spend, which favors overnight lodging over day-trip attractions. The underwriting therefore assumes no growth in park visits.

Two-bedroom units are the largest single class in Townsend, at 34.2% of listings, so the subject competes in the most crowded size and also the most liquid one [12]. Seasonality is pronounced: AirROI's peak month is October at 60.7% occupancy and a $298 ADR, and its trough is January and February at 25.2% and $234 [12].

The vendors disagree on supply direction (down 24.8% against up 46.3%), on occupancy (53% against 38.5%) and on revenue per listing. They nearly agree on rate. RevPAR is the steadier comparison, and it brackets the market at $102 to $129. The study underwrites $117, between the two averages and below AirROI's top quartile of $133. A new A-frame with a private hot tub can plausibly earn top-quartile RevPAR. That is an operating judgment, not market data, so the loan is not sized on it.

Supply within 15 miles is fragmented: individually owned cabins and small rental companies, with hot tubs as table stakes rather than a differentiator. The one resort-scale project is the most important supply variable in this case. It began as a 130-cabin camping resort that drew a petition of more than 1,000 signatures and a negative planning recommendation on a rezoning, then returned under a new name and was approved as a hotel on a 4-1 Townsend Planning Commission vote in January 2024 [21]. That history shows two things at once. Organized resistance to resort density is a barrier to entry that protects an 11-cabin project, and it is an entitlement risk for that same project.

The cost per cabin, $332,135, is the gap. A standard 504 structure needs top-quartile performance just to clear 1.0x. Sized to the study case, the loan carries a 1.25x cushion, and the sponsor's equity rises to $1,683,480, about 46% of cost. At AirROI's median RevPAR the sized loan still covers only 0.65x, which is why the first-year reserve and the rate shop below matter.

No cabin-resort or campground sale with a disclosed price per key or cap rate was found in Blount, Sevier or Cocke counties for 2023 to 2026. The nearest priced lodging trade is a 104-room Gatlinburg motor lodge at about $135,096 per key in early 2022 [24], a different asset at well under half the subject's cost per key. Collateral will rest on the income approach and an appraiser-sourced cap rate, so the lender will look to equity and coverage rather than loan to value.

Conditions a lender should set before commitment

  1. A paid custom comparable set from AirDNA or Key Data: two-bedroom cabins with hot tubs within 10 miles, by month. That will settle which vendor's occupancy applies to this product.
  2. A lease-up stress test against the 75 resort cabins opening in summer 2027.
  3. Written zoning verification from Blount County Planning for 11 cabins on the parcel under the vacation rental cabin standards (Zoning Resolution Section 7.11) and any density cap in force [25].
  4. A soil and site evaluation for about 3,300 gallons per day. Tennessee rules size systems at 150 gallons per bedroom per day, treat anything above 750 gallons per day as a large system needing site-specific review, and cap each field at 3,000 gallons per day, so two fields are expected [26].
  5. An appraisal with a sales comparison and cap-rate study, and the CDC's determination of SBA rural status for the debenture fee waiver.
  6. Executed cabin bids at or below $150 per sq ft, and a funded reserve sized to the January and February trough.

Questions sponsors ask about this market

What occupancy do Townsend cabins achieve?

It depends on the vendor. AirDNA reports 53% and AirROI 38.5% for overlapping periods. October peaks near 61% and January to February falls to about 25%. A lender will underwrite between the two, not at the higher figure.

Is Townsend cheaper than Pigeon Forge?

On rate, yes. Pigeon Forge runs a $344 ADR against $243 to $268 in Townsend. Guests trade rate for quiet and for direct access to Cades Cove.

What new supply is coming?

A 75-cabin resort broke ground in Townsend on September 15, 2026 and is expected to open in summer 2027. It is the largest single addition in the submarket.

Can an SBA 504 loan finance a cabin resort?

Yes. The project must pass SBA's transient test, with more than half of revenue from stays of 30 days or less, and lodging is treated as special-purpose property, which raises the minimum equity for a new business.
