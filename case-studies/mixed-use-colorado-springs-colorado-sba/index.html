Mixed-Use Feasibility Study Case: Colorado Springs, Colorado, SBA 504

Market analysis by Mark Otava · Published November 4, 2026 · Data as of November 2026

Representative worked example. The site, sponsor and project are hypothetical. The market evidence comes from the public sources listed below, with their dates. This page shows how the market analysis is built and how it sets the loan. It is not a client engagement.

Colorado Springs apartments were 94.4% occupied in the first half of 2026 [10], while average office rent fell 4.3% [9]. The owner's storefront with six apartments above it meets the 504 occupancy share on the modeled floor area, so the question is price against income: at 10.0% equity Year 2 coverage is 0.91x, and the floor is reached at 28.7%.

Study at a Glance

An owner's storefront with six apartments above it, tested against the SBA 504 rules in Colorado Springs.

Owners who search for a mixed-use feasibility study company or a mixed-use feasibility study consultant usually want one answer: how much debt the building can carry, and what the lender will ask for before it says yes. Feasibility Study Company prepares mixed-use feasibility studies for owners, developers and their lenders. Its parts are a dated market read, a count of the competing supply, a priced project and a coverage test of how much debt the income supports. This page applies them to one hypothetical project: a business that buys the two-story building it already trades from, keeps the ground floor for itself and rents the six apartments above, financed with an SBA 504 loan in Colorado Springs, Colorado. We can run these steps on your own building.

The subject is generic: an existing building of about 5,400 square feet at street level for the owner's business and 4,200 square feet above it for 6 apartments, on a lot in the central commercial core of Colorado Springs, El Paso County, along the Tejon Street corridor. The corridor is a case assumption and no parcel, owner or business is described. The market figures are dated public records, and an input table near the end shows where each model figure starts, a source or the word assumption. The project is filed under our mixed-use practice, the city is in Colorado, and the program rules are summarised on our SBA 7(a) and 504 page.

Market Conclusion

The apartments have a market. The storefront rent and the purchase price decide the loan.

The residential answer is yes. Apartment occupancy in Colorado Springs stood at 94.4% in the first half of 2026, up 150 basis points from the end of 2025, and the market absorbed 1,319 units in the half year against 172 in the half before [10]. The average rent of $1,457 a unit was up 2.3% from year-end but 2.6% lower than a year earlier [10]. Demand is strong, rents are soft, and a six-unit owner is a price taker in both.

The commercial answer is cautious. Office space is the nearest published non-residential rent, and it fell: the average direct asking rent was $17.67 per square foot on a triple-net basis, down 4.3%, while total vacancy stood at 10.4% and the half year's net absorption was negative 8,000 square feet [9]. No source in the research prices ground-floor retail or service space in the city. The model therefore charges the owner's own business a rent anchored on the office figure and labels it a proxy.

The building answer follows from the first two. More than half of the floor area belongs to the owner's business, which is the share the occupancy tests look at. The apartments pay for themselves and a little more, the storefront pays an internal rent that a lender will test against the business's own earnings, and the sum is thin against any price that a seller of an occupied building will accept. The financing answer is below.

Under the sponsor's 10.0% equity proposal the project costs $1,703,695 and Year 2 coverage is 0.91x against a floor of 1.15x. Restructured, the sponsor brings $488,503, 28.7% of project cost, the bank lends $851,050 and the debenture is $362,547. That structure reaches 1.15x in Year 2 and 1.15x at stabilization, so it meets the test with almost no room. The restructured deal differs from the one proposed, and the sensitivity table shows how thin the first two years are. Ask for a study that tests your own price against your own rent roll.

Demand

A steady jobs base, firm apartment demand and a state whose migration has turned flat.

A mixed-use building sells two kinds of space to two kinds of tenant. The upstairs tenant needs a job and a lease, and the downstairs tenant needs customers. The tables set out the public record for both, starting with the metro economy and ending with the state.

Jobs and wages

The metro added jobs at a modest pace, 0.8% over twelve months to September 2026, with the unemployment rate at 4.5% [1]. County payroll data point the other way for the first quarter: covered employment in El Paso County was 301,647 in March 2026, which is 1.2% fewer jobs than a year before, while the average weekly wage rose 5.0% to $1,437 [3]. The two readings come from different surveys, and the study reads them together: a labor market that is holding its jobs while wages move up. For a landlord the wage growth matters more than the job count, because it is what a tenant pays rent from. The mean hourly wage across all occupations was $34.03 in May 2025 [2], which at full-time hours is the income base for the upstairs tenant.

The metro's economic output was $53,092.641 million in 2023, the last year the research could read, in a series its relay labels discontinued [14]. The study uses it as scale and not as a trend.

Housing and renters

Colorado Springs is a market in which buying is expensive and renting is the alternative. The median listing price in the metro was $484,950 in September 2026, with 4,266 active listings, according to a relay of Realtor.com data [5][6]. A price of that size can keep some households renting longer, which supports the apartment half of the building. Listings sat for a median of 67 days before they sold or were withdrawn [7]. County permitting added supply: 5,707 housing structures were authorized in El Paso County in 2025 [4]. Both housing series are secondary rows, and the study labels them as relays of the original publishers.

The apartment market has its own record. Completions in the first half of 2026 were 369 units, against 840 in the second half of 2025, and net absorption of 1,319 units was well above the completions, which is why occupancy rose to 94.4% [10]. The half year's sales volume for apartment buildings was $82.5 million, up from $22.9 million in the half before [10]. Buyers returned to the asset class. For a small owner that is a double-edged signal: it supports the value of the building and it raises the price of the next one.

Visitors and the storefront

The Pikes Peak region drew 25.6 million visitors in 2025, up 0.3%, who spent $3.1 billion, up 0.4%, and 11.2 million of them stayed overnight [12]. A storefront in the central core draws some of that spending, and the study does not count on it: the release gives no retail or lodging figure, and spending that grows by less than half a percent is not a growth story. The sponsor's own demand case decides whether the ground floor can pay the rent the model charges it.

The state behind the city

Colorado's population reached 6,012,561 on July 1, 2025, a rise of 0.4%, according to the state demographer's summary of Census Bureau estimates, a secondary relay of the federal release [15]. Net domestic migration was negative 12,100, more people leaving the state than arriving, and net international migration of 15,356 more than offset it, leaving a total net gain of 3,256 [15]. A state whose growth depends on arrivals from abroad while its own residents move out is a state in which household formation continues at a slower pace. The apartment demand that Colorado Springs shows in its absorption figures rests on that base, and a landlord should not extrapolate it.

Competitor Census

No research document names a competing mixed-use building, so the census becomes a lender condition and the page counts the supply instead.

A competitor census for a mixed-use building would name the nearby buildings that offer a storefront below and apartments above, every one with a website of its own or a county assessor record. The research behind this page names none. Its Colorado Springs material is metro and county statistics, broker quarterly reports and press releases, and not one of them lists a competing building, a landlord or a listing. This study will not invent a competitor and will not describe a building that no document names, so the census becomes a lender condition: a list of named buildings and landlords, each matched to its own website or assessor record, delivered before commitment.

What the supply table says

Landlords of buildings with employees are a small industry here: the county holds 137 establishments and 550 paid employees, and the metro area adds only a handful beyond the county line [29]. The county and metro rates per 10,000 residents are almost the same, so the metro adds little landlord activity beyond the county. A new owner-occupant is not entering a crowded landlord field, but the count is a ceiling on what the register can tell a lender. Small owners who run a building through a personal entity with no payroll do not appear, and neither does any building whose owner reports under the code of its main business. That is the case for the named census that the lender conditions ask for.

Commercial competition

Office space is the weak segment. Vacancy was 10.4% overall and 14.7% downtown, rents fell 4.3%, and absorption was negative 8,000 square feet after negative 108,000 in the half before [9]. A business that can use office-type space has choices, and a landlord asking above the office average has to explain why. Industrial space is tighter, with absorption of 357,000 square feet in the half year, but 290,000 square feet under construction and more than 550,000 delivered mean new competition for any business that can work from a flex building [8]. The small-bay segment is looser, with 391,462 square feet under construction, down from peaks above 570,000 [11]. None of these is a storefront, which is why the model treats the office figure as a proxy rent.

Residential competition

New apartments arrive in lumps. The market delivered 840 units in the second half of 2025 and 369 in the first half of 2026 [10]. A six-unit owner competes with those lease-ups on price and not on amenities, and the CBRE average rent fell 2.6% over the year as they leased [10]. The model's rent for the apartments is therefore held flat in every year and set below the market average.

New supply and the rule set

The research found no list of mixed-use projects under construction in Colorado Springs and no local permit report by building type, only the county's total of 5,707 housing structures authorized in 2025 [4]. State law is also moving. A 2026 act on housing developments on qualifying properties caps a qualifying site at 5 acres and sets a deadline of December 31, 2027 for covered jurisdictions to allow qualifying residential development [24]. Whether it reaches Colorado Springs, and which parcels it covers, are legal questions for the site section and the lender conditions.

Site

Zoning, flood exposure, utilities and traffic are not in the research. The state laws and hazard record set the questions.

The subject site is generic, so this section does not describe a parcel. It lists what the public record says about the setting and sends the rest to the lender conditions. The research found no zoning ordinance for Colorado Springs, no flood zone determination, no utility provider or tap fee schedule and no traffic count for any corridor, and it does not guess at them.

State housing and parking law

Colorado passed a run of housing laws in 2024 and 2026 that bear on a building with apartments. A 2024 act on minimum parking requirements takes effect for covered local governments on June 30, 2025, and it was signed on May 10, 2024 [22]. A 2024 act on housing in transit-oriented communities sets a goal of 40 dwelling units per acre for zoned density in transit-related areas [23]. The research does not say whether Colorado Springs is a covered government or whether the subject lies in a covered area. If it does, the parking and density rules loosen what an owner may add or convert on the lot, and if it does not, the city's own code governs. A zoning verification letter settles it.

Property tax

For tax year 2026 the state sets a 25% assessment rate for commercial property and most other nonresidential property, and its 2024 property tax act limits annual growth in local government property tax revenue to 5.25% [25]. The research holds neither the El Paso County mill levy nor the assessment rate for residential property, so the tax on a building with both uses cannot be computed. The model carries 1.00% of cost as a planning level and the lender condition asks for the county assessor's record and the actual levy.

Flood, drainage and hail

Federal records show the city's drainage work. In September 2026 FEMA announced more than $2.6 million of Public Assistance for water drainage and embankment repairs in Colorado Springs [13]. The same release carried more than $8 million in hazard mitigation money for floodplain restoration on Cottonwood Creek and more than $2.9 million for Monument Creek stabilization [13]. These are single projects and the study does not count them; they show that creek flooding is a live issue in the city, and a flood zone determination for the subject parcel is a lender condition. Statewide, FEMA announced more than $90 million for Colorado communities in the same release [13].

Hail is the insurance issue. A report by the state's Division of Insurance, relayed by a newspaper, attributes 26% to 54% of a homeowner's premium to hail and 0.9% to 24.6% to wildfire, from 20 carriers that hold 80% of the market [26]. Those are homeowner premiums, not commercial rates, and the newspaper is a secondary source. They signal that insurance cost in Colorado is driven by weather; the model's insurance line is an allowance, and a broker quote is a lender condition.

What a site visit must settle

Traffic counts, parking supply, signage rules, the certificate of occupancy for each use and the fire separation between them are all outside the research, and all are on the lender conditions list. A storefront lives on exposure, and the study will not model the ground floor's demand from a traffic count it does not have.

Revenue

Two income streams: an internal rent from the owner's business and rent from six apartments.

The model tests the building and not the business. The owner's company pays a rent to the entity that owns the building, and the apartments pay rent to the same entity. The business's own sales and profit are outside the model and are tested in the lender conditions as part of a global cash flow, in which the program asks for coverage of 1:1 across the borrower's related entities.

The internal rent starts at $20 per square foot a year on 5,400 square feet and steps up 2.0% a year. It is a modified gross figure, anchored on CBRE's office rent of $17.67 per square foot on a triple-net basis [9], and it is a proxy. The apartment line is 6 units at $1,250 a month, held flat, with occupancy of 94.0% at stabilization, just under the market's 94.4% [10], and a lower figure in the first year while the renovation is done. At stabilization the building earns $196,963 a year.

Operating Costs

Taxes, insurance, repairs and a market charge for management, with growth of three percent a year.

Operating costs are allowances, because the research holds no expense data for small mixed-use buildings or for any building in Colorado Springs. The line items follow what a landlord of a building with a storefront and apartments pays: property tax on cost at 1.00%, insurance at $1.60 per square foot, owner-paid utilities, repairs at $1.75 per square foot, a management and bookkeeping charge of 5.0% of apartment rent even if the owner does the work, legal, advertising and turnover, and a replacement reserve. Costs grow at 3.0% a year while apartment rent is held flat. In the commercial lease the tenant is the owner's own business, so the model treats the rent as modified gross and charges the building with the taxes and insurance.

Operating cost totals $70,796 in Year 1 and $74,076 in Year 3 against revenue of $189,000 and $196,963, leaving net operating income of $118,204, $122,257 and $122,887 in the first three years, a margin of 62.4% in Year 3.

Sources and Uses

The price is most of the project. The renovation and soft costs are allowances.

The project buys an existing building, refreshes six apartments, fits out the ground floor for the owner's business and pays the costs of closing a federal-program loan. The purchase price is $1,250,000, a round planning figure. Loan Structure

A bank first lien, a CDC debenture and a borrower contribution sized by the 504 rules for an existing business and a general-purpose building.

Under the 504 program a bank takes the first lien and a certified development company funds the second through an SBA-guaranteed debenture. The program's standard split is Third Party Lender 50%, debenture 40%, borrower 10%; debenture 35% at a 15% contribution and 30% at 20% [31]. The sponsor in this case is an established business and the building is a general-purpose mixed-use structure, so the equity tier is 10% [31]. The tiers rise to 15% for a new business or a special purpose property and 20% for both [31], and a lender should confirm that the owner's business is more than two years old and that no feature of the building makes it special purpose.

The sponsor proposes 10.0% equity. That meets the tier and the debenture limit of $5 million [31], and the proposed debenture of $681,478 is far under the limit. What it fails is coverage: Year 2 net operating income covers the $133,340 of annual debt service only 0.91x, and the program's floor is 1.15x [31]. The engine raises the borrower's contribution until Year 2 coverage reaches the floor. At 28.7% equity the bank lends $851,050, the debenture is $362,547 and annual debt service is $106,219.

Occupancy

The test that decides whether a building fits the program at all is not in the engine. For an existing building the operating borrower must occupy at least 51% of it, and for a new building 60% [31]. The case gives the owner's business the whole ground floor, which is more than half of the building by floor area, and rents the upper floor to third parties. A small shift in the plan moves the share: an extra apartment carved from the ground floor, a ground-floor storage room leased out, or a lease that ends in Year 3 can each pull the occupied share toward the line. The appraisal should state the occupied share, and the application's square footage should match it. The study treats the share as a condition, with the floor plan measured on one basis for the appraisal, the application and the lender's file.

How the program reads rent from apartments in the same building is a question the lender and the certified development company answer under the current edition, SOP 50 10 8.1, effective October 1, 2026 [31]. The mixed-use hub sets out how the earlier edition framed residential space, and a study has to say which reading its lender applies. This page does not decide that question; it makes it a written lender condition.

Rates, term and fees

The 504 debenture is available at maturities of 10, 20 or 25 years [31], and the bank loan carries 7 years for a 10-year debenture, 10 years for a 20- or 25-year debenture as the minimum term [31]. The model takes the longest debenture and amortizes the bank loan over the same term. The bank rate is 7.50%, a planning figure half a point over Prime [33], and the debenture rate is 7.00%, an assumption, because the research holds no debenture pricing. The fiscal year 2027 upfront fee is 0.50% of the debenture and the annual fee is 0.203% [32]. The upfront fee is added to project cost in the model.

Risks

What could break the Colorado Springs projection.

  1. The price carries the deal

    The purchase price is a round planning figure with no sale behind it. The building's income of $122,257 in Year 2 supports about $1,213,598 of debt, and every extra dollar of price is a dollar of equity. Apartment-building sales volume rose from $22.9 million to $82.5 million between the last two half years [10], and a seller who has read that headline may price the building as if the market had turned for good.

  2. The storefront rent is a proxy

    No source in the research prices ground-floor space in the city. The nearest figure is the office rent of $17.67 per square foot, down 4.3% [9]. If the business can only afford a lower rent, or a lender reads the rent as above market, the largest income line moves and coverage with it.

  3. Rents are soft while supply arrives

    Average apartment rent fell 2.6% over the year [10], and 369 units were completed in the first half of 2026 after 840 in the half before [10]. The model holds rent flat and below the average, but a six-unit owner has no scale to absorb a concession war.

  4. Weather drives the insurance bill

    The state's insurance report attributes between 26% and 54% of a homeowner's premium to hail [26], and the model's insurance line is an allowance. A hail deductible written as a percentage of building value would change the owner's exposure more than the premium does.

  5. Property tax is unresolved

    The assessment rate for most nonresidential property is 25% for tax year 2026 [25], but the levy and the residential rate are not in the record. The model's planning rate of 1.00% of cost could be wrong in either direction.

  6. The occupancy share drifts

    The owner's business holds the ground floor, and the occupancy test is a share of floor area. A lease that frees ground-floor space, a unit added on the ground floor or a change to the way floor area is measured can move the share. The appraisal and the application must measure it on one basis.

  7. The lender's reading of apartments in the building

    The program edition governs how rent from apartments is treated, and the hub notes that the earlier edition used different language. A lender's reading is a condition of the commitment and cannot be assumed from this page.

  8. Flood and drainage

    The city's creeks and drainage systems drew federal money in 2026, including more than $2.6 million of Public Assistance for drainage and embankment repairs [13]. A site near a creek may need flood insurance and a different appraisal. The research holds no flood zone for the subject.

  9. The business has to carry its own weight

    The internal rent comes from the owner's business. A loss of customers is a loss of rent, and the apartments alone do not cover the debt. County covered employment was down 1.2% over the year to March 2026 even as wages rose 5.0% [3], and the visitor economy grew only 0.4% in spending [12].

  10. Rates and the timing of the debenture

    The bank rate is a planning figure and the debenture rate is an assumption. The debenture is priced when the CDC sells it, which can differ from the rate in the commitment letter. The sensitivity table shows the effect of income moving; it does not show the effect of a rate that comes in higher, and a lender will run that case.

Colorado Springs Questions

What lenders and owners ask about the Colorado Springs mixed-use case.

Does a mixed-use building qualify for an SBA 504 loan?

It can, when the operating business occupies enough of it. For an existing building the borrower must occupy at least 51%, and for a new building 60% [31]. The case gives the owner's business the whole ground floor, which is more than half of the building, and rents the six apartments upstairs. The lender and the certified development company must still confirm in writing how the current edition treats the apartments, so the page makes that a condition and not an assumption.

Is the Colorado Springs apartment market strong enough for the upstairs units?

Occupancy was 94.4% in the first half of 2026 and the market absorbed 1,319 units in the half year, but average rent per unit fell 2.6% over twelve months [10]. Demand is firm and pricing is soft. The model holds rent flat and below the market average, and it fills the six apartments to just under the market occupancy.

What rent does the case charge the owner's business, and why a rent at all?

The study tests the building on the income it earns, and the business is the largest tenant. The rent is a modified gross figure set between CBRE's average office asking rent of $17.67 per square foot on a triple-net basis and its Class A rent of $21.14 [9]. No source in the research prices ground-floor space, so the figure is a labelled proxy. The business's own earnings are tested separately, in a global cash flow, and that test is a lender condition.

Why does the competitor section name no competitors?

Because no dated public document behind this page names one, and a census that named a building without a source would be invented. The census therefore becomes a lender condition, a roster naming each competing building with its own website or assessor record, and the section carries a supply table instead. The table counts landlord establishments in the Census Bureau's County Business Patterns, a proxy for the class, for El Paso County and for the metro area [29].

Why is the equity this building needs above the program minimum?

Both tests apply here, and coverage is the one that binds. An established business buying a general-purpose building contributes at least 10% of project cost [31], and projected coverage must also reach 1.15x [31]. The building's income cannot carry a loan sized at the minimum, so the engine adds equity until Year 2 coverage reaches the floor. The coverage table sets the two structures against each other.

Is a feasibility study mandatory for a loan like this one?

The regulation reads 13 CFR 120.160(b): SBA may require professional appraisals, a survey, or a feasibility study [34], and the research note on that wording explains why that is not a blanket rule. On a mixed-use building with owner-occupancy questions a lender may ask anyway, and a study that counts the supply, separates the two income streams and tests coverage has the answers ready.

Which site and law questions could end the project early?

The research found no zoning status for either use, no flood zone and no property tax levy. Colorado's 2024 parking law and transit-area housing law may change what an owner can do on the lot [22][23], and the federal agency recorded money for city drainage and creek work in 2026 [13]. A zoning letter, a flood determination and the assessor's record settle each in turn.
