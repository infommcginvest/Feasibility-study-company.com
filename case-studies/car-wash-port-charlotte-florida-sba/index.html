Car Wash Feasibility Study Case: A New US 41 Express Tunnel in Port Charlotte, Florida, on an SBA 7(a) Loan

Market analysis by Mark Otava · Published October 29, 2026 · Data as of October 2026

Representative worked example. The site, sponsor and project are hypothetical. The market evidence comes from the public sources listed below, with their dates. This page shows how the market analysis is built and how it sets the loan. It is not a client engagement.

Charlotte County grew 16.3% between April 2020 and July 2025 [1], yet at least five express tunnels already sell unlimited plans in Port Charlotte and another is announced for US 41 [12][13][14][17][18]. At the sponsor's 10.0% equity the 7(a) request runs over the program limit and covers Year 2 debt service only 0.77x. The corridor carries $3,510,956 of debt on a $5,746,246 tunnel, so the model reaches the coverage floor only at 38.9% equity.

Study at a Glance

A new express tunnel on US 41, tested against the SBA 7(a) rules.

Feasibility Study Company prepares studies like this one for owners, developers and their lenders: a dated market read, a competitor census, a priced build and a coverage test that says how much debt a car wash can carry. This page runs that work for one hypothetical project, a new express tunnel in Port Charlotte, Florida, financed with an SBA 7(a) loan. Ask us to run the same work on your site.

The subject is generic: a single tunnel with free vacuums and unlimited plans on about 1.5 acres of commercial frontage along US 41, the Tamiami Trail. Every market figure is public and dated. Every model figure below is computed by the case engine from the inputs listed near the end, each of which carries either a source or the word assumption. The study belongs to our express car wash practice, the town sits in Florida, and the program rules are set out on our SBA 7(a) and 504 page.

Market Conclusion

A growing county, a corridor already sold on memberships.

Port Charlotte has the households a tunnel wash needs and already has the tunnels to serve them. That is the finding in one sentence, and it is why this study sizes the loan to a modest member base rather than to the county's growth curve. Charlotte County reached 217,212 residents on July 1, 2025, up 16.3% from 186,823 at the April 2020 census base [1]. The county's own growth consultant ran ahead of the Census Bureau, counting 224,147 residents in February 2025, 5.5% more than ten months earlier, and projecting 256,891 within five years [4].

Supply has kept up. Tommy's Express built a tunnel on Murdock Circle in 2022 [11]. ModWash runs one site on Veterans Boulevard and opened a second on Beatrix Boulevard, which the trade press called the brand's newest location in the market [13][14][15]. Coastal Express lists two Port Charlotte sites on its own website, one of them at Charlotte Square [17]. Bubble Down already posts a price menu for a site on US 41 that its page now marks as coming in 2027, although the page title still says 2026 [18]. A new tunnel on the same road would be the sixth or seventh membership wash a Port Charlotte driver can choose from.

So the question a lender should ask is not whether people will buy unlimited plans here. They already do, from at least three brands that post their menus. The question is how many members a late entrant can win from that menu, and how much debt that member base will carry. This study answers it with a ramp that stabilizes at 2,700 average active members by Year 3, a blended plan price of $30 a month, and single washes at $16. On those inputs the tunnel earns $1,388,000 of revenue and $529,698 of net operating income in its third year.

That income does not support the sponsor's request. At 10.0% equity on a $5,794,165 project, the 7(a) loan would be $5,214,748, more than the program allows for one borrower, and Year 2 income would cover its payments 0.77x. The request as proposed fails. The structure that clears the coverage standard in the model carries $3,510,956 of debt and asks the sponsor for 38.9% of cost. That is a finding about this corridor, not about the format: on a less crowded road the same tunnel would carry more debt.

Four gaps follow the finding into the lender conditions: no state traffic count for the stretch of US 41 in question, no confirmation that the commercial general district allows a car wash by right, no flood zone for the pad, and no published prices from ModWash, whose two sites are the newest competition.

Demand

Retirees, new rooftops and a county that keeps adding them.

Port Charlotte is a census-designated place, not a city, so its numbers come from the Census Bureau's place tables and the county's. The 2020 census counted 60,625 residents in the place, which the Bureau does not estimate between censuses [2]. The American Community Survey for 2020 to 2024 puts it at 27,888 households with a median household income of $63,844 in 2024 dollars [2]. The county's 90,542 households earn a median of $69,952 [1]. Both incomes sit below the national median of $80,734 [3].

An older population is a mixed signal for a membership wash. Retired households drive less in the commute peak, and fixed incomes are sensitive to a monthly charge. They also wash when it suits them, on weekdays, which spreads tunnel use across the day rather than stacking it into weekend afternoons. The register table below counts the county's residents aged 65 and over, which shows the scale of that group. No source measured wash frequency by age in this market, so the model draws no benefit from the age profile and treats it as neutral.

Growth is the stronger demand signal, and the county documents where it is landing. Its April 2025 report put the population at 224,147 in February 2025, up 5.5% from 212,424 in April 2024 and 11% higher than in June 2023 [4]. The same report found that the West Port area and Babcock Ranch accounted for 82% of all residential construction since 2024, and expected Mid-County to add 4,400 units [4]. The county budgeted $21,003,317 for its Murdock Village redevelopment fund in FY2025-26, against $6,043,327 for Charlotte Harbor and $2,181,274 for Parkside [6]. New homes on the west side of Port Charlotte put new cars on US 41.

The Census Bureau's housing counts agree on direction. The county issued 3,015 building permits in 2025 and held 131,960 housing units on July 1, 2025 [1]. Employment is smaller than the population would suggest, because so many residents are retired: County Business Patterns counted 44,163 employees at 4,601 employer establishments in 2023 [1]. Major employers and truck volumes were not found, so the study does not lean on a commuter or fleet market.

Visitors add a seasonal layer. Spring-training venues in Port Charlotte, North Port and Sarasota drew crowds in February and March of the 2026 season [5], though the source gives no wash-relevant measure of them. The county budgeted $12,495,000 for its Tourist Development Trust Fund in FY2025-26 and $11,135,000 for its fourth and fifth cent tourist tax [6]. Winter residents and visitors likely lift single-wash sales in the first months of the year, but monthly wash volume was not found, so the model holds volume level across the year.

The menus define a narrow band. The three operators that post dollar amounts each run a tiered unlimited ladder, and the newest entrant, Bubble Down, posts a menu that lands inside the band the other two set [12][16][18]. That suggests it expects to compete on site and service rather than price. ModWash is the gap. It is the only brand with two sites in the market and it posts no dollar amounts on either location page; the one price signal in the record is the discounted first month and locked monthly rate it offered its first Beatrix members [15]. Its full menu is the first competitor fact a lender should pull.

The appraiser's record for the Tommy's lot is the best cost and value evidence in the market. The parcel is zoned CG, the building was completed in 2022, and the 2026 roll carries a just value of $2,509,667, a county taxable value of $2,323,668 and land at $1,400,880 [11]. The land itself sold for $1,800,000 on February 7, 2020, before construction [11]. A rival paid that for the land at the Murdock Circle retail node before its 2022 build, which tells a lender that a well-placed pad is not cheap even when an appraiser later values the finished tunnel modestly.

Two more washes sit at the edges of the census. A Mister Car Wash on McCall Road in the Gulf Cove area, an outer-ring site opened in 2022 on a 1.47-acre parcel, was reported sold for $6 million to out-of-state investors in October 2026 [20]. Mister's own website refused automated access, so the site stays out of the table, but the sale price is the clearest local evidence of what a finished, leased tunnel is worth. A long-running tunnel on El Jobean Road appears in directories, but its operator's domain has lapsed and no assessor record was pulled; it is unconfirmed and carries no weight here. Bubble Down also marks a Punta Gorda site on Tamiami Trail as coming in 2027 [18], outside the Port Charlotte trade area but on the same highway.

Charlotte Square needs a note. The operator lists it on South Tamiami Trail [17], while some directories show other wash names at that address and a listing service shows a car wash there for sale. The operator's page is treated as governing, no figure from the sale listing is used, and the lender should confirm the site's status and format.

No Charlotte County permits or planning agendas for new car washes were found. Bubble Down's slipped date shows the pipeline is real but uncertain [18]. A lender should read the county's commercial permits for car wash projects on US 41, Veterans Boulevard and El Jobean Road before it accepts the member ramp.

Site, Zoning and Taxes

A hypothetical US 41 pad with flood, zoning and traffic open.

The subject is a hypothetical pad of about 1.5 acres with frontage on US 41, the Tamiami Trail, in unincorporated Port Charlotte. Land is carried at $1,100,000, a round assumption, not the price of any parcel: the reasoning in the inputs table starts from the appraiser's land value on the Tommy's lot and discounts it for a pad away from the Murdock Circle node. An appraisal and an executed contract set the value, and both are conditions.

Zoning is the first open item. The Tommy's lot is zoned CG, commercial general, and a tunnel was built on it in 2022 [11], which shows a car wash can be approved in that district. It does not show whether the use is permitted by right or needs a conditional approval, and the use table was not read for this study. Neither the approval path nor a typical timeline was found, so a written zoning verification for the actual pad is a condition.

Flood is the second. The Tommy's lot is split between Zone AE and Zone X on a FEMA panel effective December 15, 2022 [11]. A lot that straddles a special flood hazard area needs the tunnel pit, equipment room and pay stations set above base flood elevation or floodproofed, and the lender needs flood insurance on any building in Zone AE. The county's FY25 budget workshop still carried a Hurricane Ian return-to-roll item [10], a reminder that storm losses here are recent. Until the pad's zone is known, the soft-cost contingency and the insurance line are placeholders.

Traffic is the third. No Florida Department of Transportation station or annual average daily traffic count for the stretch of US 41 in question was found in the research. The only counts available came from a seller's marketing material for a listed property, which this study does not use. Traffic is therefore a lender condition: the state count, its station and year for the pad's frontage, and a check of turning access for cars entering the stacking lane from the highway.

Utilities are the fourth. The provider, capacity and connection charge for the pad were not confirmed, and the electric and gas providers were not identified. A tunnel discharges wash water, so sewer capacity and any pretreatment or reclaim requirement matter more than they would for a store.

Revenue has two lines. Plan revenue is average active members times the blended monthly price times twelve, and single-wash revenue is wash count times average ticket. Prices stay flat for five years; any price rise is upside the lender does not need.

How many members. The model reaches 2,700 average active members in Year 3 after lower counts in Years 1 and 2. No operator in Port Charlotte discloses its member count, so the ramp leans on national evidence, set out in the inputs table, and is held down for this market. At least five tunnels already sell plans to the same drivers, and two of them are recent or still to come: ModWash on Beatrix Boulevard, which the trade press calls the brand's newest location in the market, and Bubble Down, which is not yet open [15][18]. A member who joined Tommy's in 2022 has little reason to switch to a new tunnel nearby unless the new site is easier to reach on the way home.

What members pay. $30 a month is a blend across plans, not a posted price. It sits in the lower half of the ladders the three pricing operators post [12][16][18], and the model does not assume the subject can charge a premium. A sponsor who wants to price above the band needs a reason the corridor does not yet show, such as a longer tunnel or a better position on the evening drive home.

Single washes. The ticket of $16 falls between the entry and second tiers of the posted single-wash menus and well under their top tiers [12][16][18]. Single volume grows slowly while plan revenue builds. Winter residents and spring-training visitors probably buy more single washes early in the year [5], but seasonality was not measured, so the model does not count it.

What it adds up to. Revenue is $852,000 in Year 1, $1,192,000 in Year 2 and $1,388,000 in Year 3. The inputs table compares those figures with franchise cohort averages; the subject sits below them on purpose, because a tunnel arriving after five others should not be underwritten like an average site.

Three cost lines move with revenue and two do not. Labor and chemicals run at 29.0% of revenue, and utilities, repairs, card processing, software and marketing at 19.0%. The labor and chemicals ratio rests on the only audited multi-site profit and loss statement in the industry; the other-costs ratio is a judgement for a single owned site, explained in the inputs table. A 5.0% allowance for management stands in for the owner's time or a franchise fee.

Insurance is where Port Charlotte differs from an inland town. The model starts at $65,000 for property, wind, flood and liability cover and raises it 5.0% a year. That is a placeholder, not a quote. The nearest tunnel's lot is partly in Zone AE [11], and a wash building full of electrical equipment on a coastal corridor will be priced by wind and flood carriers, not by a general rate card. The bound quote is a condition, and a large miss on this line moves coverage more than a small miss on any ratio.

Property tax is set at $3,000,000 of taxable value times 1.70%. The sourced part of the rate is the county's: 6.0394 mills of countywide operating millage, adopted 3.89% above the rollback rate [6], within a county-levied series that ended at 11.6998 mills [8]. The school and district levies were not found, and they are not small, so the model carries an allowance for them. The Tommy's lot shows how Florida's assessment works on a newer tunnel: a 2026 county taxable value below its just value [11].

The result is a net operating income margin of 38.2% in Year 3. That is lower than a large chain earns before rent, which is deliberate. A single tunnel has no purchasing scale, and a crowded corridor tends to push spending into marketing and app promotions to hold members.

Two local transactions frame that total. Tommy's paid $1,800,000 for its Murdock Circle land in February 2020 [11], a land price above the subject's assumption because of the location. And investors paid a reported $6 million for the leased Mister site on McCall Road in October 2026 [20], which is the market's own price for a finished tunnel with an operator in place. The subject's cost sits just under that price. A lender should not expect the appraisal of a new, unproven tunnel to reach the price an investor paid for a seasoned, leased one, so the business itself, not the real estate, has to carry part of the value.

B+E's August 2025 net-lease report put the average asking cap rate on Florida car wash listings at 6.43% [29], a state-level read on how the market values a leased wash with an operator behind it. It is an asking rate, not a closed trade, and it applies to finished, tenanted sites rather than to a start-up that has yet to wash a car.

The pre-opening line deserves attention. ModWash opened Beatrix Boulevard with a discounted first month for its first members [15]. A new tunnel in this market will have to match an offer like that, which means months of discounted revenue before the ramp starts to show in the pro forma. The working capital line is meant to absorb that, but it is thin if the opening offer runs long.

The rules are those of SOP 50 10 8.1 with Technical Policy Updates, effective October 1, 2026 [31]. A start-up borrower injects at least 10%, and its projections have two years to reach 1.15x coverage, so the test falls on Year 2. Land, building and site work are more than half of the uses, so the loan runs 25 years. The rate is Prime, 7.00% on October 9, 2026 [33], plus the largest variable spread SBA allows for a loan of this size, 10.00% in all; a lender may price lower, and the rate is set at closing. SBA guarantees 75.0% of a loan this size [30].

The request fails before coverage is even tested. At 10.0% equity the loan would be $5,214,748, above the $5 million 7(a) maximum. A sponsor in that position usually looks to pair 7(a) with a 504 loan or a conventional piece, but no structure helps if the tunnel's income cannot cover the payments, and here it cannot: Year 2 coverage on the full request is 0.77x. The restructured loan of $3,510,956 is well inside the limit.

The guaranty fee for fiscal 2027 is charged on the guaranteed portion and added to project cost [32]. The fee waiver for rural borrowers stops at $700,000, so it cannot apply to a loan of this size whatever the county's rural status, which was not checked. SBA's list of special purpose properties names car wash businesses, and defines the class as a limited-market property with a unique physical design, special construction materials, or a layout that restricts its utility to the use for which it was built [31]. That shapes the appraisal: the lender will want an appraiser who has valued tunnels before. Owner eligibility is tested at the lender's eligibility review under the SOP in force at approval, and the program's citizenship and residency requirements were rewritten by a notice effective March 1, 2026 [35].

The study is the lender's tool here, not a program checkpoint: 13 CFR 120.160(b): SBA may require professional appraisals, a survey, or a feasibility study [34]. For a start-up wash in a corridor with five rivals, the study's job is to say how much debt the member base supports before the lender has to find out from the payment history.

As proposed, the sponsor puts in $579,416, 10.0% of a $5,794,165 project, and asks for $5,214,748. The request fails twice. The loan is over the 7(a) limit, and annual debt service of $568,637 is covered 0.77x in Year 2 and 0.93x in Year 3. Even in Year 5 the request covers only 1.00x. It meets neither the program limit nor the coverage floor.

As restructured, the engine holds the project and asks how much debt Year 2 income will carry at the floor. The answer is $3,510,956, with debt service of $382,849 a year, which leaves the sponsor to fund 38.9% of cost, $2,235,290. Coverage is then 1.15x in Year 2, 1.38x in Year 3 and 1.49x in Year 5. The program's injection rule is met many times over; it is the corridor's income that sets the equity.

The restructured case is a model result, not a recommendation and not a forecast of any lender's decision: on these inputs, a sponsor with $2,235,290 to commit and the liquidity to carry Year 1 would meet the coverage floor in the test year. A sponsor without it should look for a cheaper pad, a smaller building, or a less crowded road, because a larger loan on this corridor depends on taking members from five established tunnels.

Risk Factors

What could break a sixth tunnel on US 41.

  • Member capture. At least five tunnels already sell plans [12][13][14][16][17]. If the subject signs members at the pace of a first-year site in an open market rather than a crowded one, the ramp is too slow and Year 2 coverage misses.
  • A price war. ModWash showed that it will open with a discounted first month [15], and Bubble Down is preparing its own opening [18]. Launch discounts from two newcomers could hold the market's blended price down for a year or more.
  • Pipeline. Bubble Down has posted prices for a US 41 site whose date has slipped to 2027 [18]. When it opens, it competes for the same corridor drivers, and no county permit record was found to show what else is coming.
  • Flood and wind. The nearest tunnel's lot is partly in Zone AE [11]. An adverse zone adds elevation work, mandatory flood cover and a smaller appraisal. Insurance is a placeholder until quoted.
  • Tax uncertainty. Countywide operating millage was set above the rollback rate for FY2025-26 [6], and the school and district rates were not found. The tax line could be higher than modelled.
  • Appraisal gap. The Tommy's tunnel is carried on the 2026 roll at a just value far below the cost of building a new one [11]. Special purpose collateral narrows the lender's recovery if the business fails.
  • Construction costs. A tunnel is steel and aluminum heavy, and those prices rose sharply into 2026 [28]; the figures are in the inputs table. A bid priced late in a rising market can exceed the budget.
  • Program change. SBA rewrote its citizenship and residency requirements in a notice effective March 1, 2026 [35], and a new SOP took effect on October 1 [31]. A loan approved later should be rechecked against the rules in force.

The special purpose property note explains why lenders treat car washes as limited-market collateral, and the SOP 50 10 8.1 note covers the October 2026 rule changes.

Port Charlotte Questions

What lenders ask about the Port Charlotte tunnel case.

Why does a 7(a) start-up that meets the 10% injection still fail?

Because the injection is a floor and coverage is the test. A start-up puts in at least 10% of project cost and must show projected coverage of 1.15x within two years [31]. A tunnel sized to the minimum equity also borrows more than the $5 million 7(a) limit [31], and in a corridor with several rivals its Year 2 income does not carry the payments. The engine raises equity until Year 2 coverage reaches the floor.

How many express tunnels already operate in Port Charlotte?

At least five membership washes are listed on their operators' own websites: Tommy's Express, two ModWash sites and two Coastal Express sites [12][13][14][17]. Bubble Down has a US 41 site announced, with a page that says 2027 in the body and 2026 in the title [18]. A Mister Car Wash on McCall Road sits in the outer ring [20], and an older El Jobean Road tunnel could not be confirmed.

What do Port Charlotte tunnels charge for unlimited plans?

Tommy's Express, Coastal Express and Bubble Down each post a tiered ladder of unlimited plans with dollar amounts on their own pages [12][16][18]. ModWash names its plan tiers on both location pages and posts no dollar amounts [13][14]. This page does not print the amounts, because none of the operator pages carries a publication date, and the model's blended plan price is a labelled assumption. Pulling fresh menus is a lender condition.

Is the site in a flood zone?

Unknown for a hypothetical pad, and that is a lender condition. The nearest operating tunnel, on Murdock Circle, sits on a lot split between Zone AE and Zone X on a FEMA panel effective December 15, 2022 [11]. A pad in Zone AE needs the tunnel equipment set above base flood elevation or floodproofed, and the lender will require flood insurance on the building.

Is population growth enough to justify another tunnel?

It supports the format, not the loan. Charlotte County grew 16.3% from April 2020 to July 2025 [1], and the county's own report counted 5.5% growth in the ten months to February 2025 [4]. But growth is concentrated in West Port and Babcock Ranch, which took 82% of residential construction since 2024 [4], and rival tunnels are already selling plans on US 41. The model therefore underwrites a modest member base.

Will the 7(a) lender order a feasibility study for this tunnel?

The rule says that 13 CFR 120.160(b): SBA may require professional appraisals, a survey, or a feasibility study [34]. It is not automatic. For a start-up car wash on special purpose property, a lender may ask for one anyway, and a study that builds the competitor census and the coverage test answers the question before it is asked.
