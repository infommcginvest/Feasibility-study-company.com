Auto Dealership Feasibility Study Case: Sioux Falls, South Dakota, SBA 504

Market analysis by Mark Otava · Published November 4, 2026 · Data as of November 2026

Representative worked example. The site, sponsor and project are hypothetical. The market evidence comes from the public sources listed below, with their dates. This page shows how the market analysis is built and how it sets the loan. It is not a client engagement.

Sioux Falls counted 224,676 residents at the end of 2025 [1], yet the research names no dealer and holds no vehicle sales. On these planning inputs the bank's half of project cost alone carries more debt service than Year 2 income, so more equity cannot lift coverage from 0.45x to the floor.

Study at a Glance

A new independent auto dealership in Sioux Falls, tested against the SBA 504 rules.

Feasibility Study Company prepares auto dealership feasibility studies for owners, developers and their lenders. The work behind a dealership loan has four parts: a dated read of the market, a count of the dealers already selling there, a priced build, and a test of how much debt the store can carry. Here it is applied to one hypothetical project, a new independent used-vehicle dealership with a service department on a vacant lot in Sioux Falls, South Dakota, financed with an SBA 504 loan. Owners who search for an auto dealership feasibility study consultant, or for an auto dealership feasibility study company they can hand to a bank, will find on this page what such a study contains. We can run these steps on your own site.

The subject is a generic one-story building of about 18,000 square feet on about 3.0 acres in the 57th Street and Veterans Parkway growth area of Sioux Falls, in Minnehaha County, with 8 service bays. No parcel, owner, brand or listing is meant. The market figures are dated public records. The model figures are engine results drawn from a closing input table, every line of which says where it came from or that it is an assumption. The store sits in our auto dealership practice and, as an owner-operated used-vehicle store, to its independent used car segment. The city is in South Dakota, and the rules of the program appear on our SBA 7(a) and 504 page.

One limit governs how to read the page. The research for this state holds market evidence, wage and permit records and the federal business counts, but no dealership sales, margin or inventory figures. Those inputs are labelled assumptions, they were set before the model was run, and they were not adjusted afterwards to change the answer. The answer is negative, and the sensitivity table and the lender conditions show how far it would have to move.

Market Conclusion

Sioux Falls is growing and fully employed, but the loan does not pencil. The store needs share from dealers already there, and its debt costs more than Year 2 earns.

The demand answer is yes. The city of Sioux Falls reported 224,676 residents at the end of 2025, more than 5,000 above the year before, a rise of 2.3% [1]. The Census estimate the state labor agency tables is lower, 209,300 for 2024, with the metropolitan statistical area at 299,000 [3]; the two come from different counts, and this study uses the city's figure only for scale. Unemployment is the other half of the picture. The city's rate was 1.7% in September 2026, Minnehaha County's and Lincoln County's were each 1.6%, and the metro area's seasonally adjusted rate was 2.0% [8][9][10]. A dealer in a labor market that tight pays more for staff, but its customers are employed and financeable.

The competitive answer is harder, and it comes from the federal business counts rather than from any named rival. The register of County Business Patterns holds 92 establishments in the dealer classes for the Sioux Falls metropolitan area in 2023, with 2,308 paid employees [22]. The research names no dealer, so no row of the competitor section is a business; the supply table counts the field and the lender conditions require the named census.

The financing answer is no. Under the sponsor's 10.0% equity proposal the project costs $7,021,756 and Year 2 coverage is 0.45x against a floor of 1.15x, so the equity tier for a new business and the coverage floor both fail. Restructured to $1,053,000, 15.0% of project cost, the bank still lends $3,509,999, the debenture falls to $2,456,999, and Year 2 coverage reaches only 0.48x. Stabilized Year 3 coverage is 0.75x, also under the floor. The model is a negative result for these inputs, and the break-even section shows the volume that would have to be sold instead.

People and jobs

The city's own year-end report counted 224,676 residents and a gain of more than 5,000 in 2025, a rise of 2.3% [1]. The Census-based figure that the state labor agency published in December 2025 is 209,300 for the city in 2024 and 299,000 for the metropolitan area [3]. The gap is a difference of source and of year, not a contradiction, but a lender who sees both should ask which one a drive-time analysis uses. Employment is counted more cleanly. Minnehaha County had 139,770 covered workers in 2025 [4] and the metropolitan area 177,999, a total the state labor agency builds with Rock County, Minnesota inside it [5]. The Bureau of Labor Statistics put Minnehaha County at 138,476 employed in March 2026, with 10,147 establishments, and Lincoln County at 30,041 employed [7].

Pay and the cost of staff

Wages bear on a dealership twice, as a cost and as buying power. The metropolitan area's average annual pay was $66,768 in 2025, and retail trade paid $41,028 [5]. Minnehaha County averaged $66,906 and Lincoln County $69,492 [6]. In the first quarter of 2026 the average weekly wage was $1,369 in Minnehaha County and $1,387 in Lincoln County, against $1,211 for the state [7]. A salesperson on commission and a technician both earn more than the retail average, which is why the model carries $58,000 a year per employee before payroll costs.

Growth, and the permits that follow it

The city issued 5,615 permits in 2025 with a total valuation of $1,327,437,573, which SDPB reports as the fifth straight year above $1 billion [2][1]. The count of permits fell from 6,183 in 2024 [2]. New single-family permits were 357, the lowest in ten years according to the same report, town house units were 403 and multifamily units 1,168 [2][1]. The city's dwelling-unit total was 1,999, below its ten-year average of 2,552 and below the 2,150 of 2024 [2]. The county series from the Census Bureau, relayed by the St. Louis Federal Reserve, shows Minnehaha County with 2,044 housing units in 2025 and Lincoln County with 232, down from 2,421 and 268 in 2024 [11][12]. The reading for a dealer is a market that is still adding households but at a slower pace than a few years ago: the county's count was 4,747 in 2022 and 2,863 in 2023 [11].

Commercial activity

New commercial buildings were valued at $711.6 million in 2025, with another $314.2 million for additions, renovations and remodels [2]. Manufacturing construction alone came to $84.0 million, and 17 commercial projects were valued at $10 million or more [2]. Sioux Falls Regional Airport enplaned 754,686 passengers in 2025, up 5.9% on 2024, and handled 46,696,248 pounds of freight [15]. The metropolitan area's gross domestic product was $30,084.975 million in 2023, the last year before the series was discontinued [18]. None of these figures predicts vehicle sales. Together they describe a regional hub with a diverse payroll, which is the demand base a store that sells to employed households relies on.

Housing and household wealth

Home equity supports trade-in and down-payment behaviour. The median listing price in the Sioux Falls housing market stood at $362,200 in September 2026, and the Federal Housing Finance Agency index for the area was 365.85 in the second quarter of 2026, against 357.04 a year earlier [16][17]. Both are relays: Realtor.com's definition of the area and the agency's index, each carried by the St. Louis Federal Reserve. They are context, not a forecast of vehicle demand. The apartment survey for January 2026 found 6.62% of 14,409 surveyed units vacant, 954 in all, against 5.68% in the previous survey, with 270 multifamily units permitted through February [14]. A softer rental market eases the pressure on household formation, which is one more reason the model does not lean on the housing boom to fill its showroom.

What the counts say

The metropolitan area has about the same density of dealer establishments as the state as a whole, a little lower, at 3.02 per 10,000 residents against 3.43 for South Dakota [22]. A metro area with fewer dealers per head than its state is not an empty field, but it is not oversupplied by this measure either. The rate divides establishments by the Census population estimate for July 2023, and the metro area has grown since [22][1], so the density today is probably a little lower. Because the class also holds RV, boat and motorcycle dealers, the count overstates the direct rivals of a used-vehicle store.

The employee counts show a typical store. Dividing the metro area's paid employees by its establishments gives an average in the mid-twenties per establishment, which includes franchised stores with full parts and service departments and small independent lots with a handful of staff [22]. The subject, a single-line independent store, staffs below that average, and the model's headcount in 21 full-time equivalents in the stabilized year is set accordingly.

What the supply data cannot tell the lender

County Business Patterns does not name a business, a brand, a lot size or a sales volume. It cannot say which establishments are franchised, which sell used vehicles only or which are about to close or relocate. A store that added a manufacturer line would need the manufacturer's approval and would meet franchise rules this study does not model; the subject sells used vehicles and services all makes. The lender condition for the census asks for the named competitor census, with a website or county assessor record for every dealer, the brands and inventories on each lot, and the sales each reports to the state, so that the volume ramp in the model can be tested against real neighbors.

Site

The corridor is a growth area. The parcel, zoning and utilities are lender conditions.

The subject is a hypothetical site of about 3.0 acres in the 57th Street and Veterans Parkway growth area of Sioux Falls. The area is named to fix a place on the map: the research records a permit of more than $42 million for a senior-living community, the year's largest, near that intersection [14], a sign of new building there, and no parcel has been identified. Its zoning district, utility territory, flood status and traffic count are conditions to confirm. The research found no traffic count for any Sioux Falls arterial, no impact fee schedule and no dealer licensing rule.

Land and the commercial market

The commercial real estate record is from the Bender Commercial Real Estate Services outlook, relayed by SiouxFalls.Business. It reported retail vacancy at 8% at the end of 2025, down from 9% a year earlier, with nearly 1 million square feet of retail added over five years [13]. Industrial vacancy was 4.8%, up from 3.7% in 2024 and the highest in more than 20 years, with net absorption of 680,000 square feet against 1.1 million square feet of new construction [13]. Industrial sales volume reached a record $168 million [13]. Sales volume rose 76% for retail, 44% for industrial and 24% for office [13]. Unimproved land sales totalled 1,120 acres, the second-strongest year on record, with 388 of them in Harrisburg [13].

A dealership competes for land with retail pads on one side and light industrial users on the other. The 8% retail vacancy rate [13] says a buyer has choices among existing buildings; the industrial figure says developers are still building, so a dealer who needs a lot with a shop is shopping where speculative supply is plentiful. The research holds no price per acre or per square foot for any of that land. The model carries a round land figure, $1,400,000, as an assumption, and an appraisal of the real parcel replaces it.

Construction cost

The only construction cost in the research is the city's permit valuation: the average new single-family dwelling was valued at $321,594 in 2025, with an average size of 1,572 square feet, and the 2024 average was $265,912 [2]. That is a residential valuation, and a shop and showroom shell is a different building, but it is the only local anchor. The model carries $190 per square foot and a general contractor's bid replaces it.

Taxes and licensing

South Dakota cut its state sales tax rate to 4.2% from 4.5% on July 1, 2023, under a measure that sunsets on June 30, 2027 [19]. The research found no 2026 action on the sunset. How the state taxes vehicle sales, the dealer licence fees and the county property tax levy on a dealership are not in the research and are lender conditions. The model's property tax line is a placeholder rate of 1.50% of cost.

Services and flood status

Electric, gas, water and sewer providers, the stormwater and detention requirements for a paved display lot, and the FEMA flood panel for the parcel are not in the research. A dealership paves most of its site, so the stormwater design carries real cost, and the site work allowance in the model is high for that reason.

Revenue has three lines. The store retails 680 used vehicles in Year 3, the stabilized year, after $12,110,250 of total revenue in Year 1 and $17,709,500 in Year 2. The ramp is the assumption that matters most in the model, and the research cannot support it: no vehicle sales count for any Sioux Falls dealer was found. What the research does give is the setting. The city grew by more than 5,000 residents in 2025 [1], and its labor market is nearly fully employed, with the city's September 2026 unemployment rate at 1.7% [8]. Those are the conditions in which a store could win share, but they are also conditions the existing dealers share.

The model sells at an average retail price of $27,500 and earns $2,500 of front-end gross profit per vehicle after reconditioning. Both are round planning figures. A used-vehicle dealer's real prices and margins vary with the mix of inventory, and the lender's file should hold the sponsor's own sales history from any comparable store, which is a lender condition. The gross profit input is the one the sensitivity table tests, because it is the figure a new store has the least control over: it buys at auction and in trades before it has a base of repeat customers.

Finance and insurance income adds $950 for each vehicle retailed. The store arranges loans for its customers, and the pattern of South Dakota income supports finance penetration: Minnehaha County's median household income was $76,880 and Lincoln County's $99,166 in the 2020 to 2024 survey [21]. The research holds no finance or insurance income for any dealer, so the figure is an assumption and the lender should test it against the first twelve months of a comparable store.

The service department is built as a share of bay capacity sold. The 8 bays sell 65.0% of their capacity in the stabilized year at $210,000 of labor and parts revenue per bay at full capacity, a figure held flat through the five years. It earns a gross margin of 55.0%. The service department matters beyond its own profit: the research's wage data shows it competes for technicians in a market where the average weekly wage is $1,369 in Minnehaha County [7], and a shop that cannot hire does not reach its utilization.

Payroll with costs is the largest operating line. The model staffs the store with 21 full-time equivalents in the stabilized year at $58,000 each before a payroll-cost load of 18.0%, rising by 3.0% a year. The pay figure sits between the metro retail trade average of $41,028 and the Minnehaha County average of $66,906 [5][6], on the logic that salespeople and technicians earn above a retail clerk and below the all-industry average once the lowest-paid porters and detailers are included. The first quarter of 2026 statewide weekly wage rose 4.2% over the year [7], so the model's 3.0% is modest and the sensitivity table does not test it. An owner-operator compensation line of $120,000 is added because a lender normalizes owner pay before it counts coverage.

Floor plan interest is the dealership-specific cost. The store carries about 60 days of vehicle inventory on a floor plan line at 8.50%, which is Prime of 7.00% on the October 9, 2026 H.15 release [27] plus a spread. The inventory is financed outside the SBA project. The lender must settle how the floor plan lender's lien on the vehicles sits alongside the bank's and the CDC's liens on the real estate and equipment, and the research cannot settle it. The cost of the inventory itself is in cost of sales.

The remaining lines are advertising at $350 per vehicle retailed, utilities, repairs, supplies and fees at $230,000 in Year 1, insurance at $70,000 and property tax at 1.50% of cost. Operating costs move from $1,831,120 in Year 1 to $2,556,784 in Year 3. The model includes no vehicle inventory tax, no dealer licence fee and no sales tax effect, because the research found none of them; each is a lender condition. Net operating income runs from ($110,470) in Year 1 to $389,816 in Year 3, about 1.9% of revenue, which is the thin margin of a vehicle retailer, whose revenue is dominated by the price of the vehicle itself.

Three parties fund a 504 project, and the rules in force from October 1, 2026 (SOP 50 10 8.1 with Technical Policy Updates) [25] put the bank's first lien at half of project cost, the debenture at Third Party Lender 50%, debenture 40%, borrower 10%; debenture 35% at a 15% contribution and 30% at 20% [25]. A new business, one with two years or less of operation, contributes at least 15% [25]. The SOP defines a special purpose property as a limited-market property with a unique physical design, special construction materials, or a layout that restricts its utility to the use for which it was built [25]. A showroom and shop with above-ground lifts is a general commercial building by that test. The higher tier, 20%, therefore does not apply, but the CDC and the lender decide that on the plans, which makes the property type a lender condition.

The debenture at both structures sits well under the $5 million limit. The sponsor's 10.0% proposal fails the equity tier, so the engine restructures it to 15.0% and then looks for a share at which Year 2 coverage reaches the floor. It finds none, because the bank's half of cost does not shrink as equity rises, and the restructured structure stays at 15.0%. The 504 upfront fee on the debenture is 0.50% under the fiscal year 2027 schedule [26], added to project cost.

The debenture rate of 6.95% is a planning rate, because the research for this state holds no debenture pricing and the CDC sets the actual rate at the debenture sale. The bank rate of 7.50% is an assumption too, a half point over Prime of 7.00% [27], with the third-party spread capped by SBA at 6% over New York Prime [25]. The bank's term and amortization are assumed equal to the debenture's, and the bank may set a shorter maturity that would raise debt service.

A 504 loan is also tied to jobs. The program's job standard is $95,000 of debenture per job for a project that is not a small manufacturer or an energy project. The store's headcount in the model, 21 full-time equivalents in the stabilized year, is the sponsor's staffing plan, and the CDC will test it against the debenture. That arithmetic is a lender condition because the headcount is itself an assumption.

Risks

What could break the Sioux Falls projection.

  1. The ramp is slower than modelled

    By Year 3 the model needs 680 sales a year from customers who today buy from the dealer establishments the register counts, and the register cannot say how many of them sell used vehicles only [22]. The model already fails the Year 2 test, and a late ramp widens the gap.

  2. Gross profit per vehicle compresses

    A new store buys at auction and in trades before it has repeat customers, and the test year reads that thin first period. Front-end gross is the input the sensitivity table varies, and a shortfall on it widens the gap to the floor.

  3. The floor plan costs more, or is harder to get

    The model prices the line at a spread over Prime, which was 7.00% on the October 9, 2026 H.15 release [27]. The line sits outside the SBA project, so a floor plan lender's terms, curtailments and lien position can change the cost of inventory without touching the 504 structure.

  4. Established dealers respond

    At the register's rate the metropolitan area has slightly fewer dealer establishments per resident than the state average [22], but the stores already there hold the customers, the trade-ins and the technicians. A well-capitalized neighbor can match price on the units the new store most needs.

  5. Technicians and salespeople are scarce

    City unemployment was 1.7% in September 2026 and the metro rate 2.0% [8][10]. A store competing for technicians at a county average weekly wage of $1,369 [7] may pay above the model's pay figure, or open bays it cannot staff, which cuts the service department's utilization.

  6. Construction and land cost more than assumed

    Neither land nor building cost has a source, and an overrun adds debt to a structure that already fails. The city's construction base is large, with $1,327,437,573 of permits in 2025 [2], so a contractor has other work and may bid high.

  7. Rates differ from the model

    The debenture rate is set when the debenture is sold and the bank rate at pricing. The model's debenture and bank rates are planning assumptions, and Prime moved to 7.00% by the October 9, 2026 release [27]. A higher rate on the bank half raises debt service immediately.

  8. Farm income and drought

    SBA's June 5, 2026 release on drought relief for South Dakota lists 28 counties, including Minnehaha and Lincoln, with an application deadline of February 1, 2027 and a maximum economic injury loan of $2 million [20]. Sioux Falls is a regional hub whose customers include farm households, and weak farm income would reach a vehicle store through its rural buyers.

  9. The state sales tax rate may revert

    The reduction to 4.2% from 4.5% sunsets on June 30, 2027 [19]. The research found no 2026 action on the sunset. A return to the higher rate would raise the price of taxable purchases, and how it reaches vehicles is a lender condition.

  10. The local economy cools

    State nonfarm employment was 470,500 in September 2026, against 468,700 a year earlier [10]. That is slow growth, and the housing permit counts in Minnehaha and Lincoln counties have fallen since 2022 [11][12]. A store that needs a Year 3 ramp is exposed if hiring stalls.

Lender Conditions

Conditions before commitment.

The public record left the questions below open. A lender would want them answered before any commitment, and several of them bundle items the research marked as not found.

  1. Named competitor census

    A roster of named dealers, each linked to its own site or the county assessor record: every dealer within the trade area, its brands, its lot and building size, its inventory, its service department and its age. The research named no dealer, and the supply table above counts establishments but names none [22].

  2. Sales history and ramp evidence

    The sponsor's own sales, gross profit and finance and insurance history at any comparable store, the titling and registration counts the state keeps for the trade area, and a volume ramp tied to named competitors. The research found no vehicle sales figure for Sioux Falls.

  3. Floor plan and lien position

    The floor plan lender's commitment, rate and curtailment terms, and a written agreement on how its lien on inventory sits with the bank's and the CDC's liens on the real estate and equipment. How a CDC treats a project beside an inventory line is for the CDC to confirm.

  4. Eligibility and property type

    The CDC's confirmation that a used-vehicle dealership with a service department is an eligible business under the SOP [25], that the property is not a special purpose property, and that the owner-occupancy test is met. The jobs standard for the debenture must be tested against the staffing plan.

  5. Site, title and zoning

    The county parcel number, a zoning letter confirming that vehicle display and service bays are permitted, and the land's appraisal, survey and sales history. The research holds no Sioux Falls zoning text.

  6. Traffic, flood and environmental

    Daily traffic at a state count station near the corridor, the nearest interchange, the FEMA flood panel and a Phase I environmental site assessment. The research found no average daily traffic count for any Sioux Falls arterial. Service bays add fuel, oil and waste handling to the environmental file.

  7. Utilities, fees and taxes

    Which utilities serve the parcel, the connection and impact fees in force, the stormwater requirements for a paved display lot, and the Minnehaha County or Lincoln County property tax levy and assessment practice for a dealership.

  8. State licensing and tax treatment

    The state dealer licence requirements and fees, the tax treatment of vehicle sales, trade-ins and service work, and the status of the state sales tax rate after the sunset date [19].

  9. Costs and equipment

    Contractor bids, vendor quotes for lifts, alignment and diagnostic equipment, and bound quotes for property, garage liability and inventory insurance.

  10. Staffing and wages

    An organization chart with named roles, offer letters or wage surveys for technicians and salespeople in the trade area, and a plan to hire ahead of the opening [5][6][7].

  11. Program items

    The CDC and bank terms set at pricing, the debenture rate lock, owner eligibility documents under the SOP, and the 504 and 7(a) loan history for the class from SBA's public loan data [24]. Rural, HUBZone and Opportunity Zone status for the tract is unknown, and matters only if an incentive is part of the plan.

    Reserve cash sized to the Year 1 shortfall, when operating income does not reach debt service, kept until volume stabilizes. The reserve does not cure the Year 2 test.

Sioux Falls Questions

What lenders ask about the Sioux Falls dealership case.

Is Sioux Falls too crowded for a new used-vehicle dealership?

The record does not say it is. The register's Census count puts the metro area at slightly fewer dealer establishments per resident than South Dakota as a whole [22], and the city added more than 5,000 residents in 2025 [1]. What the record cannot show is who the dealers are, since the research names none. The model therefore builds its volume over three years, and the lender conditions ask for a named census before any commitment.

Why does a minimum-equity proposal fail, and does more equity fix it?

A new business contributes at least 15% of project cost [25], and projected coverage in the test year must also reach 1.15x [25]. Here the minimum fails both. Extra equity displaces only the debenture, because the bank lends half of cost whatever the sponsor puts in, and in this model the bank's half alone costs more to service than the store earns in Year 2. The coverage table sets the two structures next to each other.

Can the analysis use dealership sales data for Sioux Falls?

Not from this research. The research for South Dakota holds permits, wages, vacancy and federal business counts for the city, and no vehicle sales, margin or inventory figures. The model's sales volume, price, gross profit and finance income are labelled assumptions, and the sensitivity table varies two of them. A study prepared for a real project replaces them with the sponsor's history and the state's titling records.

Is a feasibility study mandatory for a dealership loan?

Not as a blanket rule: the regulation reads 13 CFR 120.160(b): SBA may require professional appraisals, a survey, or a feasibility study [28], and the research note on that wording explains the gap between "may" and "must". A bank financing a new store on bare ground often asks anyway, and a study that counts the rivals and tests coverage answers its questions before it asks.

Is vehicle inventory part of the 504 loan?

In this model it is not. The inventory sits on a separate floor plan line priced at a spread over Prime, which was 7.00% on the October 9, 2026 H.15 release [27], and the SBA budget holds land, building, site work, equipment, fees and pre-opening costs. How the floor plan lender's lien sits beside the bank and the CDC is a lender condition.

Which unknowns about the lot could end the project early?

Three the research could not settle: whether the zoning allows outdoor vehicle display beside service bays, whether the lot floods, and which utilities serve it. Dealer licensing and the way the state taxes vehicle sales are also missing. The state sales tax rate was cut to 4.2% from 4.5% under a measure that sunsets on June 30, 2027 [19].
