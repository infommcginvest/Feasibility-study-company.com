{{case:glance}}

The Hankamer interchange can support a new travel center. The evidence for that is the shape of truck parking along this stretch of I-10, not a traffic count, because no TxDOT count at the interchange was found. Between the chain travel centers at Exit 789 in Baytown and the two small truck stops at Exit 829 in Winnie, drivers have an existing Shell travel plaza at the SH 61 interchange that truck directories credit with 10 to 20 parking spaces [^16,17], two Stuckey's stores at Exit 819 [^10,11], and a pair of TxDOT rest areas that allow truck parking [^12]. Love's at Baytown lists 160 truck spaces and TA lists 173 [^13,14]. A travel center with a real truck lot at Hankamer would sit in a gap the chains have left.

The finding on the loan is less comfortable. With volumes benchmarked to a comparable independent site and margins benchmarked to a Texas convenience chain's 2025 filing, the project earns {{case:v.noi_y3}} of net operating income in its third year on a total cost of {{case:v.tpc}}. That supports a loan of {{case:v.loan_total}} at the assumed coverage floor, not the {{case:v.loan_total_proposed}} the sponsor asked for. The USDA guarantee lowers the lender's loss if the project fails; it does nothing to raise the project's income, and coverage is measured on income.

Three facts frame the rest of the page. First, the program is assumed. The case research could not confirm which loan program the sponsor would use and took the USDA Business and Industry guarantee as its working assumption; this case adopts it. Second, Hankamer's rural status for B&I was not determined: it is unincorporated, but Chambers County sits inside the Houston metro, and the site must be checked against USDA's population test [^33]. Third, the decisive demand data, the TxDOT count and truck share at the interchange, were not found, and the only traffic figure in circulation is a broker's claim of over 60,000 vehicles a day at Exit 814 [^1]. Every volume in the model is therefore a benchmark-based assumption, and the first lender condition is to replace it with counts.

The truck stop and travel center hub explains the asset's economics nationally. The Houston metro page covers the region Chambers County belongs to, and the Texas state page covers statewide taxes, fuel rules and hazards.

A travel center's customer is mostly passing through. Federal Highway Administration's calibrated truck parking model assigns 77% of truck rest parking demand to commercial truck stops and 23% to public rest areas [^51], and the drivers who stop at Hankamer are hauling between Houston, Beaumont and Louisiana. That is why this study defines the primary trade area as the I-10 mainline from Exit 810 to Exit 820 rather than a radius of households, and why the missing TxDOT count matters more here than any Census figure.

Local demand is a second, growing layer. Chambers County had 57,594 residents in July 2025, up 23.8% from 46,539 in 2020 [^5], while Texas grew 1.25% in the single year 2024 to 2025 [^9]. The county's 17,229 households average 2.97 persons [^6], its median age is 35.1 [^8], and its median household income was $109,804 (plus or minus $10,467) against $78,476 for Texas in the 2020 to 2024 survey, with per capita income of $42,258 against $40,752 [^7]. New subdivisions along SH 61 south of I-10 were reported only in marketing material, which this study does not rely on; a lender should confirm their size and pace from the county's plat filings.

SH 61 also carries recreation traffic south toward Anahuac and the coast. Texas Gatorfest in Anahuac expects about 10,000 visitors in September, timed to the alligator season [^21], and the national wildlife refuge near Anahuac, renamed the Jocelyn Nungaray refuge on July 24, 2025 [^22], is a seasonal draw for hunting and birding. Visitor counts and monthly fuel seasonality were not found. These are useful for car traffic and the store, not for diesel.

The fuel market is volatile. EIA put the 2025 retail diesel price at $3.66 a gallon and forecast $5.19 for 2026 and $4.49 for 2027 [^52]. Margins, not prices, pay the debt, but high pump prices squeeze fleet buyers and push them toward discount programs at the chains. The largest operator's results show the pressure: Pilot's 2025 pre-tax earnings fell to $190 million from $614 million on revenue of $42.2 billion, down from $46.9 billion [^49].

Two competitors at the SH 61 interchange could not be confirmed on an operator site and stay out of the table. Country Boys' Country Store and Travel Plaza, a Shell station on the northeast side of the SH 61 interchange, has no website of its own; one truck directory lists 20 truck parking spaces and 4 diesel lanes, and another lists 10 or more spaces, a conflict the lender should resolve on site [^16,17]. DJ's Country Store, an Exxon on SH 61 south of I-10, appears in directories with a deli [^18]; its fuel positions and parking were not found. Both are a lender condition: confirm the operator, fuel brand, diesel lanes and truck spaces, and pull the appraisal district record. These two are the subject's direct competitors for local car fuel and store sales.

The next ring east is small. JP's Truck Stop at Exit 829 in Winnie lists 15 parking spaces [^19], and a second stop shares the exit; neither has an operator page. To the west, a Flying J at Exit 789 could not be confirmed because its page refused automated access.

The chains are expanding parking, not abandoning it. Love's planned $700 million for 20 new stores, 35 remodels and 1,500 more truck spaces in 2026, on a network of more than 50,000 [^50]. Buc-ee's has scheduled a groundbreaking on November 9, 2026 for a 74,000 square foot store in Lafayette, Louisiana, about 150 miles east, with about 120 fuel pumps and nearly 700 parking spaces at a cost of $82 million including about $12.5 million of public infrastructure, opening in 2028 [^20]. That store competes for car travelers on the corridor, not for trucks.

No travel center permitted or under construction in the Exit 789 to 829 ring was found for 2025 or 2026, but county permit logs and Commissioners Court agendas were not searched item by item, which is a condition. Land listings show that others see the same opportunity: a 6.82-acre tract at Exit 814 marketed for a truck stop or travel plaza sold on June 5, 2026 for a disclosed $482,001 to $552,000 against a $668,428 list price after 92 days [^1].

The hypothetical travel center sits on about 10 acres with access from the I-10 frontage road in the Hankamer segment of the corridor, the stretch from Exit 810 to Exit 820 around the SH 61 interchange, in unincorporated Chambers County [^4]. No particular tract is assumed: the site's position, its ramp access and its frontage are conditions. The land cost in the model, {{case:v.in_land}}, is an assumption at $80,000 an acre, set inside the land evidence the research holds for the area. Unincorporated Chambers County has no zoning, so a travel center needs no rezoning wherever the site falls in the corridor [^1]; the lender confirms that no deed restriction on the chosen tract bars the use. The flood rule matters more than the fees. Chambers County requires all new construction to sit at least 24 inches above base flood elevation regardless of flood zone, and revised its floodplain rules in June 2025 [^24]. The site's FEMA zone must be confirmed on FEMA's map before the loan is sized. Raising a store pad, a fuel canopy area and a heavy-duty truck lot two feet above base flood elevation across a 10-acre site on the coastal prairie is a material cost, and the model's site line is an assumption until an engineer prices it.

{{case:loan}}

A B&I loan is a single conventional loan from an approved lender with a partial USDA guarantee. For fiscal 2026 the guarantee is 85% for loans under $5 million and 80% for loans of $5 million to $25 million, the program maximum; the initial guarantee fee is 3.0% of the guaranteed amount and the annual retention fee 0.55%, and a guarantee issued before construction is complete costs a further 0.50% [^32]. This model assumes a construction-to-permanent loan guaranteed before completion, so it adds that 0.50% to the initial fee. USDA announced the move to 85% for smaller projects on March 27, 2026 [^33]. Both the sponsor's request and the restructured loan exceed $5 million, so the model applies 80%. A lender that trimmed the restructured loan below $5 million would gain the higher guarantee, at the cost of more sponsor equity.

USDA sets no rate. Rates are negotiated between lender and borrower, fixed, variable or a mix, and a variable rate must track a published base rate the Agency accepts [^35]. The model's all-in rate of {{case:v.in_bi_rate}} is an assumption: Prime of 7.00% on October 9, 2026 [^54] plus two points, plus the retention fee on the guaranteed share. The term may run to the justified useful life of the project or its collateral, at most 40 years [^35]; the model uses {{case:v.in_bi_amort_years}}, blending long-lived buildings with shorter-lived fuel equipment.

The feasibility study is mandatory here, which sets B&I apart from SBA lending. Under 7 CFR 5001.306(a)(3)(i), a guaranteed loan of more than $1,000,000 to a new business requires a study by an independent qualified consultant acceptable to the Agency; at $1,000,000 or less the Agency may still require one [^34]. The projections in the application must run at least two years at full capacity or stable operations, with assumptions listed and a pro forma closing balance sheet [^34], and the lender's credit evaluation must include its own written evaluation of the study [^36]. By contrast, 13 CFR 120.160(b): SBA may require professional appraisals, a survey, or a feasibility study [^56]. The note on USDA's five feasibility components shows how the study maps to the Agency's review, and the USDA Rural Development practice page describes how we scope these studies.

The program itself carries risk. B&I obligations reached $2,111,702,000 in fiscal 2025, up from $1,828,800,000, and Texas took $289.2 million, 13.7% of the national total [^38]. Fiscal 2026 program levels conflict: $1,428,899,083 in USDA's own table and $1,750,000,000 in the enacted language as CRS reports it [^38,39]. The fiscal 2027 budget request proposes no B&I program level at all [^38]. Participation has narrowed: loans fell from 309 in fiscal 2015 to 176 in fiscal 2024 while the average loan rose from $2.75 million to $8.1 million [^40], and on May 12, 2026 USDA removed ten lenders whose portfolios held about $620 million of delinquent loans, about 47% of Rural Development's delinquencies, leaving over 750 approved lenders [^41]. USDA's loan-level Lender Lens data, published January 19, 2026, is where a sponsor finds the lenders still active in Texas [^42].

Two siblings on this site use the same guarantee: the Bastrop County RV park case applies it to a campground, and the Texas meat processing case to a plant. The Terrell truck stop case finances the same asset class on I-20 with SBA 504, where gas stations sit on SBA's special purpose list [^55]; reading the two together shows how differently the programs treat equity and coverage.

The matrix moves the two inputs that make diesel gross profit, volume and margin, by up to 20% each way. Coverage in Year 3 ranges from {{case:v.sens_min}} to {{case:v.sens_max}}. {{case:inputs}}
  • Volume without a count. Every gallon in the model is benchmarked; the only traffic figure is a broker's claim for a nearby tract [^1]. If truck share on this segment is lower than the benchmark site's, the cushion disappears.
  • Interchange competition. Country Boys' Shell plaza already has diesel lanes and truck parking at the SH 61 interchange [^16], and an Exxon store sits on SH 61 [^18]. Their response to a new entrant, on price or on parking, is unknown.
  • Chain discounting. Love's, TA and a Flying J at Exit 789 sell fleet diesel under national programs, and Love's is adding 1,500 truck spaces in 2026 [^50]. The subject competes for the same fleets with no network.
  • Flood and storms. The county's freeboard rule [^24] raises costs, wind and flood premiums were not found, and a Gulf coast storm can close an interstate fuel stop for days.
  • Environmental liability. Underground tanks are the asset's main loss channel; the county permits tanks [^23], but state tank registration and a Phase I assessment were not found.
  • Program funding. The fiscal 2027 request would end new B&I lending [^38], and the lender base has narrowed [^40,41]. A delay in Texas allocations could stall the project after design costs are spent.
  • Rural eligibility. If USDA finds the site ineligible under the population test [^33], the program assumption fails and the project needs a different structure.
  • Fuel price swings. EIA's forecast of $5.19 diesel in 2026 and $4.49 in 2027 [^52] shows how fast pump prices move; high prices squeeze fleet buyers, and falling prices can compress retail margins in a price war.
