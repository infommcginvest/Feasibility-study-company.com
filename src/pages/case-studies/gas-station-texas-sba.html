The subject is hypothetical: a new branded fuel and convenience store with a made-to-order kitchen, on about 1.5 acres of commercial land fronting US 183 inside Lockhart's city limits. Every market figure is public and dated. Every model figure below is computed by the case engine from the inputs listed further down, each of which carries either a source or the word assumption.

{{case:glance}}

The market answer is yes. Lockhart counted 18,159 residents on July 1, 2025, up from an estimates base of 14,394 in April 2020 [^1]. Caldwell County added 2,211 people in the year to July 2025, a 4.2% gain that ranked tenth among all U.S. counties, and 87.2% of that gain came from people moving in [^2]. A city adding households at that rate, on the highway that carries most of its retail, can support another fuel and convenience store, and a modern store with a kitchen can take share from an older field.

The financing answer is no, at least not as asked. The only priced tract with US 183 frontage in town was offered at $1,850,000 for 1.52 acres in October 2026 [^16]. The study carries the subject's land at that asking price because no closed land sale was found. With land at that level, the project costs {{case:v.tpc_proposed}} before restructuring, and the sponsor asks for a {{case:v.loan_total_proposed}} 7(a) loan at {{case:v.rate}}. Stabilized net operating income of {{case:v.noi_y3}} covers that debt {{case:v.dscr_y2_proposed}} in the Year 2 test and {{case:v.dscr_y3_proposed}} in Year 3. The proposal fails by a wide margin, and the failure does not come from weak demand.

Restructured to the coverage the program asks of a start-up, 1.15x within two years [^45], the store carries {{case:v.loan_total}} of debt and needs {{case:v.equity}} of sponsor equity, {{case:v.equity_pct}} of cost. That is a bankable structure on these numbers, but it is a different deal from the one proposed: either the sponsor brings far more cash, or the land is bought well below the ask, or both. The sensitivity table later on this page shows how much a land discount is worth.

Two items sit ahead of the loan question. The city has not been shown to allow gasoline sales by right in the CLB district, and an entrance-corridor overlay adopted in March 2026 would require a Specific Use Permit for any gas station on the US 183 frontage once its map is approved [^23]. This case belongs to our gas station and convenience store practice; Caldwell County sits in the Austin metro, and the statewide context is on our Texas page.

Growth that arrives by moving van

The Caldwell County gain is mostly migration [^2], and that matters for a fuel store. New households arrive with vehicles and with jobs elsewhere. Lockhart residents average a 28.6-minute trip to work [^1], long enough that most commuters leave town on SH 130 toward Austin or on SH 142 toward San Marcos and I-35 [^3]. A commuter fills where the trip begins or ends. A store on US 183 inside town sits on the home end of those trips, which is the steadier end: it does not depend on a toll road's through-traffic choosing to exit.

The national trend runs the other way. EIA reports that U.S. gasoline use fell 1% in 2025, to 8.9 million barrels a day, and expects it to keep declining in 2026 and 2027 as fleet fuel economy improves [^10]. A typical vehicle used 457 gallons in 2025 and came to a station 54.4 times [^9]. In a flat or shrinking national pool, a site's volume grows only if its local vehicle count grows. Lockhart's does, which is the strongest single argument for this store. Our note on gas station demand per capita explains why the per-vehicle decline still leaves room for a well-placed store in a growing town.

A county seat that sells to the county

Lockhart's retail sales of $319,643,000 in 2022 came to $21,412 per resident [^1], the profile of a county seat that draws shoppers from the surrounding county. Its largest employers, as the economic development corporation lists them, are the school district, Walmart and H-E-B [^4]; the corporation gives no date for its headcounts, so the study uses none of them, and daytime employment becomes a lender condition. Household income is middle-market: a median of $68,400 and a per capita figure of $30,208 [^1]. A store selling to this base sells everyday baskets, fountain drinks and fast food, not destination grocery.

Visitors on a calendar, not a curve

The Texas Legislature named Lockhart the Barbecue Capital of Texas in 1999 [^6], and the town's visitor draw follows that reputation. The 2026 Texas Monthly BBQ Fest brings more than 35 of the magazine's Top 50 barbecue joints downtown on November 7 and 8 [^5]. The Chisholm Trail Round-Up reported 20,000 attendees in June 2025, a promoter's figure [^7]. Lockhart State Park recorded 74,469 visits in 2025 and added 188 acres in 2026, figures taken from an encyclopedia entry because the parks department's own count was not retrieved [^8]. These are peaks. No source gives monthly fuel volumes, so the model carries no event revenue; visitor traffic is upside, not underwriting.

A well-supplied town

Texas had 16,504 convenience stores at the end of 2025, more than any other state, and the country had one store for every 2,257 residents [^11]. Lockhart's directories list 16 fuel sites for 18,159 people, far denser than that national ratio before any through-traffic is counted. The town is not underserved. The case for a new store is not a gap in coverage; it is a newer, larger format taking share from an older field while the vehicle count grows.

A Murphy USA would matter, because it is a low-price format. Every one of these becomes a lender condition.

A 7-Eleven at 2410 W San Antonio, on SH 142 toward the toll road, was offered for sale in October 2026 at $12,146,000 on a 5.15% capitalization rate for a 4,853-square-foot building [^16]. The price per square foot looks high and the research flags it as unverified. Even at face value it tells a lender something narrow: investors price a credit-tenant lease in Lockhart richly. It says nothing about an owner-operator's going-concern value, which is what secures this loan.

Who sets the price

The H-E-B fuel center is the reference price on US 183. Grocery fuel centers tend to price low to bring shoppers into the store, and that limits how far any nearby station can push its margin. The model reflects it with a fuel margin below national averages (see the revenue build). Posted prices would test this directly; none was found on any operator's site, so a pump-price survey is a lender condition.

New supply in and around town

What the field means for the subject

The confirmed competitors are a grocer's fuel center and older branded stations. The subject's advantage is format: a larger store, a kitchen and modern forecourt. That is the ground a branded fuel store with foodservice competes on, and it is also exactly what Top Stop is building. If Top Stop opens first and on US 183, the subject's ramp slows; the sensitivity table shows what a 10% or 20% volume shortfall does to coverage.

The subject is a hypothetical site of about 1.5 acres zoned CLB, Commercial Light Business, with frontage on US 183 inside city limits. The one listed comparable fits that description: 1.52 acres of CLB land asking $1,850,000 in October 2026 [^16]. The study borrows its zoning and price as the subject's, and nothing else.

Zoning: two open questions

The first is whether CLB lets a convenience store sell gasoline by right. Lockhart's code defines a convenience store as one that may include gasoline sales and separates uses that are "Allowed", meaning by right, from uses that are "Permitted", meaning subject to a Specific Use Permit [^24]. The CLB entry for fuel sales was not found. A written determination from the city is the first zoning condition.

The second question is the entrance-corridor overlay. The city council adopted its text on March 17, 2026. Inside the overlay, gas stations and convenience stores need a Specific Use Permit, truck stops and travel plazas are prohibited, buildings sit behind a 50-foot front setback and a 15-foot landscape buffer is required [^23]. The map that would apply the overlay to US 183 properties went to a Planning and Zoning hearing on August 26, 2026, was continued, faces a workshop on October 28, 2026 and returns for hearing on November 18, 2026 [^23]. If the subject's frontage is mapped, the store needs a permit regardless of what CLB allows, and the setback moves the canopy deeper into a 1.5-acre lot. No typical permit timeline was found.

{{case:loan}}

The loan is tested under SOP 50 10 8.1 with Technical Policy Updates, in force for applications from October 1, 2026 [^45]. A start-up puts in at least 10% of project cost, and its projected coverage must reach 1.15x within two years, which is why Year 2 is the test year here. A single 7(a) loan tops out at $5 million; the sponsor's {{case:v.equity_proposed_pct}} equity proposal exists to stay under that line. Because real estate is well over half of the proceeds, the whole loan can run 25 years.

The rate is Prime of 7.00% on the October 9, 2026 H.15 release [^47] plus the largest spread SBA allows at this loan size, {{case:v.rate}} in all. A lender may price lower, and the final rate is set at pricing; a lower rate helps, but even a full point off does not close a gap this wide. SBA guarantees {{case:v.in_sba_guaranty_pct}} of a loan this size [^44], and the FY2027 upfront guaranty fee follows the schedule in the program's fee notice [^46].

Three program rules bear on a fuel store in particular. Gas stations are on SBA's list of special purpose properties [^45]; under 504 a new business on such a property puts in 20%, which is one reason this sponsor chose 7(a). Fuel supply agreements covered by the Petroleum Marketing Practices Act fall within SBA's definition of a franchise [^53], so the brand agreement goes into the file. And since March 1, 2026, every owner must be a U.S. citizen or national; the notice that briefly allowed up to 5% foreign ownership was rescinded [^49]. Our note on special purpose property risk covers why lenders lean on going-concern value for assets like this, and our SBA 7(a) and 504 practice page sets out the full rule set.

The feasibility study itself is the lender's call. Under 13 CFR 120.160(b): SBA may require professional appraisals, a survey, or a feasibility study [^48]; on a ground-up special purpose project with a start-up borrower, most lenders ask for one.

There are three ways to reach a structure like this, and the lender should ask which one the sponsor intends. The sponsor can bring the cash. Set beside the other fuel cases in this series, Lockhart is the one where the market is strongest and the cost is the obstacle. The Opelika, Alabama case shows the acquisition route, where a buyer is tested on a seller's actual earnings rather than a ramp, and the Cartersville, Georgia case tests an interstate exit. Read together, they show that the land line and the competitive set decide a new store's loan long before the pump count does.

  1. The overlay map reaches US 183

    If the November 18, 2026 hearing maps the frontage into the entrance-corridor overlay, a gas station needs a Specific Use Permit and a 50-foot front setback [^23]. A denial ends the project; an approval with conditions can shrink the forecourt on a 1.5-acre lot.

  2. CLB does not allow fuel by right

    The code distinguishes by-right uses from permitted uses [^24], and the CLB entry for gasoline sales was not found. A permit requirement adds time and a public hearing to a project already carrying land at full price.

  3. Land bought at the ask

    The study carries land at {{case:v.in_land}}, the only price on record [^16]. If the sponsor pays it, the equity requirement above stands. An appraisal below the price also shrinks what a lender will advance against the real estate.

  4. A new store opens first

    Top Stop's 7,178-square-foot project is expected to start construction in November [^19]. If it opens first on the same highway, the subject competes for the same new households with a similar format, and its ramp slows.

  5. Margins revert

    Listed operators earned 35.1 to 52.6 cents a gallon in the second quarter of 2026 on a supply shock [^38]. The model's {{case:v.in_fuel_margin}} margin already assumes reversion, but a price war led by a grocer's fuel center could take it lower; the break-even table shows the volume equivalent.

  6. Construction prices keep rising

    Steel mill products were up 16.9% year over year in June 2026 [^41]. Every dollar of cost overrun at the restructured equity share adds debt the store can only just carry, so the contingency should not be spent on scope.

  7. The environmental file

    The parcel's environmental history was not found. Fuel sites carry a Phase I and tank testing as a matter of course [^43]; a recognized environmental condition on land priced at the ask would be expensive to resolve.
