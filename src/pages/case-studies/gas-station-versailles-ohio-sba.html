Nothing about the subject is real except its setting. The sponsor is hypothetical, and so is the store: a branded fuel and convenience store with a pizza and breakfast kitchen on about two acres of B-2 land with State Route 121 frontage in the Village of Versailles. The village's code, its competitors, its taxes and its festival calendar are real, and each is dated below.

{{case:glance}}

Versailles can support a fourth fuel and convenience store, but only a small one, and only at roughly half equity. The village had 2,715 residents in the 2020 to 2024 survey [^1], almost unchanged from 2,692 at the 2020 Census [^2]. Growth will not carry this store. What carries it is the village's role as a crossroads and an employment center: three state routes meet in town [^12], Midmark keeps its headquarters, innovation hub and largest manufacturing plant here [^7], and the surrounding Wayne Township counted 4,491 people in 2020 [^6].

Three fuel retailers already serve that market [^23]. A new store with modern pumps and a kitchen can take a share, but the share has a ceiling. The model underwrites fuel volume at a little over half of a typical store's and inside sales below an older rural store's. On those terms the store earns {{case:v.noi_y3}} a year at stabilization. The sponsor asks for {{case:v.loan_total_proposed}} at the start-up minimum of 10% equity, and coverage reaches only {{case:v.dscr_y2_proposed}} in the Year 2 test. Restructured, the loan the store supports is {{case:v.loan_total}}, with {{case:v.equity}} of equity, {{case:v.equity_pct}} of cost.

Cheap land helps but does not decide this case. Land is a small share of cost here, so the debt the store can carry is set by what a four-dispenser store in a small village can earn against the cost of building one. The more pressing question is legal. The village's 2026 zoning code allows a convenience store by right in B-2 but treats a fuel station as a conditional use, and it bans underground petroleum storage inside the drinking-water protection zone [^15]. Whether the subject's land sits in that zone was not found. Until the village answers, the lender cannot know whether it is financing a gas station or a convenience store without fuel.

This case completes the small-town end of our gas station and convenience store casebook. Ohio does not yet have a state page on the site; the national rules are on our SBA 7(a) and 504 page.

A village this size cannot be studied by radius alone. The research set the trade area as the incorporated village, because all three competitors and the meeting of SR 47, SR 121 and SR 185 lie inside it, and treated Wayne Township as the next ring. Our note on why the trade area is the study explains why that choice matters more than any single statistic.

Stable, older and middle-income

The village's population barely moved between the 2020 Census and the latest survey [^1,2], and its median age of 46.0 sits well above Ohio's 39.8 [^4,5]. Households are small, 2.15 people on average, and owners occupy 865 of 1,249 homes [^1]. The median household income of $70,717 is close to the state's $72,212 [^3,5], though the village estimate carries a margin of error of $8,049 [^3]. This is a settled, middle-income community. It will not grow a store's volume on its own; it will not shrink it quickly either. For an underwriter that cuts both ways: the downside is limited, and so is the upside.

Daytime demand from industry

The village's daytime population is larger than its census count. Midmark says its headquarters, innovation hub and largest manufacturing facility are in Versailles [^7]. No dated headcount for Midmark or any other Versailles employer was found; the county's own employer list gives no date, so the study uses no employment figure, and a dated employer list is a lender condition. The county markets two industrial parks in Versailles inside a Community Reinvestment Area [^13]. Workers on shifts buy coffee, breakfast and lunch on the way in and fuel on the way home, which is the trade a pizza-and-breakfast store is built for.

One weekend that changes the town

Poultry Days, now in its 75th year, filled the village on June 12 to 14, 2026 with free admission [^8]. The festival served 40,179 chicken dinners in 2025 and more than 1.2 million since 1952 [^8,9]; local press describes thousands of visitors but no count was published [^10]. That is a peak weekend, not a season, and no monthly fuel volumes exist to measure it. The model carries no festival revenue.

Ohio's store base

Ohio had 5,833 convenience stores at the end of 2025, 38 more than a year earlier [^34]. The state is still adding stores slowly, while chains such as Casey's look for small towns without one of their stores [^35]. Versailles already has its Casey's, which makes it a less obvious target for another chain entrant and a harder market for an independent.

Three fuel and convenience operators trade inside the village, on East Main Street and North 2nd Street: Casey's, an S&G Marathon and Little John's [^23]. Only Casey's has an operator store page; in the next ring, one Sunoco in Greenville does too. The table holds those two, and the others are described below.

The S&G Stores Marathon at 7 North 2nd Street, downtown by Main Street, posted the same $2.66 regular price as Casey's on October 15, 2026 [^23] and had 40 GasBuddy reviews averaging 4.3 [^25]. Its delivery menu that day priced a sausage breakfast burrito at $3.29, a 20-ounce soda at $2.89 and a 12-pack of Budweiser at $13.99 [^26], a useful check on what a breakfast trade can charge here. Neither station could be confirmed through an operator site or a Darke County Auditor record, whose search requires interactive entry, so both stay out of the table and their confirmation is a lender condition. One related county record exists: a 0.32-acre property next to Little John's sold on February 7, 2019 for $193,500 [^21], an improved small site that says more about Main Street commercial values than about the subject's vacant land.

How Casey's arrived

In January 2018 Casey's was negotiating to combine three lots at East Main and Maple for its Versailles store; the county's first Casey's had opened in Ansonia in April 2017, and the company said then that it had no other Darke County stores planned [^27]. No fuel or convenience store project for Versailles or Wayne Township appears in the public record for 2025 or 2026; the January 14, 2026 council agenda, which carried the new zoning ordinance and the zoning of 6.782 newly annexed acres, lists none [^16]. The subject would be the first new entrant since Casey's.

What a fourth store competes on

Casey's runs the format the subject copies, a branded fuel store with pizza. It earned a 58.3% margin on prepared food and dispensed beverages through the third quarter of fiscal 2026 [^39], a level an independent kitchen will not match. The subject's advantages are newer pumps, a second location on the village's other main route, and longer or earlier hours if the sponsor chooses them. Our note on underwriting a c-store franchise covers why the brand agreement and the inside store, not the forecourt, carry the going-concern value.

The subject is a hypothetical parcel of about two acres zoned B-2 with frontage on State Route 121, which ends at SR 47 and SR 185 inside the village [^12]. The village's one B-2 commercial listing, a vacant lot of 4.99 acres, asked $299,100 in 2026, which works out to $60,000 an acre [^18,19], and the lot has been listed and relisted at that price since 2016 [^20]. The study uses the per-acre figure for its land assumption and nothing else from the listing.

What the zoning code allows

The village adopted a new zoning code by Ordinance 26-01 on February 11, 2026 and revised it by Ordinance 26-36 on July 22, 2026 [^15]. In B-2, "Drive-Through Carry Outs and Convenience Stores" are permitted by right under Section 305.2. "Automobile Service Stations" are conditional uses under Section 305.3, and Section 413 makes filling stations conditionally permitted in the business and industrial districts [^15]. A fuel station therefore needs a conditional use permit from the Board of Zoning Appeals under Section 441, with a development plan under Section 413.D [^15]. Should a rezoning ever be needed, Section 209.4 sets public hearings at least 30 days after notice at both the Planning Commission and Council, each with a 30-day decision window [^15].

The filling station standards

Section 413 sets the physical program. A filling station site must have at least 12,000 square feet and 100 feet of frontage [^15]. Buildings sit 40 feet from the front lot line and pumps 15 feet, with 20-foot side and 40-foot rear yards [^15]. The site needs at least two driveways at least 20 feet apart, with curb cuts no wider than 40 feet; canopy lights must be recessed and deliver at least 1.0 foot-candle at grade; a 6-foot screen is required next to residential districts; and one pole sign is allowed [^15]. A two-acre parcel meets all of these comfortably.

The rule that can end the fuel side

Section 424 governs the Drinking Water Source Protection Plan Zone. It prohibits underground storage of petroleum products inside the zone, requires existing and new underground tanks to meet the federal UST rules in 40 CFR 280 and 281, and sends non-residential uses in the zone to Planning Commission site-plan review [^15]. A fuel store stores its fuel underground. If the subject sits inside the zone, it cannot sell fuel, and the project falls back to a convenience store without fuel. The research did not find the zone map, which makes the zone determination the first lender condition, ahead of the market questions.

  1. The site is in the wellhead zone

    Section 424 bans underground petroleum storage inside the Drinking Water Source Protection Plan Zone [^15]. If the parcel is inside it, the fuel business does not exist.

  2. The conditional use is denied or conditioned

    A fuel station is a conditional use in B-2 and needs a Board of Zoning Appeals permit and a development plan [^15]. Neighbors on Main Street and the existing operators can be heard. No observed approval timeline for the village was found.

  3. Casey's answers

    Casey's can match a newcomer on price and outspend it on food. Its prepared-food margin [^39] shows the gap in kitchen economics an independent starts with.

  4. A small, flat market

    The village barely grew from 2020 to the latest survey [^1,2]. If Midmark or another large employer cut shifts, the store's daytime trade would fall with no growth to replace it.

  5. Prices after the tax holiday

    When the fuel tax returns after January 2, 2027 [^29], pump prices rise by the full tax overnight. Customers who learned new habits during the holiday may shop on price more than before, which tests the margin assumption.

  6. Construction cost

    Tanks and the canopy are steel-heavy, and steel prices rose sharply into 2026 [^44]. In a store this small, a cost overrun of a few hundred thousand dollars is a large share of the loan.
