Feasibility Study Company prepares feasibility studies for owners, developers and their lenders, and this page shows what one looks like for a self-storage project. The subject is a hypothetical new facility in Bardstown, the Nelson County seat, built in two parts: a climate-controlled building of {{case:v.in_nrsf_cc}} net rentable square feet and a drive-up wing of {{case:v.in_nrsf_du}}, on about {{case:v.in_land_acres}} acres along the US 150 corridor. It is financed with a USDA Business and Industry guaranteed loan. If you are weighing a project like this one, you can request a methodology briefing before you commit to land.

The market evidence below is real and dated, drawn from public sources. The site, the sponsor and the numbers in the model are hypothetical, and every model input that no source supports is labelled an assumption. For the wider picture, see our pages on self-storage feasibility, Kentucky markets and the USDA Rural Development programs.

{{case:glance}}

Bardstown is a better storage market than its size suggests. Nelson County counted 49,036 residents on July 1, 2025, up from an April 2020 base of 46,732 [^1]. Its households own their homes and stay in them: 9.7% of residents had moved in the past year, against 12.5% across Kentucky [^2]. Tourism built around the bourbon trade produced an estimated $90.9 million of economic impact in 2024 [^4], and distillers have kept committing capital to the town [^5,6]. All of that creates storage demand of the ordinary kind (households with a full garage, retirees downsizing, small businesses with stock) and a seasonal commercial layer.

The supply side is where a climate-controlled project would find its opening. The census behind this page confirmed operators on their own websites. Only Heartland Self Storage and Bluegrass Storage advertise climate control [^19,20]. The largest local multi-site operator describes itself as an outdoor operation [^21]. The research found many more operators with Nelson County addresses in directories, almost all of them selling drive-up space. A climate-controlled building would not be the first in town, but it would join a very short list.

What the research could not find matters as much. No operator in the census posts a verified rate. No competitor's occupancy, unit count or assessed tax bill was found. No planned or permitted storage project turned up for 2025 or 2026, and absence in a search is not absence in the field. The zoning that makes the site easy to entitle also lets a competitor arrive without a public hearing, as the site section explains [^8].

Those gaps shape the loan. With rents set as labelled placeholders, the model produces net operating income of {{case:v.noi_y3}} in Year 3 on a project that costs {{case:v.tpc}}. The sponsor proposed {{case:v.equity_proposed_pct}} equity and a {{case:v.loan_total_proposed}} guaranteed loan; that structure covers Year 3 debt service only {{case:v.dscr_y3_proposed}}, against a {{case:v.floor}} floor, and it falls short of the equity test as well. The engine restructures it to {{case:v.equity_pct}} equity and a {{case:v.loan_total}} loan, which reaches {{case:v.dscr_y3}} in Year 3. The finding is not that Bardstown cannot hold a new storage facility. It is that, at these costs and these placeholder rents, the market carries a modest loan, and a rent survey that comes back higher is the fastest way to a larger one.

Residents and households. Nelson County is growing faster than Kentucky as a whole: its population is up from 46,732 in April 2020 to 49,036 in July 2025, while the state moved from 4,506,287 to 4,606,864 [^1]. The five-year survey for 2020 to 2024 counts 19,150 households in 20,330 housing units, and a median age of 39.5 years against 39.1 for the state [^2]. Storage demand follows households, and a county of this size with this many homes has room for the garage-overflow customer who rents the first unit. The register's tenure and age counts, set out in the second table below, show how many of those homes are owner-occupied and how many residents are 65 or older.

A settled population. The survey puts the share of residents who moved in the past year at 9.7% in Nelson County and 12.5% statewide [^2]. Low mobility cuts both ways for a storage owner. It means fewer move-in rentals of the kind that fill a facility in a college town or a military market. It also means the tenants who do rent may stay long, because their belongings come from an estate, a downsizing or a project that will take a year, and not from a short assignment. The research found no tenure data for any Bardstown facility, so the model does not claim a stay length; it assumes only a normal ramp to the Year 3 occupancy shown in the inputs.

Income and housing cost. Median household income in the county is $69,562, against $63,726 for Kentucky, and the median owner-occupied home is valued at $238,000, against $205,600 [^2]. Residents who own a house worth that much have things to store and the means to rent space for them. The same survey puts the mean commute at 26.6 minutes, against 24.0 for the state [^2]. A longer commute is a small sign that many residents work outside the county, and it raises the value of a facility on the way home.

Employers and the bourbon economy. A 2022 profile counted 2,612 manufacturing jobs at 59 establishments and 2,610 retail jobs in the county [^3]. It named American Fuji Seal, Tower International, Heaven Hill, Sazerac and Toyota Boshoku Kentucky as the major employers [^3]. Heaven Hill's new distillery, announced at a groundbreaking on June 6, 2022, carried a $135 million investment, 38 full-time jobs and an average wage of $33.62 an hour including benefits [^5]. Bardstown Bourbon Company announced a $28.7 million expansion that spring on the same footing, adding 29 jobs and 55,000 barrels of annual capacity [^6]. These are industrial wages, and employees on them rent storage for boats, seasonal gear and second vehicles.

Visitors. Visit Bardstown, citing the Kentucky Department of Tourism, put the county's 2024 tourism impact at $90.9 million, of which $62.6 million was direct visitor spending, supporting 450 tourism jobs directly and 623 in all [^4]. A trade publication counted 11 distilleries within 16 miles of the court square in April 2025, when the town was preparing for its second National Bourbon Week [^7]. Visitors do not rent storage. The businesses that serve them do: event suppliers, bourbon retailers, restaurateurs and the vendors who work the festival weeks. The model gives that commercial layer no revenue of its own and leaves it as upside.

What the research did not tie to Bardstown. Elizabethtown and Fort Knox lie in the adjacent region, and the research recorded one local operator naming Fort Knox among the areas it serves. The research found no figure that links the installation to Bardstown storage demand, so the model gives it no weight. It found no radius or drive-time demographics either, which is why the tables use the county as the reporting geography. Defining a trade area by drive time from the actual site is the first task of a full study.

Operators found only in directories, chamber listings or review sites are not in the table, because a directory entry can be out of date and a review site is not an operator's own statement. Their existence is a lender condition to confirm, not a fact this page relies on.

An earlier search snippet had shown drive-up rates for one operator, but the page behind it did not reproduce them when the research rechecked it, and its home page shows none today [^23]. What the table shows. Most of the confirmed operators sell only drive-up space, and Heartland and Bluegrass are the only ones that advertise climate control. Heartland describes its Bardstown facility as one of the town's largest for climate-controlled space [^19]. Bluegrass shows climate control on its own page but gives no sizes [^20]. For a lender, that is a thin but real competitive set: a short list of climate-controlled alternatives for a county of this size [^1].

What the table cannot show. The research found no unit counts, rentable square footage, land areas, assessed taxes or occupancy for any competitor. The competitor size that would matter most to the model, the climate-controlled square footage already in the county, is unknown. If Heartland and Bluegrass hold between them many times the subject's {{case:v.in_nrsf_cc}} square feet, the Year 3 occupancy ramp is optimistic, and if they hold far less, it is cautious. {{case:loan}}

The guarantee. For fiscal 2026 the Business and Industry program guarantees 85% of a loan under $5,000,000 and 80% of a loan from $5,000,000 to $25,000,000, with an initial fee of 3.0%, an annual retention fee of 0.55% and a 0.50% fee for a guarantee issued before construction is complete [^24]. A reduced-fee category charges 1.0% and 0.50% but guarantees only 80% [^24]. The 85% tier replaced 80% in a notice published on March 9, 2026, effective October 1, 2025, and USDA announced it on March 27 [^24,28]. A loan of {{case:v.loan_total}} is well inside the 85% tier.

Eligibility. USDA's December 2021 rule removed the old ineligibility of self-storage facilities, so a storage project is no longer excluded by type [^33]. Kentucky's program page lists the purchase and development of land, buildings and infrastructure for commercial properties among eligible uses and requires hazard insurance on collateral [^34]. The project site must be in a rural area, which the program defines as outside a city or town of more than 50,000 people, with the project site governing [^29,28]. The research did not retrieve the eligibility map result for any Bardstown parcel, so a determination for the actual site is a lender condition.

Rate and term. The lender and the borrower negotiate the rate, fixed or variable, and the term runs to the useful life of the collateral, up to 40 years or the state-statute limit, whichever is less [^26]. The model carries {{case:v.rate}}, which is Prime of 7.00% on October 9, 2026 [^35] plus 0.75 points, and amortizes over {{case:v.in_bi_amort_years}} because roofs, paving and mechanical systems wear out well before 40 years.

What the terms sheet leaves open. The USDA terms in the research print no equity minimum and no coverage ratio for B&I, and no separate term for real estate. The study therefore tests two lender-style parameters and labels both as assumptions: {{case:v.equity_req_pct}} equity and a {{case:v.floor}} coverage floor in Year 3. The regulation asks for projections through at least two years at stable operations [^25], which is why Year 3, the first stabilized year, is the test year. The lender's credit evaluation must include a written evaluation of the feasibility study [^27].

The feasibility study. A guaranteed loan of more than $1,000,000 to a new business requires a study prepared by an independent qualified consultant acceptable to USDA [^25]. The restructured loan of {{case:v.loan_total}} is over that line, and for a loan of $1,000,000 or less USDA may still require one if the analysis is insufficient [^25]. This page shows the market and financial sections that such a study contains; USDA sets the scope case by case.

The program's own risks. The fiscal 2027 budget request proposes no Business and Industry program level [^29]. In May 2026 USDA revoked the approved-lender status of ten lenders whose portfolios held about $620 million in delinquent loans, roughly 47% of Rural Development's delinquent loans, and said more than 750 lenders remained [^30]. A trade-association study reported that Kentucky received $470 million in guaranteed volume across 74 loans from 2012 to 2022 [^31], and a $9.95 million guaranteed loan for an 81-room hotel in Campbellsville was announced in October 2026 [^32]. The research found no Nelson County closing from 2024 to 2026, and the city's industrial development board lists local banks without any recorded Business and Industry activity [^12]. Finding a lender that is both approved and active is a task of the engagement, not a given.

  1. Unverified rents

    No Bardstown operator posts a rate on its own site [^19,20,21,22,23]. The model's climate-controlled rent is a placeholder, and the determination swings with it. The survey is not optional.

  2. Easy entry for competitors

    Mini-warehouses are a principal permitted use in the B-3 district [^8], so a rival can file a site plan and build without a hearing. The research found no storage project in the pipeline, but it did not search the planning agendas in full.

  3. A short list of climate-controlled rivals

    Only Heartland and Bluegrass advertise climate control [^19,20]. If either expands, or a national operator from the next ring enters Bardstown, the scarcity that supports the premium rent disappears.

  4. Demand tied to one industry

    Tourism produced $90.9 million of impact in 2024 [^4], and the major employers named in 2022 center on distilling and its suppliers [^3]. A downturn in bourbon would reach commercial storage demand and household income at once.

  5. Construction cost

    The research found no local cost index, and a metal building with heating, cooling and dehumidification is a mechanical project as well as a shell. A cost overrun comes straight out of the loan capacity shown here.

  6. Wind and insurance

    Insurance terms were not found. A light metal building in central Kentucky needs wind cover, and a premium far above the placeholder would cut net income directly.

  7. Program funding and lender base

    The fiscal 2027 request proposes no Business and Industry program level [^29], and ten lenders lost approved status in May 2026 [^30]. A delayed appropriation or a lender exit can stall the guarantee after the land is bought.

  8. Tax district

    The city schools' real property rate is 79.10 cents per $100 and the county schools' is 67.0 cents [^14,16]. The district for the actual parcel changes the tax line.
