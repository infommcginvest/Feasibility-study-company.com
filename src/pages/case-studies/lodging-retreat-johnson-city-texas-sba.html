The subject is hypothetical: a Hill Country retreat of {{case:v.in_cabins}} guest cabins and a lodge with a meeting hall on {{case:v.in_site_acres}} acres with US 290 frontage west of Johnson City, financed with an SBA 7(a) start-up loan. The market evidence is real and dated. Inputs the research could not source are labelled as assumptions in the model.

{{case:glance}}

The Johnson City trade area supports a small retreat, but not a 90% loan. Demand is real and priced high on weekends: for the week of October 12, 2026, Carter Creek's lowest nightly prices ran $137 on Monday, $129 on Tuesday, $120 on Wednesday and $144 on Thursday, then $263 on Friday and $293 on Saturday [^20]. Friday and Saturday more than double midweek. The resort's most direct guest review on Tripadvisor is titled "Don't stay on weekdays!" [^22]. That is the market in one line: a weekend wine-trail destination that has not yet built a weekday business.

The organized supply is small. Four operators run 160 keys: Carter Creek's 78 villas, the Best Western's 53 rooms, the Presidio's 14 rooms and Walden's 15 safari tents [^18,23,29,27]. No new lodging was found under construction in the trade area. The 2026 change was a repositioning, when a Houston boot company bought the 12-acre wine castle property and reopened its 14-room hotel as the Presidio in June 2026 [^30,29]. The real supply wave is in the next ring, around Fredericksburg and Lake Travis, and it is luxury.

A retreat's case rests on filling midweek nights with groups: company offsites, wedding parties and small conferences that book the whole property. The model builds that business slowly. On that basis the retreat earns {{case:v.noi_y2}} of net operating income in Year 2 and {{case:v.noi_y3}} in Year 3.

The sponsor asked for the start-up minimum: {{case:v.equity_proposed_pct}} equity and a {{case:v.loan_total_proposed}} 7(a) loan. Year 2 coverage on that loan is {{case:v.dscr_y2_proposed}}. The loan the market supports is {{case:v.loan_total}}, with {{case:v.equity_pct}} equity ({{case:v.equity}}), which reaches {{case:v.dscr_y2}} in Year 2 and {{case:v.dscr_y3}} in Year 3. The gap is driven by three facts: a 40-acre tract on the corridor asks $3,195,000 to $3,995,000 on two listing search pages [^6,7], small cabins cost more per guest than hotel rooms, and the SBA test falls in Year 2, before a new retreat's group calendar has filled.

Johnson City's demand base changed in two years. The town's historic anchor was the Lyndon B. Johnson National Historical Park. Visits there fell from 119,278 in 2024 to just over 38,000 in 2025, a drop of nearly 70%, according to NPS data reported by the Houston Chronicle, and the Texas White House has been closed to tours since August 2, 2018 [^33,34]. A lodging plan built on park visitors would be built on a number that no longer exists.

The wine corridor took its place. Fredericksburg, west along US 290, counts more than 75 wineries, vineyards and tasting rooms [^29]. A land broker on the corridor claims more than 60 wineries along the highway itself [^6]; that is a listing claim, not a count. The Hill Country's luxury wave confirms the demand: Forbes listed a 210-room Kimpton in Fredericksburg targeting late 2027, a Waldorf Astoria with 60 rooms, 42 resort villas and 50 residences on 106 acres, a 41-room boutique hotel approved at a Fredericksburg winery, and Canyon Ranch's 141-room resort near Lake Travis taking stays from October 15, 2026 [^29]. Developers do not commit that capital to a region without weekend demand.

The local economy is small, affluent and older. Blanco County had 13,581 residents on July 1, 2025, up 19.4% from 11,378 in April 2020, against 8.8% growth for Texas [^1]. Median household income was $92,425 in the 2020 to 2024 survey, 17.8% above the Texas median of $78,476 [^1,3]. The median age was 51.5 against 35.6 for the state [^2]. The county has 709.25 square miles of land and 16.0 people per square mile [^1]; the 2020 Census counted it as 0.00% urban [^5]. Johnson City itself had 1,627 residents in 2020 and about 2,200 by the city's 2026 estimate, and the city calls itself "mainly a tourist center" [^9]. Its net taxable value rose from $155.19 million in 2017 to $433.00 million in 2026 [^9].

Local residents do not fill cabins, so these figures matter for staffing and for the event business, not for room nights. The county had 5.62 thousand employed residents in 2024, led by construction (809), professional and technical services (595) and manufacturing (553) [^4]. Housekeeping and event staff will be hard to hire in a county that size, which is why the model holds payroll as a fixed cost.

Blanco County lies just west of the Austin metro, not inside it. Its guests come from Austin, San Antonio and Houston, and its competitors include Austin-area retreats. Our Austin metro page covers the region the guests drive from, and the Texas market page places the Hill Country in the state picture.

The next ring adds hundreds of luxury keys aimed at the same drive-in guest: the Albert Hotel's 105 rooms opened in Fredericksburg in 2025, and the Kimpton, the Waldorf Astoria and a 41-room winery hotel are approved or under development there [^29]. Those projects confirm the region's pull and take the top of the market. A 14-cabin retreat cannot compete with Canyon Ranch's spa or a Kimpton's event space. It competes on price, privacy and the whole-property buyout, which a large resort cannot offer a 20-person company offsite. That is a narrow position, and it is the one the model prices.

The hypothetical site is {{case:v.in_site_acres}} acres with US 290 frontage west of Johnson City, about five minutes from town. The land price comes from a 40-acre unrestricted US 290 tract listed in October 2026, which the listing services price differently: town-level search pages show $3,995,000 on one page and $3,195,000 on another [^7,6], and a third listing site, not cited here so that no real parcel is identified, shows a higher ask. The conflict cannot be resolved from the record. {{case:loan}}

Under SOP 50 10 8.1 with Technical Policy Updates, effective October 1, 2026, a 7(a) start-up must inject at least 10% equity and reach coverage of 1.15x, on the basis that historical and/or projected, with 1:1 global coverage; start-ups reach 1.15x within two years [^43]. The model therefore tests Year 2. The loan cannot exceed $5 million [^43]; the sponsor's request was below that, so the cap did not bind. SBA guarantees 75% of a loan between $350,001 and $5 million [^46]. Because real estate is most of the project, the loan runs 25 years.

The rate is an assumption at {{case:v.rate}}. Prime was 7.00% on October 9, 2026 [^45], and SBA caps the spread a lender may charge over Prime for a loan of this size [^43]. Bed-and-breakfast 7(a) loans averaged 9.49% across 26 loans and $35.1 million in 2025 [^47]. The model carries Prime plus 2.5 points, and the lender sets the rate at pricing. The fiscal 2027 upfront guaranty fee applies to the guaranteed share under the notice in force [^44].

The lodging rule. A lodging borrower must show more than 50% of the prior year's revenue from transients who stay 30 days or less at a time; a start-up shows it in projections [^43]. A retreat whose guests stay two or three nights passes easily, and its projection should say so in a line of its own. The test bites only if the sponsor later leases cabins by the month to workers or long-stay tenants.

Ownership. Since March 1, 2026, every direct and indirect owner and every SBA-required guarantor must be a U.S. citizen or national; Policy Notice 5000-876441 rescinded the earlier notice that had allowed a small foreign-owned share, and lawful permanent residents no longer qualify [^49]. The sponsor's ownership documents should be checked before any credit work.

Why not a 504. A 504 would split the project between a bank lien and a fixed-rate debenture. For a new business in a lodging property, which SBA treats as special purpose, the 504 contribution is 20% [^43]. The 504's lower blended rate would lift coverage, but the restructured equity here is far above either program's minimum, so the binding constraint is the market, not the program. Our SBA 7(a) and 504 practice page compares the two. On the study itself: 13 CFR 120.160(b): SBA may require professional appraisals, a survey, or a feasibility study [^48]. For a start-up lodging project with no history, the lender will usually want one.
