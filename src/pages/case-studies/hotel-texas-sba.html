The subject is hypothetical: a branded limited-service hotel of {{case:v.in_keys}} keys on a {{case:v.in_site_acres}}-acre pad with SH 71 frontage in Bastrop, financed with an SBA 504 loan. The market evidence is real and dated. Every model input the research could not source is labelled as an assumption in the inputs table further down.

{{case:glance}}

Bastrop can absorb another branded limited-service hotel, but not at the leverage the sponsor requested. The city had 13,383 residents on July 1, 2025, against 12,720 a year earlier, and has grown 38.2% since April 2020 [^1]. The county reached 118,908 [^1]. The Starlink campus on FM 1209 employs more than 1,000 people, and permit filings describe a 1.1 million square foot factory with up to 500 more jobs [^41]. When Everhome opened its 114 suites on February 18, 2025, the opening was tied to demand from SpaceX and The Boring Co. [^25,24]. On the night of Wednesday, July 22, 2026, Choice's own booking page showed that hotel sold out [^26].

The same evidence carries the warning. Branded supply in the city jumped in 2025: Everhome and Home2 Suites added 228 extended-stay keys between February and July [^24,37]. Four more branded hotels are in the state record or under construction, with completion dates from December 25, 2026 to July 3, 2028 [^16,19,20,21]. A hotel that closes its loan in 2027 opens into the middle of that wave. Its first two years are the years in which the new Marriott and IHG product competes for the same Starlink contractors, wedding blocks and SH 71 travelers.

The model therefore ramps occupancy slowly and prices rooms below the national average. On that basis the hotel earns {{case:v.noi_y2}} of net operating income in Year 2 and {{case:v.noi_y3}} in Year 3. The sponsor asked for a 504 structure with {{case:v.equity_proposed_pct}} equity. That fails twice: the equity tier for a new business in a special purpose property is 20%, and coverage in the Year 2 test comes to {{case:v.dscr_y2_proposed}}. The structure that clears the 1.15x floor carries {{case:v.equity_pct}} equity ({{case:v.equity}}), a {{case:v.tpl}} bank first lien and a {{case:v.debenture}} debenture, for {{case:v.loan_total}} of debt. Coverage then reaches {{case:v.dscr_y2}} in Year 2 and {{case:v.dscr_y3}} in Year 3.

That is a financeable hotel, but it is a different deal from the one proposed. Three facts would move the answer, and each is a lender condition below: an STR trend report for the named competitive set, a land price set by appraisal rather than by an asking price, and the adopted city tax rate. A trend report that shows the set running above our occupancy and rate would release equity. One that shows the 2025 openings diluting the older hotels would not.

Hotel demand in Bastrop comes from four sources, and the public record measures none of them in room nights. That is the central limit of this study. The research found no hotel occupancy tax receipts series, no state park visitation for the town's park, no hospital volumes and no seasonality data. What it did find are the drivers' sizes and their direction.

Industrial and contractor travel. Starlink is the anchor. Texas awarded Starlink a $17.3 million Semiconductor Innovation Fund grant for its Bastrop manufacturing in March 2025 [^42]. Local reporting in September 2025 described an expansion of 1 million square feet over three years, with an $8 million phase starting that month [^43]. The July 2026 coverage of the newest hotel filing listed a $440 million data center and a film studio among the other projects drawing workers to town [^21]. These are weekday travelers: engineers, vendors and construction crews. They book branded limited-service and extended-stay rooms, and they are the demand that justified Everhome and Home2.

Leisure and events. Bastrop's leisure base is older and concentrated. From 2007 to 2015 the Hyatt Regency Lost Pines produced more than 75% of the city's hotel occupancy tax revenue, and a marketing agreement of about $900,000 a year was funded from that tax [^44]. A resort that dominant spills weddings, tournaments and golf groups into the limited-service hotels on SH 71 when it fills. The destination office now markets weekend getaways, business meetings, filming and festivals [^21], but the record gives no event attendance.

Highway travel. SH 71 is the town's through route, and Bastrop has no interstate. TxDOT publishes station counts [^4], but no count for the subject's segment was extracted, so highway demand is a lender condition rather than a number.

Planned public projects. The city contracted Hotel & Leisure Advisors on December 9, 2025 to study a hotel and convention center [^7], and a designer has floated a west-side hospital, hotel and convention concept that it says could reach about $1.6 billion [^45]. Both are at the idea stage. Neither belongs in a projection, and a city-sponsored convention hotel would be a competitor, not a feeder.

Two demographic facts shape the product. Median household income in the city was $81,551 and per capita income $41,634 in the 2020 to 2024 survey [^2], which supports midscale and upper-midscale rates for leisure guests. The city's accommodation and food services sales were $102,439,000 in the 2022 Economic Census, against retail sales of $97,973 per resident [^2]. Bastrop is a regional shopping and services town, which adds weekday traffic from the county's 118,908 residents [^1].

{{case:loan}}

The 504 program splits a project three ways. Under SOP 50 10 8.1 with Technical Policy Updates, effective October 1, 2026, the standard split is Third Party Lender 50%, debenture 40%, borrower 10%; debenture 35% at a 15% contribution and 30% at 20% [^62]. The borrower's share rises with risk. It is 10% for an established business in a general-purpose building, 15% for a new business or a special purpose property, and 20% when both apply [^62]. Hotels are on SBA's special purpose property list, and a borrower formed to build and run a new hotel is a new business. The sponsor's {{case:v.equity_proposed_pct}} treats the hotel as one or the other, not both. Our note on special purpose property risk explains why the program asks more of single-use buildings.

The debenture is capped at $5 million for a non-manufacturer [^62]. At the sponsor's {{case:v.equity_proposed_pct}}, the debenture came to {{case:v.debenture_proposed}}, just inside the limit. A slightly larger hotel would have hit it. Combined 7(a) and 504 exposure can now reach $10 million [^62], which gives room for a later renovation loan but does not change this test. The upfront 504 fee for fiscal 2027 is 0.50% of the debenture, financed [^63].

Coverage for a 504 is 1.15x [^62]. A new hotel has no history, so the test runs on projections, and this case tests Year 2. The bank rate is an assumption at {{case:v.in_tpl_rate}}, set from Prime of 7.00% on October 9, 2026 [^64] and broker-reported 2026 hotel pricing. The debenture rate is the 25-year effective rate of {{case:v.in_debenture_rate}} from the October 8, 2026 pricing [^61]. Both are set at pricing, and both are conditions below.

Two eligibility rules come before the credit analysis. First, lodging borrowers must earn more than 50% of the prior year's revenue from transients who stay 30 days or less at a time; a start-up shows it in projections [^62]. The subject's transient positioning meets that by design, and its projection should show it. Second, since March 1, 2026, all direct and indirect owners and SBA-required guarantors must be U.S. citizens or U.S. nationals with a principal U.S. residence, and entity owners must be organized in the United States (Procedural Notice 5000-876626) [^48]. If any owner is a lawful permanent resident rather than a citizen or national, the borrower is ineligible under the current rule, so the check comes first.

On the study itself: 13 CFR 120.160(b): SBA may require professional appraisals, a survey, or a feasibility study [^65]. Lenders and CDCs often ask for one on a ground-up hotel. Our note on the SBA feasibility myth separates the rule from the practice, and the SBA 7(a) and 504 practice page describes what our studies cover for both programs.

{{case:breakeven}}

In Year 3 the restructured hotel covers its debt service until occupancy falls to {{case:v.breakeven_value}}, a cushion of {{case:v.cushion_pct}} below the underwritten level. It meets the {{case:v.floor}} floor down to {{case:v.breakeven_floor_value}}. Both figures sit close to the national average occupancy of 2025, which means the cushion is real but not wide.

{{case:sensitivity}}

The matrix moves occupancy and rate together across the range the pipeline makes plausible. Coverage in Year 3 runs from {{case:v.sens_min}} in the worst cell to {{case:v.sens_max}} in the best. The row and column that matter are the downside ones: a 7.5% cut in occupancy with a 5% cut in rate is a fair description of what a new Courtyard and a new Fairfield could do to an unbranded or weaker-flagged newcomer in its first stabilized year.

  1. Opening into the pipeline

    The Courtyard is scheduled to finish December 25, 2026, the Sendero dual-brand in early 2027, the Residence Inn in November 2027 and the Fairfield and Staybridge on July 3, 2028 [^16,19,20,21]. The subject's ramp years coincide with all four. The key counts of three of them are unknown, so the supply shock cannot yet be sized.

  2. One employer drives weekday demand

    Starlink and SpaceX are the reason two hotels opened in 2025 [^25]. A pause in expansion, a shift of work to another campus or a slowdown in data center construction would hit the weekday base that the model counts on.

  3. Rate erosion from new product

    New hotels open with introductory rates and loyalty promotions. Midscale hotels lost 5.7% of gross operating profit in 2025 while their property taxes rose 12.3% [^50]. A rate war in a small market reaches coverage faster than an occupancy dip.

  4. Site entitlement under a new code

    The 2026 development code and zoning map replaced the old code [^5]. If the pad needs a zoning change and the commission recommends denial, council approval needs a supermajority. Entitlement delay pushes the opening further into the pipeline.

  5. Flood, drainage and wildfire

    The 2018 to 2019 drainage moratorium and the 2011 county fire [^66,67] point to insurance cost and availability risk that the model carries only as an allowance.

  6. Ownership eligibility

    The March 1, 2026 citizenship rule makes any owner who is not a U.S. citizen or national an eligibility failure [^48]. Hotel ownership groups should be checked before the credit work starts.

  7. Tax growth

    The county's rate rose 6.22% for 2026-27 [^12], and the city's adopted rate is unconfirmed. A new hotel is assessed near cost, so tax is a large, fixed line.
