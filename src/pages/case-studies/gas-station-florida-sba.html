The project is a hypothetical one: a new, unbranded fuel and convenience store selling gasoline and auto-lane diesel on about two acres of US 27 frontage south of the SR 60 interchange. The sponsor, the site and the store do not exist. The traffic record, the competitors, the tax rates and the cost benchmarks do, and each one is dated in the sources at the foot of the page.

{{case:glance}}

Lake Wales has more traffic and more growth ahead of it than most towns its size. The city counted 18,650 residents in July 2025, 16.1% more than its April 2020 estimates base [^1]. In March 2024 its commission had approved more than 15,660 new homes, expected to house 42,000 people [^6]. Traffic at the US 27 and SR 60 junction had already risen by 3,000 to 5,000 vehicles a day over the four years to 2021 [^4], and FDOT projects 42,800 to 52,800 vehicles a day on the US 27 segment south of SR 60 by 2040 [^3]. None of that is the problem.

The problem is who already sells to that traffic. A Love's travel stop holds the US 27 node at Hunt Brothers Road [^15]. RaceTrac operates two stores, one on each highway [^18]. Murphy USA sells gasoline at the Walmart Supercenter on SR 60 [^19]. Directories add a Wawa, a Shell, a Gate, a Circle K and half a dozen independents. Price leaders set the street price, and the largest-format operators set the food and convenience standard. A new unbranded store gets the traffic its location and price can win, not the traffic that passes it.

The model reflects that. Gasoline volume stabilizes below the national store average, the margin sits just above Murphy USA's reported figure, and inside sales sit below an older Yesway store, because Lake Wales households earn well under the Florida median. On those assumptions the store earns {{case:v.noi_y3}} a year once settled. Against a {{case:v.loan_total_proposed}} loan at the sponsor's {{case:v.equity_proposed_pct}} equity, coverage is {{case:v.dscr_y2_proposed}} in the Year 2 test. The engine finds that the store supports {{case:v.loan_total}} of debt, which leaves the sponsor to fund {{case:v.equity_pct}} of cost.

That is not a financing problem a lender can structure around. It is a market finding: this format, on this corridor, does not earn enough to justify new construction with SBA leverage. The page sets out the evidence in our gas station and convenience store practice's format, and the statewide picture is on our Florida page. A sponsor who wants to be on US 27 should look at buying an existing station, smaller construction or a different node, each of which needs its own test. The case is modelled as new construction rather than the purchase of the older station the research found listed, for reasons the site section explains.

Three kinds of demand meet in Lake Wales: through-traffic on two state highways, a resident base that is older and lower-income than Florida as a whole, and a building pipeline that is converting groves into subdivisions and industrial land. Each pulls fuel demand in a different direction.

A regional shopping town with a modest household base

The figure that stands out is retail sales: $972,416,000 in 2022, or $58,863 for every resident [^1]. Lake Wales sells far more than its own population buys, because the Walmart and the SR 60 shopping corridor serve a wide rural area east of the Ridge. Fuel follows that pattern. A store on the way to the shopping corridor sees shoppers from across the county, but they are already passing the Murphy USA in the Walmart lot and the RaceTrac on SR 60.

Household income is the constraint inside the store. The median household earns $56,347 against $74,568 statewide, and 22.4% of residents live below the poverty line against 12.6% for Florida [^1,2]. More than a quarter of residents are 65 or older [^1]. Price-sensitive customers buy fuel where it is cheapest and buy fewer prepared-food baskets; that is why the model's inside sales sit below the older-store benchmark rather than above it.

Short commutes, local trips

Lake Wales residents average 23.9 minutes to work, against 28.1 minutes for Floridians as a whole [^1,2]. Shorter commutes mean more of the town's driving stays local, which favors a store on a local route over one that waits for long-distance traffic. It also means fewer miles per household and fewer fill-ups than a commuter town of the same size, a modest drag on per-capita fuel demand that the model's below-average volume already absorbs. Accommodation and food services sales of $65,025,000 in 2022 [^1] show a visitor and dining economy large enough to feed a store's food counter, but the chain stores on SR 60 are positioned to take most of it.

Groves becoming subdivisions and warehouses

The building pipeline is real but slow. The 15,660 approved homes [^6] are entitlements, not rooftops, and the March 2024 article that reports them gives no delivery schedule. Peace Crossing alone carries 6,100 units plus highway commercial land west of US 27 [^6], which means the subject's future competitors may be built inside the same subdivisions that add its customers. On Hunt Brothers Road, 173 acres of former groves are moving toward industrial zoning, with 183 acres to be annexed [^7], and landowners along the road have agreed to ongoing annexation [^8]. Not every proposal advances: a 297-acre annexation north of SR 60 drew a staff recommendation of denial, and the owners withdrew their map amendments in 2025 [^9]. The model does not count pipeline households; it treats them as the reason the market can absorb competitors over time, not as volume for this store.

Freight and tourism

US 27 is a freight route. The FDOT project page reports truck counts and daily volumes for this segment but gives no year for them, so the study does not use them; it does use the department's projection of 42,800 to 52,800 vehicles a day by 2040 [^3], which signals where the corridor is heading. Transportation and warehousing firms in the city took in $63,125,000 in 2022 [^1]. Tractor-trailer fuel, however, already has a home at the Love's. Tourism adds a seasonal layer: Bok Tower Gardens has welcomed more than 23 million visitors since 1929 [^10] and drew 8,200 people to one autumn plant sale in 2025 [^11]. The older population and the Ridge's winter residents point to a winter peak, but no monthly fuel data was found.

Florida had 9,730 convenience stores at the end of 2025 [^14]. Lake Wales is not short of them. Our note on underserved gas station markets describes the opposite kind of town, where the gap is in supply; this is a town where the gap, if any, is in price and format.

The research named 14 fuel sellers on the Lake Wales corridors. Four have an operator page that confirms them. All four are national chains, and together they cover both highways and both kinds of customer, car and truck.

A truck stop that keeps growing

Love's opened its Lake Wales store on August 29, 2019 with 68 truck spaces, 71 car spaces and 7 diesel bays [^16]. Its own page now lists 83 truck spaces [^15], so the site has added parking since opening; the operator's current figure controls where the two disagree. A truck stop that adds capacity on a freight corridor is telling the market where the professional drivers stop. That is the strongest reason the subject's diesel is modelled as a pickup and farm-truck business.

What the chains mean for a new store

Each confirmed competitor does one thing the subject cannot match. Love's serves the professional driver with parking, showers and diesel lanes; the subject's diesel is for pickups. RaceTrac runs large-format stores on both highways with the fuel prices and food program of a chain. Murphy USA prices gasoline to bring Walmart shoppers to the pump: its owner-posted regular price of $2.96 in a 2026 capture [^20] sat below the $3.07 a user reported at a Shell on SR 60 [^21]. Those GasBuddy captures look low next to EIA's national average of $4.35 for September 2026 [^27], so they are probably older than the autumn price spike; a dated local price survey is a condition.

For the margin, Murphy's own number is the clearest benchmark. Its 2025 retail fuel margin was 28.1 cents a gallon [^32], against 39.7 cents for the national gasoline average in 2024 [^28]. A station that sits near a Murphy and two RaceTracs earns closer to Murphy's figure than to the national one, because the price signs are visible from the road.

Listings the study could not confirm

Ten more stations appear in directories without an operator page or a Polk County Property Appraiser record behind them, so they stay out of the table. A Wawa at 23623 US 27 sells regular, midgrade, premium, diesel and propane and carries 319 GasBuddy ratings averaging 4.9 of 5 [^21], which makes it the busiest-looking store on the corridor even without operator confirmation. A Shell at 1349 SR 60 East, a Gate with a car wash at 2950 Ridge Way, a Sunoco at 19300 US 27, a CITGO Food Mart at 20500 US 27, a Circle K at 14581 US 27, a CITGO at 800 North Scenic Highway, a Chevron at 900 SR 60, a Marathon truck stop at 14907 SR 60 East and an unbranded station at 4 South Scenic Highway complete the list. No new fuel station was found in planning or under construction in 2025 or 2026. Confirming these stations, their fueling positions and their opening years is a lender condition.

Where an unbranded store fits

An unbranded or independent store buys fuel without a refiner's brand and competes on price and convenience. On a corridor like this one it is up against chains that do both at scale. The highway fuel format works where a store owns a node; here the nodes are taken. That is the market finding behind the determination.

The subject is a hypothetical tract of about two acres with US 27 frontage south of the SR 60 interchange, and the case tests a ground-up start-up. That is a deliberate departure from the research. The only fuel property the research found listed on this stretch is an operating older station and truck stop, and an acquisition case would test it on its trailing earnings under the change-of-ownership rules. No public source gives those earnings, and the property's listings disagree with each other on its acreage, its asking price and its building size [^47]. An acquisition model built on that record would rest on invented history. The question this case can answer from public evidence is the one a sponsor faces before buying or building on the corridor: can a new store earn its debt here? The acquisition route is shown in our Opelika, Alabama case.

Access and the interchange

FDOT and the city rebuilt the US 27 and SR 60 junction as a single-point interchange, a $45 million project scheduled for completion in early 2025 [^5]. A rebuilt interchange changes how drivers reach roadside parcels: right-in, right-out access on a divided highway rewards sites on the side of the road that matches the commute direction. Which side the subject sits on, and whether it has a median opening, is a site question no listing answers.

{{case:loan}}

The governing rules are SOP 50 10 8.1 with Technical Policy Updates, effective October 1, 2026 [^40]. The sponsor's request meets the start-up injection of 10% exactly. Coverage on the projections has two years to reach 1.15x, so the store is tested in Year 2. Real estate is more than half of the proceeds, so the loan carries a 25-year term. The rate is Prime of 7.00% [^42] plus the maximum spread for the loan size, {{case:v.rate}}; the lender sets the actual rate at pricing. SBA guarantees {{case:v.in_sba_guaranty_pct}} of loans of this size [^39].

The FY2027 fee schedule waives the upfront fee for loans of up to $700,000 to businesses in a rural area, defined as a county that the U.S. Census Bureau has defined as at least 30 percent rural [^41]. Whether Polk County meets that test was not verified, and the proposed loan is far above the cap in any case, so the model charges the fee.

A fuel store raises three program points. Gas stations are on SBA's special purpose list [^40], which shapes the appraisal and, under 504, the equity tier. A brand or supply agreement under the Petroleum Marketing Practices Act counts as a franchise [^45]; an unbranded store usually has a jobber supply contract, and that contract goes into the file too. And every owner must have been a U.S. citizen or national since March 1, 2026, after SBA rescinded the short-lived 5% foreign-ownership allowance [^44]. The wider rule set is summarized on the SBA 7(a) and 504 practice page. Under 13 CFR 120.160(b): SBA may require professional appraisals, a survey, or a feasibility study [^43], and on a case like this one the study's value is in saying no early.

{{case:inputs}}

Ten inputs are taken from a source: the two EIA price forecasts, the diesel margin and the wage, the card fee per gallon, the two industry card-fee totals, the city millage and the non-ad valorem charge, plus the SBA guaranty share. Land, construction, volumes, the gasoline margin, inside sales and margin, staffing and every operating allowance are assumptions, each with its benchmark named. The three to verify first are the land price, the gasoline margin and gasoline volume.

  1. A price war on SR 60 and US 27

    Murphy USA's 28.1-cent margin [^32] is the low-price benchmark, and the subject sits just above it. If the price leaders cut, the subject follows or loses volume. The break-even margin leaves little room.

  2. Chain food programs

    RaceTrac and the unconfirmed Wawa sell food at a scale an unbranded store cannot match. Inside sales could settle below the model's level.

  3. Jurisdiction and zoning

    Whether the parcel is in the city or the county was not found, and neither zoning district nor use permission was confirmed. An approval path that adds a public hearing adds months.

  4. Wind, flood and insurance

    The storm history is documented [^12]; the flood zone is not. Insurance cost and availability can change a thin store's income by more than its margin assumptions do.

  5. Septic and site work

    An onsite wastewater system on a fuel site needs space, permits and maintenance. If central sewer is unavailable and the soil does not take a system of the needed size, the site does not work.

  6. Tank history

    Older fuel corridors carry leaking-tank histories, with median cleanups of $27,120 to $265,883 per site in the 2022 study [^37]. A Phase I that finds a recognized environmental condition changes the cost and the timeline.

  7. Pipeline competitors

    Peace Crossing's highway commercial land and the industrial rezonings [^6,7] can bring a new chain store closer to the subdivisions than the subject.

These conditions turn the research's missing items into documents. Related items are grouped.

  1. Land and site record

    An appraisal of the land and of the completed store as a going concern; the Polk County Property Appraiser record for the parcel, with acreage, just value and tax district; and confirmation of city or unincorporated status.

  2. Zoning and approvals

    The zoning district, written confirmation that a fuel and convenience use is allowed, the approval timeline, and impact and connection fees.

  3. Traffic count

    A current FDOT station count, with station number and count year, for US 27 at the site, and truck volume for the same station.

  4. Flood, storm and environmental

    The parcel's FEMA map designation, a Phase I report, its state storage tank record, and binding quotes covering property, wind, flood, liability and pollution.

  5. Utilities

    The electric provider for the parcel, sewer availability or an approved onsite wastewater design, and water service.

  6. Competitor confirmation

    Operator or appraiser confirmation of the ten unconfirmed stations, fueling positions and store sizes for every competitor except Love's, opening years, parcel records with taxes and acreage, and any new station in planning in the trade area or the next ring.

  7. Street-price check

    Posted prices, with the date and time of each reading, at Murphy USA, both RaceTracs, the Wawa and the closest independents, so the margin assumption can be tested against the street.

  8. Demand detail

    Ring or drive-time demographics, daytime employment, monthly seasonality and the delivery schedule of approved housing on the corridor.

  9. Costs, taxes and licenses

    Contractor bids or a construction cost index for Polk County, the statutory assessment basis, and the license list for fuel, storage tanks, alcohol and tobacco.

  10. Program items

    The rate set at pricing, the county's rural status for the FY2027 fee waiver, ownership documents for the citizenship rule, the fuel supply contract, and recent Polk County 7(a) approvals pulled from SBA's FOIA file [^46]. The research did not establish whether the tract is USDA-rural, a HUBZone or an Opportunity Zone.

  11. Opening reserve

    A funded reserve that covers Year 1 debt service in full, given Year 1 income near break-even on operations alone.
