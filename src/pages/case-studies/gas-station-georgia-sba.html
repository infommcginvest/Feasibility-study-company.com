{{case:glance}}

Exit 296 is a freight interchange first. Trucks made up as much as 34% of Cass-White Road traffic at the Pilot truck stop and about 48% on the I-75 ramps when GDOT counted for the road's widening in 2019 [^5]. Pilot Travel Center #67 and TA Cartersville #0146 serve those trucks at scale, with 10 fuel lanes at one and 212 truck spaces at the other [^13,14]. Around them, branded stations under CITGO, Exxon, Chevron and Texaco flags work the car traffic coming off the twin roundabouts that took over the ramp traffic in March 2024 [^4,15,16,19]. A new diesel-led store would enter the most contested segment of the interchange with the worst position in it.

The customer that is underserved is closer to home. Bartow County grew 11.0% between April 2020 and July 2025, twice Georgia's rate of 5.5% [^2,3], and the county's newest employers sit along Cass-White Road and US 411: a 1 million square foot Amazon cross dock on Cass-White Road, the Hyundai and SK On battery plant that began production in June 2026, and the Qcells solar campus [^10,7,9]. Commuters to those plants, and the households that follow the jobs, buy gasoline, coffee and breakfast on the way to work. A {{case:v.in_building_sf}} square foot store with a food counter, on a commercial lot in the interchange's trade area, is built for them.

That store has a market. What it does not have is a loan at the structure the sponsor proposed. Built new at 2026 costs, the project totals {{case:v.tpc}}. Financed as a 7(a) start-up with 10% down at Prime plus the maximum spread, it carries {{case:v.ds_proposed}} of annual debt service. In Year 2, the year in which a start-up must show it can reach the 1.15x standard, the store earns {{case:v.noi_y2}} of NOI, which covers that debt {{case:v.dscr_y2_proposed}}. The case engine restructures to {{case:v.equity_pct}} equity, where Year 2 coverage reaches {{case:v.dscr_y2}}.

A sponsor who can put more than half the cost into a single new store is rare, and the finding should be read that way. The store's earnings at stabilization, {{case:v.noi_y3}} in Year 3, are reasonable for a store of this size and volume; the problem is that a new-build cost basis and a variable rate of {{case:v.rate}} on all but the 10% injection need a yield the format does not produce. The workable versions of this project are a lower-cost build, a site with frontage on Cass-White Road where volume would be higher, or a different capital structure, discussed under the loan section. On the 7(a) start-up as proposed, the determination is no.

This case sits beside two other ways to finance fuel on the interstate. The Opelika acquisition on the I-85 corridor buys an operating store and is tested on the seller's history; the Terrell truck stop on I-20 goes after the diesel customer this case avoids, with an SBA 504 loan. Our gas station and convenience store guide covers the asset class as a whole, its interstate and highway fuel page sets out how interchange position shapes volume, and the Georgia market page covers the state's regulatory and cost context.

The primary trade area is a 3-mile ring around Exit 296. It captures the interchange and the residential roads behind it and leaves out most of the Exit 293 (US 411) cluster to the south. The Census Bureau does not publish estimates for radius rings and no radius report was available, so the tables use Cartersville city and Bartow County as the nearest published geographies. The site is in unincorporated Bartow County with a Cartersville mailing address, so the county figures describe its customers better than the city's.

The job announcements are large, and the study discounts them on purpose. The battery plant's 3,500 jobs and Qcells' 2,000 were commitments at announcement, and no primary source gives either plant's 2026 headcount [^6,9]. The battery plant's own spokesperson described it in July 2026 as being in the early stages of production with a gradual scale-up [^7]. A lender should treat these employers as the reason the county is growing, not as a count of customers the store will see. The model uses no plant-by-plant capture.

The Amazon cross dock and the Crow Holdings business park matter more to this store because they sit on Cass-White Road, the road the site's customers use to reach the interstate [^10,11]. GDOT's 2019 projection for the widening project expected employee traffic on Cass-White Road to peak near 1,700 vehicles a day plus about 300 freight trucks [^5]. The second phase of the widening was listed for an October 15, 2026 letting in the county's January 2024 project report [^12]; its current schedule is a lender condition, because construction on the access road would hurt a new store during its ramp.

No visitor counts, event calendar or seasonal index was found for the interchange. This is a commuter and freight market, and the model treats demand as steady through the year.

The travel centers set the competitive terms. Pilot and TA together offer truck parking, scales, service bays, showers and national loyalty programs that a single store lot cannot match [^13,14]. Both also hold Georgia coin-operated amusement machine licenses, and TA lists a video gaming terminal [^14,17]. A store off the interstate frontage should not try to win diesel; it should take the gasoline-and-coffee trip that the travel centers serve only incidentally, from drivers who live or work north of the interstate and do not want the truck traffic at the ramps.

October prices during a tax holiday

The only operator-posted prices at the exit, TA's of October 15, 2026, were taken during a state fuel tax suspension. Georgia's motor fuel excise for 2026 is 33.3 cents a gallon on gasoline and 37.3 cents on diesel [^27]. The Governor suspended it by emergency order from 12:01 a.m. on September 29 through October 29, 2026, the second suspension of the year after a 60-day holiday signed on March 20 [^29,30]. TA's $3.699 unleaded price may or may not reflect the full suspension, and nothing in the record says which. It sat well below the $4.35 national average EIA reported for September 2026 [^32,14]. For underwriting the point is simple: an October 2026 price check in Georgia cannot be compared with a price from any other month, and a lender who wants to judge how this store will price against the interchange should repeat the survey in November, after the excise returns. Federal excise of 18.4 cents on gasoline and 24.4 cents on diesel applied throughout [^31], and Georgia's prepaid local sales tax on fuel kept running on a Department of Revenue base price of $3.000 a gallon for both fuels from July 1, 2026 [^28].

The model does not depend on any of these prices, because it underwrites cents of margin per gallon, which taxes pass through. The suspension does matter to the first months of operation of any store opening during a holiday: retailers' margins can widen or narrow when the tax steps on or off, and the timing of the step is political, not seasonal.

The coverage standard for a start-up is the general 7(a) standard: 1.15x, on a basis that is historical and/or projected, with 1:1 global coverage; start-ups reach 1.15x within two years. That is why the engine tests Year 2. The underlying regulation is permissive about studies, 13 CFR 120.160(b): SBA may require professional appraisals, a survey, or a feasibility study [^51], and for a start-up with no history the study is where the projection gets tested. The sponsor's ownership also has to qualify: since March 1, 2026 the program lends only to businesses wholly owned by U.S. citizens or nationals who live in the United States, and the same test applies to guarantors [^46].

The structure fails because a variable 7(a) rate of {{case:v.rate}} on all but the 10% start-up injection needs a store yield the format rarely reaches in its second year. Three alternatives deserve a look before the project is abandoned. A cheaper build: a smaller store without a kitchen would lower cost but also the food margin that makes the store distinctive. A different site: a lot with frontage on Cass-White Road would earn more volume, at a higher land price. A different program: an SBA 504 loan fixes the rate on the debenture portion, though a new gas station falls in the 504 program's highest equity tier as a new business on special purpose property. Our SBA lending practice compares the two programs for fuel sites, and the research note on SOP 50 10 8.1's changes for feasibility studies covers what the October 2026 rules changed for start-ups.
