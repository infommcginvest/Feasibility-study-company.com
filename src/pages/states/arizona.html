Arizona's record for 2025 and 2026 runs on two tracks that do not move together. In the Phoenix area the water rules decide which new subdivisions can be approved at all, and in 2026 they were being settled in court. Across the state the population and payroll series still rise: the fastest county growth in the year to July 2025 was Pinal's, inside the Phoenix metro, while Apache, Coconino and Gila counties lost residents [^17].

The second unsettled file is federal disaster money. FEMA refused a major disaster declaration for the September 2025 flooding in Gila and Mohave counties and then the state's appeal, leaving SBA disaster loans and other federal programs, not Public Assistance grants, open to the two counties [^18].

Beyond the Phoenix and Tucson metros the published evidence thins quickly. Brokers publish apartment, industrial and retail readings for those two, and Matthews a hotel submarket for Scottsdale; for the rest of the state the record is the Office of Economic Opportunity's county populations, the tourism office's county hotel table and ADOT's traffic counts.

In June 2023 the Department of Water Resources stopped granting new assured-water certificates in the Phoenix AMA that rest only on local groundwater; its groundwater model had found the area's 100-year supply 4% short [^1]. High Country News wrote in October 2025 that the moratorium halted construction of nearly half a million Phoenix-area houses [^6].

The challenge

A Republican bill that would have let applicants from before 2023 ask for a re-evaluation failed in the Arizona House in March 2025 [^19]. The Home Builders Association of Central Arizona, with the Goldwater Institute as counsel, had sued the department in January 2025, and on April 21, 2026 the Maricopa County Superior Court found that the department's area-wide unmet-demand and depth-to-water rules had been adopted improperly and enjoined their use [^1].

Two routes that opened before the ruling

In October 2025 EPCOR received the first Alternative Designation of Assured Water Supply in the Phoenix AMA, which High Country News described as the right to build up to 60,000 new homes in parts of Buckeye and Surprise; once outside renewable water arrives, those subdivisions have to cut their groundwater use by 25% [^6]. In December 2025 the department approved the first application under its ag-to-urban program, from Forestar Group [^20].

Beyond the Valley

The groundwater map has also grown outside Phoenix: the Willcox Active Management Area was created in 2024 and the Ranegras Plain Active Management Area in 2025, as the Arizona Capitol Times recounted on October 1, 2026 in a report on how the 2026 election bears on the state's groundwater rules [^21]. Willcox's traffic count is in the rural section below.

For a subdivision in the Phoenix AMA, the study therefore names the route to water the subject relies on, whether an existing certificate, EPCOR's designation, an ag-to-urban approval or another, and the state of the court record on the date it was read.

The Census Bureau's Vintage 2025 estimate put Arizona at 7,623,818 residents on July 1, 2025, a gain of 67,394, or 0.9%, over the year; the Phoenix Business Journal reported on January 28, 2026 that the gain ranked seventh among the states in numbers and eighth in percent, after a sharp slowdown from the year before [^2].

The Office of Economic Opportunity credits 97% of the state's growth from 2020 to 2024 to net migration [^23].

The Office of Economic Opportunity put Maricopa County at 4,787,790 residents on July 1, 2025 [^3], and the Maricopa Association of Governments (MAG) builds its 2025 estimates on an April 1, 2020 base of 4,425,320 for the county, beside the published Census count of 4,420,568 [^26].

Nonfarm employment in the metro rose by 33,200, or 1.4%, between June 2025 and June 2026 before seasonal adjustment, in the research center's Phoenix summary of August 2026 [^27]. Apartments

Kidder Mathews put the metro's apartment vacancy at 11.3% in the second quarter of 2026 and its asking rent at $1,536 a unit, down 2.17% on the year, with 15,974 units under construction, 35.5% fewer than a year before [^4]. Solex, citing CoStar for June 2026, gives 11.5% vacancy, 16,122 units under construction and a rent of $1,566 [^29], and Colliers' brief of September 24, 2026, on its own definition, counts 23,000 units under construction and about 14,000 deliveries expected in 2026 [^30].

Industrial and retail

Sedona, which sits mostly in Yavapai County, regulates short-term rentals under City Code chapter 5.25: a permit is needed even for one rental day a year, and rentals of 30 days or more need none, in the code as amended through Ordinance 2025-13 of December 9, 2025 [^10]. A secondary source reports that the Court of Appeals struck down Sedona's ban on short-term rentals in mobile home parks in November 2025; the court record is not cited there and has to be read [^48].

Flagstaff's own hotel tax, its permits and the traffic counts at the I-17 and I-40 interchange and on SR-89A were not retrieved.

Short-term rentals

A.R.S. § 9-500.39, from SB 1350 of 2016, bars cities and towns from prohibiting vacation or short-term rentals, while leaving them free to license, enforce and penalize [^7]. HB 2429, which would have added occupancy limits and licence suspension for health and safety violations, passed the House in March 2026 [^60] and stalled in the Senate, so caps and spacing rules remain unauthorized [^8]. Since January 1, 2025, under SB 1131, residential rentals of 30 days or more owe no city transaction privilege tax, while short lodging stays remain taxable [^61]. Scottsdale's licence and Sedona's permit are in their market sections.

Wages

The state minimum wage is $15.15 an hour in 2026, with a tipped wage of $12.15, and is indexed to the CPI each January 1 under A.R.S. § 23-363; it was $14.70 in 2025 [^62]. It becomes $15.65, tipped $12.65, on January 1, 2027, by the Industrial Commission's poster as RecordingLaw reports it [^63]. Two cities set higher floors for 2026, Flagstaff at $18.35 and Tucson at $15.45, by a secondary compliance site [^64].

Care licences

The Department of Health Services licenses assisted living in three settings under A.R.S. § 36-401, the assisted living home, the larger assisted living center and the adult foster care home, through its Bureau of Residential Facilities Licensing [^65]. Licences are issued for supervisory, personal or directed care services, and memory care operates under directed care with added staffing rules in R9-10-816 [^66]. A child care center's capacity is set by its indoor floor area and the ages it serves and is printed on the licence, a group home operates in the provider's own residence, and the smallest home settings need no certification [^67].

Arizona homeowners pay an effective property tax of 0.48% of owner-occupied value, which ranks 47th of the 50 states in the Tax Foundation's March 2026 tables built on 2024 ACS data [^68]. An effective commercial rate and rank for 2025 or 2026 was not found.

A room night carries the state's 5.5% transaction privilege tax under the transient lodging classification, with local levies on top [^69]. The tourism office's July 2024 ranking gives combined rates of 12.57% in Phoenix, 14.02% in Scottsdale, 14.07% in Tempe, 14.27% in Mesa and 11.325% in Prescott, with Bisbee the highest of the places listed at 15.05% [^69]. Phoenix's combined rate is reported at 13.07% after the city's increase in mid-2025, by a secondary source [^70].

Arizona has no FAIR plan or other state insurer of last resort [^14]. In high fire-risk areas owners rely on the surplus lines market [^15], and the Department of Insurance and Financial Institutions' Resiliency and Mitigation Council reviewed catastrophe modeling and other states' strategies, an insurer of last resort among them, in its December 2025 final report [^16]. Three gaps in the research bear directly on the Arizona cases: it holds no count of self-storage space per resident for any Arizona market, no senior housing occupancy reading, and no count of RV parks and campgrounds, statewide or for any county.
