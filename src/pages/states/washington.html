The jobs count is the quiet part of Washington's record for 2026. The Employment Security Department's August release, beside its yearly gain, shows 13,700 jobs lost between July and August [^1]. The state's own projections, released on August 13, 2026, expect job growth of 0.85% a year from 2024 to 2029 and 0.84% a year from 2029 to 2034 [^15].

Other rules that set a project's costs moved over the same months. A 2025 law, 2SSB 5786, raised liquor licence fees, and the Liquor and Cannabis Board filed the rules carrying them out in 2026 [^16]. The state's heating oil tank insurance policy expired on June 30, 2025 and a loan and grant program took its place [^17]. The minimum wage went up on January 1, 2026, and the rate for January 1, 2027 is already set [^18,19].

Two emergencies added to the file. Atmospheric-river storms in December 2025 led to a federal emergency declaration within days and a major disaster declaration in April 2026 [^20,21]. In August 2026, with wildfires burning, the Insurance Commissioner's emergency order restricted property cancellations and non-renewals in the affected areas through September 30 [^22].

Growth in residents is concentrated, not general. OFM's April 2026 estimates name Franklin, Adams and Benton as the fastest-growing counties [^23], the counties of the Tri-Cities and Columbia Basin sections below. The Seattle and Spokane pages carry those two markets' hotel, housing and employer records.

Sales tax on services

ESSB 5814 brought advertising, live presentations, IT services, custom website development, custom software, investigation, security and armored car services, and temporary staffing into the retail sales tax from October 1, 2025 [^2]. For contracts signed before that date, the Department of Revenue gave transition relief on invoices dated from October 1, 2025 to March 31, 2026 [^2]. For a hotel, a senior housing community or a child care center, those can be lines an operator buys every month: a security contract, agency staff, the booking software and the marketing behind the opening. The research did not find the tax treatment of construction materials and business equipment, so the development budget takes that rate from the Department of Revenue for the site's own location.

Wage floors

The state minimum wage is $17.13 an hour from January 1, 2026, up from $16.66 in 2025, with $14.56 for workers aged 14 and 15 [^18]. The salary threshold for an overtime-exempt employee is 2.25 times the minimum wage, $80,168 a year [^18]. Labor & Industries has set the 2027 rates at $17.73 an hour and $15.07 for ages 14 and 15, effective January 1, 2027 [^19].

Property insurance

The Insurance Commissioner's office counts 11,763 homeowners whose policies were non-renewed or cancelled by their insurer in 2021 and 24,106 in its latest data [^4]. The insurer of last resort is the Washington FAIR Plan Association, a joint reinsurance association of every licensed property insurer, operating under chapter 284-19 WAC, supervised by the commissioner and without taxpayer funding [^24]. Stateline reported in August 2026 that its book had plateaued at roughly 400 policies over the previous three quarters [^25]. In the 2026 session, SB 5928, on wildfire risk score transparency, passed the Senate and did not advance out of the House Consumer Protection and Business Committee, so it was not enacted in 2026; the commissioner also endorsed SB 6079 on February 6, 2026 [^26]. No approved commercial property rate change and no commercial carrier withdrawal for 2025 or 2026 was found, so the commercial premium in a Washington file comes from the broker's quote for the subject.

Health care: Certificate of Need

Washington keeps Certificate of Need under chapter 70.38 RCW, in the text certified on August 15, 2025 [^10]. A certificate is required for hospitals, including a sale, purchase or lease of one, psychiatric hospitals, nursing homes that add beds or spend above the expenditure minimum, kidney dialysis centers, ambulatory surgical facilities that are not in-office, continuing care retirement communities, hospices and home health agencies [^10]. An ambulatory surgical facility that is an individual or group practice is exempt, as is a nursing home owned by a continuing care retirement community that meets the statute's conditions [^11]. The Department of Health's rules in chapter 246-310 WAC were last updated on October 2, 2025, and its project register shows reviews in progress: an ambulatory surgery application denied on December 19, 2024 went to a reconsideration hearing on February 24, 2025 [^27]. No licensing category or bed threshold for assisted living and memory care was found in the research, so a senior housing study confirms with the state which licence its unit count falls under before it is sized.

Child care

WAC 110-300-0356 sets staff ratios and maximum group sizes for each age band, and each center licence states its total capacity, its capacity per space and the ages served [^28]. A family home and a center are separate licence classes, and a DCYF licensor visits the site before a licence is issued [^29]. A child care study therefore reads a competitor's capacity from its licence, not from its floor area.

Restaurants, bars and liquor stores

Washington has no quota on on-premises or spirits retail licences, by a 2026 secondary guide [^30]. Fees rose under 2SSB 5786, chapter 343 of the Laws of 2025: the spirits retail annual fee went from $166 to $550, the spirits distributor fee from $1,320 to $1,980 and the endorsement fee from $100 to $150, in the rules filed as WSR 26-03-072 in 2026 [^16]. Section 66.24.420 of the Revised Code sets the spirits, beer and wine restaurant licence fee by the share of the premises given to dining, and sets a separate fee for a service bar only [^31]. A price for a licence transfer was not found.

Car washes and RV parks

No state rule on car wash water use or recycling and no state licensing scheme for campgrounds and RV parks was found in the research, so a study for either use takes the water, sewer and operating conditions from the county and the city that would permit the site, in writing and dated.

Fuel tanks

Owners of underground petroleum storage tanks must carry pollution coverage, and the Pollution Liability Insurance Agency reinsures the private insurers that write it and runs a revolving loan and grant program for tank work under chapter 70A.345 RCW [^32]. The agency's separate Heating Oil Insurance Program ended: its policy expired on June 30, 2025, registrations closed on August 1, 2025, and the Heating Oil Loan and Grant Program, at up to $75,000 per facility, replaced it [^17]. A fuel study confirms the tank's coverage on the owner's policy; an oil-heated building no longer has the agency's insurance behind it.

Hotels and short-term rentals

The House bill report on HB 2559 in the 2026 session lays out the lodging tax stack: the 6.5% state retail sales tax, a 2% state-shared local lodging tax credited against it, a special lodging tax of up to 2%, or up to 4% in Seattle, a convention center lodging tax in counties of 1.5 million people or more, and a combined cap of 12% outside Seattle [^6]. Under chapter 64.37 RCW a short-term rental is a dwelling offered for fewer than 30 consecutive nights; the operator carries $1,000,000 of primary liability cover or the platform's equivalent, operators or platforms remit the lodging, sales and occupancy taxes, and platforms register with the Department of Revenue [^7]. The state issues no permit and does not preempt local rules, and bills for a local short-term rental tax died in 2025 and 2026 [^33]. The local ordinance is therefore the rule a rental study is tested against.

The December 2025 storms

FEMA declared emergency EM-3629 on December 12, 2025 for severe storms with straight-line winds, floods, landslides and mudslides that began on December 9 [^20]. On January 21, 2026 the Governor asked for Individual Assistance of about $21.3 million for 10 counties, Chelan, Grays Harbor, King, Lewis, Pacific, Pierce, Skagit, Snohomish, Thurston and Whatcom, and for 15 federally recognized tribal nations, for the storms of December 5 to 22, 2025 [^34].

The Public Assistance request of February 17, 2026 put public infrastructure damage at $182.3 million, which the Governor's office called the largest such amount submitted to FEMA from Washington in more than 40 years; FEMA Public Assistance pays up to 75% of eligible costs [^35]. The major disaster declaration for the storms of December 5 to 19, 2025 was approved on April 11, 2026 [^21]. For a site in one of the ten counties, the request letters are the dated record of what the storms reached; a site elsewhere is not in them.

The August 2026 wildfires

The Governor issued a statewide emergency proclamation for the August 2026 wildfires [^36]. The Insurance Commissioner's Emergency Order 26-01, in force from August 3 to September 30, 2026 in the affected ZIP codes, gave policyholders a 45-day grace period on premiums, barred cancellation for non-payment, lengthened the notice for a property non-renewal from 60 to 120 days and for an auto policy from 20 to 60 days [^22]. In Spokane the commissioner sent additional staff to help with claims after the Spokane Complex fires [^4].

The research did not retrieve a National Risk Index ranking or a seismic or wind exposure measure for the state. Hazard exposure in a Washington file is therefore read at the site, with the declarations above used only where they name its county.

The Census Bureau's Vintage 2025 estimate counts 8,001,020 Washington residents on July 1, 2025; its base for April 1, 2020 is 7,707,519 [^12]. Axios, reporting the release, gave the gain over the year to July 2025 as 73,062, with net international migration of about 46,200, down from about 71,000 in the year before [^13]; the Spokesman-Review's report put combined domestic and international net migration at about 55,000 and natural change at about 17,000 for the same year [^37]. A separate Census figure for net domestic migration was not found.

The state's Office of Financial Management counts on April 1 and by its own method. It put Washington at 8,115,100 on April 1, 2025, a gain of 79,400 in the year, with net migration of 61,750 [^3], and at 8,176,300 on April 1, 2026 [^23]. The two series have different reference dates and methods, so this page gives each with its date and does not set one against the other.

Builders took out permits for 34,913 residential units statewide in 2025, 8% fewer than in 2024, as WCRER tallies the Census Bureau's building permit survey [^5].

Washington had 3,064,820 households in the 2020 to 2024 American Community Survey, 1,954,777 of them in owner-occupied homes, and the survey puts the median owner-occupied home at $564,600 [^38]. In the same estimates 1,299,185 Washingtonians are 65 or older [^38], a statewide count that a senior housing study narrows to its own county.

ESD counted 3.655 million nonfarm jobs in Washington in August 2026, 14,900 more than in August 2025 [^1]. OFM's monthly economic report, on the series not adjusted for season, counts 3,668,300 jobs in July 2026, 16,100 more than in July 2025 [^39]. The two figures differ in month and are not one series.

Within August, Seattle Red's report of ESD's figures has government adding 8,100 jobs over the month while the private sector lost 9,000, with professional and business services down 4,500, leisure and hospitality down 3,700 and information down 2,300 [^40]. A twelve-month change for every major sector was not found in ESD's published text, so a study whose demand rests on one sector reads it from ESD's monthly workbook.

Data centers

Cascade PBS reported in August 2026 that the research firm Baxtel counts approximately 126 data centers and related facilities in Washington, with a footprint of nearly 7 million square feet [^14]. Income

SBA lending

In SBA's FOIA loan files, Washington projects account for 13,978 loans under 7(a) approved in fiscal years 2016 to 2025, for $7,741.8 million, and for 1,245 loans under 504 carrying $1,237.8 million of debentures; loans later cancelled are excluded [^44]. Those are counts for every project in the state, Clark County's included.

When the federal shutdown stopped approvals, SBA's state analysis of October 21, 2025 put the Washington 7(a) and 504 lending blocked each week at 45 loans and $24,098,402, based on fiscal 2025 lending [^45].

State programs

Washington's State Small Business Credit Initiative programs are administered by lenders and CDFIs under the Department of Commerce. The Owner-Occupied CRE Loan Program makes companion loans through Heritage Bank's Community Development Entity, and its Collateral Support Program posts cash collateral behind SBA 504 interim loans through Evergreen Business Capital Community Finance; revenue-based financing, venture capital and free legal and accounting help make up the rest [^48]. No Washington loan guarantee or capital access program run apart from SSBCI turned up in the research.

For 2025 Commerce set the private activity bond cap at $1,034,563,400, with $258,640,850 for small issues, $206,912,680 for exempt facilities, and $347,613,302 for housing plus $86,903,326 for local housing authorities [^49]. The conduit for private projects is the Washington Economic Development Finance Authority, which issues nonrecourse taxable and tax-exempt revenue bonds, mainly for manufacturing, processing and waste-disposal facilities, cannot assist retail projects and takes no state appropriation [^50].

USDA, HUD and other capital

USDA's Washington REAP page, as read in October 2026, said no REAP grant applications were being taken [^51]. Washington figures for Business and Industry guarantees and Community Facilities lending in fiscal 2025 were not found, nor was the address of the state office, so a rural file states its program from the USDA Rural Development rules and the lender's own term sheet. Washington files financed by banks, life companies or agency lenders are covered under conventional and institutional lending, and files that raise foreign investment under EB-5.

SBA's Seattle District serves every Washington county except Clark, Cowlitz, Skamania and Wahkiakum, which belong to the Portland District, and it also serves ten counties of north Idaho; it has offices in Seattle and Spokane [^9,8]. A Clark County project therefore reaches SBA through its Portland District, the cross-border link to Oregon. SBA may require a feasibility study under 13 CFR 120.160(b): SBA may require professional appraisals, a survey, or a feasibility study [^52], and the tests a Washington file is held to are explained on our SBA 7(a) and 504 lending page.

Washington's statewide record holds no count of competing supply for any asset class, so in a Washington file supply is a condition the study meets for its own market. Each condition below is written for the state's own record.

  • Apartments. The readings sit with brokers and WCRER at the level of a county or a region, on the Seattle and Spokane pages and in the sections below. A study sets the subject against the reading for its own county, with the reading's quarter.
  • Hotels. Seattle is the only market whose hotel occupancy, rate and revenue per room were found, so a hotel study anywhere else in the state buys a competitive set from STR for the properties it names.
  • Self-storage. No market has a sourced figure for storage space per resident, so a storage study counts the competing facilities and their units by name and address.
  • Fuel and convenience stores. No count of stores was found; the tank record runs through the Pollution Liability Insurance Agency's reinsurance of private tank insurers, which a fuel study confirms for the subject's tanks [^32].
  • Child care. No count of licensed capacity was found; since every center licence states its capacity [^28], a child care study adds up the licensed capacity of the competitors it names.
  • Senior housing and health care. No occupancy reading and no count of hospitals or surgery centers was found; the Department of Health's Certificate of Need project register [^27] is the dated list of what is under review.
  • Industrial and data centers. Broker readings exist for the Seattle and Spokane areas and for the two-state Portland metro, and the Columbia Basin's limit is power delivery, not floor space, so an industrial study there starts from the utility's capacity.
