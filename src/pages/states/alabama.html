Deaths outnumbered births statewide and in all but 17 of the 67 counties in 2025 [^2], and CBER at the University of Alabama names migration the primary driver of the state's uneven growth [^9]. The Census Bureau counted Alabama's net domestic migration at 23,358 for July 2024 to June 2025, above Florida's [^69]. The international reading is unsettled. CBER counts 8,946 net international migrants in 2024 to 2025 against 15,763 the year before [^9]. PARCA, reading the same Census vintage, describes a drop of 13,156 in international migration from 2024 and a fall of 1,595 in net domestic migration [^10]. The research leaves the two unreconciled.

Innovate Alabama's five programs

Innovate Alabama administers Alabama's State Small Business Credit Initiative money through five programs. Collateral support, at about $26.9 million, is the largest of them, and beside the credit programs sit a co-investment program of $16 million and a fund of funds of $9 million for venture capital [^25]. Treasury approved up to $97.9 million for Alabama in this round, which launched in June 2024 [^25]. In the 2011 round, ADECA received $31,301,498 for the state's capital access, loan guarantee and loan participation programs [^26].

Site readiness has separate state money. In May 2026 the Governor announced more than $20 million of Growing Alabama funding for 10 sites, with $29 million available for the year [^75].

SBA's Alabama District Office is in Birmingham [^68]. The program tests in SBA's current SOP [^67] are on our SBA 7(a) and 504 practice page, and the clause that lets the agency ask for a study is 13 CFR 120.160(b): SBA may require professional appraisals, a survey, or a feasibility study [^66].

A memory care building in Alabama answers to Public Health, which requires a separate specialty care license, issued in group and congregate classes, to serve residents with dementia-level impairment [^15], and beds converted from ordinary assisted living to specialty care have come before SHPDA's Certificate of Need Review Board on its recent agendas [^29]. The assisted living license beneath it is divided into family, group and congregate categories by resident count, from family homes of 2 to 3 residents to congregate facilities of 17 or more [^30], and no new license is issued for a facility of fewer than three beds [^31].

SHPDA's reach goes well beyond memory care. New institutional health services need a Certificate of Need from the agency [^13], and the same recent agendas carried dialysis, ambulatory surgery, inpatient hospice and rehabilitation, a children's hospital relocation and substance use treatment [^29]. The statute's reach takes in nursing home beds, and the State Health Plan carries its own bed-need methods for inpatient psychiatric, inpatient rehabilitation and alcohol and drug treatment beds [^13]. The agency's revised program rules took effect on December 15, 2025 [^4]. Earlier that month, press coverage of a Hoover health care project reported a moratorium on processing multi-specialty surgery center applications [^14].

For fuel retail the state still backs cleanup. ADEM's trust fund reimburses eligible assessment and cleanup of gas station tank releases [^36], it revised its contractor per diem rates from April 1, 2025, and it closed a new contractor qualification round at the end of 2025 [^37].

The Department of Human Resources posts a permit stage before a child care center receives its full license, and its application asks for background clearances and the director's qualifications [^32]; a secondary guide classes family homes at up to 6 children, group homes at 7 to 12 and centers above 12, each on a two-year license [^33].

For bars and restaurants, a secondary guide reports no quota for most alcohol license types [^35]; a primary source on quota status was not found.
