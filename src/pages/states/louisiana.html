Louisiana's gate for care uses is Facility Need Review, not a certificate of need [^10]. Every provider type in the table passes it before first licensure, among them adult residential care, the license behind assisted living, and psychosocial rehabilitation, a behavioral health service [^10]. The state's residents aged 65 and over numbered 772,404 in the five-year American Community Survey for 2020 to 2024 [^28]. For nursing facilities, R.S. 40:2116.1 adds a moratorium, with exceptions for a replacement that adds no beds and for nursing beds converted to adult residential care [^11]. The review rule refers to adult residential care level 4 [^40]; definitions of the levels and bed thresholds were not found.

Louisiana has no statute that enables or preempts local short-term rental rules: HB 469, which would have written local authority into statute, died on June 12, 2025 [^46], and SB 225, creating a private right of action against unpermitted rentals, reached the reengrossed stage, and its final status was not found [^47]. Act 387 of 2025 amended the state's New Orleans rental provisions [^74], and the city writes its own rules. Louisiana's minimum wage in 2026 follows the federal rate of $7.25 an hour, parishes and cities are barred from setting their own under R.S. 23:642, and HB 353, which would have set $12 rising to $15, was rejected 7 to 5 by the House Labor Committee on April 9, 2026; it is not law [^48].

A gas station or travel center sits under DEQ's Motor Fuels Underground Storage Tank Trust Fund, set up by Act 767 of 1988, and under R.S. 30:2195.4 its cleanup costs must be approved in advance to be reimbursed [^43]. The Legislative Auditor audits the fund's dedicated account and its transfers for FY2024 [^45]. DEQ's separate upgrade grant takes up to 20 owners a year [^49].

Child care centers are licensed under BESE Bulletin 137 as Type I, II or III, and a Type III center also needs academic approval under Bulletin 140 [^41]. The bulletin sets staff-to-child ratios by age band and requires at least two staff whenever more than four children are present [^41].

Since January 1, 2025 manufacturing machinery and equipment has been exempt from the state sales tax, and parishes report that exemption on a line of their own [^51]. On other purchases the rate at an address combines the state levy with parish and municipal ones; the Tax Foundation's average of the combined rates, 10.11%, is the highest of any state [^7]. The research found no hotel-specific state room tax and no typical combined local rate.

Louisiana has not imposed a state property tax since 1972, and its constitution would permit at most 5.75 mills [^50]; neither an effective commercial rate nor a national ranking was found.

Louisiana Citizens ended its 1.36% assessment in April 2025, ahead of its original June 2026 end date [^12]. In September 2026 a private carrier won approval to assume 4,764 Citizens commercial policies, and the Insurance Commissioner counts six Citizens depopulation rounds since 2024 [^13]. Leaders for a Better Louisiana reports new homeowners insurers licensed through April 2026, and lists modified comparative fault and the repeal of the Housley presumption among the 2025 tort reforms [^14].

The January 2026 winter storm produced two federal declarations. The emergency declaration EM-3638 came on January 24 for an incident period of January 23 to 27, and FEMA had obligated $3,787,500 of Public Assistance under it by May [^3]. The major disaster declaration DR-4900 followed on February 18, with $151,178.60 of Public Assistance obligated and designations including Ouachita, Morehouse, Richland and De Soto parishes; the SBA economic injury loan deadline under it runs to December 4, 2026 [^4].

Tropical Storm Arthur brought DR-4927, declared on June 30, 2026 [^5]. The incident period is recorded two ways: June 17 to 24 on the disaster page [^5], June 17 to 18 on FEMA's Louisiana page [^53] and in SBA's amendment [^52].

Wind is not the only damage driver. Insurify, citing NOAA's National Centers for Environmental Information, reports that severe thunderstorms rather than hurricanes caused the most property damage in Louisiana over 2025 to 2026 [^54], and neither of 2026's major disasters was a hurricane: DR-4900 followed a winter storm and DR-4927 a tropical storm [^4,5].

Two offices for one permission

A Louisiana alcohol retailer holds two permits: one from the state's Alcohol and Tobacco Control office and one from the parish or municipality [^42]. The local one carries its own price, as Shreveport's published fee for a Class B off-premises license shows [^87].

Arthur's parishes and dates moved after June 30

Lafourche and Pointe Coupee, parishes the Houma-Thibodaux and Baton Rouge sections cover, joined DR-4927 with the July 20 amendment [^52], after the June 30 declaration [^5]. A loss date is checked against both incident windows the hazards section gives, since FEMA and SBA record them differently [^5,52,53].
