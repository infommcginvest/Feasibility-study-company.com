Mayes County had 40,416 residents on July 1, 2025, against an estimates base of 39,050 in April 2020 [^64], and Pryor Creek itself counted 9,723, up from 9,451 [^65]. The Frontier reported in December 2025 that PSO already had 11 new large-load customers under letters of agreement or contract, about 779 MW, as of September 2025, that PSO's projected deficit by 2031 stood at 31%, and that OG&E's 2025 resource plan showed a shortfall of 3,459 MW by 2035 [^20].

The rules changed in 2026. HB 2992, signed in May, requires new loads of 75 MW or more that connect after July 1, 2026 to sign long-term agreements covering their infrastructure costs [^21]. OG&E filed a large-load tariff with the Corporation Commission in June 2026 with 100% upfront connection cost, minimum billing and minimum 10-year terms, and the decision was pending [^22].

Water is reported campus by campus. The Frontier reported in February 2026, citing a Payne County commissioner, that Google estimates it will eventually use nearly 3 billion gallons of water a year for its data centers in Stillwater; the same report said the city can withdraw up to 50 million gallons per day from Kaw Lake and uses 7 to 14 million, and that a homeowners' association had sued over construction runoff; it reported that MidAmerica Industrial Park's permit from the Oklahoma Water Resources Board allows up to 27.375 billion gallons a year [^72]. For 2025 and 2026 the research found no statewide water allocation limit or sewer moratorium.

Oklahoma's average homeowners premium was $6,133 a year in LendingTree's data, against a national average of $2,801, 2.2x the national figure, and it rose 50.8% from 2019 to 2024 [^16]. Rates are file-and-use, and the Insurance Department has no statutory authority to set or approve homeowners rates outside extraordinary circumstances [^16]. There is no FAIR plan, so no insurer of last resort stands behind an owner the voluntary market declines, although more than 30 states have one [^17]. A written department statement in October 2025 counted 113 insurers with active homeowners policies and approximately 60 actively writing business [^18]; the department's own page in May 2025, as Oklahoma Watch reported it, put the number writing new policies at over 50 [^77]. Losses ran ahead of premiums: in 2023 the top 20 homeowners insurers in Oklahoma paid out $129 in claims for every $100 of premium collected, by the department's May 2025 account as an insurance agency quotes it [^78]. A law signed May 14, 2026 requires rate changes to be filed 30 days before use in a competitive market and 60 days in a non-competitive one, but it takes effect only on July 1, 2027 [^19]. All of those are homeowners figures; the research did not find commercial property rate filings for 2025 or 2026.

Median household income stood at $65,039 in the 2020 to 2024 five-year American Community Survey estimate [^39]. On labor cost, State Question 832, which would have raised the minimum wage and then indexed it to prices, failed at the June 16, 2026 election [^35], and the minimum in 2026 follows the federal rate, adopted by reference [^79].

SBA set July 21, 2025 as the deadline for physical disaster loans and February 23, 2026 for economic injury loans [^25]. The storms of July 4, 2026 became DR-4950-OK on October 8, 2026; Cleveland, Nowata, Osage and Washington counties receive Public Assistance under it [^26]. Wind and hail make up 85% of Oklahoma homeowners claims, the Insurance Commissioner told NonDoc in 2025 [^18].

Health care and senior housing

Oklahoma keeps a Certificate of Need program for part of the health care market. The State Department of Health rule text covers long-term care, meaning nursing facility and ICF/IID beds, psychiatric and chemical-dependency units, and acquisitions of covered facilities [^29]. The department's application instructions, revised March 5, 2025, set the fee for a psychiatric or chemical-dependency project at 0.75% of capital cost, with a $1,500 minimum and a $10,000 maximum, and for long-term care at a $1,000 minimum with no maximum [^76]. Whether the psychiatric part still applies is unresolved: a secondary source reports that HB 2330 repealed it in May 2024, while the department's 2025 form still refers to it [^30]. The department licenses senior and care settings as nursing facilities, assisted living centers, residential care homes, continuum of care facilities, adult day care and specialised Alzheimer's facilities [^31]. The research found no bed or unit thresholds for assisted living or memory care.

Short-term rentals and alcohol

Short-term rentals have no state preemption and no enabling statute. HB 2800 lost its rental language and died in 2025, SB 1519 died in committee [^33], and HB 3296, which would have authorised local bans, was only introduced in 2024 [^34]. Retail liquor licensing has no quota system, with fixed fees under the state alcohol code as rewritten after a state question vote, according to a secondary guide [^32]; the research did not find a record of license transfer prices.

Child care

Three settings need a Human Services license: a family child care home for up to 7 children, a large family child care home for 8 to 12, and a center, for which the rules set no statutory capacity cap; no provider may accept children until the department has given permission [^28]. Those rules took effect November 1, 2024 [^28].

The Department of Human Services' licensing data show capacity falling in both settings between FY2023 and FY2024, to 112,781 places in centers and 15,325 in homes, with 2,053 more classed as exempt in FY2024 [^27].
