Alcohol: one wholesaler, and its 2026 backlog

Mississippi is a control state. ABC, a division of the Department of Revenue, is the sole wholesaler of spirits and wine and issues the state's alcoholic beverage permits, each sold at one combined price that takes in the state privilege tax, the local tax and an application fee [^1]. Its warehouse at Gluckstadt is state-owned and has been operated by Ruan Transport since March 2023; early in 2026 new software there turned out to be incompatible with the conveyor system [^40]. Mississippi Today counted the backlog at 170,000 cases on April 7, 2026, reported that the Legislature had failed to pass an emergency measure letting businesses buy straight from distributors, and reported borrowing of $95 million authorized for a new warehouse [^2]. In March it had described a new warehouse under construction as a $55 million project with double the current capacity [^40]; WLBT reported in June that the new warehouse was expected to be operational in 2027, and that by May 24 the backlog was just under 106,000 cases [^41]. On October 12, 2026 a federal judge denied the operator's motion to dismiss the class action over the shortage [^3].

A retailer's permit does not pass with a sale: a new owner files a fresh application, and a temporary retailer's permit can cover the changeover [^42]. Since July 1, 2025 a direct wine shipper permit lets a resident receive up to 12 nine-liter cases a year, by Park Street's account [^33]. No numeric quota on retail permits, and no price for a permit changing hands, was found.

Certificate of Need after HB 3

Governor Reeves signed HB 3 on February 4, 2026. It confines UMMC's academic exemption from Certificate of Need to the Jackson area and asks the State Department of Health to study exempting small hospitals for dialysis and adult psychiatric services, reporting by December 1, 2026 [^4,43]. Mississippi Today reported a capital threshold of $10 million for clinical improvements under the new law [^44]. The Center for Mississippi Health Policy's June 2026 report lists psychiatric residential treatment facilities, nursing facilities and long-term care hospitals in specific counties among the facilities the bill reaches, beside two other 2026 bills: HB 41 as introduced, to take ICF/IID facilities, intermediate care for people with intellectual disabilities, out of the program, and HB 1622, passed by the House, to exempt rural hospitals within a 5-mile radius of their main campus [^45,46]. The statute's full list of reviewable facility types was not found in the sources reviewed, and neither were licensing categories or thresholds for assisted living and memory care.

Child care

Child care centers are licensed by the State Department of Health under the Mississippi Child Care Licensing Law, and a center's indoor capacity is set from its square footage on the Department's capacity worksheet [^47]. The regulation of January 2020 sets the licence fee by capacity: $97.50 up to 12 children, $195.00 for 13 to 30, $260.00 for 31 to 50 and $390.00 for 51 to 100, with higher tiers above that [^48]. In 2023 County Business Patterns counted 808 child day care establishments with paid employees in Mississippi [^49].

Mississippi taxes non-residential construction through a contractor's tax on the contract, collected in place of sales tax on the component materials, which the contractor buys under a Material Purchase Certificate [^16]. Contracts above a set value must be prepaid or bonded with the Department of Revenue. Residential work is excluded, but apartment and condominium construction carries the contractor's tax [^16].

Furniture, fixtures and equipment bought for a project pay the general sales tax rate, while manufacturing machinery and repair parts pay a reduced rate and raw materials used in manufacturing are exempt [^54]. Purchases financed with Mississippi Business Finance Corporation industrial revenue bonds, and certified data center computing equipment, can qualify for exemption [^54,55]. No published effective tax rate on commercial property was found for the state, nor an approved commercial property rate change or a carrier withdrawal dated 2025 or 2026.

On the coast, wind and hail cover comes from the Mississippi Windstorm Underwriting Association where the voluntary market does not write it. The statute requires the association's rates to be at least adequate to buy reinsurance for the 100-year probable maximum loss, a standard that lengthens by 5 years every 2 years [^17]. The Insurance Department's December 2023 bulletin recorded a flat 50.0% increase in the association's commercial property rates from February 1, 2024, and an overall -6.0% loss cost change for AAIS commercial property filings in the voluntary market from April 1, 2024 [^18]. Residential fire and extended coverage has its own residual market, the Mississippi Residential Property Insurance Underwriting Association [^56].

On December 11, 2025 the Governor announced more than $100 million of MDA, ARC, DRA, RESTORE, GOMESA and SCRC grants for site development, infrastructure and workforce, among them $20,891,932 from ARC and $3,918,949 from SCRC [^20].

SBA's Mississippi District Office serves the entire state from offices in Jackson and Gulfport, under District Director Robert Coffey [^89]. SBA's loan-level FOIA data count 3,210 loans under 7(a) for Mississippi projects approved between fiscal 2016 and fiscal 2025, after cancellations are removed, with loan status as of June 30, 2026 [^66].

The FY2025 year-end activity reports, issued on November 13, 2025 with data to September 30, 2025, were not read into this page, so it prints no single-year state total [^90]; the district's own lender report is published only as an interactive dashboard [^91]. Coleman Report's ranking for fiscal 2023 put Peoples Bank first among Mississippi 7(a) lenders, with 61 loans for $25,154,700 [^92].

The rule under which SBA can call for a feasibility study is 13 CFR 120.160(b): SBA may require professional appraisals, a survey, or a feasibility study [^93]. Loan tests come from SOP 50 10 8.1 with Technical Policy Updates [^94], which our SBA 7(a) and 504 practice page walks through.
