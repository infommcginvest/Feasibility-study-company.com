Las Vegas is a drive market and a fly market at once, and the visitor authority counts both doors. On I-15 at the Nevada and California border, NDOT's counts in the authority's 2025 summary give an average of 43,301 vehicles a day, 1.7% fewer than in 2024, with July and August left out of the average because of construction at Primm [^1]. Harry Reid International handled 54,988,547 passengers in 2025, 5.9% fewer than the year before [^1]. The road count is taken where the drive market from California meets the state line.

Visitor numbers fell in 2025: the authority counted 38,545,700 visitors in Las Vegas in 2025, 7.5% fewer than in 2024 [^1]. Those are visitors, not residents, and none of them is in the population figures further down. The authority's portion of room tax collections was $34,630,032 in October 2025 [^1]; the record holds no dated local room tax rate for Las Vegas, so the authority's collections are the dated measure of what the rooms bring in. The county figures on this page are Clark County's, and Clark County alone makes up the federal Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas metro; the brokers read their own Las Vegas market, and DETR's employer list is its Las Vegas list; the Laughlin resort town, also in Clark County, is read on its own figures in the Nevada page's Laughlin section, and nothing is counted in both places.

Las Vegas had 150,300 hotel rooms in December 2025 [^1]. For the year, the visitor authority's table puts occupancy at 80.3% across Las Vegas, 3.3 points lower than in 2024, at 83.2% on the Strip and at 67.5% downtown [^1]. Rates split the same way: an average daily rate of $183.52 for Las Vegas as a whole, 5.0% lower than in 2024, against $196.54 on the Strip and $97.98 downtown [^1]. Revenue per available room was $147.30 for Las Vegas as a whole, 8.8% lower, and $163.52 on the Strip [^1].

The Review-Journal reported a RevPAR of $158.62 for 2025 [^11], which does not match the authority's table; this page uses the table's $147.30, the publisher's own figure, and a study should say which it took.

In February 2026 Las Vegas hotels ran at 81.7% occupancy, with rooms averaging $193.23 a night and RevPAR at $157.87, 5.3% above February 2025, by the authority's monthly executive summary [^12]. That is one month's report and is not set against the year's figures here.

No list of hotels opening in 2025 or 2026 and no pipeline count was found. The authority's room inventory report of September 18, 2025 and its tourism construction bulletin are the records a hotel study reads for that [^13]. The Nevada hotel case is set in a Las Vegas submarket, and the hotel page sets out how a hotel study is built.

Colliers' second-quarter 2026 report, on RealPage data, puts apartment occupancy at 94.5%, 0.6 points lower than a year earlier, and the average effective rent at $1,433 per unit, 2.7% lower over the year; the same report states the rent as $1,431 in another place [^2]. Construction has slowed: 4,206 units were under way in the quarter, against 6,712 a year before [^2].

Northmarq reads the market on a different definition. Its stabilized vacancy was 9.4% at the end of 2025, and it counts nearly 4,000 units delivered in 2025, about 30% fewer than the year before; in the first half of 2026, 2,000 units were delivered and 1,800 absorbed, and it expected roughly 4,200 deliveries over 2026 [^5]. Colliers' occupancy and Northmarq's stabilized vacancy are not the same measure, so neither is turned into the other.

CBRE's second-quarter 2026 figures place about three quarters of the quarter's absorption in North Las Vegas and the bulk of the 1.3 million square feet delivered at Apex, with the Airport and Southwest submarkets the tightest [^3]. CBRE puts vacancy at 9.0%, up 20 basis points on the quarter, and counts 5.9 million square feet under construction [^3].

Colliers' report for the same quarter has vacancy at 9.1%, a triple-net asking rent of $1.10 a square foot and net absorption of 762,333 square feet [^6]. CBRE's 9.0% and Colliers' 9.1% rest on each broker's own definitions, so a warehouse study names the broker and, for a site in North Las Vegas or Apex, reads that submarket's own row. The industrial and warehouse page sets out the method.

Retail space was tight in the second quarter of 2026. Colliers puts vacancy at 4.5% and records a triple-net asking rent of $2.12 per square foot, a record for the market [^16], and CBRE's reading in Nevada Business's ReD Report is 5.0% [^17]. A center that depends on visitors reads those vacancies against the visitor counts above, and one that serves residents reads them against the county's household figures. The retail page covers the method, and the Nevada retail case works one grocery-anchored center through to its loan.

No headcount for the county's hospitals or medical anchors was found. Colliers' medical office report stands in: 8.77 million square feet of medical office space in the second quarter of 2026, at 8.3% vacancy [^18]. A medical office study starts from that inventory and the health systems' own announcements.

The Department of Taxation's certified estimate puts Clark County at 2,448,576 residents on July 1, 2025, against 2,265,461 in the 2020 Census and 2,320,107 in the state's July 2020 estimate [^4]. Its principal places, on the same date: the city of Las Vegas 687,050, Henderson 363,568, North Las Vegas 292,114, Enterprise 259,959, Spring Valley 227,835 and Paradise 191,215 [^4]. The Census Bureau's own Vintage 2025 figure for the county reaches the record only secondhand, so it is not printed; the Nevada page sets the two state series side by side.

DETR counted 1,186.9 thousand seasonally adjusted nonfarm jobs in the Las Vegas metro in August 2026, up 14,200, or 1.2%, from August 2025 [^20], and the area's unemployment rate was 4.8% [^21].

DETR's 2026 Las Vegas list, drawn from an establishment database, gives bands rather than headcounts. Clark County's own offices come first at 10,000 or more. The Las Vegas Metropolitan Police Department and the Flamingo, MGM Grand and Orleans resorts each have 5,000 to 9,999 employees, and Las Vegas Sands, Mandalay Bay and Caesars Palace sit in the same band; UNLV has 1,000 to 4,999 [^22]. Nellis Air Force Base is listed at 10,000 or more on DETR's 2024 statewide list [^23]. Exact headcounts are not published, so an employer-driven demand case cites the band and the release.

New investment shows up in two records. Colliers' second-quarter 2026 report counts $19.8 million of GOED abatements approved for the Las Vegas incentive pipeline, with 534 projected jobs, and Haas Automation's plant in Henderson nearing completion at a value not published [^24]. No list of the county's large projects with their values was found.

No USDA eligibility, Opportunity Zone or HUBZone result for a Clark County parcel is in the record, so each is a lookup on the federal map for the subject's tract.

About 90% of the Southern Nevada Water Authority's supply comes from the Colorado River, by its fiscal 2022 federal grant application, and under AB 356 that water may not irrigate non-functional turf on property other than single-family homes after December 31, 2026 [^7]. A district judge's temporary restraining order of January 29, 2026 barred the authority from enforcing its turf designations pending a hearing [^8]. For a hotel, apartment complex, office park or shopping center with ornamental grass, the designation of the parcel and the state of that case set the landscaping budget.

Clark County licenses short-term rentals under an ordinance adopted on June 21, 2022, which sets distances between rentals and from resort hotels and caps the number of licences [^9]. A federal judge froze the county's enforcement program in December 2025, and the Ninth Circuit heard argument in September 2026, by a broker's 2026 guide [^10]. In August 2026 the county was weighing code changes for data centers [^30].

The county's development fees were found only in an undated secondary guide, so this page prints none; a study takes the current schedule from the county and the city where the parcel sits.

The Nevada hotel case is the one worked case its own page places in Clark County: a new full-service hotel off the resort corridor in a Las Vegas submarket, with a senior construction loan and an EB-5 tranche in the capital stack. It is hypothetical, and it tests demand from market segmentation and the hotel's penetration of a competitive set, the coverage the senior lender documents, and the job count the EB-5 filing needs. The hotel tables in this page's hotels section are the county record it sits in.

The Nevada retail case works a new grocery-anchored center on the suburban edge of a larger Nevada metro its page does not name, under a bank's construction-to-permanent loan. The Nevada page describes both.
