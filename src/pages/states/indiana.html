Indiana counted 6,973,333 people on July 1, 2025, 2.8% above the April 2020 estimates base of 6,786,605 [^3], and the latest year alone added 38,579 [^5]. The newsroom that reported that gain called the Indianapolis area key to it [^5], and the Indianapolis page follows that growth out to a drug plant and a data center campus in Boone County. Elsewhere the growth the research documents stands beside particular projects: a memory-chip plant and one winter month of apartment permits in West Lafayette, battery plants in Kokomo whose 2026 status the research could not confirm, and a hyperscale campus planned next to shrinking lakefront cities in Lake County. A rate computed for the whole state describes none of those trade areas.

What changed most between 2025 and 2026 is how much of a site's outlook now rests on a public decision with a date: a county board's vote, a city's pause, a statute's effective date, a federal declaration. The rules, costs, hazards and events sections below date each one, and every market section says which of them reach its counties. Until a study places its parcel against the decision that governs it, the site is undescribed.

Four places on this page belong to federal metros that cross a state line, and each section below opens with its Indiana-only scope: Lake and Porter counties in the Chicago region, the South Bend metro with Cass County, Michigan, Evansville with its Kentucky side, and Clark County across the river from Louisville. Counties across the eastern line are Ohio's.

  • 504 debentures Indiana Statewide CDC, then SomerCor
  • Rural guarantees USDA, outside the larger cities
  • State layer Legend Fund, IFA bonds, IHCDA credits
  • Agency and EB-5 no Indiana office or project count found

Indiana's 2025 gain had three sources, and the Indiana Business Research Center's reading of the Vintage 2025 estimates shows each moving its own way. Arrivals from abroad, net of departures, added 17,852 people, a figure 53% below the prior year's [^1]. Net domestic migration climbed to 12,197 from 6,024 [^1]. Births over deaths contributed 8,561, down 7% [^1]. Projects that sell to newcomers from abroad, apartments at a campus edge or beside a large employer among them, need a case in which that inflow keeps falling; a suburban childcare center or grocery-anchored pad leans more on households moving within the country, the source that rose.

Payrolls run the other way. Over the twelve months to August 2026 Indiana lost 16.1 thousand nonfarm jobs, leaving 3,266.5 thousand on the preliminary, seasonally adjusted count [^2]. Government carried the loss. In the Joint Economic Committee's August figures, state and local payrolls were 18,500 lower over that year, while private employers as a group were 4,500 higher [^6]. The private gains came in trade, transportation and utilities (5,500) and in private education and health services (6,700), and leisure and hospitality lost 13,600 [^6]. A restaurant, hotel or attraction therefore has to show from local evidence why its trade area escapes its own sector's statewide decline.

For affordable housing, IHCDA's 2025 plan requires a bond-financed share of 50% to 55% for 4% credits paired with tax-exempt bonds [^15], a test the study's cost and sources tables must be built to meet.

Behind Indiana Statewide CDC, which tops the district's 504 awards (see the capital section), the loan-level aggregation ranks SomerCor second in Indiana 504 lending since FY2010 [^12]. On the bank side, 1st Source Bank took the Community Lender Gold award for a twelfth straight year, by its own March 2025 release [^74].

Under SOP 50 10 8.1 [^16], a 504 project carries 15% for a business in its first two years of operation or a building that is limited or special purpose, and 20% when it is both. Our worked 504 storage example takes a facility with no operating history through the 504 split at the new-business tier, covering debt service on the bank's first lien and on the debenture from projected revenue with that equity in place.

The rest of the SBA rule set, from coverage tests to loan limits and fees, is the same in every state and sits under SBA 7(a) and 504 lending. Whether a given Indiana file needs a feasibility study at all is a question 13 CFR 120.160(b) leaves to SBA, which may require one [^17]; our note on that paragraph reads its text.

The research's Indiana Business and Industry total has no fixable fiscal year, so it is left out, and no FY2025 Community Facilities total was found; current obligations come from the state office.

Indiana projects meet two different rural tests, and a study should keep them apart. USDA's is a population test: the research reads it as excluding Fort Wayne, South Bend, Mishawaka, Elkhart, Evansville, Columbus, Kokomo, Lafayette, Hammond, Gary, Carmel and Fishers, with the urbanized areas next to them, though it cites no USDA document for the threshold; outer townships, and small towns such as those around Warsaw, can qualify subject to the agency's map, read parcel by parcel. SBA's FY2027 fee notice uses another: on 7(a) loans of $700,000 or less it waives the upfront fee for a business in a rural area, judged by the Census Bureau's rural share for the county [^18]. A parcel can pass one test and fail the other, so the sources and uses should say which test each saving rests on. USDA's own study requirement is described, for any state, under USDA Rural Development; the five USDA components, worked through and the funding calendar cover the rest.

Those four HUD analyses cover Indianapolis as of January 1, 2026 [^47], Fort Wayne in September 2024 [^52], Evansville in January 2024 [^63] and Gary as of April 1, 2023 [^57]. A market study for an agency loan in one of those markets should reconcile its vacancy and absorption with HUD's, or explain the difference. Which HUD multifamily office has Indiana jurisdiction is not in the research, nor any Indiana total for Section 221(d)(4), Section 232 or the Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac programs, so the sponsor confirms the office before scoping.

A study for a bank or life company loan answers the same core questions without the agency forms: where the trade area stops, which competitors sit inside it, how fast the latest projects leased and where coverage breaks; see conventional and institutional lending.

Restaurants, bars and hotels: the permit quota

A beer, wine and liquor permit cost $1,000 a year in 2025 [^20]. For a full-service restaurant or a hotel bar the permit decides whether the concept opens at all, so the study prices one of three routes: a quota permit still free in the town, a transfer bought from a holder, or a development-area permit. The research's only transfer prices, from an undated state report for Bloomington, give way to current ones from the Alcohol and Tobacco Commission.

Fuel stations: tanks and the trust fund

Underground storage tanks are regulated under 329 IAC 9 [^21]. The Excess Liability Trust Fund reimburses cleanup [^21]. How much the fund holds today is not in the research. A fuel and convenience study should ask IDEM, because slow reimbursement moves cleanup risk back onto the borrower and the collateral.

Lodging: state and county taxes

Wages

Indiana's wage statute sets three rates and indexes none of them: $2.13 in cash for tipped staff, $4.25 for workers under 20 in their first 90 days, and $7.25 an hour for everyone else, the level in force since July 24, 2009 [^23]. The statute therefore adds no scheduled step to an Indiana payroll line; wage growth entered in the projections is an assumption of the study, shown with its source.

Health care and senior care

Indiana's Certificate of Need reaches only comprehensive-care, that is nursing home, beds, and NCSL recorded no moratorium as of January 2025 [^24]. The research gives no licensing categories or size limits for assisted living homes or memory care homes. Since August 1, 2026 a six-month moratorium has barred new Medicaid providers of home and community-based services, after an FSSA audit of 5 attendant-care providers extrapolated $198,031,230.18 in improper payments, interest included, over January 2022 to March 2025 [^25]. A home care or adult day project counting on Medicaid enrollment needs an opening date after the freeze, or a payer mix that does without it.

Childcare

Senate Enrolled Act 463 (2025) introduced a new staff-to-child ratio chart, in force from December 1, 2025 [^26]. The new values are not in the research, so a childcare study takes the chart from the Office of Early Childhood, since ratios fix both staffing cost and licensed capacity.

Short-term rentals and rental caps

A 2018 law bars local bans on short-term rentals, and House Enrolled Act 1210, signed March 12, 2026, outlaws local limits on how many homes may be rented and takes bed and breakfasts out of the short-term rental definition [^27]. The law was scheduled to take effect on July 1, 2026, and ordinances adopted before January 1, 2026 must comply by January 1, 2028 [^28]. In leisure markets a hotel's competitive set grows as a result, and suburbs that used caps to hold back build-to-rent and single-family rental competitors lose that tool.

In 2025 Indiana defined a large load as 5% of a utility's peak or 150 MW, whichever is lower, and assigned 80% of the cost to the large-load customer [^29]. The expedited generation path in House Bill 1007 is voluntary, and by September 2026 no utility had used it [^29]. By an undated ServerCountry summary, Indiana Michigan Power's approved large-load tariff carries long terms, minimum demand charges, collateral and exit fees [^30], and in September 2026 the regulator was reconsidering a $71 million AES Indiana rate increase [^31]. A car wash, cold storage room, ice rink or hotel laundry sits far below those thresholds, but the AES case could reach its bill, so the study shows the utility line at the current tariff and a higher one.

Property tax is a strength. The Tax Foundation's 2026 index ranks Indiana 4th on property tax and 10th overall [^32]. No 2025 effective commercial rate was retrieved, so the subject's bill and any abatement are the county auditor's numbers, not a statewide average. Nor does the research price construction in Indiana cities, say how sales tax treats building materials and business equipment, give impact fee ranges or prevailing wage rules on state-assisted work, or describe the commercial insurance market; a sponsor relying on a national assumption for any of these should label it and test it.

DR-4882-IN, declared July 22, 2025 for storms between March 30 and April 9, 2025, made Public Assistance available in 25 counties [^33]. DR-4933-IN followed on August 25, 2026, for storms and flooding from August 11 to 24, with Individual Assistance in 21 counties and Public Assistance in 11 [^34]; Marion, Hamilton, Lake, Porter and LaPorte are among the Individual Assistance counties [^34]. SBA's notice of September 14, 2026 set disaster loan deadlines of October 25, 2026 for physical damage and May 25, 2027 for economic injury [^35].

Where Individual Assistance was granted, the storm reaches a pro forma twice: households waiting on repairs take apartments, extended-stay rooms and storage space for a season, so the first year is shown with and without that season, and the insurance line rests on a premium quoted after August 2026 rather than a budget carried over from before the storm. The state's emergency agency logged two smaller 2026 events: the January 23 winter storm, which drew a statewide state declaration and a federal emergency declaration, and March 10 storms that led to a state declaration for Jasper, Newton and Starke counties [^36]. The research retrieved no FEMA National Risk Index score for any Indiana county.
