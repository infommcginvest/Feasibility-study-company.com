The property tax law in the first screen reaches every commercial project in Montana. HB 231 and SB 542 put commercial and industrial property on two statutory rates split by market value from tax year 2025, and from 2026 they tax a home that is neither a principal residence nor in long-term rental, which takes in short-term rentals, at a residential rate of its own [^1]. A pro forma drafted before May 13, 2025, when the bills became law, carries a tax line written for the old schedule.

On the demand side the population rose because people moved in: net migration of +7,247 in the year to July 2025 against a gain of +7,137, with natural change at -90 [^4,5]. The payroll count for August 2026, set beside August 2025 in the payroll section, is the other demand record, and the sectors behind it point different asset classes in different directions.

The third thread is local. In 2025 and 2026 data center proposals reached Yellowstone, Cascade, Silver Bow and Missoula counties, and county commissioners, a ballot initiative and the Montana Supreme Court shaped what became of them [^15,16,13]. They bear on any project that needs land in an industrial park, a large water allocation or a new electric load.

The seven markets on this page stand on population, payroll and local rules. A reading on hotels, self-storage, industrial or retail space or senior housing for any of them was not found in a public primary source, and each section says what its own record holds. The Legislative Fiscal Division's summary of July 11, 2025 sets out what HB 231 and SB 542 did. For tax year 2025 commercial and industrial property pays 1.40% on market value up to $400,000 and 1.89% on the excess, and the graded structure continues into 2026 [^1]. On the residential side the summary gives a first-tier rate of 0.76% for 2025, and for 2026 a rate of 1.90% for non-principal residences and homes not in long-term rental, against 1.10% for multifamily property with qualifying long-term rentals [^1].

Three consequences follow for a budget. A small shop or office building and a large plant do not face one rate, so the tax line is computed on the assessed value split at the line, not at an average. An apartment owner's rate depends on whether the units qualify as long-term rentals. And a short-term rental, or a cabin held as a second home, is taxed at the higher residential rate from 2026, which a lodging or rental pro forma for Bozeman, Kalispell or a resort county has to carry.

Montana has no general sales tax. NexusByState quotes the Department of Revenue that the state "does not have a general-use sales tax", so construction materials and business equipment bought in Montana carry none [^17]. Lodging is taxed instead: the Department of Revenue levies a lodging facility use tax and a lodging sales tax on stays [^18], campgrounds are subject to the state lodging taxes [^19], and a local resort tax applies where a resort community has enacted one [^17]. A hotel or campground budget adds its town's resort tax, where there is one, to the two state taxes.

The state's hourly minimum went to $10.85 on January 1, 2026, from $10.55, with no tip credit, as the Ekalaka Eagle reported on January 9, 2026 [^20]. A restaurant, hotel or care operator in Montana budgets the full hourly rate for tipped staff.

Insurance cost could not be documented the same way. Approved commercial property rate changes, carrier withdrawals and an insurer of last resort for Montana were not found, nor were impact fee ranges outside Bozeman or prevailing wage requirements on projects with state assistance. Health and senior care

Under Montana's Certificate of Need law, review reaches long-term care facilities and nursing homes, the use of hospital beds for skilled or intermediate care, home health service-area expansion, inpatient chemical dependency services and certain acquisitions of a long-term care facility; it excludes residential care, which is assisted living, and hotels [^62]. SB 88, signed on April 3, 2025 and effective July 1, 2025, moved letter-of-intent notices to the program's website [^62]. Assisted living is licensed in categories A to D under the 2025 rules, Category C being memory care with dementia-specific training [^63]. A memory care project therefore goes to licensure, not to a need review; a new nursing home goes to both.

Liquor

All-beverage and on-premises beer and wine licences are capped by a population-based quota, and new quota licences are sold at auction under HB 2 of the 2017 special session, with a minimum bid set from the department's determination of market value [^8,64]. The average licence up for auction in January 2025 cost $275,472, as The Montana Chronicles reported from Department of Revenue data, a secondary source [^7]. A price for licences changing hands between holders was not found in a primary source, so a restaurant or hotel bar budget states the licence it will buy and the source of its price.
