On January 8, 2025 a major disaster, DR-4856-CA, was declared for wildfires and straight-line winds in Los Angeles County, for an incident period that ran from January 7 to 31 [^10]. SBA's matching disaster declaration named Los Angeles as the primary county and Kern, Orange, San Bernardino and Ventura as contiguous ones [^11]. When the Department of Finance published its January 2026 estimates, it counted 11,160 Los Angeles County housing units lost to the Eaton and Palisades fires and attributed the county's 2025 population decline to the fires, to ordinary movement into the Inland Empire and to deportations [^3].

The insurance record moved in dated steps. A $1 billion assessment on the FAIR Plan's member insurers was approved in February 2025 [^12]. On March 31, 2025 higher commercial limits for the plan were approved [^13]. In October 2025 AB 226 allowed the plan to seek bond financing through IBank, the state's infrastructure and economic development bank [^1]. The dwelling increase approved in April 2026 reaches policies from October 15, 2026 [^2,14].

The Los Angeles County section carries the county's population and housing counts, and the insurance section the plan's own record.

The California FAIR Plan Association is the state's insurer of last resort, organised under Insurance Code section 10090 and the sections after it [^15]. The Assembly Insurance Committee's 2026 update counts 668,609 policies in force and $724 billion of exposure in December 2025 [^1].

For dwellings, the Department of Insurance approved an average increase of 29.1% against the 35.8% the plan had asked for, effective for new and renewal policies on or after October 15, 2026 [^2]. The department's list of approved filings shows the filing closed as approved on April 6, 2026 [^14].

For commercial property, Division I limits of $20 million per building and $100 million per location were approved on March 31, 2025, to be available within 120 days [^13]. Among the department's 2026 approvals up to August 31, 2026, none is a commercial FAIR Plan rate filing [^14]. No source gathered for this page names a carrier that withdrew from California in 2025 or 2026.

The plan's own finances changed in the same period. AB 226, by Calderon and Alvarez and signed in October 2025, lets it seek bond financing and credit through IBank, with the Insurance Commissioner's approval [^1]. The February 2025 assessment of $1 billion was charged to the insurers that make up the plan's membership, under an order of February 11, 2025 [^12].

Child care and senior care

A small family child care home may care for up to 8 children and a large one for up to 14 children, and a tenant operator needs the owner's written consent above 6 or 12 children, under Health and Safety Code sections 1597.44(d) and 1597.465(d) [^32]. Before a licence issues, the Community Care Licensing Division runs an orientation, takes the application and inspects the premises, licensing guides report [^33]. A staffing ratio for child care centers from a primary source was not found. Nor were the scope of any Certificate of Need review or the licence categories and thresholds for assisted living and memory care, in a state whose residents aged 65 and over number 6,158,371 in the 2020 to 2024 American Community Survey [^22].

Liquor licences

On-sale and off-sale general licences are capped by each county's population under Business and Professions Code sections 23816 and 23817, which makes the general licence types quota licences that ABC issues new by priority drawing [^34]. The state fee and the private transfer prices that licence guides quote carry no date, and ABC's county quota reports are the record a restaurant or bar file reads.
