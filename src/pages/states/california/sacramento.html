In the Department of Finance's May 2026 release, Sacramento County added more residents over 2025 than any other of California's ten most populous counties, and Placer County grew faster than any county in the state, in a year when the state's own January estimate fell [^1]. El Dorado County, the fourth county of the market, went the other way, and Yolo County barely moved [^1]. Within the region the gains were visible in the cities too: the City of Sacramento and Folsom each added residents, and Roseville and Elk Grove added 1,151 and 1,138 single-family homes [^1].

The region's dated supply record is narrower than its population record. It holds three brokers' readings of the same industrial quarter, a city storage pipeline counted twice in one year, and a metro storage pipeline, and it holds no apartment, hotel or senior housing count for any of the four counties.

Industrial

CBRE puts Sacramento industrial vacancy at 6.0% in the second quarter of 2026, with 780,000 square feet of net absorption and an asking rent of $0.81 per square foot a month, triple net [^7]. For the same quarter Kidder Mathews reads direct vacancy at 6.5% [^8], and Colliers 7.5%, down 70 basis points on the quarter [^9]. Each is its publisher's own measure, and this page does not combine them. The absorption figures differ in the same way: CBRE's 780,000 square feet of net absorption [^7] and Colliers' 1.05 million square feet of warehouse net absorption [^9] are two firms' counts for the same quarter.

Self-storage

In the City of Sacramento, RentCafe's August 2026 report expects 139,233 square feet of new storage space to open during 2026, about 2% of inventory, in a city with 5.1 square feet of storage per resident [^5]. Its March 2026 report had counted 230,919 square feet for the city for the same year [^6]. Across the whole metro, StorageCafe's 2026 supply report counts 481,000 square feet due, also about 2% of inventory [^4].

Senior housing and the other classes

No Sacramento count was found for apartments, hotels, retail, senior housing, RV parks or gas stations. For senior housing the nearest dated figure is the survey's count of 404,158 residents aged 65 and over in the four counties [^12].

Peck CRE's 2026 industrial guide places most of the market's new industrial development, and its largest rise in vacancy, along I-5 north of downtown Sacramento, and describes West Sacramento, between the river and I-80, as supply-constrained and tight [^11]. That is a broker's map of the market, not a traffic record.

The only Caltrans station count found anywhere in the market, on I-5 at Elk Grove Boulevard, is a 2011 volume in a District 3 data sheet [^14]. Caltrans publishes 2024 counts in its AADT dataset [^15]; current station counts for this market were not drawn from it, so no 2026 traffic figure is given here.

EDD's release of July 17, 2026 counts 1,112,700 nonfarm jobs in the Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom metro in June 2026, 7,600 more than a year earlier, a gain of 0.7% [^3]. A dated list of the market's largest employers with headcounts was not found, and neither was a register of the region's large announced projects.

The demand the record does document is industrial. Colliers' second-quarter 2026 report describes large leases and move-ins in West Sacramento and Woodland, and counts 1.05 million square feet of warehouse net absorption in the quarter [^9].

In the federal income series, BEA puts per capita personal income in the four counties at $74,552 in 2024 [^13], and the American Community Survey's 2020 to 2024 estimate puts median household income at $97,188 [^12]. The same survey counts a resident labor force of 1,216,191 over 2020 to 2024 [^12]: people counted where they live, where EDD's payroll count is of jobs where they are, for a different period.

The Department of Finance's May 2026 release counts 1,628,349 residents in Sacramento County on January 1, 2026, up 0.58% from 1,619,023 a year earlier [^1]. Placer County went from 428,880 to 434,823 over the same year, Yolo County from 225,554 to 225,904, and El Dorado County from 190,397 to 189,852 [^1]. The release names Placer the fastest-growing county in California, and Sacramento County the largest numeric gainer among the state's ten most populous counties [^1].

That gain came in a year when the state's own January estimate fell, as the California page records. Among the region's cities, the City of Sacramento held 539,765 residents on January 1, 2026, after a gain of 6,809 over 2025, and Folsom 95,680, after a gain of 2,001 [^1]. The same release records 1,151 new single-family homes in Roseville and 1,138 in Elk Grove during 2025 [^1].

An April 2020 count for the four counties was not retrieved, so the record here runs one year, from January 2025 to January 2026, and no five-year growth figure is given.

Sacramento County had 612,589 housing units on January 1, 2025 and 622,665 a year later [^1]. In calendar 2025 it permitted 6,561 residential units, 4,629 of them single-family and 1,932 multifamily, in Census Building Permits Survey figures that EDD published on September 23, 2026 [^2]. A calendar 2025 permit total for Placer County was not found.

Across the four counties, the 2020 to 2024 American Community Survey counts 881,889 households, 326,239 of them in renter-occupied homes and 555,650 in owner-occupied ones [^12]. Renters who pay cash have a median gross rent of $1,819, and owner-occupied homes carry a median value of $589,500 [^12]. Those are five-year estimates for 2020 to 2024, a different period from the single-year permit and population counts above.

No count of apartment vacancy, asking rent or units under construction was found for any of the four counties, so the survey's rent and the county permits are the housing record this page holds.

Sacramento County offers pre-approved plans for detached accessory dwelling units, funded through a LEAP grant, and they apply in the unincorporated county only [^16].

Two statewide records bear on projects here as anywhere in California. The FAIR Plan's average dwelling increase applies to new and renewal dwelling policies from October 15, 2026, as the California page's insurance section sets out, and single-walled underground fuel tanks had to be closed by December 31, 2025, under the state rules by asset class; the 2026 wage floor is in the state taxes and labor section. The City of Sacramento's 2026 hotel tax rate was not found in a primary source, and impact fees, zoning changes, short-term rental rules and water and sewer limits were not found for any of the four counties.

The four counties sit in SBA's Sacramento District, whose office also serves Amador, Butte, Nevada, San Joaquin, Shasta, Sutter and Yuba counties, among others [^10]. SBA's loan-level files hold 4,114 loans under 7(a) for projects in those counties, approved in fiscal years 2016 to 2025 for $2,301.3 million, and $795.6 million of 504 debentures over the same years, cancelled loans left out [^17]. A ranking of the lenders and development companies active in the market was not found; the state's fiscal 2025 lender table is on the California page, and SBA's own program tests are explained on the SBA 7(a) and 504 lending page.

Whether the outlying parts of El Dorado, Placer and Yolo counties are rural for USDA purposes, and where Opportunity Zone tracts and HUBZones fall in the four counties, was not settled by the sources gathered here.

The assisted living and memory care case, worked under HUD 232 and conventional financing, is the California case nearest this page's count of residents aged 65 and over. The SBA 504 case on RV and boat storage rests its demand on households moving inland, and greater Sacramento is among the destinations its page names. Neither case page places its subject inside these four counties, and neither carries this page's figures; each sets out its own market evidence and its own loan test. The state's third case and the full list are on the California page.
