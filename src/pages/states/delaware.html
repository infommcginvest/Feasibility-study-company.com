The contested project in Delaware's 2025 and 2026 record turned on where an industrial use may go, not on demand. Starwood Digital Ventures filed Project Washington in July 2025: 11 buildings and about 6 million square feet of data center space with 1.2 GW of planned capacity on roughly 580 acres near New Castle and Delaware City [^1]. The developer spoke of an economic impact of $9.7 billion, 3,500 construction jobs and $75 million a year in local taxes, none of it verified [^1]. On February 3, 2026 the Department of Natural Resources and Environmental Control ruled that the use is not allowed in the coastal zone under the 1971 Coastal Zone Act, pointing to 516 backup diesel generators and more than 2.5 million gallons of stored diesel [^2]. The Coastal Zone Industrial Control Board upheld the ruling unanimously, Starwood dropped its Superior Court appeal, and on August 28, 2026 Spotlight Delaware reported that the project cannot move forward under its current design [^2].

The filing drew three answers before the ruling: a New Castle County ordinance, a large-load tariff proposal before the Public Service Commission, and SB 205, a bill that would let the commission approve or refuse new large-load customers partly on what they would do to household electric rates [^7,2]. No figure for Delaware's place in the PJM interconnection queue was found, so the tariff proposal and the bill are the dated record of the state's response to a large new electric load.

The Census Bureau's 2025 delineation places Delaware's three counties in three different kinds of area [^8]. Kent County is the entire Dover metro area. Sussex County is the Seaford micro area by itself, and the Salisbury metro is now Wicomico and Somerset counties in Maryland, which our Maryland page reads in its Eastern Shore section; no Sussex figure on this page belongs to a Maryland area. New Castle County is the one Delaware county of the Philadelphia-Camden-Wilmington metro, which also takes in Cecil County, Maryland, four New Jersey counties and five Pennsylvania counties [^8]. The Pennsylvania page and the New Jersey page each read counties of their own state in that metro, and every New Castle figure here is the county's alone.

Below the county level the dated readings are few: the BLS summary for Dover, Sussex County's budget and ordinances, and a set of project announcements whose sizes and costs describe single properties. The market sections give each with its date.

The Vintage 2025 county table puts New Castle County at 588,026 people for July 1, 2025 and 570,722 for its April 1, 2020 estimates base [^9]. Permits issued there covered 1,322 housing units in 2025; the count was 1,586 in 2024 and 1,796 in 2020, according to the Census permit counts FRED publishes for the county [^6]. The 2020 to 2024 American Community Survey sets the county's median gross rent at $1,448, a median taken over renter-occupied units that pay cash rent [^10].

A hotel now stands beside Merck's campus at CRISP, the Chestnut Run Innovation & Science Park: a Residence Inn with 127 rooms opened there on June 16, 2026, and the Delaware Business Times described the campus as a $1 billion one in its report of the opening [^11]. No occupancy or rate series for Wilmington hotels was found.

The county's other anchors appear in broker reports without figures. CBRE's 2022 brief on Northern Delaware describes the Port of Wilmington as a full-service deep-water port [^12], and Newmark's first-quarter 2026 Wilmington report records Wilmington University's purchase of a fully vacant building in the fourth quarter of 2025 [^13].

BLS publishes payrolls for a Wilmington DE-MD-NJ metropolitan division, an area whose title takes in Maryland and New Jersey as well as Delaware, so its jobs series is not printed here as New Castle County's [^14]. New Castle County may levy a lodging tax of up to 3% under the 2024 law that gave the power to it and to Sussex County [^15]; an adopted New Castle rate was not found.

Dover City Council approved a moratorium on data center construction unanimously, the first by a Delaware municipality, Spotlight Delaware reported on October 13, 2026 [^16]. The same report describes Kent County's rules for data centers on unincorporated land: buffers, noise limits and a ban on cooling systems that use large amounts of water [^16]. A data center site in Kent is read against both.

Kent's Vintage 2025 estimate is 194,786 for July 1, 2025, against an April 2020 base of 181,849 [^9]. Kent builders drew permits for 1,261 units in 2025, set against 1,201 a year before and 2,136 in 2020 [^17]. In the 2020 to 2024 American Community Survey the county's median household income was $74,477 and its median gross rent $1,354 [^10].

Dover Air Force Base is on the federal CareerOneStop list of Delaware's largest employers, which gives no date for its counts [^18], and no headcount for Delaware State University was found. The BLS area summary of September 4, 2026 puts government at 20.9 thousand of the metro's 73.0 thousand nonfarm jobs in July 2026, with total payrolls up 0.4 thousand on the year [^5]. Education and health services held 12.5 thousand jobs and professional and business services 6.7 thousand, while leisure and hospitality, at 7.4 thousand, was down 2.6% on July 2025; unemployment in the Dover area was 5.9%, against 5.4% in July 2025, and in the city of Dover itself 7.3% [^5].

Bayhealth had 4,803 employees across its system in January 2025, the Delaware Business Times reported on October 17, 2025, with about 125 new positions planned for its Kent County expansion [^19]. The first phase of that expansion in Dover, announced that month, is a $248.67 million building of 225,000 square feet with 64 inpatient beds, set to start construction in late 2026 if approvals come; the system's chief executive put the need at 97 or more new inpatient beds by 2033 [^20].

Downtown Dover's master plan needs at least $500 million, of which more than $30 million had been secured by November 2025, with a parking garage under construction, and its later phases need wastewater improvements whose funding was not yet in place [^21]. In June 2026 the state announced that the Delaware State Housing Authority would buy Delaware State University's Living & Learning Commons on US 13 for $11.243 million to expand the Hope Center [^22], and in February the Sports Tourism Capital Investment Fund awarded Dover Motor Speedway $1 million [^23]. A new industrial park on Lafferty Lane in Dover already had a signed lease when the Delaware Business Times reported on it in January 2025 [^24].

The 2024 law that lets New Castle and Sussex counties levy a lodging tax gives Kent County no such power [^15], and no Dover city hotel tax was found, so a Kent hotel or rental budget carries the state's lodging taxes. Dover is also where USDA Rural Development keeps the one state office it runs for Delaware and Maryland [^25]. A program total that office reports for the pair is a two-state figure, and none is printed on this page as Delaware's.

Delaware levies no state or local sales tax. Sellers pay a gross receipts tax at rates that vary by activity instead, so the equipment and materials bought for a project carry no sales tax line in its budget [^26]. The Tax Foundation's 2026 edition lists the corporate income tax at 8.7%, on top of the gross receipts tax, puts the effective tax on owner-occupied homes at 0.50%, and ranks Delaware 24th on its State Tax Competitiveness Index [^27]; a commercial property rate was not found.

Lodging carries three layers. Hotel, motel and tourist home rents pay the state's 8% public accommodations tax [^28]. Short-term rental agreements entered on or after January 1, 2025 pay a separate state lodging tax of 4.5%, New Castle and Sussex counties may add up to 3%, and accommodations intermediaries need a $25 annual license [^29]. Towns add their own: Lewes charges 5% on rentals [^28], and a secondary summary of August 2026 gives Newark and Rehoboth Beach a 3% hotel levy each [^30]. A typical combined rate for the state as a whole was not found.

Delaware's wage floor reached $15.00 an hour on January 1, 2025, the last step of its schedule, and no increase is set for 2027 [^31]. Prevailing wage reaches a private project only where the state or a political subdivision is party to the contract and puts money into it: new construction above $500,000, or alteration, repair, renovation or demolition above $45,000. Its rates have followed collective bargaining agreements since January 1, 2016 and are published each year by March 15, and a violation carries a civil penalty of $1,000 to $5,000 [^32].

The Department of Insurance's Forms and Rates Bulletin No. 41, issued in July 2025, bars carriers from adding policy fees beyond installment, late-payment and rejected-payment fees; what a policy costs to administer has to sit in the filed rate [^33]. No carrier withdrawal from Delaware was documented for 2025 or 2026, and no insurer of last resort for Delaware property was identified, so the premium in a budget is the quote a carrier gives for that building.

The Census Bureau's county table gives Sussex 277,140 people for July 1, 2025, with an estimates base of 237,379 [^9]. The county permitted 3,885 housing units in 2025, with 4,354 the year before and 4,523 in 2020 [^34]. The 2020 to 2024 American Community Survey counts 77,249 Sussex residents aged 65 or older, and it values the median owner-occupied home at $382,600 [^10].

The county's FY2026 budget, adopted on June 17, 2025, totals $285.1 million, and $76.7 million of it is for wastewater infrastructure: expansions at the Inland Bays and Wolfe Neck treatment plants, new service areas and added capacity [^3]. Private capacity is growing as well. Artesian Wastewater Management's Sussex Regional Recharge Facility would treat up to 1.875 million gallons a day after its third phase, with its disposal capacity unchanged, under a DNREC notice heard on December 18, 2025 [^35]. No sewer moratorium in Delaware was documented for 2025 or 2026.

The council is narrowing the rules that decide how much of that capacity new subdivisions will use. In January 2025 a councilman's proposal for a one-year moratorium on large housing applications won no other support on the council [^36]. A working group's 20 recommendations, approved in September 2025, yielded six ordinances introduced on March 10, 2026 [^37], and on June 9, 2026 the council adopted the first ordinances of its land-use reform series, a revamped county rental program and technical code changes [^38]. By July 2026 the draft that would end cluster development in low-density areas was on hold and the open-space draft still pending, with density possibly left to the 2028 comprehensive plan [^39]. Nearby Dagsboro adopted a six-month moratorium on major and minor subdivisions on May 19, 2025 [^40].

Sussex County has taxed hotel and motel stays in its unincorporated areas at 3% since January 1, 2020, and the Cape Gazette put the collections at about $1 million a year in August 2024 [^41]. A matching county tax on short-term rentals in those areas failed on December 10, 2024 [^42]. Rehoboth Beach taxes hotel stays and home rentals under two separate city taxes [^43]. Impact fees were among the council's priorities for 2025 [^36]; no county impact fee schedule was found. No occupancy, rate or room-revenue series for the beach hotels was found.

No beach-area employer with a headcount was found. The Sussex entries on the federal list of largest employers are inland poultry processors in Millsboro, Mountaire Farms of Delaware and Allen Harim Foods, with counts the list does not date [^18], and no headcount for Beebe Healthcare, the beach market's medical anchor, was found. A beach market's demand evidence is therefore its county's population, age and home value figures above, not a payroll series.

Developers announced on January 27, 2025 that they aim to invest $75 million in the Seaford Industrial Park: about 1.2 million square feet on a US 13 tract of 128 acres, with a largest building of at least 400,000 square feet and first warehouses of 88,000 to 120,000 square feet each [^24]. The Council on Development Finance approved a $1.9 million Site Readiness grant for the park at the same time [^24]. These are one project's figures; no vacancy reading for industrial space in Kent or Sussex County was found, so the park is the dated record of warehouse supply in this part of the state.

The second redevelopment is the Nylon Capital Center, where the Delaware Business Times names TidalHealth, Delaware Tech, The Mill and Community Bank Delaware among the anchors [^44].

Seaford's medical anchor is TidalHealth Nanticoke. Its Form 990 for the fiscal year ending June 2025 reports $228.6 million of program service revenue [^45].

No impact fee, moratorium or hotel tax of the city's own was found. The county's 3% accommodations tax applies only on unincorporated land [^41], so a Seaford hotel inside the city limits carries the state's lodging taxes and any levy of the city's own.

Delaware's certificate-of-need review is the Certificate of Public Review under 16 Del. C. ch. 93, decided by the Health Resources Board [^46]. A review is required to build or establish a hospital, a nursing home, or a freestanding birthing, surgical, acute inpatient rehabilitation or emergency center, or to acquire one of these that is nonprofit, and for capital spending above $5,800,000, a threshold adjusted for inflation; an approval is valid for one year [^47]. Two 2026 bills went different ways: HB 17 took effect on July 1, 2026 and lifts the review for major medical equipment bought below a cost threshold, while HB 318, released on March 17, 2026 to amend the board's chapter, died in the House Health & Human Development Committee without a hearing [^48]. An earlier sunset review bill, HB 394 of 2024, had proposed renaming the review a certificate of need and making the board advisory [^49].

Child care needs a license from the Office of Child Care Licensing before it opens, in one of three classes: a family child care home, a large family child care home or a child care center [^50]. The capacity a license allows is further limited by the State Fire Marshal and by local zoning [^51].

No state preemption of short-term rental licensing was identified: towns and counties license rentals themselves, and the Division of Revenue's part is collecting the lodging tax [^29]. For assisted living and memory care, liquor licenses, fuel tanks, car washes and campgrounds no primary Delaware rule text was found, and this page states no rule for them.

On September 20, 2026 Delaware Public Media reported that Middletown had unanimously approved an Amazon facility on Middle Neck Road of about 1.95 million square feet on two stories, 52 feet high, in place of an approved manufacturing plan of 3.9 million square feet [^52]. Bluewater Property Group, the developer, said the building would be assessed at over $90 million and bring at least 500 permanent full-time jobs at an average of $22.50 an hour, plus more than 300 construction jobs [^52]. In May 2026 Spotlight Delaware had reported land bought for two Amazon buildings designed for robotics, with 1,500 jobs [^53]. These are statements by the developer and the company; no hiring figure was found.

The town runs its own water, wastewater and electric utilities, charges an impact fee for each, and waives part of those fees inside its Downtown Development District [^54]. In a letter of May 9, 2025 it told DNREC that its sewer system had sufficient capacity for Chapel Creek, a subdivision of 216 single-family houses and 190 townhouses with a design flow of 97,000 gallons a day [^55]. For a residential project here, the town's own capacity letter is the utility record, and its fee schedule was not found. No hotel opening in Middletown could be confirmed: one aggregator lists a planned property there with no date.

The University of Delaware's STAR Campus had 1.2 million square feet built and room for at least 5 million more when its first residential project broke ground in May 2025 [^56], and WDEL put the workforce on the campus at more than 3,000 that month [^57]. The project, The Asters, is two seven-story buildings with 229 units by Buccini Pollin Group and the university; it topped out in June 2026 at a cost REBusinessOnline gave as $75 million, against $71 million in UDaily's account of the groundbreaking [^58,56]. The Newark Post reported in April 2026 that the buildings are set to open in 2027 and named Bloom Energy and Chemours among the campus's tenants [^59]. The Asters is the only apartment project with a unit count found anywhere in New Castle County, so a Newark rental study surveys the city's other properties itself.

A Residence Inn with 131 rooms opened on August 12, 2025 beside Delaware Park, in Stanton, by Newark [^60]. In March 2024 the city sought authority to tax gross residential rental receipts at up to 1%, including rentals the university owns, which would need a charter amendment; it estimated the revenue at more than $2 million [^61]. Enactment was not found, so a student or workforce housing budget in Newark carries the proposal as a possible cost, not a rate. A 2024 city planning report reviewed inclusionary zoning options and the enforcement of rental permits, and the adoption of either was not found [^62]. Renters occupied 69,449 housing units in New Castle County in the 2020 to 2024 American Community Survey [^10].

Census QuickFacts counts 1,059,952 Delawareans for July 1, 2025; the Vintage 2025 estimates base for April 1, 2020 is 989,950 [^63]. The News Journal's reading of the same Vintage 2025 release puts the gain from July 2024 to July 2025 at 9,829 [^4]. USAFacts splits that year into about 6,900 net arrivals from other states and about 3,500 from abroad, while natural change took away 554 residents, deaths having outnumbered births [^64].

BLS's seasonally adjusted count of nonfarm jobs in Delaware was 496,000 in August 2025 and a preliminary 496,400 in August 2026, an over-the-year change of 400 jobs [^65]. The Dover figures in the market section come from a different BLS release and a different month, July, so the statewide and metro counts are quoted separately. A Joint Economic Committee update dated September 18, 2026 puts private payroll growth over the same year at 1,500, with education and health services up 2,100 and professional and business services up 1,800, while manufacturing lost 1,100 jobs and leisure and hospitality 1,000 [^66]. Unemployment was 4.7% in August 2026, against 5.0% in August 2025, and labor force participation 57.9%, which the committee called a ten-year low and ranked 44th among the states [^66].

Per capita personal income in Delaware was $68,061 in the Bureau of Economic Analysis's 2024 accounts [^67]. The CareerOneStop list of largest employers also names ChristianaCare, Nemours and the Amazon fulfillment center in Middletown; the list carries no as-of date, and this page prints no headcount from it [^18].

SBA's Delaware District Office in Wilmington serves all three counties [^68], and the statewide totals in SBA's loan-level files are therefore that one district's. Counting approvals from fiscal 2016 through fiscal 2025 and leaving cancellations out, they hold 1,589 loans under 7(a) for Delaware projects and $42.6 million of debentures under 504, with loan status as of June 30, 2026 [^69].

M&T's own release of November 19, 2025 places the bank at the top of its footprint for fiscal 2025, counted by 7(a) loans, and gives its Delaware total for that year as 113 loans and $8.7 million [^70]; the rank is the bank's, not an SBA table.

The U.S. Treasury awarded Delaware $60.9 million under the State Small Business Credit Initiative in 2023, to be released in three tranches through the Division of Small Business; on February 26, 2026, having met its deployment mark of $14 million, the division said it was preparing to deploy the next $20 million; the program requires the state to deploy 80% of its initial tranche within three years [^71]. The money runs through the Delaware Loan Participation Program, the Delaware Capital Access Program, the Delaware Accelerator & Seed Capital Program and, new in 2026, the Delaware Early Stage Venture Capital Program, with Del-One FCU, True Access Capital and Community Bank among the participating lenders [^72]. The Capital Access Program is a loan-loss reserve whose loans may not fund passive real estate [^73].

A Delaware SBA lender that asks for a study does so under 13 CFR 120.160(b): SBA may require professional appraisals, a survey, or a feasibility study [^74]. Equity, coverage and term tests are set out on our practice page for SBA 7(a) and 504 lending. Delaware's state totals for fiscal 2025 are in the year-end activity reports SBA issued, which were not opened for this page, so SBA's loan-level counts in the capital section are the program figures used here.

The Delaware Economic Development Authority issues tax-exempt and taxable revenue bonds on behalf of manufacturers, 501(c)(3) organizations, charter schools and health care borrowers, advised by the Council on Development Finance [^75], and it holds public TEFRA hearings on its bonds, one of them noticed for March 31, 2025 [^76]. Executive Order 16 of February 2026 sets Delaware's volume cap for private activity bonds at $397,625,000 for 2026, against $388,780,000 for 2025, allocates $99,406,250 of the 2026 cap to the authority, and moves $97,195,000 of its unused 2025 cap to the Delaware State Housing Authority to carry forward [^77]. For a manufacturing or nonprofit project, the bond route and its cap are the dated state record a lender can test.

Rural eligibility for USDA's guaranteed lending was not found for any of the six markets above, so a Kent or Sussex site's status is checked on the agency's own map before a USDA Rural Development structure is proposed. No primary source confirmed which HUD multifamily office has jurisdiction over Delaware, a point an apartment file headed for FHA insurance settles first; bank and bond structures for such projects are covered under conventional and institutional lending.

FEMA's data, as USAFacts counted them on September 3, 2026, show no federal disaster declaration for Delaware in 2025 [^78]. The 2026 declaration followed the winter storm of February 22 and 23: declared on May 29, 2026 as DR-4916, it covers Kent and Sussex counties for public assistance only, with no individual assistance named in the release [^79]. SBA's notice of June 18, 2026 opens its disaster loans for that declaration to private nonprofits only, with deadlines of July 28, 2026 for physical damage and March 1, 2027 for economic injury [^80]. The previous major declarations on FEMA's list were for Tropical Storm Isaias in 2020 and the remnants of Hurricane Ida on October 24, 2021 [^81].

Neither a National Risk Index ranking for Delaware nor a quantified measure of its flood, wind or wildfire exposure was found, which leaves a coastal Sussex parcel's flood exposure to be documented for that parcel.

County Business Patterns counted 185 establishments among Delaware's hotels and motels in 2023 [^82], and the Delaware Business Times, citing Data Center Map, put the state's operating data centers at five in July 2025 [^1]. No statewide Delaware series for apartment vacancy, storage space or senior housing occupancy was found.

Below the state level the record is a set of single properties. The hotel rooms added in the record are two Residence Inns, at Delaware Park in August 2025 and at Merck's campus in June 2026, the second with opening rates from $149 [^11], and the one apartment project with a unit count is The Asters in Newark, Delaware; each is in its market's section.
