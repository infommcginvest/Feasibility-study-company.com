The state took in 131,000 net migrants in the year to July 2025, third among the states, and its population rose by 146,000 over the same twelve months [^2]; the Census estimates base for April 2020 was 10,441,392 [^1].

One state law is in court and before the legislature at once. A Wake County three-judge panel upheld the Certificate of Need in December 2025 and the ruling is on appeal [^17]; for the repeal bills filed in 2025 and SB 1040, heard in May 2026, enactment was not found [^13,14,16]. For a surgery center or a regulated bed, North Carolina still sets supply by permit before any market does.

The Census estimates base was 10,441,392 in April 2020 [^1], and the year to July 2025 added 146,000 residents, the third-largest numeric gain of any state [^2]. Net migration came to 131,000, third among the states [^2]. The state budget office then splits the stream: net international migration came to 47,000 in that year, about half what it had been the year before, and international arrivals since 2020 total about 232,000, or 31% of the state's growth over the period [^3]. For arrivals from other states, the Governor's office ranked North Carolina first that year [^2].

Total nonfarm employment stood at 5,112,000 in July 2026, seasonally adjusted, up 51,600 over twelve months, and private employers accounted for 48,400 of that gain [^4]. Professional and business services added 19,400 jobs and construction added 15,400, while manufacturing lost 9,900 [^4].

Income sets what that population can pay. The 2020 to 2024 five-year American Community Survey estimate of median household income for North Carolina is $72,388 [^5], and per capita personal income, BEA's mean over all residents, was $65,634 in calendar 2024 [^6].

Of the up to $201.9M Treasury approved for North Carolina in 2022, in the second round of the State Small Business Credit Initiative [^10], the state's plan of that year put $160M into a Loan Participation Program, $10M into a Capital Access Program and $31.9M into venture capital, with the NC Rural Center chosen to manage the money [^11].

In December 2025 a Wake County three-judge panel upheld the Certificate of Need law, and the challenger took the case to appeal [^17]. The legislature has the same law in front of it. SB 1040, which would end certificate review for ambulatory surgical facilities and inpatient rehabilitation, was set for a Senate committee hearing in May 2026, after the full-repeal bills SB 370 and HB 455 were filed in 2025; for all three, enactment was not found [^13,14,16]. Meanwhile the statute in Article 9 of Chapter 131E still reaches new health care facilities. Its covered list, as the 2025 repeal bill reproduces it, runs from hospitals, rehabilitation facilities and nursing homes to adult care homes, kidney disease treatment centers, home health agencies and ambulatory surgical facilities, so a surgery center is among them [^13]. The 2023 Medicaid expansion law removed certificate review for psychiatric facilities and chemical dependency treatment and exempted certain ambulatory surgical centers in counties of more than 125,000 people, subject to a charity-care requirement [^15].

Short-term rental registration is closed to towns and counties by statute: G.S. 160D-1207 bars a local government from requiring registration or a permit to rent residential property, except for a property with verified violations, while zoning rules on rentals remain valid [^26].

An RV park is designed to a state wastewater number: the rules set its design flow, in gallons per day, at 100 for each standard RV site and 150 for each park model [^25].

DHHS licenses care by size and by rating. The adult care statute names three settings: adult care homes, adult care homes for elderly residents only, and multiunit assisted housing with services [^18]. A home that advertises dementia care must be licensed as an adult care home with a special care unit, while multiunit assisted housing with services registers each year instead of holding a licence [^19]. The state's 2024 counts of those homes are under supply [^20]. A child care licence carries a rating of one to five stars, and only a center rated three stars or more can accept subsidy [^21]. Since July 2023 a center run in a residence has its own capacity classes, 3 to 12 children where any are of preschool age and 3 to 15 where all are of school age, with staff ratios by age band [^22], and rated centers follow a ratio and group-size table with a tighter version for the higher tier [^23].

For a fuel station, every tank carries an annual state registration fee of $420, and the state's commercial cleanup fund stands behind leaks: in fiscal 2025 it closed 502 commercial releases, replaced 59 contaminated wells and paid $14,031,105 in reimbursements to responsible parties [^24].

A state decision ahead of the town's

Several uses meet a state permit or licence before a local one. A new regulated bed, or a surgery center outside the 2023 exemption, needs a Certificate of Need [^13,15]. An assisted living building is licensed by size class, and the larger memory care buildings change building class with it [^19]. A child care center's star rating decides whether it can take subsidized children [^21]. An RV park's wastewater is designed to the state's flow per site [^25]. For each, the study names the licence or permit, the agency that grants it, and where the application stands on the study date.
