The National Institutes of Health is first on Montgomery County's employer list in Commerce's 2025.2 profile [^2], Fort Detrick is first on Frederick County's [^10] and Naval Air Station Patuxent River first on St. Mary's [^18]. The dates are what a reviewer checks. The profiles count staff for 2023 to 2024 in Montgomery, Prince George's and Frederick, for 2023 to 2025 in Anne Arundel and for 2024 to 2025 in Charles and St. Mary's [^2,3,10,4,19,18]. Federal employees' jobless claims in the state are counted from January 19, 2025 [^1]. A headcount from a profile therefore dates from before the claims period or from its first months, and the claims series is the dated record that runs after it.

Baltimore runs on a different anchor. The city's largest employers are the Johns Hopkins hospital system and university and the University of Maryland Medical System [^20], and the November 2025 study of Hopkins' economic impact named federal funding cuts as a risk to it [^21].

State credit runs through the Department of Commerce. Its financing authority, MSBDFA, offers contract financing, a long-term guaranty, surety bonding, equity participation and SSBCI programs, managed by Meridian Management Group, and the DECADE Act of 2026 (HB 898 and SB 388) restructured it from July 1, 2026 as the Maryland Small Business Development Financing Fund [^49]. One aggregator's 2026 page prints two different calendar 2025 totals for Maryland's 7(a) lending, so neither appears here.

For an SBA loan, the agency's authority to ask for a study is 13 CFR 120.160(b): SBA may require professional appraisals, a survey, or a feasibility study [^50], and the program tests sit in SOP 50 10 8.1 with Technical Policy Updates [^51]. Those tests are the same in every state, and our SBA 7(a) and 504 practice page explains them; in Maryland the variable is which district office a county answers to.

USDA Rural Development serves Maryland from its Delaware-Maryland State Office in Dover, Delaware, with an area office in Hagerstown [^52]. Bond and bank structures of that kind are on our conventional and institutional lending page.

Maryland replaced its assisted living regulations in 2025. The new COMAR 10.07.14 was published as final in the Maryland Register on April 18 and took effect on April 28 [^13]. It licenses three levels, Level 1 for low, Level 2 for moderate and Level 3 for high care needs, and a program may serve residents at its licensed level or below [^14]. The Office of Health Care Quality's FAQ of October 21, 2025 sets no minimum number of residents for a licence and asks for 60 days' notice of a licence change in programs of 49 or fewer beds and 75 days' in programs of 50 or more [^15]. No separate memory care or Alzheimer's unit category was found.

Certificate of Need, administered by the Maryland Health Care Commission under Health-General section 19-120, reaches the building or establishment of a new health care facility, its relocation, a change in its type, scope of service or bed capacity, and capital spending above the review threshold; the 2025 fiscal note on HB 735 lists home health, hospice and freestanding ambulatory surgical facilities among the establishments it covers, and nursing home, residential treatment, psychiatric and rehabilitation services among the expansions [^62]. Two 2026 bills would narrow it: HB 632 would exempt psychiatric services [^63], and HB 498, the successor to a 2025 bill the Commission supported, would replace full review for ASAM Level 3.7 intermediate care facilities with a 45-day notice and Commission approval [^64]. Enactment of either was not found.

The state's Oil Contaminated Site Environmental Cleanup Fund no longer pays commercial tank claims, whose statutory application deadline was December 31, 2007, and its residual residential heating oil program is listed as closed; the fiscal 2024 annual report, published on April 2, 2025, records 354 residential applications reimbursed that year [^16]. The fiscal 2025 report followed on February 12, 2026 [^65]. The cleanup funds are paid for by an oil transfer fee of 5.0 cents a barrel since July 1, 2021, down from 8.0 cents [^66].
