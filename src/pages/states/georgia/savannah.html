The Savannah metro is Chatham County, which holds the city, the port and the islands, plus Bryan County to the southwest and Effingham County upriver to the northwest. Its population record arrives in two hands. HUD's market analysis, written as of January 1, 2026, is the source of the 2020 Census count of 404,798 residents across the three counties [^1]. The July 1, 2025 total of 438,314 reached the research through a Wikipedia list that transcribes the Census Bureau's Vintage 2025 metro table [^2]. The more recent number is therefore the less direct one, and this page carries it as a transcription until the Bureau's own rows for Chatham, Bryan and Effingham are read. HUD reports that the area's growth has slowed sharply since 2022 [^1].

The research did not capture the 2025 Census permit totals and holds HUD's under-construction counts in their place, which put rental units under construction in the area at 2,550 and for-sale homes at 670 on January 1, 2026 [^1]. The places where the record shows new building are suburban: HUD's account of Memorial Health's expansion puts its new emergency facilities in Pooler, west of the city along I-16, and in Richmond Hill, the Bryan County town on US 17 [^1].

For all three counties together, the 2020 to 2024 five-year American Community Survey estimate puts median household income at $77,180 [^17] and the median owner-occupied home value at $302,000 [^17]. The BEA's per capita personal income for the metro was $61,535 in calendar 2024 [^18].

Savannah's largest employer builds business jets. Gulfstream Aerospace leads HUD's January 2026 list with 13,125 employees [^1]. Fort Stewart and Hunter Army Airfield come next with 6,575, a count of civilians only [^1]. The two hospital systems follow: HUD records 4,975 employees at St. Joseph's/Candler and 4,300 at Memorial Health University Medical Center [^1], and the Savannah Area Chamber's employer list, modified in June 2026, gives St. Joseph's/Candler 5,553 [^4]. Walmart is fifth on HUD's list, with more than 3,900 employees [^1]. The universities appear apart from the employer list: SCAD reported 1,675 direct employees in fiscal 2023, and Georgia Southern's Armstrong Campus enrolled 5,175 students in fall 2025 [^1].

The newest anchor stands at the I-16 and GA 280 interchange in Bryan County. The Hyundai vehicle plant represents a reported $7.6 billion investment and has operated since October 2024 [^5]; WSAV counted 1,232 employees at its March 2025 grand opening [^6], and HUD's January 2026 analysis expects a second phase to be completed in 2028 [^1].

The hospital system is building outward. HUD records an announced $265 million Memorial Health expansion that includes the new emergency facilities in Pooler and Richmond Hill [^1], the same two suburbs where the growth section finds new building.

Total payroll gives the scale. HUD reports average nonfarm employment of 207.4 thousand for calendar 2025 [^1], and BLS shows a preliminary 215.9 thousand for April 2026 [^3]: an annual average from one agency and a single preliminary month from another.

The port's reach is measured with a 2021 number. HUD's January 2026 analysis carries the Port of Savannah as supporting 59,050 jobs in 2021 [^1], a count of jobs the port supports across the economy rather than its own payroll.

The record names its growth points by road. I-16 meets GA 280 at the Hyundai plant in Bryan County [^5]; Pooler and Richmond Hill carry Memorial's new emergency facilities [^1]; and US 17 is where HUD records a direct truck route for port access [^1]. At the airport, an $80 million air cargo complex at Savannah/Hilton Head International began construction in 2024 [^1]. None of these stretches has a Georgia DOT count on this page.

Our pages on Jacksonville and Charleston cover the neighbouring port markets.

Industrial: the newest figures on the page

CBRE counted 5.1 million square feet of industrial space under construction in Q3 2026, with an average asking rent of $7.32 per square foot [^8], and Colliers put industrial vacancy at 10.01% in Q2 2026 [^9].

Multifamily: a federal forecast with no room in it

HUD put the average apartment rent at $1,681 in Q4 2025 and rates the rental market very soft [^1]. The metro has 61,020 renter households in the 2020 to 2024 five-year American Community Survey estimate [^17]. HUD's January 2026 analysis expects the apartments already vacant, together with those under construction, to meet the rental demand it forecasts through 2029 [^1]. A multifamily study in Savannah therefore opens against a federal finding that the forecast period needs no new building, and has to show which renters the subject draws despite it.

Hotels: a visitor trade read through its taxes

CBRE's Q2 2026 U.S. hotel figures show Savannah RevPAR down 2% from a year earlier, the largest decline among the four U.S. markets where it fell [^14]. The latest occupancy reading the research found was 69.2% for Q4 2024, from Colliers [^7]; no average daily rate was found, and no list of named 2025 to 2026 openings. County Business Patterns counts 4,112 employees in hotels in 2023 (County Business Patterns) [^16]. The firmest measure of the visitor economy is fiscal: lodging taxes contributed $56.2 million to Chatham County, Savannah and Tybee in 2024 [^1], a sum shared by three governments, one of which, Tybee, amended its rental rules in 2026 [^13].

Fuel stations and older residents

The Census register counts 272 establishments in gas stations and convenience stores across the three counties in 2023 (County Business Patterns) [^16], a count for the whole metro rather than for a trade area. For an assisted living or memory care project, the same Census program's survey counts 64,279 residents aged 65 and over in the 2020 to 2024 five-year American Community Survey estimate [^17].

Savannah's record stops short of self-storage, retail, office, senior housing occupancy and RV parks.

Each part of the metro stands somewhere different on impact fees. The City of Savannah charges one: local reporting put its single-family fee at $3,930.85 per house in August 2025, in a story about Liberty County's own fee hearings, outside the metro [^10]. Bryan County, home of the Hyundai plant, has charged South Bryan a transportation fee of $3,241 per single-family home since April 1, 2019, and extended a transportation fee to North Bryan from January 1, 2026, after ordinance amendments in 2025 [^11]. Effingham County has a fee on its books that it has not collected, according to the Georgia Public Policy Foundation [^12]. A budget in the city carries a fee in force, one in North Bryan a fee in its first year, and one in Effingham a fee that exists on paper but has not been charged.

Tybee Island amended its short-term rental rules on August 28, 2026, after a suit filed in January 2026 [^13], so a rental home or small lodging project on the island is read against a rule with little operating history behind it.

SBA's loan-level files put 7(a) lending in Chatham, Bryan and Effingham at $449.2 million approved in fiscal years 2016 to 2025, net of cancellations [^15]. On the 504 side the same files hold 104 loans and $147.5 million of debentures, both approved in fiscal years 2016 to 2025, net of cancellations [^15].

All three counties are served by SBA's Georgia District Office [^21].

Whether a Savannah loan needs a study at all rests on 13 CFR 120.160(b): SBA may require professional appraisals, a survey, or a feasibility study, under SBA's current SOP 50 10 8.1 with Technical Policy Updates [^19,20]; the SBA section of our Georgia page links the program tests.

Even the Hyundai plant's Bryan County site has no USDA, Opportunity Zone or HUBZone reading in this page's record, and neither has any other part of the three counties; the Georgia site tests send each question to the agency's own tool.

No published case names a site in Chatham, Bryan or Effingham. The truck stop and travel center case puts its interchange on the I-16 freight corridor between Macon and the Port of Savannah, which its page places in the Savannah port-and-Hyundai coast economy, and it names no county or town along that road, so this page places it on the corridor. The Georgia agency multifamily case is set in a fast-growing suburb of metro Atlanta; it runs a new garden and low mid-rise community through a bank construction loan and a Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac permanent takeout, building demand from qualified renter households and testing capture against competing lease-ups, which is the question HUD's 2029 forecast puts to any new Savannah apartment project.
