The Maine Department of Labor's release for August 2026, published on September 18, 2026, described nonfarm payroll jobs as holding within a narrow range near 660,000 for 28 consecutive months [^1]. Over the year health care and social assistance and retail trade added jobs, and modest losses in several other sectors cancelled them out. The same release gives the seasonally adjusted unemployment rate as 3.2% and private average hourly earnings as $33.77, 4.2% higher than a year before [^1].

A flat state total changes the question a demand model has to answer. A hotel, an apartment building or a clinic in Maine cannot borrow its occupancy from statewide job growth, because there is none to borrow; it has to show that its own trade area gains households, visitors or patients, or that it wins them from the places already serving them.

Below the state line the department's monthly release gives resident employment by county: people who live in the county and hold a job, wherever the job is, rather than jobs located there. Jobs located in a county come from the Bureau of Labor Statistics' quarterly census instead, which the Portland and York County sections cite for September 2025, and the Bath section for wages [^7].

The department's county lists of the largest private employers, for the first quarter of 2026, give headcounts only as ranges and leave public employers out [^8], so the University of Maine, Portsmouth Naval Shipyard and the Bangor Air National Guard base carry no headcount here; a study resting on one of them gets the number from the institution.

The Maine State Economist published the state-level results of the Census Bureau's Vintage 2025 estimates on January 30, 2026. From July 2024 to July 2025 the state's population changed by +6,436. Net domestic migration was +7,406, ranked seventh among the states; net international migration was +4,040, ranked 25th; and natural change, births less deaths, was -5,019 [^3]. Since April 2020 the release puts cumulative net migration at 77,918 [^3]. Census QuickFacts gives the estimates base for April 1, 2020 as 1,363,218, the 2020 Census count as 1,362,359, and the estimate for July 1, 2025 as 1,414,874 [^9].

Natural decrease is the fact a Maine demand case cannot set aside. In a state where deaths outnumber births, population grows only as people move in, and a market study names which stream, from other states or from abroad, its trade area draws on. The State Economist's release describes 2025 as a year in which domestic arrivals made up for a lower inflow from abroad [^3].

The release does not divide the components by county; the market sections give each county's Vintage 2025 level against its April 2020 base, except Androscoggin and Hancock, whose 2025 counts appear only in a secondary compilation and are left out.

Maine's Certificate of Need Act, 22 M.R.S. chapter 103-A, still covers hospitals, nursing facilities, ambulatory surgical facilities, rehabilitation and kidney treatment centers and independent imaging and cardiac catheterization centers [^61]. The Department of Health and Human Services' 2025 annual report on the act, covering 2024, gives the review thresholds in effect that year: $13,793,936 for capital expenditures, $6,896,968 for nursing facility capital expenditures, $4,414,059 for new major medical equipment and new technology, $3,000,000 for a new health facility and $5,000,000 for a new nursing facility, and $4,138,181 of capital or $1,379,394 of third-year operating cost for new health services [^61]. Each threshold moves with inflation every year, and a study quotes the one for its filing year. An ambulatory surgery or medical office project checks the list before it budgets.

Nursing facility beds must be MaineCare-neutral, and the nursing facility MaineCare funding pool held $147,665 on December 31, 2024 [^61]. A 2025 bill, House Paper 485, proposed eliminating Certificate of Need requirements; its outcome was not found [^62].

The Bureau of Insurance's September 2025 report on the property and casualty market calls commercial insurance relatively healthy, but finds that condominium associations, child care facilities and other niche commercial lines face fewer options and higher premiums, with double-digit increases for some condominium associations; commercial insurers may non-renew a policy for any reason [^37]. Surplus lines wrote 5.2% of the Maine market in 2024, with 253 eligible surplus lines insurers as of September 2025, and 16% of the medical malpractice market, where Medical Mutual of Maine held 85% and nursing homes rely on surplus lines [^37].

Maine has no FAIR plan. Its statutory backstop is the Mandatory Property and Casualty Insurance Market Assistance Program, under which admitted insurers write basic property and casualty cover for eligible applicants [^69,70]. In 2024, 99 companies actively wrote homeowners insurance and the ten largest wrote 50% of it [^37]; U.S. News puts the average approved homeowners increase at about 3% in 2025, after about 13% in 2024 and about 10% in 2023 [^70].

Labor cost moved by statute. Maine's minimum wage became $15.10 an hour on January 1, 2026, up from $14.65, with a tipped direct wage of $7.55 and an overtime-exempt salary threshold of $871.16 a week, $45,300.32 a year, and the rate is indexed to the Northeast consumer price index for wage earners [^71]. A hotel, restaurant or care operator in Maine therefore budgets the wage step every January, not once.

Property tax is set town by town. The Tax Foundation's 2026 profile gives an effective residential rate of 0.98% on owner-occupied housing value [^72]; with no 2025 commercial effective rate located, the town's mill rate and assessment ratio for the parcel stand in for one.

Fuel sites have a state cleanup fund, the Ground and Surface Waters Clean-up and Response Fund, financed by a fee on each barrel of oil brought into Maine and covering cleanup and third-party damage from underground and aboveground tanks [^73]. A gas station study cites the fund's coverage and FAME's tank replacement loans, in the capital section, rather than assuming either.
