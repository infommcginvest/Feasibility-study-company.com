The Census Bureau's Vintage 2025 estimates count 2,029,733 Idahoans on July 1, 2025, from an estimates base of 1,839,123 on April 1, 2020 [^13]. The Department of Labor's August 2026 payroll count, seasonally adjusted, is 890,700 nonfarm jobs, which it puts 7,800 above the count a year earlier [^14]. The market sections below give each county its own Census and Labor Department counts, and because the department publishes employers only by headcount band, every employer size on this page is a band or a floor, never a single number.

The record that moved in 2025 and 2026 sits on the other side of a project, in what it costs to supply and to carry. Idaho administers water rights by priority date, and that date now decides whether some wells may pump [^1]. Property insurers have raised homeowners premiums, narrowed where they write and in some cases left the state's market, in the Insurance Department's own account [^4]. And Idaho Power is planning for a rising summer peak while a statute of 2026 governs how the very largest new loads are served [^6,7].

Federal lending splits the state in two as well. SBA's Seattle District serves the ten northern counties, Boundary, Bonner, Kootenai, Shoshone, Benewah, Latah, Clearwater, Nez Perce, Lewis and Idaho, and its Boise District serves the southern counties together with part of eastern Oregon [^15]. Coeur d'Alene and Lewiston are on the Seattle side of that line; the Treasure Valley, Idaho Falls, Pocatello, Twin Falls and the Sun Valley area are on the Boise side.

The department's final order of May 14, 2026 cut off pumping under Eastern Snake Plain Aquifer groundwater rights junior to October 11, 1900, and the department continued it on July 21, 2026; the research lists it with a projected shortfall for 2026 of 181,600 acre-feet [^1,2]. East Idaho News reported that the order reaches 924 groundwater rights, and that an earlier curtailment order in Butte County had taken effect on November 21, 2025 [^2]. A well on the plain therefore raises a question before any market question does: the priority date of the right the project relies on, and which side of the 1900 cutoff it falls.

Two orders limit new supply elsewhere: the south Canyon County groundwater moratorium of March 20, 2026 and the Boise Front geothermal order the department lengthened on May 5, 2026 [^3]. Canyon County is half of the Treasure Valley, and the Treasure Valley page sets out both orders and what they mean for a site there.

Public systems drew new money in the same spring: USDA Rural Development announced a $38 million investment in Idaho water and wastewater infrastructure on April 8, 2026 [^16]. The research found no sewer moratorium anywhere in the state.

The Department of Insurance's findings of October 2025 describe a property market that has been hardening for five years or more, in which insurers sharply raised premiums, cut back the parts of the state they would write, declined to renew policies in high-risk zones and, in some cases, left property insurance altogether [^4]. Its data call put the average annual homeowners premium at $1,308 in 2022, $1,468 in 2023 and $1,798 in 2024, as Capital Press reported the figures [^5].

The number of homeowners policies in force was 464,364 in 2022 and 424,113 in 2023 [^5]. The department's director, Dean Cameron, told the paper that about 8,000 of the policies no longer on the books had gone to surplus lines and that the department did not know where the other 32,000 went [^5]. The department's news release of April 22, 2025, which announced the mandatory data call under Bulletin 25-02, counted 91 property insurers in Idaho in 2023, and the director estimated that roughly 22 to 25 of them had declined to renew some or all of their policies, partly because of wildfire [^17].

Every one of these readings is for homes. On the commercial side no rate filing, published rate change or insurer of last resort turned up for Idaho, so for a hotel, a store or a warehouse the data call describes the market around the building rather than the policy on it.

Idaho Power's 2025 Integrated Resource Plan, issued in June 2025, forecasts a summer peak load of 4,949 MW in 2031, rising by about 200 MW a year on average between 2026 and 2031, and it runs two additional large-load scenarios of 300 MW and 500 MW [^6]. On the supply side the utility now studies generator interconnection requests in clusters, with a window that opens each March 1 and a request portal open from March 1, 2026 [^18].

Demand of the largest kind now needs a contract first. Idaho Code section 61-335, enacted in 2026 as H0911 and in force from July 1, 2026, lets a utility serve a new large load only under a service contract that the Public Utilities Commission has approved. A large load is one that adds 50 MW or more within 60 months; the commission has 270 days to approve or deny; and the filing must carry a no-harm test, full funding of the cost of service and financial security [^7]. Those terms are as ServerCountry, a secondary site, summarises the statute, and a project whose plan depends on them cites the code text.

Data centers are the large projects in Idaho's 2026 record. The two at Kuna are taken up on the Treasure Valley page. A third plan was reported in September 2026: a data center campus on Bureau of Land Management land in southern Ada County, with rights-of-way into Elmore County and a natural gas plant of its own, whose federal application the reports describe as at a very early stage [^19]. The Tax Commission's exemption for certified data centers, in the costs section, is another state rule such a project meets.

Major disaster declaration DR-4905-ID, made on April 7, 2026 for straight-line winds of December 16 to 18, 2025, gives Public Assistance to Benewah, Bonner, Boundary, Clearwater, Idaho, Kootenai, Latah, Lewis, Nez Perce and Shoshone counties, and FEMA had obligated $285,773.55 under it as of July 11, 2026 [^9]. They are the same ten counties that SBA's Seattle District serves [^15], Kootenai and Nez Perce, the counties of the Coeur d'Alene and Lewiston sections, among them. The declaration covers Public Assistance only, so SBA's disaster loans under it were open to private non-profit organizations alone, with physical damage applications due by June 10, 2026 [^20].

A second major disaster, DR-4917-ID, followed on June 30, 2026, covering the storms of March 11 to 15, 2026 and the winds, floods, landslides and mudslides they brought [^21]. The summer before, FEMA approved Fire Management Assistance declaration FM-5607-ID for the Sunset Fire on August 15, 2025, for a fire that ran from August 14 to September 1 [^22].

Wildfire also shows up in what insurers pay. Homeowners losses paid with wildfire were $16.05 million in 2022, $18.83 million in 2023 and $24.52 million in 2024 by the data call, as Capital Press reported it [^5]; the department itself calls wildfire a small share of yearly paid losses, but a growing one [^4]. No National Risk Index ranking from FEMA was found for Idaho, and no seismic or flood exposure figure for the state as a whole.

HB 583 rewrote Idaho Code section 67-6539 with effect from July 1, 2026. No city or county may now require a licence, fee, permit, certification or registration for a short-term rental, or cap rentals or keep them apart by distance; a rental counts as a residential use, and a local rule may reach only the safety items the statute names [^27]. Ketchum's repeal of its permit chapter, in the Sun Valley section, is that law working through one city.

HB 243, signed on March 27, 2025 and in force from July 1, 2025, made the Department of Health and Welfare the only licensing authority for child care and barred cities and counties from stricter rules [^8]. It kept staffing on a points system, with at most 12 points per staff member and a child aged 5 to 12 counted at 0.48 points, down from 0.5 [^8]. BoiseDev reported on March 17, 2025 that ratios had been added back to the bill, at equivalents of 1:6 for children under two, 1:9 from two to three, 1:13 from three to five and 1:25 from five to thirteen [^30]. A center's staffing budget is built on those ratios, not on any stricter city rule.

Liquor by the drink stays under a population quota: two licences for each incorporated city and one more for each 1,500 residents. SB 1120 of 2023 bars the sale, lease or transfer of any licence issued after July 1, 2023 [^31]. Licences issued before then can still be bought, with the state charging a transfer fee of 10% of the purchase price or of goodwill, whichever is greater [^32]. On November 24, 2025 the Idaho State Police proposed raising the state licence fee for cities of more than 3,000 people from $750 to $1,200 a year; whether the increase was adopted was not found [^23].

Residential assisted living facilities are licensed by the Department of Health and Welfare under Idaho Code section 39-3340 and IDAPA 16.03.22 [^33], and memory care is delivered inside a licensed facility rather than under a licence of its own [^34]. The research did not establish what a Certificate of Need covers in Idaho, if anything, or the bed and unit thresholds for assisted living.

Underground fuel tanks are regulated by the Department of Environmental Quality under IDAPA 58.01.07, a state program held equal to the federal rules, with financial responsibility required of owners [^35]; the condition of the state's petroleum cleanup fund was not found. Campground lodging owes the 2% Travel and Convention tax under the Tax Commission's rule text of 1999 [^36]. The research turned up no licensing rule for RV parks or campgrounds, no car wash water rule and no verified 2026 minimum wage.

A contractor improving real property in Idaho is the consumer of the materials it installs and pays the sales or use tax on them [^37]. The Tax Commission's Form ST-103C, revised on March 11, 2025, lists the exemptions that change that: certified data centers under section 63-3622VV, qualified semiconductor projects, production equipment and nontaxing-state jobs [^37]. Equipment used primarily and directly in production is exempt under section 63-3622D, but motor vehicles and repair and administrative equipment are not [^38].

The research holds no effective commercial property tax rate, construction cost index, impact fee range or prevailing wage rule for Idaho. Local costs appear market by market instead: the Idaho Falls section gives that city's estimates for burying power lines, and the Treasure Valley page lists the impact fees Nampa charges.

In its release of September 18, 2026 the Department of Labor put Idaho's seasonally adjusted nonfarm count for August at 890,700: up 2,800 from July and 7,800, or 0.9%, from a year before. The state's unemployment rate held at 3.6%, against 4.1% for the nation [^14]. The Joint Economic Committee's update of the same day, built on BLS data, has private payrolls 10,200 higher over the year [^39].

Seven sectors lost jobs over those twelve months, by the department's release: arts, entertainment and recreation, down 8.1%; federal government, down 7.0%; administrative, support and waste management, down 5.9%; state government, down 4.6%; information, down 3.3%; nondurable goods manufacturing, down 1.9%; and transportation, warehousing and utilities, down 1.2% [^14]. The release says that most major sectors added jobs, without giving counts for them, and the research found no sector-level gains in numbers.
