The August 2025 storms produced an Illinois request for Individual Assistance that FEMA denied, and an appeal letter that reached the state in February 2026 denied it a second time, Capitol News Illinois reported on February 10, 2026; the state had proclaimed its own disaster on September 15, 2025 [^1]. A year later the request for the August 2026 storms named Champaign, Cook, Ford, Fulton, McLean, Tazewell, Vermilion and Will counties, and FEMA answered that a declaration was "not warranted" [^2]. The flash flooding of July 25 to 28, 2025 in Calhoun, Cook and Jersey counties brought a call from the state's two senators, joined by other Illinois Democrats, for a federal declaration [^14].

The federal help that did reach Illinois borrowers in these months was credit. SBA issued administrative declaration IL-20015 on its own authority on May 14, 2025, for the April 2 to 6 storms in Marion County and the counties bordering it, and priced business physical disaster loans at 8.000% for firms with credit available elsewhere and 4.000% for firms without [^7]. Such a loan pays a contractor and leaves the owner a second monthly payment.

A storm case in an Illinois feasibility study is therefore built from the subject's insurance quote: the property limit, the wind and hail deductible, and the business interruption cover with its waiting period. Health facilities

Illinois still reviews health facilities through its Certificate of Need program under the Health Facilities Planning Act. Above the capital expenditure thresholds, hospitals, long-term care facilities, dialysis centers, ambulatory surgical treatment centers, freestanding emergency centers, birthing centers and alternative delivery models need a permit from the state's Health Facilities and Services Review Board [^69]. The Board's rules were amended effective July 1, 2025 [^70], a 2025 act on non-clinical service areas took effect on January 1, 2026, and the Act's review section is scheduled for repeal at the end of 2029 [^71,70]. A surgery center, nursing facility or dialysis study shows the permit path and its dates, since a project that is refused a permit has no revenue line at all. County Business Patterns counted 793 skilled nursing establishments in Illinois for 2023 [^72], a count of operators rather than of beds.

Assisted living and memory care

Assisted living and shared housing establishments are licensed under the Assisted Living and Shared Housing Act; Illinois has no separate memory care license, dementia programs follow a special care disclosure statute, and supportive living facilities are certified for Medicaid by a different agency [^73]. From July 24, 2026, initial assessments for residents of dementia programs must be done by a physician, physician assistant or advanced practice nurse experienced in geriatric dementia care [^73], a clinical cost and an admission step for the lease-up schedule. ACS estimates for 2020 to 2024 put 2,156,214 Illinois residents in the 65 and over group [^34], the population a senior housing model narrows by age, income and distance.

Childcare

A childcare center in Illinois is licensed before it opens, after a facility inspection, and its licensed capacity is set at licensing rather than by statute [^74]; the center rules, Part 407, were last amended effective June 18, 2025 [^75]. The rules fix a staff ratio and a maximum group size for each age band, so the planned mix of infants, toddlers and preschoolers sets the teacher count and the payroll before tuition is set. Competitors come in three licensed formats: day care homes under Part 406 [^76], group day care homes under Part 408 [^77] and centers, each with its own capacity limit, so a supply count weighs each provider by its license. The childcare page sets out the method.

Fuel sites

Leaking tank cleanups are paid from a state Underground Storage Tank Fund financed by a per-gallon motor fuel tax and an environmental impact fee, both due to expire in 2030 [^78]. Registration and eligibility sit with the State Fire Marshal and cleanup reimbursement with the Illinois EPA, which gave notice on October 22, 2025 of coming changes to deductibles and reimbursement [^79]. County Business Patterns counted 4,664 gasoline station and convenience establishments in Illinois for 2023 [^72]. A fuel station acquisition study obtains the tank registration, the fund eligibility and deductible decision and any open incident, because the lender's environmental exposure turns on whether the fund pays.

Lodging

The state hotel operators' occupation tax is 6%, made of 5% and 1%, applied to 94% of gross receipts, under the Department of Revenue's 2023 ruling [^35]. Non-home-rule municipalities may add a local hotel tax only up to a statutory cap, while home-rule units may go higher [^80], so the rate on a room depends on the city, and no typical combined rate outside Chicago was found. The state tax reached short-term rentals on July 1, 2025 [^81], and stays booked through platforms from January 1, 2026 [^81,82], which narrows the price gap between a new hotel and the listings around it. A lodging study quotes the full local rate from the city and county ordinances.

Construction materials and equipment

Inside an Enterprise Zone or a River Edge zone, building materials incorporated into the project can be free of the 6.25% state sales tax with a Certificate of Eligibility [^83,84], and manufacturing machinery is exempt statewide under a primary-use test [^85]. A study notes whether the site lies in a zone and whether the budget assumed the exemption, because a missed certificate raises the hard cost.

Wages

The state minimum wage has been $15.00 an hour since January 1, 2025, with no step in 2026; the tipped minimum is $9.00, and workers under 18 who work fewer than 650 hours a year may be paid $13.00 [^11]. Chicago and suburban Cook County set their own floors, $17.05 and $15.40 from July 1, 2026 [^11].

County Business Patterns gives Illinois 25,557 restaurant establishments and 572 self-storage establishments for 2023 [^72]. Counts of that kind place a project in a crowded or a thin class, but they count locations, not seats, rooms, units or square feet. The figure a lender checks against demand is the study's census of named competitors in the trade area, each with its size, opening date, rates and occupancy where observed, plus anything permitted or under construction (our note on trade areas sets out how the area is drawn).

For health facilities the Review Board's inventory and need determinations serve as the published supply count [^70]. For apartments, hotels and warehouses the dated series on this page are the brokers' Chicago figures in the Chicago section; no comparable statewide or downstate series was found.
