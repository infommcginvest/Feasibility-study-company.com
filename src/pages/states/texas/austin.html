Austin's economy rests on government, a university, health care and a handful of very large technology employers, and the last group moves fastest. The Dallas Fed names the State of Texas as the metro's largest employer and UT Austin second, without headcounts [^5]. Among private employers, the Austin Business Journal's July 2026 list puts H-E-B first with 27,721 workers, Tesla second with 16,500, Ascension Texas third with 16,200, St. David's HealthCare fourth with 13,259 and Amazon fifth with 11,000 [^4]. Two of the five are hospital systems, which gives medical office and senior housing a steadier demand base than the headlines suggest.

The tech side is where a lender should look hardest. Tesla's Giga Texas headcount fell to 16,506 at the end of 2025 from 21,191 a year earlier, a drop of 22%, according to its Travis County compliance report as reported by the Austin American-Statesman [^6]. Samsung's fab in Taylor began trial production in September 2026 and plans mass production in 2027 [^7]. East of the city, SpaceX's Starlink operation in Bastrop had more than 1,500 employees in May 2026 [^59], after a $17.3 million grant from the state's semiconductor fund in March 2025 [^60]. BAE Systems' May 2026 expansion plans put $50 million in Austin [^8], and UT's Dell Medical Center hospital near the Domain is planned for 2030 [^7]. Those are real anchors, but each one is a single company's decision. A hotel near Giga Texas, apartments in Taylor or retail on Parmer Lane should be underwritten with a case in which that employer's headcount falls, because one already has.

The research found no military anchor and no other project over $50 million with a confirmed budget. That leaves a feasibility study consultant in Austin to document any employer the demand case relies on with a permit, an incentive agreement or the company's own filing.

The Austin-Round Rock-San Marcos MSA added 53,796 residents in the year to July 2025, per the Austin American-Statesman's reading of the Census Vintage 2025 release [^2]. The research did not retrieve the MSA total or its 2020 base, so this page gives no five-year growth rate. The City of Austin passed 1 million residents, reaching 1,002,632 on July 1, 2025 [^1]. Growth is fastest at the edges: KXAN reported that Hays and Williamson counties each grew more than 3% in the year [^12].

Payrolls rose from 1,399.7 thousand in August 2025 to 1,415.9 thousand in August 2026, a gain of 16,200 or 1.2% (preliminary, not seasonally adjusted) [^3]. That is solid growth for a correcting market, and it is the number that will eventually absorb the vacant apartments and warehouses. A study should not assume it accelerates.

The supply response is already visible in permits. Trailing twelve-month multifamily permits fell to 8,760 units by June 2026, against a peak of about 22,000 [^11]. HBWeekly's universe counted about 7,275 residential permits for 2025, down 3%, with Travis County at 3,390 (up 5%) and Williamson County at 2,609 [^9]; CultureMap Austin reported 32,294 units for 2024 on a different basis [^10]. The two universes should not be combined. The message for a lender is that the builders have pulled back hard, which is how a correction ends, but the units started at the peak are still leasing up.

Much of Austin's new industry sits east and north of the city, along SH 130, the toll road that parallels I-35, and out along US 290 and SH 71. The research names three employment nodes rather than retail corridors: SH 130 at SH 71, where Giga Texas sits; Parmer Lane in north Austin, home to Apple and Samsung's Austin plant; and Taylor in Williamson County, where Samsung's new fab is ramping [^7]. Projects that serve plant workers, from apartments to quick-service restaurants and hotels, cluster around those nodes and carry their concentration risk.

The one TxDOT count in the research is east of the metro: on US 290 east of Elgin, 2022 traffic volumes were mostly below 20,000 vehicles per day, and 17% of eastbound trips continued past Elgin, according to TxDOT's final traffic study of January 15, 2025 [^39]. For any other Austin corridor, the study must pull the station, year and position from TxDOT's AADT layer [^40] and state whether the site faces mainlanes, frontage road or a toll segment without direct access.

Travel demand on I-35 also brings large competitors. Buc-ee's opened a 74,000-square-foot San Marcos travel center with 128 fueling positions on August 12, 2026 [^38]. A fuel and convenience study anywhere on the I-35 or SH 130 corridors between Austin and San Antonio has to treat a store of that size as part of the competitive set.

Apartments: occupancy recovering, rents still falling

Matthews reports Q2 2026 vacancy of 12.29%, down 289 basis points in a year, with effective rent down 3.88% to $1,414 and Class A asking rent at $1,741 [^13]. There were 14,610 units under construction, 4.55% of inventory, and 10 of 25 submarkets had none [^13]. Austin delivered 31,000 apartments in 2024 [^14]; by Q3 2025 rents were down nearly 8% [^15], and RealPage forecasts rent growth below 1% for the four quarters from Q2 2026 [^16]. Build-to-rent adds about 1,700 units under construction [^17].

This is the clearest oversupply correction among the Texas metros, more severe than Dallas-Fort Worth, where strong absorption is keeping pace with a larger pipeline. For a lender, the correction cuts both ways. A project delivering in a submarket with nothing under construction may lease up into a recovering market. A project in a submarket still absorbing peak-era buildings competes on concessions. A market study for an Austin apartment project should use effective rent (see effective rent versus asking rent), document the competing lease-ups, and test coverage at today's rent with no recovery assumed. Rider Levett Bucknall put Austin multifamily hard costs at $185 to $275 per square foot in Q2 2026 [^18], with Austin cost escalation of 4.32% over the year to Q1 2026 [^19]. See multifamily feasibility.

Industrial: about double its long-term vacancy

Industrial vacancy was 22.3% at Cushman & Wakefield [^20], 21.4% at Colliers with 12.7 million square feet still under construction [^21], and 22.5% at KBC Advisors against a long-term average of 11.2% [^22]. Speculative space is the problem; owner-user projects with their own demand are a different credit. A small-bay or flex study here must prove the tenant, not the market.

Retail: the exception

Retail is tight. Partners Real Estate measured 3.6% vacancy and 5.1% availability in Q2 2026 [^23]; Cushman & Wakefield measured 4.2% [^24]; and CBRE, as reported by ICSC, ranked Austin first among U.S. markets for retail space shortfall [^25]. Neighborhood retail serving the new eastern and northern subdivisions has the best supply story in the metro, with shopping-center hard costs of $205 to $335 per square foot in Q2 2026 [^18]. See retail feasibility.

Hotels and short-term rentals

For economy through upper-midscale hotels, CoStar data reported by a broker showed 65.7% occupancy, a $101.65 average daily rate and $66.79 RevPAR for the 12 months to January 2025, with 896 rooms under construction against an inventory of 20,329 [^26]. RevPAR then fell 14.3% in Q2 2025 and 6.3% in Q3 2025, when the convention center was closed [^27]. Austin's hotel pipeline held 116 projects in Q2 2026 [^28], and one advisory firm called Austin among the weakest-performing U.S. markets in late 2025 because of inventory growth rather than a collapse in demand [^29]. At the luxury end, Canyon Ranch Austin was set to open with 141 rooms on more than 600 acres near Lake Travis from October 15, 2026 [^30]. Short-term rental listings grew 32.2% in the year to March 2025, with occupancy of 45% to 50% [^31]. A hotel study must test break-even against new supply (see the break-even occupancy trap and hotel feasibility).

Senior housing, storage, health care and RV parks

Active adult occupancy rose from 85.2% in 1Q 2026 [^32] to 88.0% in 2Q 2026 [^33], a recovering trend for senior housing studies. Austin was one of only two U.S. markets with year-over-year gains in climate-controlled self-storage rates in August 2026 [^34]. HCA's St. David's HealthCare took over 14 urgent care centers in August 2026 [^35]. The Jellystone Park north of Austin opened in June 2025 with more than 400 paved RV sites [^36], a large competitor for any RV park in the region. In San Marcos, a Texas State University student housing project of 260 units and 759 beds closed a $116 million construction loan in May 2026 [^37].

City of Austin

On November 2, 2023, the City Council eliminated motor vehicle parking minimums citywide, keeping accessible and bicycle requirements [^41]; the vote was 8-2 [^42]. Austin is the opposite of Houston, which still sets ratios for most uses. A project above 2,000 vehicle trips per day needs a traffic impact analysis under the Land Development Code [^43], which matters for a large retail center, a travel center or a mixed-use site. The state's 2023 law on extraterritorial jurisdiction has also changed the map at the edge: by December 15, 2023 Austin had received 191 petitions for direct release from its ETJ and released 74 properties [^44]. A parcel that has left the ETJ answers to the county, which regulates subdivision and infrastructure but not use. The study should state which government approves the site.

Water, aquifer and flood

Hays County, Travis County and Williamson County fall under the Edwards Aquifer protection program, so a site over the recharge or contributing zone needs an approved plan from the state environmental agency before construction [^46]. In February 2026, Hays County tabled a proposed moratorium on water-heavy projects over questions about its legal authority [^45]; the proposal shows where local sentiment sits, and a study for a car wash, water park or data-heavy user in the county should document the water supply in writing. After the July 2025 Hill Country floods, FEMA's DR-4879 declaration added Travis and Williamson counties for Individual Assistance [^47], and the SBA amended its disaster-loan declaration to include them on July 10, 2025 [^48].

What the research could not confirm

The research found no primary-source hotel occupancy tax rate for the City of Austin; the only figure came from an unnamed blog, and we leave it out. The rate stack has to come from the Comptroller and the city before a hotel pro forma is final; the statewide structure is on our Texas page. Austin's short-term rental rules, impact fees and utility capacity were also not found, so each is a closing condition, met by a fee letter from the city and a utility commitment in writing.

Austin has no SBA district of its own. Travis, Williamson and Hays counties are served by the SBA's San Antonio District Office [^49], so district-level totals mix the two metros; see the San Antonio page for the district figures. The research found no official fiscal 2025 ranking of 7(a) lenders or certified development companies for the Austin area. The SBA's list of certified development companies includes PeopleFund CDC, based in Austin [^50]. Rather than lean on a ranking, ask the bank and the CDC to show their recent closings in the asset class.

Under 504, the borrower's injection rises to 15% when the business is new or the building is special purpose, and to 20% when both apply, under SOP 50 10 8.1 (see SBA 7(a) and 504). Institutional capital is active here too: USCIS approved CMB's Travis South industrial project on August 25, 2025, with up to $15.2 million of EB-5 capital in a $61.08 million total [^51], an example of the job-creation evidence an EB-5 study must carry (see how EB-5 jobs are counted). Bigger apartment and industrial financings are bank, debt-fund and agency deals (see conventional and institutional lending).

The research made no USDA rural or HUBZone finding for any Austin-area parcel, but it does show where the programs are in play. Our USDA worked cases in Bastrop and Elgin sit in Bastrop County, east of Austin.

  • USDA. For a B&I guarantee, the site must sit outside every city or town above 50,000 population [^53]. Austin, Round Rock and the large suburbs fail; the small towns of Bastrop and Caldwell counties are the places to run USDA's eligibility map. Above $1,000,000, USDA makes an independent feasibility study, by a consultant it accepts, a condition of guaranteeing a loan to a new business [^54]. See USDA Rural Development.
  • Opportunity Zones. Bastrop County contains three designated tracts on the Governor's 2018 list [^52]; any other Austin-area parcel must be matched to the list.
  • HUBZone. Run the exact parcel through the SBA's HUBZone map at filing.
  • Disaster lending. Travis and Williamson counties were added to the July 2025 SBA disaster declaration [^48], a separate program from the 7(a) and 504 guarantees.

The Austin region holds five of our worked cases, all in smaller towns where a feasibility study company has to separate spillover demand from Austin from the town's own. The gas station case in Lockhart, Caldwell County shows how a fuel and convenience site on US 183 is tested under SBA 7(a) when the use approval is still open. The limited-service hotel case in Bastrop tests a branded hotel on SH 71 against new rooms already registered in the same town, under SBA 504. The RV park case in Bastrop shows how leisure and workforce demand are separated for a USDA Business and Industry guarantee. The meat processing case in Elgin shows how a USDA-financed plant proves its livestock supply and its customers. Just west of the metro, the Hill Country lodging and retreat case near Johnson City tests a small resort against luxury and short-term rental supply under SBA 7(a).
