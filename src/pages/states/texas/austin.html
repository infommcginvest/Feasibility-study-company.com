Apartments: occupancy recovering, rents still falling

Matthews reports Q2 2026 vacancy of 12.29%, down 289 basis points in a year, with effective rent down 3.88% to $1,414 and Class A asking rent at $1,741 [^11]. There were 14,610 units under construction, 4.55% of inventory, and 10 of 25 submarkets had none [^11]. The 2024 delivery count was 31,000 apartments [^12]; by Q3 2025 rents were down nearly 8% [^13], and RealPage's forecast for the four quarters from Q2 2026 is rent growth below 1% [^14]. Build-to-rent adds about 1,700 units under construction [^15].

This is the clearest oversupply correction among the Texas metros, more severe than Dallas-Fort Worth, where strong absorption is keeping pace with a larger pipeline. For a lender, the correction cuts both ways. A project delivering in a submarket with nothing under construction may lease up into a recovering market. A project in a submarket still absorbing peak-era buildings competes on concessions. For Austin apartments the test is coverage at today's effective rent with no recovery in the numbers (our note on effective rent versus asking rent explains why asking rent misleads in a market like this), with every competing lease-up in the submarket named.

Costs have not corrected with rents. Austin construction prices rose 4.32% in the year to Q1 2026, above the 3.94% average for Rider Levett Bucknall's Central region in the same report [^17]; a quarter earlier Austin had run below the region, at 4.18% against 4.41% [^47]. The firm's Q2 2026 range for Austin multifamily hard costs is $185 to $275 a square foot [^16]. Falling rents and rising costs squeeze a new project from both sides, which is why the study should show coverage on today's budget and today's rent together. See multifamily feasibility.

Industrial: about double its long-term vacancy

Industrial vacancy was 22.3% at Cushman & Wakefield [^18], 21.4% at Colliers with 12.7 million square feet still under construction [^19], and 22.5% at KBC Advisors against a long-term average of 11.2% [^20]. Speculative space is the problem; owner-user projects with their own demand are a different credit. A small-bay or flex study here must prove the tenant, not the market.

Retail: the exception

Retail is tight. Partners Real Estate measured 3.6% vacancy and 5.1% availability in Q2 2026 [^21]; Cushman & Wakefield measured 4.2% [^22]; and CBRE, as reported by ICSC, ranked Austin first among U.S. markets for retail space shortfall [^23]. Neighborhood retail serving the new eastern and northern subdivisions has the best supply story in the metro, with shopping-center hard costs of $205 to $335 per square foot in Q2 2026 [^16]. See retail feasibility.

Hotels and short-term rentals

For economy through upper-midscale hotels, CoStar data reported by a broker showed 65.7% occupancy, a $101.65 average daily rate and $66.79 RevPAR for the 12 months to January 2025, with 896 rooms under construction against an inventory of 20,329 [^24]. Austin's hotel pipeline held 116 projects in Q2 2026 [^25]. At the luxury end, Canyon Ranch Austin was set to open with 141 rooms on more than 600 acres near Lake Travis from October 15, 2026 [^26], on a total development cost its chief executive estimates at $500 million [^49]. That project is no comparable for a smaller Hill Country resort: HVS's 2026 survey of 2025 budgets puts the national median luxury hotel at $1,604,356 per key and the median full-service hotel at $467,244 [^52], and a resort study should build its budget from its own program. Short-term rental listings grew 32.2% in the year to March 2025, with occupancy of 45% to 50% [^27]. A hotel study must test break-even against new supply (see the break-even occupancy trap and hotel feasibility).

Senior housing, storage, health care and RV parks

Active adult occupancy rose from 85.2% in 1Q 2026 [^28] to 88.0% in 2Q 2026 [^29], a recovering trend for senior housing studies. Austin was one of only two U.S. markets with year-over-year gains in climate-controlled self-storage rates in August 2026 [^30]. Urgent care is consolidating: 14 Austin centers passed to HCA's St. David's HealthCare in August 2026 [^31]. The Jellystone Park north of Austin opened in June 2025 with more than 400 paved RV sites [^32], a large competitor for any RV park in the region. In San Marcos, a Texas State University student housing project of 260 units and 759 beds closed a $116 million construction loan in May 2026 [^33].

Austin's economy rests on government, a university, health care and a handful of very large technology employers, and the last group moves fastest. The Dallas Fed names the State of Texas as the metro's largest employer and UT Austin second, without headcounts [^5]. Among private employers, the Austin Business Journal's July 2026 list puts H-E-B first with 27,721 workers, Tesla second with 16,500, Ascension Texas third with 16,200, St. David's HealthCare fourth with 13,259 and Amazon fifth with 11,000 [^4]. Two of the five are hospital systems, which gives medical office and senior housing a steadier demand base than the headlines suggest.

The tech side is where a lender should look hardest. Tesla's Giga Texas headcount fell to 16,506 at the end of 2025 from 21,191 a year earlier, a drop of 22%, according to its Travis County compliance report as reported by the Austin American-Statesman [^6]. Samsung's fab in Taylor began trial production in September 2026 and plans mass production in 2027 [^7]. East of the city, SpaceX's Starlink operation in Bastrop had more than 1,500 employees in May 2026 [^50], after a $17.3 million grant from the state's semiconductor fund in March 2025 [^51]. BAE Systems' May 2026 plans put $50 million of a $135 million two-state facility program in Austin [^8], and UT's Dell Medical Center hospital near the Domain is planned for 2030 [^7]. Those are real anchors, but each one is a single company's decision. A hotel near Giga Texas, apartments in Taylor or retail on Parmer Lane should be underwritten with a case in which that employer's headcount falls, because one already has.

The research found no military anchor and no other project over $50 million with a confirmed budget. That leaves a feasibility study consultant in Austin to document any employer the demand case relies on with a permit, an incentive agreement or the company's own filing.

Median household income in the same survey was $100,431 [^55], and the Bureau of Economic Analysis measured per capita personal income of $85,549 in 2024 [^56].

The Austin-Round Rock-San Marcos MSA added 53,796 residents in the year to July 2025, per the Austin American-Statesman's reading of the Census Vintage 2025 release [^2]. The research did not retrieve the MSA total or its 2020 base, so this page gives no five-year growth rate. The City of Austin passed 1 million residents, reaching 1,002,632 on July 1, 2025 [^1]. Caldwell County to the southeast, home of our Lockhart case, counted 55,150 residents in the same estimates, as CultureMap Austin reported [^53].

Over the same twelve months to August 2026 the metro added 16,200 payroll jobs, 1.2%, taking the preliminary, unadjusted BLS count to 1,415.9 thousand from 1,399.7 thousand [^3]. For a correcting market that is solid, and it is the number that will eventually absorb the vacant apartments and warehouses. A study should not assume it accelerates.

The supply response is already visible in permits. Trailing twelve-month multifamily permits fell to 8,760 units by June 2026, against a peak of about 22,000 [^10]. In HBWeekly's 2025 tracking, Travis County led with 3,390 permits (up 5%) and Williamson County followed at 2,609, out of about 7,275 in all, 3% fewer than in 2024 [^9]. The message for a lender is that the builders have pulled back hard, which is how a correction ends, but the units started at the peak are still leasing up.

The 2020 to 2024 American Community Survey puts the median value of an owner-occupied home in the Austin market at $465,000 [^55], the five-year level a build-to-rent or for-sale absorption assumption is set beside.

City of Austin

On November 2, 2023, the City Council eliminated motor vehicle parking minimums citywide, keeping accessible and bicycle requirements [^36]; the vote was 8-2 [^37]. No other large Texas city in the research has gone that far: Dallas kept tiers for apartments, and Houston still prints a ratio for most uses. The state's 2023 law on extraterritorial jurisdiction has changed the map at the edge: by December 15, 2023 Austin had received 191 petitions for direct release from its ETJ and released 74 properties [^38]. A parcel that has left the ETJ answers to the county, which regulates subdivision and infrastructure but not use. The study should state which government approves the site.

Water, aquifer and flood

Hays County, Travis County and Williamson County fall under the Edwards Aquifer protection program, so a site over the recharge or contributing zone needs an approved plan from the state environmental agency before construction [^40]. In February 2026, Hays County tabled a proposed moratorium on water-heavy projects over questions about its legal authority [^39]; the proposal shows where local sentiment sits, and a study for a car wash, water park or data-heavy user in the county should document the water supply in writing. After the July 2025 Hill Country floods, FEMA's DR-4879 declaration added Travis and Williamson counties for Individual Assistance [^41], and the SBA amended its disaster-loan declaration to include them on July 10, 2025 [^42]. That disaster program is separate from the 7(a) and 504 guarantees, but a parcel's flood record belongs in the study either way.

The hotel tax the research could not confirm

No primary source in the research gives the City of Austin's hotel occupancy tax; one unnamed blog does, and we leave it out. The rate stack has to come from the Comptroller and the city before a hotel pro forma is final; the statewide structure, and the written conditions that stand in for fees and utility capacity, are on our Texas page.

Much of Austin's new industry sits east and north of the city, along SH 130, the toll road that parallels I-35, and out along US 290 and SH 71. The research names three employment nodes rather than retail corridors: SH 130 at SH 71, where Giga Texas sits; Parmer Lane in north Austin, home to Apple and Samsung's Austin plant; and Taylor in Williamson County, where Samsung's new fab is ramping [^7]. Projects that serve plant workers, from apartments to quick-service restaurants and hotels, cluster around those nodes and carry their concentration risk.

The one TxDOT count the research holds for the Austin area sits east of the metro: on US 290 east of Elgin, 2022 traffic volumes were mostly below 20,000 vehicles per day, and 17% of eastbound trips continued past Elgin, according to TxDOT's final traffic study of January 15, 2025 [^35]. That is a corridor reading, not a site count. Everywhere else the study pulls its own station as our Texas page sets out, and on SH 130 it has to show whether the parcel faces the tolled mainlanes, which give no direct access, or a frontage road that does.

Travel demand on I-35 also brings large competitors. Buc-ee's opened a 74,000-square-foot San Marcos travel center with 128 fueling positions on August 12, 2026 [^34]. A fuel and convenience study anywhere on the I-35 or SH 130 corridors between Austin and San Antonio has to treat a store of that size as part of the competitive set.

County Business Patterns counted 1,003 gas station and convenience store establishments in the Austin market in 2023 [^57], the base a new interstate fuel site's share has to be argued against, large travel centers included.

Austin has no SBA district of its own. Travis, Williamson and Hays counties are served by the SBA's San Antonio District Office [^43], so district-level totals mix the two metros; see the San Antonio page for the district figures. The research found no official fiscal 2025 ranking of 7(a) lenders or certified development companies for the Austin area. PeopleFund CDC, a certified development company headquartered in Austin, is on the SBA's CDC list [^44]. Rather than lean on a ranking, ask the bank and the CDC to show their recent closings in the asset class.

Under 504, the borrower's injection rises to 15% when the business is new or the building is special purpose, and to 20% when both apply, under SOP 50 10 8.1 (see SBA 7(a) and 504); hotels are on SBA's special purpose list, so a new hotel business pays the top tier. Institutional capital is active here too: USCIS approved CMB's Travis South industrial project on August 25, 2025, with up to $15.2 million of EB-5 capital in a $61.08 million total [^45], an example of the job-creation evidence an EB-5 study must carry (see how EB-5 jobs are counted). Bigger apartment and industrial financings are bank, debt-fund and agency deals (see conventional and institutional lending).

Program maps matter most in the eastern counties. Bastrop County holds three designated Opportunity Zone tracts on the Governor's 2018 list [^46], and the small towns of Bastrop and Caldwell counties, where our USDA cases sit, are the places a site can pass USDA's rural test; Austin, Round Rock and the large suburbs cannot. How each test is run on the parcel is on our Texas page.

Projects in the Austin market's own counties drew 4,228 7(a) loans worth $2,608.1 million in fiscal years 2016 to 2025, net of cancellations, and $501.7 million under 504, by SBA's loan file [^54].

All five of our Austin-region cases are in smaller towns, and each one has to separate spillover demand from Austin from the town's own. Lockhart, in Caldwell County, is the setting of the gas station case: a fuel and convenience site on US 183 under SBA 7(a), with the use approval still open. Bastrop holds two: the limited-service hotel case tests a branded hotel on SH 71 under SBA 504 against new rooms already registered in town, and the RV park case splits leisure from workforce demand for a USDA Business and Industry guarantee. The meat processing case in Elgin proves a USDA-financed plant's livestock supply and its buyers. West of the metro, the Hill Country lodging and retreat case near Johnson City sets a small resort against luxury and short-term rental supply under SBA 7(a).
