An interstate or highway fuel station is defined by where it sits, not by what it sells: an interchange or highway frontage site whose customers are passing through as often as they live nearby. The format overlaps travel centers at one end and ordinary branded or independent stores at the other. Its earnings model leans harder on gallons than a town-center store does, because the interchange delivers traffic that a neighborhood cannot, and because highway customers compare prices on signs before they exit.

The research found no published revenue for Buc-ee's or any other megasite operator, so the per-store benchmark has to come from a public company. Murphy USA is the one the research uses. Its stores sold 235,751 retail gallons each per month in 2025 [^1]. Its total fuel contribution was 30.7 cents a gallon in 2025 against 30.5 cents in 2024 [^1], and its retail fuel margin was 28.1 cents, flat on the year [^2]. Inside, each store earned $41,134 of merchandise margin a month at a unit margin of 20.2% [^1]. That is a volume model: thin cents on a very large number of gallons, with a store built around speed rather than food.

For a lender the Murphy figures are a ceiling check, not a target. An SBA-size interchange store with fewer positions and a less known name should not be projected at Murphy's throughput without a traffic count and a capture analysis that get it there. Big-box fuel sites, which NACS counted at 6,862 in December 2024 and which sell roughly twice the volume of a traditional retailer per store [^17], set the low-price end of the same market. Where one sits near the exit, the study should show the subject's posted price against it. The pipeline at the volume end is still growing: Murphy guided to as many as 50 new stores for 2025 and opened 29 in the fourth quarter alone [^3].

FHWA's December 2025 figures put seasonally adjusted vehicle-miles at 277.2 billion for the month, up 0.4% on a year earlier [^4]. Rural roads carried 81.8 billion vehicle-miles in the month and urban roads 183.9 billion [^4]. Neither number says anything about one interchange. A rural exit on a long interstate stretch can carry heavy through traffic with few local residents, while a suburban exit draws commuters whose trips repeat daily. The research found no published interstate traffic benchmark per station, so the study has to take the state DOT's annual average daily traffic for the ramps and the crossing road, the share of heavy trucks where the count reports it, and the directional split that tells the lender which side of the interchange the subject serves.

Capture follows from the census. Every fueling position at the subject's exit, and at the exits on either side, competes for the same through traffic; the stations on the far side of the bridge compete mostly for the opposite direction. A study that counts only the stations within a radius misses that geometry. At an interchange, defining the trade area means drawing the corridor rather than a circle, and our research on underserved gas station markets supplies the test for whether an exit has room for one more store.

Access is the third piece of interchange evidence, and it is physical rather than statistical. The study should say which ramp terminal the subject sits on, how far a driver travels from the ramp to the driveway, whether the turn into the site is protected, and whether the canopy and price sign can be seen before the exit decision point. A store on the far side of a crossing road, or one turn off a roundabout, can lose most through-traffic capture even when the count at the ramp is strong. None of those measures has a national benchmark in the research, so they are recorded from the site visit and the DOT's driveway permit, and the capture rate is set from them.

The truck side of the exit

Diesel and truck parking are part of the demand at most interstate exits, even for a store built around cars. FHWA's handbook counts 2,925,210 registered combination trucks and more than 175.3 billion combination-truck miles in 2019, and carries a forecast of 1.5% average annual growth in those miles from 2017 to 2030 [^19]. That forecast is well above recent growth in all driving, which FHWA put at 0.4% year over year in December 2025 [^4], which is why a store with diesel lanes and some truck parking draws on a faster-growing pool than a car-only forecourt. FHWA's national parking model assigns 0.77 of commercial truck parking demand to truck stops and 0.23 to public rest areas [^20], and its 2019 survey found an average of 143 spaces per truck stop [^21]. A small interchange store will not compete for overnight parking, but the study should say whether it serves trucks at all, because a diesel island without parking draws only the drivers who need fuel and nothing else.

Driver loyalty is measurable at the chain level. Berkshire Hathaway reported that Pilot's Pro Preference score, the share of drivers naming it their preferred stop, rose from 27% in 2022 to 35% in 2025, second in the industry [^22]. That is the closest published analogue to the fair-share capture a study assigns, and it shows how much of the truck trade the chains already hold before an independent opens.

The largest interstate operators are still adding sites, but their results show the same margin squeeze a single store faces. Pilot ran 675 travel centers and 82 fuel-only sites at the end of 2025, two fewer travel centers and five more fuel-only sites than a year earlier [^23,24], while its revenue fell from $46,891 million to $42,198 million and its pre-tax earnings fell 69.1% to $190 million [^23]. Love's reached 668 locations in 42 states [^25], opened 18 of the 20 sites it targeted in 2025 with 1,408 new truck spaces [^26], and kept opening exits in 2026, including Mandeville, Louisiana with 125 spaces and Dora, Alabama with 123 [^27]. Getty Realty's management describes the top three operators as owning about 30% of travel centers [^29], and NATSO's directory lists 2,279 locations [^28]; the rest of the corridor belongs to regional chains and independents, the borrowers an SBA or USDA lender is most likely to see.

Investors' numbers set the value side. Service Properties Trust leases 175 travel centers to BP's TA under five master leases running to 2033 [^30], at a minimum rent of $254.0 million a year with 2% annual increases set when BP bought TA [^31], and rent coverage on those leases ran from 1. The same filing carries $2,254,950 thousand of investment and $180,329 thousand of annual minimum rent on the TA sites, and $1,015,156 thousand and $83,933 thousand on the Petro sites [^35]; rent set against the landlord's cost is the yield an investor accepts for an interstate location, and it is the test a sale-leaseback buyer will apply to a borrower's site.05x to 1.35x at the end of 2025 [^32]. Getty Realty bought its first four travel centers in 2025 for $47.1 million [^33], and a franchised TA under construction at Laurel, Mississippi carries a budget of $15 million to $20 million [^34]. For a lender, the coverage range is the useful benchmark: even master leases guaranteed by an investment-grade parent carry thin coverage at the weaker interstate locations, so a single-site borrower's projected coverage should be read against that range rather than against a national average.

The research found no cost per interstate store; Buc-ee's is private and publishes neither cost nor revenue. The one project with a reported budget, Greenwood, Indiana, was approved at $85 million [^5], many times the $10 million combined 7(a) and 504 limit [^14]. Nothing in this table is a cost comparable for an SBA loan. What the table shows is the competitor a smaller interchange store may face: a store of more than 70,000 square feet with 120 or more positions and a staff of about 200, built after an entitlement process that can include eight-figure road obligations. The company states a build time of 18 to 24 months from the start of construction [^7], and it reported a pipeline of 15 more travel centers through 2031, with its Goodyear, Arizona store employing more than 200 people [^11].

For the SBA-size store itself, the cost evidence has to be built from the specific program: fuel system, canopy, store, site work and the access improvements the state DOT will require at an interchange. The fuel-and-store ranges on our branded fuel page test reasonableness; a contractor's bid and the DOT's driveway permit conditions set the number.

SBA's list of special purpose examples includes gas stations [^14], so an interchange store under 504 carries a borrower contribution of 15%, or 20% for a new business, against a coverage floor of 1.15x [^14]. A new store under 7(a) reaches 1.15x within two years, and the purchase of an operating interchange store is tested at 1.25x on the seller's historical cash flow [^14]. The loan rules make a study discretionary: 13 CFR 120.160(b): SBA may require professional appraisals, a survey, or a feasibility study [^15]. The SBA 7(a) and 504 practice page shows the file structure we deliver.

Rates are set at pricing, and the published quotes are broker marketing. One broker listed an indicative 504 fixed rate of 6.95%, a 7(a) variable rate of 7.00% and loan-to-value up to 90% on special purpose loans on October 3, 2026 [^12]; a gas station page from the same broker family showed a 7(a) variable rate of 6.75% in August [^13]. A study should model the lender's term sheet, not a quote, and show coverage at a rate above it.

Two things set interchange files apart. First, schedule: an interchange often needs DOT access permits and sometimes off-site road work, and the coverage test in Year 2 assumes the store opened on time. Second, rural eligibility: an interchange outside the limits of any large city may qualify for USDA's Business and Industry guarantee, which is why our Hankamer, Texas travel center case is financed through USDA rather than SBA. The USDA Rural Development practice page covers those rules.

  1. The opening date is treated as fixed

    Large interchange projects slip. Buc-ee's moved its Gallaway, Tennessee site from summer 2026 to May 2027, its West Memphis, Arkansas site to June 2028, two years late, and its Mebane, North Carolina site to the fourth quarter of 2027; New Kent, Virginia may not open until 2029 because of road construction [^8]. A smaller project faces the same permits on a smaller budget. The study should show coverage with a one-year delay and the interest carry it adds.

  2. Entitlement is assumed

    In Palmer Lake, Colorado, legal challenges led to a proposal's withdrawal [^7]. A study should state the zoning, any conditional use or special use permit needed, the hearing dates and the conditions a lender should expect, and make the permit a lender condition until it issues.

  3. Off-site obligations are missing from the budget

    Greenwood's approval came with $13 million to $15 million of traffic improvements [^5]. An SBA-size store will not face that scale, but a turn lane, signal or driveway rebuild can still exceed the contingency. The DOT's permit conditions belong in the sources and uses.

  4. The megasite at the next exit is left out

    Buc-ee's opened in Huber Heights, Ohio on April 6, 2026 and in Goodyear, Arizona in late June 2026, with Benton, Arkansas targeted for August 2026 [^8]. A census drawn as a radius can miss a megasite one exit down that captures the long-distance traveler. The study should map the corridor, not the circle.

  5. National traffic stands in for the exit count

    A 0.4% rise in national vehicle-miles [^4] is not evidence for one interchange. Files without the state DOT count for the ramps, dated and cited, are incomplete.

The Opelika, Alabama case at I-85 Exit 64 buys an existing branded store on the interchange frontage road. Two 24-hour stations already flank it at the same exit, the next exit holds a RaceTrac travel-center format, a QuikTrip that opened in 2025 and a Circle K, and a Buc-ee's operates further down the corridor at Auburn. It shows the change-of-ownership test at an exit where the competition is still arriving.

The Cartersville, Georgia case at I-75 Exit 296 is the new-build version. The subject sits one turn off the exit's roundabouts, without interstate frontage or visibility, at an exit already served by a Pilot and a TA travel center and several branded stations. That case shows why a store that cannot win the through traveler has to be sized for commuters and local trade.

The two travel-center cases show the large end. The Terrell, Texas truck stop at I-20 and FM 148 is an SBA 504 file on highway corridor zoning, and the Hankamer, Texas travel center at I-10 and SH 61 is a USDA Business and Industry file on an unincorporated tract with no county zoning. Both sit closer to the truck stop and travel center asset class than to a car-oriented store. The Lockhart, Texas case is a useful contrast: it has no interstate exit, yet a Buc-ee's on I-10 at Luling sits inside its outer ring.

On our gas station and c-store hub, interstate fuel is a location rather than a format, so it overlaps every other subtype. A branded interchange store carries the supply agreement questions on the branded fuel page; a store that competes with travel-center restaurants needs the kitchen evidence on the foodservice-led page; and the chargers now appearing at megasites are covered on the EV charging page.
