Hawaii

A visitor economy on four islands, read on four county records.

Lodging is the one asset class with a complete statewide series in the published record. The state's Visitor Plant Inventory counts the units every year 1, and DBEDT's hotel reports, built on STR data, give occupancy, rate and revenue per available room for the state, for each island and for the main resort regions 57. Almost everything else a lender asks about in Hawaii is published by county or not at all.

Labor and building costs moved in 2025 and 2026 as well. The minimum wage stepped to $16.00 on January 1, 2026, with $18.00 scheduled from January 1, 2028 16. Honolulu led the North American cities in Rider Levett Bucknall's annual construction cost change for the second quarter of 2026, at 5.93% 17. Property insurance, the third, is set out in the costs section.

On the demand side, the state's resident estimate has fallen in each interval since the 2020 base, and the Vintage 2025 release lowered every earlier year as well 3. Statewide payrolls barely moved, from 641,700 jobs in August 2025 to 643,000 in August 2026 on the seasonally adjusted count 18. Lending is split too: SBA's Hawaii district also serves Guam and other Pacific jurisdictions, so no district total stands in for a Hawaii figure on this page 19.

Lodging

The visitor plant, its 2026 room rates and the tax on a night's stay.

DBEDT's inventory counts 85,227 visitor units in 2025, 2.6% more than a year earlier 1. In its May 2026 report the state's hotels were 75.3% occupied, rooms averaged $370.94 a night, and RevPAR stood at $279.29, 2.1% above May 2025 5. For the first quarter of 2026 the March report gives 76.4% occupancy, a $379.65 rate and $290.10 of RevPAR, up 0.8% on the year 20. The island figures are in the market sections, read from the same reports. For June 2026, Skift's lodging report, citing Powell & Aucello, put RevPAR growth on the year at +6% on Oahu, +14% in Maui County, +10% on Hawaii Island and +7% on Kauai 21.

The room tax is the line that moved. Act 96 of 2025 raised the state transient accommodations tax from 10.25% to 11% from January 1, 2026 and extended it to cruise fares 2. Each of the four taxing counties adds its own 3%, for a combined accommodations tax of 14%, and the general excise tax of 4.0% to 4.5% by county sits on top of it, with a pass-on to the guest of up to 4.712% 22. The county ordinances behind the 3% are named in the Maui, Hawaii Island and Kauai sections; no primary Honolulu document for the county rate was found, so an Oahu budget confirms it with the city.

No statewide hotel pipeline or list of named openings was found. A lodging study in Hawaii therefore counts its competitive set and any rooms under construction from the island's own permits and the operators' own announcements, and reads the island's hotel year beside it.

Statewide Supply

Lodging is the statewide supply series; Oahu's industrial and retail space is the only other.

The statewide supply record is the visitor plant and DBEDT's hotel reports, set out just above, with each island's hotel year in its own section below. Beyond lodging, Colliers' quarterly reports for Oahu industrial and retail space are the only published series for a whole island, and they are in the Oahu section. For hospitals and surgery centers, senior housing, licensed child care, self-storage, campgrounds and RV parks, convenience stores and apartments, whether vacant or under construction, no statewide or neighbor-island count was found. A study in any of those classes counts its competitors by name on its own island.

The latest year's change was -2,132, or -0.15%. Its parts were 14,631 births and 12,607 deaths, net international migration of +4,768 and net domestic migration of -8,876 3. The same vintage revised the 2024 estimate by -11,194 against the vintage before it 3. DBEDT's release of the 2025 Data Book ranks the state's change from 2020 to 2025 the lowest of the states and the District of Columbia 23.

Costs

Homeowners rate filings, a reopened hurricane fund, wage steps and Honolulu building costs.

Property insurance

Insurance Business reported in January 2026 that at least 20 homeowners rate increases were approved in Hawaii in 2025. Among them was State Farm's on wind, hail and hurricane cover, effective November 15, 2025: +28.5% overall, up to 48.4% for an individual policy, reaching 98,970 policyholders and adding +$45.5M of written premium 6. These are homeowners lines. No approved commercial property rate change was found in a primary source, and no carrier withdrawal was documented by name.

Act 296 (SB1044), signed on July 7, 2025, reactivated the Hawaii Hurricane Relief Fund and gave more powers to the Hawaii Property Insurance Association, Hawaii's insurer of last resort 15. The Insurance Division's condominium guidance limits the fund to associations of apartment owners that at least two state licensed insurers have denied hurricane cover, with insured value above $10 million 15. A reserve figure for the fund was reported on television but not confirmed in a primary source, so none is given here. Two further bills, HB1057 and SB1376, would widen the association's and the fund's reach with insurer assessments and a recording fee; both were carried over to the 2026 session, and their final status was not found 28.

Building and labor

No index of Hawaii building costs against a U.S. average was found. The nearest reading is the cost consultant's 5.93% annual change for Honolulu, the top of its North American table for the second quarter of 2026 17, so the hard cost in a Hawaii budget is priced by a contractor for the island in question.

The state minimum wage was $14.00 from January 1, 2024, is $16.00 from January 1, 2026 and is scheduled to reach $18.00 on January 1, 2028 16. Act 115 of 2025 set a minimum civil penalty of $500 for violations of the Wage and Hour Law 29. Staffing plans for hotels, care homes and restaurants that open after 2027 carry the third step in their later years. For tipped staff, a 2026 employer guide gives a cash wage of $14.75 with a tip credit of $1.25, subject to conditions 30.

Property tax

The Tax Foundation's 2026 table puts Hawaii's effective property tax rate on owner-occupied housing at 0.29%, the lowest of the states 31. That is a residential figure. Commercial property is taxed by each county at class rates it sets for itself, and no statewide effective commercial rate was found, so a study takes the rate for the parcel's class from its own county.

Rules

Certificate of Need, and the 2024 law that handed vacation rentals to the counties.

Hawaii keeps a broad Certificate of Need regime under HRS chapter 323D, which the State Health Planning and Development Agency runs: a certificate is needed to build, expand, alter or convert a health care facility, or to start or change a service, above the statute's expenditure thresholds 32. A county or state building permit for hospital work is not issued without a current certificate or the agency's statement that none applies 10. HRS 323D-54 exempts assisted living facilities and extended care adult residential care homes, bed changes of 10% or 10 beds, whichever is less, within two years, non-patient areas such as parking and offices, and primary care clinics under the thresholds 9. SB1423 SD1 of 2025 would add state health department facilities, dialysis centers and psychiatric behavioral health services to the list; its enactment was not found 33.

Act 17 of 2024 (SB2919), signed on May 3, 2024, treats the provision of transient accommodations as a use that is not residential, lets the counties phase it out or amortize it, and lets them regulate its time, place, manner and duration; it defines transient as fewer than 180 consecutive days 34. Honolulu's own minimum-stay rule is in the Oahu section; no 2025 or 2026 short-term rental ordinance was retrieved for Hawaii County or Kauai.

Licensing detail for child care, liquor, fuel storage tanks, car washes and campgrounds was not sourced in the research, and no statewide rule on sales tax for construction materials was found beyond the general excise tax itself. An asset study in those classes takes each licence from the agency that issues it.

Hazards

A Kona low declaration in April 2026, after two drought declarations in 2025.

Major disaster declaration DR-4909-HI, approved on April 8, 2026, answers the Kona low of March 10 to 24, 2026, whose severe storms brought flooding, landslides and mudslides; Honolulu, Hawaii and Maui counties received individual and public assistance, and applications for individual aid closed on June 14, 2026 12. Kauai County is not in it. A project in one of the three declared counties that was damaged in March 2026, or that relies on a road or utility repaired with public assistance, states where that repair stands in its schedule.

SBA declared statewide drought disasters twice in 2025. The first, announced on March 28, 2025, made economic injury loans available for a drought that began on January 1, 2025 in Hawaii, Honolulu, Kalawao, Kauai and Maui counties 35. The second, announced on September 3, 2025 for a drought that began on June 24, 2025, again covered all five counties, with loans of up to $2 million at 4% for businesses 36. The 2023 Maui wildfires and the rebuild that followed are in the Lahaina section.

No FEMA National Risk Index ranking for Hawaii's counties was retrieved, and no quantified measure of wind, seismic, wildfire or flood exposure. Flood exposure for a site therefore comes from the effective FEMA panel for the parcel, and the wind and fire terms from the insurer's quote.

Honolulu County

Oahu: Honolulu County, which is the whole Urban Honolulu metro.

Oahu is one county, and the county is the Urban Honolulu metropolitan area. DBEDT's Vintage 2025 county estimates put Honolulu County at 988,703 residents on July 1, 2025, against 1,012,334 on July 1, 2020 and an April 2020 base of 1,016,504; the county's average yearly change from 2020 to 2025 was -0.5%, and its net migration averaged -6,794 a year 4. The Honolulu metro held 460,400 nonfarm jobs in August 2026 against 461,600 a year before, a change of -1,200, and its unemployment rate rose to 2.9% from 2.2% 18. No Oahu-only federal job count was found; the statewide federal figure is in the payroll section above.

Hotels

Oahu's hotels filled 78.6% of their rooms in 2025 at $284.12 a night on average, for $223.33 of RevPAR, 1.3% below 2024; Waikiki's RevPAR was $212.87 7. The DBEDT region it calls Other Oahu, which takes in the Ko Olina resorts, ran 74.5% occupancy at $371.34 and $276.72 of RevPAR 7. A Waikiki hotel and a Ko Olina resort therefore start from different lines of the same table.

Industrial and retail space

Oahu is the one island with a quarterly broker series for space other than hotels. Colliers measured islandwide industrial vacancy at 1.40% in the first quarter of 2026; the inventory was 42,447,605 square feet and the quarter's net absorption -24,288 square feet 11. The asking base rent was $1.60 a square foot a month and operating expenses $0.62 11. In Colliers' Central Oahu submarket, 11,023,071 square feet stood 1.49% vacant 11. Retail vacancy was 6.01% islandwide in the same quarter, the highest in three quarters by Colliers' account, which ties it to anchor-tenant closures in urban Honolulu 37.

Apartments and rentals

No 2025 or 2026 apartment vacancy, rent or construction count was found for Honolulu. CBRE's first-quarter 2024 figures, the latest the research holds, give 92.7% occupancy, an average effective rent of $2,899 a unit and fewer than 2,000 units underway 38. A multifamily study on Oahu surveys current rents and occupancy at its competitors instead of carrying the 2024 figures forward. Honolulu's Ordinance 22-7 set a 90-day minimum stay for short-term rentals; a federal judge enjoined it on October 13, 2022, leaving the earlier 30-day minimum in force 39. A Honolulu Board of REALTORS presentation of April 11, 2025 refers to a 2025 ordinance, Ordinance 25-2, with an effective date of September 30, 2025 40; whether the city now enforces the longer minimum was not confirmed in a primary city source, so an Oahu rental study takes the rule from the city's own permitting office.

Honolulu County

Kapolei and Ewa, read on Honolulu County figures.

The Census Bureau publishes no annual population estimate for Kapolei or Ewa: in Hawaii the Urban Honolulu census designated place is the only area below the county that it estimates each year 41. A Kapolei study therefore works from Honolulu County's figures, named as the county's, and from its own count of the competitors and projects around its site.

Honolulu County authorized 1,446 units of new private housing in 2025, after 1,638 in 2024, in the Census Bureau's permit series as FRED republishes it 42. No count for the Kapolei and Ewa area alone was found. For population, payrolls and hotels, the county and metro figures in the Oahu section are the ones a Kapolei file uses; for the Ko Olina resorts, the Other Oahu line there is the nearest published hotel reading, since no Kapolei or Ko Olina figure was found.

The research's Kapolei record is otherwise one of master-planned housing, industrial parks and Hawaiian Home Lands homestead lots, each a named project or park. No sub-area employer count, no Kapolei impact fee and no 2025 or 2026 moratorium or zoning change was found, and Honolulu's county rules apply here as on the rest of the island.

Maui County

Maui County: hotels, permits and payrolls, and the vacation rental bill signed in December 2025.

Maui County, which also takes in Molokai and Lanai, counted 160,674 residents in the July 1, 2025 estimate; five years earlier the figure was 164,842, and the April 2020 base 164,836 4. DBEDT gives the county's average yearly change since 2020 as -0.5%, with a fall of 1.0% from 2024 to 2025; net domestic migration was -2,226 in that year and -2,652 the year before 4. Permits fell as well, to 902 units in 2025 from 1,118 units in 2024 43. Payrolls in the Kahului-Wailuku metro, which DBEDT reports with Kalawao County included, reached 75,200 in August 2026, +1,300 from 73,900, and the metro's unemployment rate rose to 2.9% from 2.4% 18.

Hotels

Maui County's hotels charged an average daily rate of $536.80 in 2025, at 64.4% occupancy and $345.95 of RevPAR, a RevPAR 2.2% above 2024; the Wailea resort region's RevPAR was $552.60 7. For May 2026 the report gives the county 66.1% occupancy, 7.2 points above May 2025, a $494 rate and $326 of RevPAR, which was 12.0% higher 5.

Bill 9

The Maui County Council passed Bill 9 on final reading, five votes to three, on December 15, 2025 44, and the mayor signed it the same day 45. It ends transient vacation rental use in the A-1 and A-2 apartment districts from January 1, 2029 in West Maui and from January 1, 2031 in the rest of the county 44; the units it reaches are the apartment-zoned rentals of the Minatoya list 46. The county's Department of Finance had counted 6,208 apartment-zoned short-term rentals as of May 29, 2024 45. A proposal of the council's investigative group, carried in Resolution 25-230, would let 4,500 units keep operating by rezoning them into new H-3 and H-4 hotel districts; that rezoning is a proposal, not law 44. Civil Beat reported that litigation was expected 46. A councilmember who voted against the bill put a figure on the tax revenue it would cost, a political estimate rather than an audited one, and none is used here.

For a buyer or a lender, a unit in one of those districts has a fixed date for its last vacation rental night, and the rezoning route is still only proposed. A hotel study on Maui reads the same dates from the other side: the units that leave the vacation rental market from 2029 compete with it until then. The county's own 3% transient accommodations tax, under Ordinance 101 of 2021, has applied since November 1, 2021 on Maui, Molokai and Lanai 47.

West Maui

Lahaina: rebuild counts since the 2023 fire, and the West Maui resort region.

No population estimate exists for West Maui alone, so the rebuild's own counts are the nearest published measure of residential supply there. AsAmNews, citing county data on January 23, 2026, gave 559 permits issued, 112 rebuilds completed and 334 permits in processing 48.

By August 2026, Honolulu Civil Beat's reading of the county's figures was nearly 600 homes rebuilt, 315 homes under construction and about 1,000 more units with permits issued or in process, against only about 45 homes rebuilt at the fire's second anniversary 8. Commercial rebuilding has moved more slowly, and Civil Beat describes cleared lots where businesses have not been rebuilt: five commercial properties received the first historic-district approvals in July 2026, and the county plans to use $50 million of federal funds to buy out about 10 oceanfront properties 8. The Grassroot Institute, an advocacy organization, counted fewer than 30 non-residential rebuild permits issued and 13 completed in August 2026, and reported that the county had not waived rebuild permit fees 49. No impact fee schedule for West Maui was found, so a rebuild budget there asks the county which fees it will pay.

Hotels in the resort region

DBEDT reports Lahaina, Kaanapali and Kapalua as one resort region. The region sold 63.2% of its rooms in 2025, at a $430.77 average rate and $272.24 of RevPAR, 0.9% above 2024. Room nights supplied totaled 2,592.3 thousand, 0.9% fewer than the year before, and the 1,638.3 thousand room nights sold were 3.9% more 7. May 2026 put occupancy at 64.3%, 9.1 points higher than a year before, with rooms at $412 and RevPAR at $265, up 18.8% 5. A West Maui lodging study reads the full 2025 year beside the May 2026 month rather than carrying the May rise forward on its own.

Maui County released draft design guidelines for Lahaina's Front Street commercial district on January 2, 2026, covering walkway widths, heights, workforce housing and sea-level rise 50. West Maui also carries Bill 9's earlier date, set out in the Maui section above.

Hawaii County

Hawaii Island: net arrivals, the Kona coast counts and two hospital projects.

On DBEDT's estimates Hawaii County grew to 210,043 residents by July 1, 2025, from 200,744 in July 2020, with an April 2020 base of 200,632; that is +0.9% a year on average, with net arrivals averaging +1,871 a year 4. Its housing stock grew 5.5% between April 2020 and July 2025 51, and 26.0% of its residents were 65 or older in mid-2025 52. Its permits rose to 1,230 units in 2025, from 982 units the year before 53. No county job count was retrieved; unemployment stood at 3.4% in August 2026, against 2.5% in August 2025 18.

Hotels

On Hawaii Island the 2025 hotel year closed at 69.5% occupancy, a $443.56 rate and $308.43 of RevPAR, 7.5% more than in 2024, and the Kohala Coast resort region's RevPAR was $420.29 7. In May 2026 occupancy was 67.2%, 2.7 points lower on the year, while the rate rose 11.4% to $427 and RevPAR 7.0% to $287 5.

Care and public projects

The Hilo Benioff Medical Center expansion, 66,000 square feet with a new 19-bed intensive care unit and 36 beds more, was reported on schedule in March 2025 for completion in winter 2026; its cost was not disclosed 55. A West Hawaii medical office of 50,000 square feet was proposed in December 2025 with $50M of state general obligation bonds in the fiscal 2027 supplemental budget, its enactment not verified 56. The health system's own East Hawaii page calls the Hilo hospital one of the largest employers in Hilo, without a headcount 57. A senior housing or care study on the island counts those beds in its supply and takes its demand from the county's own count of residents 65 and older.

Hawaii County levies its 3% transient accommodations tax under Ordinance 21-89, passed on December 8, 2021 58. USDA Rural Development's state office for Hawaii and the Western Pacific is in Hilo 59.

Kauai County

Kauai: hotel occupancy, Kuhio Highway counts, and the water and sewer notices.

Kauai's population barely moved over the five years: 73,400 in July 2025, 73,210 in July 2020 and 73,295 at the April 2020 base, an average change of +0.1% a year with net migration of -59 a year 4. DBEDT's housing estimates add 2.4% to the island's units between April 2020 and July 2025 51, and Kauai permitted housing for 130 units in 2025 and 141 units in 2024 60. Its unemployment rate was 2.5% in August 2026, up from 2.0%; DBEDT's county job counts were not part of the record 18.

Hotels

Kauai hotels were 75.4% occupied over 2025, with an average rate of $416.69 and RevPAR of $313.99, up 4.1% on 2024 7. May 2026 brought 77.2% occupancy, 2.8 points more than in May 2025, a $409.40 rate and $316.15 of RevPAR, which rose 9.2% 5.

Sewer and water

Lihue, the focus of the county's transit-oriented development planning, needs a capacity assessment of its wastewater system before it can take further transit-oriented and affordable housing connections; Public Works' fact sheet of September 19, 2025 set the work to begin in the second quarter of fiscal 2026, on a system that includes 20 miles of gravity sewer 13. On September 16, 2026 the Department of Water lifted its islandwide conservation notice for the Kalaheo-Koloa and Poipu and the Lihue-Kapaa and Puhi systems, and left it in force for Anahola, Anini, Haena-Wainiha and Kilauea 14. A project in Lihue, or on one of the four systems still under notice, confirms its sewer or water connection with the county before its schedule is set.

Kauai County's 3% transient accommodations tax dates from Bill 2829, signed on September 16, 2021, and the county opened a new payment portal on February 1, 2026 61.

Capital

One SBA district for Hawaii and the Pacific territories, and the state's own credit programs.

State credit and bonds

Treasury approved Hawaii's SSBCI 2.0 allocation for loan participation, collateral support and a venture fund of funds under the names HI-CAP Loans, HI-CAP Collateral and HI-CAP Invest 68. The Hawaii Technology Development Corporation and the Hawaii Green Infrastructure Authority, both attached to DBEDT, administer it 69, and the authority's lender guide of December 2023 requires that at least 90% of the funds support businesses headquartered in Hawaii 70. Outside SSBCI, no state guarantee, participation or capital access program turned up.

For a private project, tax-exempt debt means a special purpose revenue bond, issued by the Department of Budget and Finance once the Legislature authorizes it under HRS chapter 39A. Act 262 of 2023 placed a moratorium on new authorizations that need the state's private activity bond cap after June 30, 2023, and individual 2025 bills override it by name 71. HB341 of 2025, for example, authorizes up to $80,000,000 for the West Hawaii Community Health Center, lapsing on June 30, 2030 72.

SBA

Where SBA's study rule sits for a Hawaii loan.

SBA's rule on asking for a study is 13 CFR 120.160(b): SBA may require professional appraisals, a survey, or a feasibility study 73. In Hawaii such a request reaches a borrower through the one district office and the lenders above. The program tests that such a file then faces are covered under SBA 7(a) and 504 lending.

  • Assisted Living Feasibility Study, Hawaii: A HUD 232 Worked CaseA hypothetical new care community on Oahu, from its senior demand to its HUD loan coverage.

Hawaii has one worked case, and it is hypothetical: a new community that combines assisted living with memory care, built with a bank construction loan that HUD's Section 232 program is to take out as the permanent debt. Its current page places the site only in an Oahu submarket where supply is constrained. The case sizes demand from the older population of its market area, screened by age and by the income to pay for care privately, sets the subject against a thin field of competitors, and ends on whether the community's cash flow covers the HUD loan's payments. The site lists no other Hawaii case, published or promised.

Hawaii Questions

Questions about Hawaii feasibility studies, answered from the island records.

Does DBEDT publish hotel figures for resort regions smaller than an island?

Yes. Its 2025 table gives the Lahaina, Kaanapali and Kapalua region 63.2% occupancy, a $430.77 average rate and $272.24 of RevPAR; Wailea's RevPAR was $552.60, the Kohala Coast's $420.29 and Waikiki's $212.87; and Other Oahu, the region with the Ko Olina resorts, was 74.5% occupied at a $371.34 rate with $276.72 of RevPAR 7. A resort hotel is read on its region's line rather than on its island's.

How many Lahaina homes were rebuilt or under construction by August 2026?

Honolulu Civil Beat, citing the county's figures in August 2026, put the burn zone at nearly 600 homes rebuilt and 315 homes under construction, with about 1,000 more units holding permits or in the permit process 8.

Can hospital work in Hawaii get a building permit before its Certificate of Need?

No. Under HRS 323D-49 no state or county agency issues a building permit for hospital work until the project holds a current certificate or the planning agency has stated that none is needed 10. The certificate itself does not reach assisted living facilities or extended care adult residential care homes, which HRS 323D-54 exempts, nor bed changes within two years of no more than 10% or 10 beds, whichever is less 9. SB1423 SD1 of 2025 proposed adding psychiatric behavioral health services, dialysis centers and state health department facilities to the exemptions; whether it was enacted was not found 33.

Does Central Oahu have more empty industrial space than the island as a whole?

Slightly. Colliers put Central Oahu's 11,023,071 square feet at 1.49% vacant in the first quarter of 2026, against 1.40% across the 42,447,605 square feet it tracks islandwide, where the quarter's net absorption was -24,288 square feet and the asking base rent $1.60 a square foot each month, with operating expenses of $0.62 11. 6.01% of Oahu's retail space stood vacant that quarter, Colliers' highest reading in three quarters, which it attributes to closures of anchor tenants in urban Honolulu 37.

When do Maui's apartment-zoned vacation rentals have to stop under Bill 9?

In West Maui the A-1 and A-2 apartment districts lose transient vacation rental use on January 1, 2029; the rest of the county follows on January 1, 2031 44. Bill 9 cleared the council on December 15, 2025 by five votes to three 44, with the mayor's signature the same day 45. As of May 29, 2024 the county's Department of Finance count of apartment-zoned short-term rentals stood at 6,208 45. Resolution 25-230 would rezone 4,500 units into two new hotel districts, H-3 and H-4, so they can keep operating, a proposal rather than law 44. Litigation was expected, Civil Beat reported 46.

Which Kauai water systems were still under a conservation notice in September 2026?

Anahola, Anini, Haena-Wainiha and Kilauea. The Department of Water's release of September 16, 2026 lifted the islandwide notice for Kalaheo-Koloa, Poipu, Lihue-Kapaa and Puhi and kept it on those four 14. Sewer has its own gate in Lihue, where Public Works' fact sheet of September 19, 2025 requires a capacity assessment of the wastewater system, a network with 20 miles of gravity sewer, before more transit-oriented and affordable housing connections are taken, the work to start in the second quarter of fiscal 2026 13.
