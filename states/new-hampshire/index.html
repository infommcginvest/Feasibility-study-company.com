New Hampshire Feasibility Studies

HB 631, as HB 1010 amended it, has required New Hampshire towns to allow multifamily housing on land they zoned for commerce since July 1, 2026 12. The mains did not move with the zoning: the St. Anselm College zoning atlas update that NHPR reported on December 1, 2025 found either public service on 12% of the state's buildable land and both on less than 6% 3. The water and sewer department's dated document for the lot, or the well and septic design a lot without a main needs, is therefore the record a New Hampshire feasibility study cites before any rent; where a main does reach the lot, the rent record follows: the 2024 New Hampshire Housing survey put Hillsborough County's median two-bedroom gross rent at $1,989 4.

Zoning and Service

New Hampshire opened its commercial zones to housing; water and sewer decide which of those parcels can take it.

The 2025 and 2026 housing laws changed what a town must permit. They did not lay a main.

On July 15, 2025 Governor Ayotte signed a housing package that the Legislature had passed with support from both parties: HB 577, which lets an owner add an accessory dwelling unit by right, HB 631, which opens commercial zones to residential use, HB 296, HB 413 and HB 457, and SB 282, which allows a single stair in sprinklered buildings of up to four floors 28. A year later, on July 15, 2026, HB 1010 was signed as Chapter 319, amending HB 631 with the same July 1, 2026 effective date, so that from that day towns must allow multifamily housing on land they have zoned commercial 1. For a lender this turns a common New Hampshire site, a vacant pad behind a shopping plaza or a former office lot on a state road, into a parcel that may now carry apartments as a matter of zoning.

The law reached the zoning map and stopped there. Mapping for the St. Anselm College New Hampshire Zoning Atlas, as NHPR reported it on December 1, 2025, showed that just 12% of the state's buildable land had access to either public water or public sewer, and less than 6% to both 3. Before HB 631 passed, the New Hampshire Municipal Association told legislators on February 7, 2025 that the bill could push towns into building moratoriums 9; no sewer moratorium dated 2025 or 2026 has turned up in the sources this page draws on, which leaves the question parcel by parcel.

That sets the order of a New Hampshire study. On commercial land a dense residential plan stands or falls on whether a main reaches the lot line, and a lot without one needs a well and septic design sized for the units before any rent is modelled. The same test reaches hotels, restaurants, care homes and car washes. A study that relies on a town connection cites the water and sewer department's own document with its date, and a study on private systems cites the design and the state approval it needs.

The politics have not settled. By October 29, 2025, the New Hampshire Bulletin reported, more than a dozen bills had been filed for the 2026 session to roll the zoning changes back 10, and in June 2026 two further bills, on parking requirements and on limits for dead-end roads, went to the Governor; the reporting does not confirm that she signed them 11. A study written in late 2026 therefore reads the ordinance of the town as it stands on the study date, after July 1, 2026, and says which version of the parking and road rules it applied.

Rental Housing

A rental market with few vacancies, where the newest permits cluster in the south and on the coast.

Census measures vacancy for the state and New Hampshire Housing prices units by county; below the county a study counts its own.

The Census Bureau's Housing Vacancy Survey put New Hampshire's rental vacancy rate at 3.9% for 2025, after 4.0% in 2024 and 4.7% in 2023 12. New Hampshire Housing's 2024 Residential Rental Cost Survey prices two-bedroom units county by county; the survey reported no vacancy rate for 2024 because its sample was too small to support one 4. So a rental study in this state has a statewide vacancy series and county rents, and builds its own competitive set below the county.

Where new housing is being built is on record in words rather than counts. In NHPR's report of September 24, 2026, New Hampshire Housing described the permits issued from 2020 to 2025 as concentrated on the Seacoast and around Nashua and Manchester 13. The pipelines the market sections find are of three kinds: infill buildings on Elm Street in Manchester, Tuscan Village in Salem and employer-built staff housing in the Upper Valley.

The register's statewide reading of rent comes from the American Community Survey and covers rental units of every size, not only two-bedroom apartments, so it is a different measure from the survey's two-bedroom medians in the market sections and is no check on them.

Eight Markets

Eight New Hampshire markets on nine of its counties; Cheshire County sits in none of them.

Hillsborough, Rockingham, Strafford, Merrimack, Grafton, Sullivan, Belknap, Carroll and Coos: each market below is read on its own New Hampshire counties.

None of the eight has a page yet; they follow here in the order of their housing record: Manchester's downtown approvals first, then the border belt and the Seacoast, the Upper Valley's employer housing, Nashua's ring towns, the capital, the Lakes Region and the North Country. Three geography rules hold throughout. Manchester and Nashua share Hillsborough County, the whole Manchester-Nashua metropolitan area, so a county or metro figure is printed once, in the Manchester section. Salem and the Seacoast share Rockingham County the same way, and the Rockingham rows sit in the Seacoast section. The Seacoast's two counties and the Upper Valley's two belong to statistical areas that cross into Massachusetts and Vermont, and only the New Hampshire counties are read.

Manchester · Hillsborough County

Manchester: six-story buildings on Elm Street, each approved under a variance or the new ordinance.

The state's largest city, read with the county it shares with Nashua.

Manchester counted 116,818 residents in July 2025 and 115,660 in April 2020, a change of +1%, on the Vintage 2025 Census estimates that Business NH Magazine and Nashua Ink Link set out city by city on May 20, 2026 6. Hillsborough County, which is the whole Manchester-Nashua metropolitan area, held 433,415 residents in July 2025 against 422,944 in April 2020 6. Those two county counts serve the Nashua section as well and are not repeated there.

New Hampshire Employment Security's April 2026 release, published May 19, 2026, put Manchester-Nashua nonfarm employment at 201,900, not seasonally adjusted, against 205,300 in April 2025, a change of -1.66% 14. The Bureau of Labor Statistics gives a preliminary 203.3 thousand for July 2026 15. A study that sizes demand on jobs quotes the month and the release it uses, since the April and July counts come from different releases.

Downtown supply arrives one building at a time. On December 23, 2025 the Planning Board approved the former Elm Street diner site for a six-story building of 40 apartments and 49,765 square feet 16. At the former North End Superette site, also on Elm Street, the Zoning Board of Adjustment voted in May 2025 to allow 51 units where 15 were permitted 17, and on July 29, 2026 the Planning Board approved the site plan under the city's new zoning ordinance, under which, as under the old code, downtown buildings need no parking 18.

For an apartment lender the point is the approval route. A comparable building approved by a density variance in 2025 shows what the board would grant then; a building approved under the new ordinance in 2026 shows what the code now allows. A Manchester study names which route its own density rests on and which route each comparable took, because the two are not the same evidence.

For rent, the 2024 survey priced the median two-bedroom unit in Hillsborough County at $1,989 a month, affordable on a household income of $79,600 4. The city's lists of its largest employers carry no publication date, so their headcounts stay off this page; a study takes them from the employer or from a dated city profile.

Salem, New Hampshire · Rockingham County

Salem, New Hampshire, with Londonderry and Derry: Tuscan Village turns its master plan toward housing.

The border towns of Rockingham County, read as New Hampshire towns.

Salem, New Hampshire counted 31,869 residents in July 2025, +5.73% from April 2020, in the Vintage 2025 tabulation of May 20, 2026; Londonderry counted 27,844, +6.63%, and Derry 33,990, a change of -2.76% over the same period 6. The county these towns sit in is Rockingham, whose population, rent and payroll rows are printed in the Seacoast section; no Massachusetts figure enters either section.

Tuscan Village carries the pipeline on record for Salem, and its plan keeps changing shape. Its Building 1300, The Epicurean, was described in September 2025 with 299 units and more than 70,000 square feet of retail anchored by Whole Foods Market, and a target completion of May 2026; whether it was finished is not on record 19. On July 12, 2026 the Union Leader reported a revised West Village concept of 392 apartments, 72 workforce units and a 120-room hotel, on the Planning Board's agenda for July 28, 2026 and not approved 20. A master-plan condition of 2023 asks every later Tuscan residential proposal to include 10% workforce housing on site 21.

For a Salem apartment or hotel file the pipeline therefore has three stages to keep apart: units built and leasing, units approved, and units in a concept that has already been redrawn once. A lender reading the study wants each Tuscan building placed in one of those stages on the study date, with the document that places it, because the 2026 concept is itself a revision of an earlier plan 20.

Industry is arriving too. Onshore Technologies said on September 30, 2026 that its new Salem plant was operational, without disclosing its cost or headcount 22.

Seacoast · Rockingham and Strafford Counties

Portsmouth and the Seacoast: two New Hampshire counties, a court-backed limit on daily rentals and a shipyard in Maine.

Portsmouth, Dover and Rochester, read on Rockingham and Strafford counties only.

The Seacoast section reads two counties. Rockingham County held 324,077 residents in July 2025, +3.10% from April 2020, and Strafford County 135,043, +3.12% 6. Inside them, Portsmouth counted 23,028 residents, +4.9%; Dover 34,623, +5.7%; and Rochester, in Strafford County, 33,117, +3.8% 6. In Employment Security's tables the pair is the Rockingham County-Strafford County Metropolitan Division, the New Hampshire part of a Boston metropolitan area that also takes in Massachusetts counties; this page prints nothing for the Massachusetts side, and a reader who needs it turns to the Massachusetts page. The division held 207,400 nonfarm jobs in April 2025 and 206,900 in April 2026, not seasonally adjusted, a change of -0.24% 14.

New Hampshire Housing's 2024 rent survey set Rockingham County's median two-bedroom rent at $2,069, which takes a household income of $82,800 4, and the Insurance Department's data call gave the county an average homeowners premium of $1,551 over its 2018 to 2022 data 5. These are Salem's county figures as well as Portsmouth's.

Portsmouth's own rules set the lodging test. The New Hampshire Supreme Court upheld the city's reading that renting a single-family home by the day is a transient occupancy, outside the zoning code's meaning of a dwelling unit and not a permitted use, in the Working Stiff Partners case 23. In June 2026 the city's Housing Committee was drafting an ordinance that would apply RSA 79-E tax relief to a citywide housing opportunity overlay, and named office-zone strip-mall sites, the McKinnon's and Cinemagic sites among them, as candidates for conversion to housing 24. That overlay is a city tool, not a federal Opportunity Zone. A short-term rental study in Portsmouth starts from the zoning use, not from booking data; an apartment study on a strip-mall site cites the overlay's status on the study date.

Portsmouth Naval Shipyard is in Kittery, Maine, not in New Hampshire, and the Navy awarded its multi-mission dry dock to 381 Constructors on August 13, 2021 for $1,731,477,644 25. Lodging or rental demand drawn from the yard is demand from across the state line, and any study that uses a shipyard headcount has to bring its own dated source for it.

Upper Valley · Grafton and Sullivan Counties

Lebanon, Hanover and the Upper Valley: a medical center and a college that build and finance housing themselves.

Grafton and Sullivan counties only; the Vermont side of the Lebanon area is outside this page.

The Lebanon-Claremont micropolitan area also takes in Windsor and Orange counties in Vermont. This section reads only its two New Hampshire counties and prints no two-state total. Grafton County counted 93,225 residents in July 2025, +2.28% from April 2020, and Sullivan County 43,961, +2.06% 6. Grafton's median two-bedroom unit rented for $2,027 a month in the same 2024 survey 4.

Demand here is anchored by two institutions, and both have moved into housing. Dartmouth Hitchcock Medical Center, with 460 inpatient beds, began an expansion in April 2025 that adds 64 beds and expects 4,500 more inpatients a year by 2028, the Valley News reported on August 28, 2025 26. Dartmouth held the ribbon-cutting for Sugarwood Circle, a staff neighborhood of 21 units in West Lebanon that cost $15.2 million, on February 17, 2026 27, and in February 2025 it doubled its commitment to the Upper Valley Loan Fund to $3 million, a fund that had financed 67 units the year before 28.

The need those projects answer is on record as a projection. The Valley News report of February 17, 2026 cited the regional planning commission's 2023 study for more than 4,000 housing units needed on the New Hampshire side of the Upper Valley by 2030, 850 of them in Lebanon and 530 in Hanover 27. A projection is not absorption, and the Lebanon study that cites it says so.

The lender's question in Lebanon and Hanover is which units compete. Staff housing that an employer builds or finances and leases to its own people is not a comparable for a building that must lease to the public; a rental study in the Upper Valley lists the employer-built and employer-financed units separately from the open-market set it rents against, and says how many of the hospital's added beds its staffing demand assumes.

Taxes

No general sales tax, no personal income tax since 2025, and a property tax that carries local government.

The cost side of a New Hampshire budget turns on one line, set by the town.

New Hampshire levies no general sales tax, so the materials and equipment in a construction budget carry none, and the repeal of the Interest and Dividends Tax, effective January 1, 2025, left the state with no personal income tax 29. Corporate income is taxed at a flat 7.5% under the same 2026 tables 29. The weight falls on property instead: property taxes supplied 59.5% of state and local tax revenue in FY2023 29.

For the owner-occupied home the Tax Foundation's 2026 county tables give an effective property tax rate of 1.62% for 2023 30. Their 2024 reading is not settled: one table on the page gives 1.50% and a second gives 1.51%, with different national ranks, and the two have not been reconciled 30. A study that quotes a statewide 2024 rate names which table it took. Neither describes an income property. The Lincoln Institute's 50-State Property Tax Comparison Study for taxes paid in 2025 has a commercial table 31, but no commercial effective rate was extracted from it for this page, so an apartment building, hotel or warehouse in New Hampshire is taxed in the pro forma at its own town's current rate on an assessment the study supports with the assessor's values for comparable parcels.

Insurance

An insurance market without an insolvency in eleven years, and homeowners premiums measured by county.

The Insurance Department's own reports, dated, are the record here.

The Insurance Department's 174th annual report, for FY2025, counted 1,250 active licensed insurers and 198 eligible surplus lines insurers at June 30, 2025; it recorded no insurer insolvency for the eleventh year in a row and one property and casualty company withdrawn through a merger 32. Workers' compensation rates fell for the fourteenth consecutive year, a cumulative reduction of 66% since 2011 on the Department's account 32.

For homes, the Department's Homeowners Market Data Call of July 11, 2025 measured $699 of coverage for each $1 of premium in 2022, with written premium up 26.2% from 2018 to 2022 5, and its press release of July 17, 2025 found at least 60 companies writing homeowners insurance in each area of the state 33. The data call's county averages for Rockingham and Coos counties are printed in the Seacoast and North Country sections. Those are household lines; for an apartment building or a hotel the property cover in the budget comes from the carrier's quote for that building, because no commercial insurer of last resort and no approved 2025 or 2026 commercial rate change for New Hampshire turned up on the record.

An InsuranceNewsNet account of a 2024 Union Leader report, citing First Street, identified precipitation as New Hampshire's main climate exposure for insurance cost 34. The flood section below gives the declarations on record.

People and Payrolls

A population that grows by migration, payrolls that barely moved, and construction employment that fell further than in any other state.

Statewide counts from the Census Bureau, BLS and Employment Security, each for its own month.

New Hampshire held 1,415,342 people on July 1, 2025 in the Census Bureau's Vintage 2025 QuickFacts, 2.7% above the 1,377,573 of the April 1, 2020 estimates base 35. The New Hampshire Fiscal Policy Institute's reading of those estimates, published in February 2026, is in its title: the state continued to rely on migration from Massachusetts for its population growth in 2025 36. For a project whose demand rests on new households, the question is whether that inflow is still running, and the study cites the year of the estimate it uses.

Payrolls were close to level. BLS counted 703,300 nonfarm jobs in New Hampshire in August 2026, seasonally adjusted and preliminary, 1,300 more than a year earlier, or +0.2% 37. Employment Security put private nonfarm employment at 615,800 that month, up 1,800 over the year, and the seasonally adjusted unemployment rate at 2.8% 38. The two southern metros, read in the Manchester and Seacoast sections, show twelve-month declines in their April counts.

One sector stands out. NAHB's analysis of the August 2026 state data put New Hampshire's construction employment change at -3.7% over the year, the largest percentage decline of any state 39. A builder's schedule or a general contractor's price that assumes crews will be found locally is a schedule to test, and the study rests its construction timeline on the contractor's dated bid and staffing, not on the statewide payroll.

No primary list ranking New Hampshire's biggest private employers by headcount was found. The largest project announcement on record spans two states: BAE Systems' May 2026 program, read in the Nashua section.

Nashua · Hillsborough County

Nashua and the towns around it, with a defense campus renovating in Hudson.

The city and its ring towns, on the Hillsborough County figures printed in the Manchester section.

Nashua counted 92,435 residents in July 2025 and 91,335 in April 2020, +1.2%, on the same Vintage 2025 tabulation, while the town of Merrimack, a different place from Merrimack County, reached 29,699, +10.88%, and Hudson 26,001, +2.40% 6. Nashua has no payroll series of its own on record; its jobs sit inside the Manchester-Nashua metro count printed in the Manchester section, and a study does not present that count as the city's.

The industrial anchor that is on record is in Hudson. On May 20, 2026 BAE Systems announced a $135 million program across Austin, Texas and its Hudson campus, of which about $85 million goes to Hudson, with a 65,000 square foot renovation, as IndustrySelect relayed the company's release 40. The release states capital, not hiring. A housing or service project that leans on the Hudson work cites the investment for what it is and takes any job figure from the employer.

Flex space is coming back on the market. Colliers' Q2 2026 New Hampshire industrial report recorded sizable flex move-outs in Nashua, Manchester and Salem, among them a block of 170,000 square feet in a town the report does not name 7. A Nashua industrial or flex study counts that space as competing supply until it is relet.

Industrial Space

Warehouse and flex space: a statewide asking rent, a southern regional vacancy and the closures of 2025.

Two brokers, two geographies, and no submarket figure in the public record.

Colliers' New Hampshire industrial report for Q2 2026 put statewide vacancy at 6.1% and the asking rent at $11.92 a square foot, triple net 7. Cushman & Wakefield's Southern New Hampshire MarketBeat for the same quarter, published July 24, 2026, put vacancy at 6.7% 41. The two readings cover different areas, and a study names which one it cites and why its site belongs in that area.

2025 brought closures as well as tightening. Colliers' review of 2025 counted 635,000 square feet of negative industrial absorption from closures, Anheuser-Busch's Portsmouth operation among them, without stating the geography of the total, and named Salem and Manchester as the leaders of 2025's warehouse tightening 42. A warehouse study in the southern tier therefore has both a tightening record and a closure record behind it, and its competitive set lists the vacated buildings near the site by name.

No submarket vacancy for any of the eight markets was found in public sources; a lender who needs one for a specific corridor asks the study to buy it from a broker's subscription data and to date it.

Concord · Merrimack County

Concord and Merrimack County: the capital's market, read at the county line.

The Concord micropolitan area is one county, and its figures here are county figures.

Merrimack County, which is the whole Concord micropolitan area, counted 158,078 residents in July 2025, +2.74% from April 2020 6. Every Concord figure on this page is a county figure. No 2025 or 2026 employment change, project pipeline or local rule change for this market turned up in public sources, so a Concord study starts from the city's own planning records and from Employment Security's payroll series for the Concord micropolitan area.

What the capital does hold is the offices a lender deals with. SBA's New Hampshire District Office, the one district office for the whole state, is in Concord 43. For industrial and flex space the nearest published reading is Cushman & Wakefield's Southern New Hampshire vacancy of 6.7% for Q2 2026 41. A Concord warehouse study treats it as a regional backdrop and builds the county's own set of buildings.

Health Care and Senior Housing

No Certificate of Need since 2016, a bed moratorium that remains, and Medicare Advantage carriers pulling back.

A health care project in New Hampshire is licensed, not rationed by need, with two exceptions on record.

New Hampshire's Certificate of Need program ended with the disbanding of the Health Services Planning and Review Board under RSA 151-C on June 30, 2016. What survived is narrower: an enhanced licensing review by the state health department for specified services, such as open-heart surgery, cardiac catheterization and radiation therapy with megavoltage equipment, and a provision for a moratorium on beds 45. No move to reinstate review was found for 2025 or 2026. A clinic or surgery center whose services are not on the specified list goes to licensing without a need test; a nursing facility project checks the moratorium provision before anything else.

The repeal left a gap that matters to a market study. The Granite State News Collaborative reported on July 28, 2025, amid what it called a proliferation in services, that the state has had no thorough way to track the health care industry's growth since the review ended 46. With no state inventory of new providers, a health care study in New Hampshire builds its own competitor list from licenses, websites and site visits, and dates it.

Payer risk is on record for senior care. On August 18, 2025 the Insurance Department reported that six Medicare Advantage carriers were active in the state, that it expected further withdrawals, and that about 44,000 enrollees had been affected by withdrawals the year before 47. A home health, clinic or skilled care operator paid through Medicare Advantage plans shows which carriers its revenue depends on.

The Dartmouth Hitchcock expansion in Lebanon is set out in the Upper Valley section. For assisted living, memory care and child care the licensing classes are not on this page; the project's own licensing file supplies them.

Lakes Region and White Mountains · Belknap and Carroll Counties

Belknap and Carroll counties: the Lakes Region, the Mount Washington Valley and a worked campground case in Ossipee.

Two counties whose visitor economy is documented far less than their growth.

Carroll County, the county of the Mount Washington Valley, counted 52,784 residents in July 2025, +5.21% from April 2020, the highest county growth in the state on Business NH Magazine's tabulation, and Belknap County, which is the Laconia micropolitan area, counted 65,147, +2.22% 6. That growth is the market's best-documented fact. Hotel occupancy, campground counts and short-term rental activity, the series a lodging lender here would want first, were not found in public sources, so a hospitality study for these counties assembles its own competitive set and buys whatever booking data it relies on.

The banking record is local. SBA named Meredith Village Savings Bank, a Lakes Region bank, its New Hampshire Small Business Lender of the Year for FY2024, for the number and dollar amount of its 7(a) loans, at a ceremony in Laconia on December 3, 2024; the bank's own announcement does not give the counts 48.

The site's campground case is set here. The Ossipee case works through the expansion of a seasonal campground in Carroll County on a USDA Business and Industry guaranteed loan; this section prints no Ossipee figure of its own, only the county's. Carroll County is also the one county named in the October 2026 flood declaration set out in the next section.

Hazards

Flood declarations: Carroll County in October 2026, and the July 2024 storms in Coos and Grafton counties.

On October 7, 2026 FEMA declared DR-4947-NH, a major disaster for the severe storm and flooding of June 10 and 11, 2026, with Public Assistance for Carroll County 4950.

Two 2024 declarations are on record by number: DR-4761 was declared on February 27, 2024 and DR-4771 on April 19, 2024, both for storm and flood damage 5152. North Country · Coos County

Coos County and Berlin: a federal distress designation, and a grant program proposed for elimination.

The state's northernmost county, where a study plans for flat demand.

Coos County counted 31,343 residents in July 2025, a change of +0.236% from April 2020 6. In July 2025 it was designated a distressed place-based economy, a status meant to improve its standing for federal funds; Business NH Magazine reported on July 31, 2025 that the designation came as the Northern Border Regional Commission, a principal source of grants for the region, was proposed for elimination in the President's FY2026 budget, a proposal and not an enacted cut 54.

The household insurance line is lower in the north on the Insurance Department's data call: Coos County's average homeowners premium was $1,058 over the 2018 to 2022 data, against Rockingham County's $1,551 in the same report 5. Coos and Grafton were also the primary disaster counties for the July 2024 storms set out in the flood section.

For a lender the North Country file is about whether demand holds rather than whether it grows. A project in Berlin or elsewhere in the county is underwritten on a population that stood almost still from 2020 to 2025, and a project that relies on a federal or regional grant shows the award letter, not the program, because the program's own funding was in question in the FY2026 budget proposal. Berlin's labor force and payroll counts come from a community profile listed in September 2026 with no publication date, and they are left out.

Rules by Use

One statewide rooms rate, short-term rentals taxed like hotels, and a fuel tank fund whose funding changed in July 2025.

Hotels, rentals and fuel sites meet state rules that apply the same way in every town.

Lodging pays the Meals and Rooms (Rentals) Tax at 8.5% for periods beginning October 1, 2021, down from 9% 65, and there is no local-option rooms tax on top of it; local option bills have been debated without being enacted 66. Short-term rentals sit inside the same tax. The Department of Revenue Administration's 2026 booklet requires a short-term rental to hold a Meals and Rentals license and collect the tax even for a one-day rental, and treats room facilitators as operators 67. No state law licensing short-term rentals or preempting town rules was identified, so a town's zoning, as in Portsmouth's case in the Seacoast section, decides whether a daily rental may operate.

For a hotel or rental pro forma this makes the tax line simple and the use question local: the rate is the same in Conway as in Portsmouth, and whether a daily rental is allowed at all is a town zoning matter.

Fuel sites carry a state cleanup backstop with conditions. Underground storage tanks are governed by RSA 146-C; a tank out of compliance can be red-tagged, with fuel deliveries prohibited, and non-compliance can void reimbursement from the state fund 68. That fund, the Oil Discharge and Disposal Cleanup Fund under RSA 146-D, acts as excess insurance above any other collectible insurance for cleanup and third-party damage, and corrective action needs the Department of Environmental Services' approval first 69. Its funding subparts under RSA 146-A:11-b were set to sunset on July 1, 2025 70. HB 658 of 2025 would raise the caps on on-premise use and reauthorize the oil import fees, which bring in about $17,000,000 a year for the two petroleum funds, but its final enactment is not confirmed in the sources 7071. A gas station or truck stop study therefore records the tank's compliance status and the fund's coverage as they stand at closing, with the department's letter.

New Hampshire Questions

Seven New Hampshire questions, from the lot's water line to the USDA office shared with Vermont.

Is every commercial lot in New Hampshire now an apartment site?

In zoning, close to it. The housing package signed on July 15, 2025 carried HB 631, opening commercial zones to residential use 28, and HB 1010, signed as Chapter 319 on July 15, 2026, amended it so that towns must permit multifamily housing on commercial land 1. Service is another matter: in the zoning atlas update NHPR reported on December 1, 2025, public water or public sewer reached 12% of buildable land statewide and both reached less than 6% 3. A pad behind a plaza that no main reaches is an apartment site only with a well and septic design sized for its units, and by October 29, 2025 bills to roll the zoning changes back had been filed for the 2026 session 10.

Has Tuscan Village's West Village in Salem, New Hampshire been approved?

Not on this page's record. The Union Leader reported the revised West Village concept on July 12, 2026, with 392 apartments, 72 workforce units and a 120-room hotel, for the Planning Board's July 28, 2026 agenda; the concept is itself a redrawing of an earlier plan 20. The Epicurean, Building 1300, carried 299 units and a May 2026 completion target when it was described in September 2025, and whether it opened is not on record 19. Since 2023 a master-plan condition has called for 10% workforce housing on site in every later Tuscan residential proposal 21. A Salem apartment or hotel study places each building in one of three stages, leasing, approved or concept, with the document that puts it there.

What does a New Hampshire project pay in state and local tax with no sales tax and no income tax?

Property tax, above all. No general sales tax touches building materials or equipment, and the Interest and Dividends Tax repeal of January 1, 2025 left New Hampshire without a personal income tax; corporate income pays a flat 7.5% on the Tax Foundation's 2026 tables, and 59.5% of all state and local tax revenue in FY2023 came from property taxes 29. The publisher's county tables give an owner-occupied effective rate of 1.62% for 2023 and two unreconciled 2024 readings, 1.50% and 1.51% 30. The Lincoln Institute's 2025 comparison study tabulates commercial rates, but none was taken from it for this page 31, so the pro forma for an apartment building, hotel or warehouse uses the town's own rate and an assessment backed by the assessor's values for comparable parcels.

How does a New Hampshire clinic or surgery center study build its competitor list without a state inventory?

From licenses, websites and site visits. Certificate of Need ended when the Health Services Planning and Review Board was disbanded under RSA 151-C on June 30, 2016; what remains is an enhanced licensing review for specified services, open-heart surgery, cardiac catheterization and megavoltage radiation therapy among them, and a bed moratorium provision 45. On July 28, 2025 the Granite State News Collaborative reported that no thorough state tracking of health care growth has existed since the review ended 46. A clinic outside the specified services is licensed with no need test, a nursing facility reads the moratorium provision first, and an operator paid through Medicare Advantage names its carriers: the Insurance Department counted six active in August 2025 and expected more withdrawals 47.

Does USDA Rural Development publish a total for New Hampshire alone?

Not in the sources this page uses. One state office serves Vermont and New Hampshire together, with its business programs run from Montpelier 44, and its March 2026 releases on maple syrup funding give one figure for the two states 63. No figure for New Hampshire alone was found for Business and Industry guarantees, the Community Facilities program or REAP. The office's FY2025 notice for Rural Business Development Grants invited applications even as it said program funds were not then available 64; REAP stood as an active announcement on the office page 44. A borrower in Coos or Carroll County relies on the rule for its program and on its own application, not on a two-state total; the USDA practice page explains the programs.

Is the Portsmouth Naval Shipyard in New Hampshire?

No. The yard is in Kittery, Maine, and 381 Constructors won the Navy's multi-mission dry dock contract on August 13, 2021, at $1,731,477,644 25. Demand it sends to Seacoast lodging or rentals comes from across the state line, and a study that leans on the yard brings its own dated headcount, which this page lacks. On the New Hampshire side, Rockingham County held 324,077 residents in July 2025, +3.10% since April 2020, and Strafford County 135,043, +3.12% 6. Portsmouth's own record is its 23,028 residents in July 2025, +4.9% 6, and the Supreme Court's upholding of the city's reading, in the Working Stiff Partners case, that a single-family home let by the day is a transient occupancy and not a permitted use 23.

Does Nashua have a payroll count of its own?

No. Nashua's jobs sit inside the Manchester-Nashua metropolitan area, which is Hillsborough County entire, and Employment Security's release of May 19, 2026 put that area at 201,900 nonfarm jobs in April 2026, not seasonally adjusted, 1.66% below the 205,300 of April 2025 14; the Bureau of Labor Statistics' preliminary July 2026 reading is 203.3 thousand 15. The city does have its own population: 92,435 residents in July 2025 against 91,335 in April 2020, +1.2%, while the town of Merrimack reached 29,699, +10.88%, and Hudson 26,001 6. A Nashua study cites the payroll as the metro's and the population as the city's, and takes any Hudson headcount from BAE Systems itself, since the company's May 2026 release states capital, not hiring 40.
