Pennsylvania sits inside PJM's capacity market, so these results reach Pennsylvania projects, but each one is a regional number. Two readings follow for a Pennsylvania file. The years from June 2026 to May 2029 are already priced by auction, so a project opening in that window does not forecast its capacity price; it names the delivery year of each operating year and takes the charge its utility passes through. The years after May 2029 are not priced by any auction on this page, so a pro forma that runs past them states the capacity price it assumes and labels it an assumption.

The smaller auctions matter less to a project and more to a careful reader. PJM's incremental auction for 2026/27, with results on March 9, 2026, cleared at $164.70 per megawatt-day, where the base auction for the same year had cleared at $329.17 111. The base auction's price is the one a budget cites for the year; the incremental result belongs in the file only as the later auction it was, under its own date. The backstop procurement proposed in July 2026 had no result when reported, so a study written now gives it no price at all.

Licenses and Permits

Pennsylvania licenses most uses on entry, and sells expired restaurant liquor licenses at auction.

Restaurant liquor licenses

The Liquor Code limits the R, E, C and CC licenses by county population, a limit the City of Philadelphia's fact sheet sets out 12, so in a county at its limit a new restaurant or hotel bar acquires a license that already exists. Licenses that lapse return to the Liquor Control Board, which sells them by sealed bid. Its 14th auction of in-county restaurant licenses closed on October 9, 2025 with an average top bid of $129,904; top bids ran from $32,501 for a license in Ohio Township, Allegheny County, to $302,501 for one in Easton, Northampton County 6. Each is the price of one license in one county, so a study quotes the bid or transfer price for its own county rather than the statewide average.

A license bought from a current holder moves by transfer, from one person to another, from one place to another, or both, and each kind carries its own fee, which the city's fact sheet lists 12. For a lender the timing is the risk: a restaurant whose opening date assumes the transfer has cleared needs the Board's approval in the file, or a schedule that holds the opening until it arrives.

The 16th regular auction offered 20 licenses in 20 counties, with sealed bids due on October 5, 2026, a minimum bid of $25,000 and a surety of $5,000 or 5% 7. Between the two the Board also held its first auction of excess licenses offered across county lines 13; that release carries no date in the copy cited here, so its bids are not printed on this page. A restaurant or hotel pro forma carries the license as a purchase line in the uses of funds, and the lender reads whether it is already bought or still to be won.

Health facilities and the lapsed Certificate of Need

Certificate of Need review ended in Pennsylvania when the statute lapsed on December 18, 1996, and the services it once covered are presumed to fall under licensure through the Health Care Facilities Act 1415. The lapse left one tail in Medicaid: only facilities certified before that date may contract for skilled nursing, ICF/MR care, or inpatient psychiatric and rehabilitation beds 14. A new surgery center therefore answers to licensure, while a skilled nursing project that counts on Medicaid residents has to show it holds one of the older certificates.

Personal care homes and assisted living

The Department of Human Services licenses personal care homes and assisted living residences as two classes 16. A home for 4 or more adults who are not the operator's relatives needs a license, and the Department grants none before a Certificate of Occupancy is in hand 17. An assisted living residence must also provide supplemental health care 16, and its physical plant asks for larger construction, air conditioning and private bathrooms, with a licensed nurse and a dietitian among the staff 17. Memory care sits inside assisted living licensing as a special care designation, and a provider may hold both licenses 18. For an assisted living study, the license class shapes the construction budget.

Child care centers

Chapter 3270 holds a center's certificate of compliance until the operator produces a Certificate of Occupancy from Labor & Industry, and it requires training in infectious disease and medication administration before the first certificate issues 19. A child care schedule that opens enrollment before the occupancy permit is dated has no license to open under.

Campgrounds and RV parks

A campground needs an annual permit from the Department of Health or the local health department; its plans are approved before building starts, and the water supply and the sewage system need approvals of their own 20. For an RV park the health permit, the water source and the sewage approval are three separate dates in the construction schedule.

Who licenses what in Pennsylvania

  • R, E, C and CC liquor licenses Liquor Control Board, by county quota
  • Personal care homes and assisted living Department of Human Services
  • Child care centers Department of Human Services, after the Labor & Industry occupancy certificate
  • Campgrounds and RV parks Department of Health or the local health department
  • Hospitals and other health facilities Licensure under the Health Care Facilities Act
  • Underground storage tanks USTIF, administered with the Insurance Department
Costs and Insurance

Refused premium increases, a wage floor with no change scheduled, room taxes by place, and a tank fund that pays.

Four cost lines where Pennsylvania has its own dated record.

Insurance

The Insurance Department's January 2026 count of what it turned down in 2025 comes to $227.9 million of requested rate increases across property and casualty lines, with title cover at $103.6 million 5. That is a record of increases refused, and the release gives no figure for commercial property rates approved, so the premium in a commercial budget is the carrier's own offer for the building at the study date. Pennsylvania's FAIR Plan, the insurer of last resort, was created by the FAIR Plan Act of 1968 and runs through the Insurance Placement Facilities of Delaware and Pennsylvania, which writes basic property insurance for residential and commercial properties 21.

Wages

The state minimum wage stood at $7.25 an hour in 2026, with a tipped cash wage of $2.83 and no change scheduled, as the Governor's office reported on the House vote of March 24, 2026 22. That bill, HB 2189, would raise the floor in steps to $15 by 2029; it passed the House, a discharge resolution followed in the Senate on June 10, 2026, and it was not law when reported 23. A restaurant, hotel or care home budget carries the current floor, with the bill's schedule as a stated sensitivity rather than a base case.

Room taxes

The Department of Revenue sets the state hotel occupancy tax at 6% of rent and collects an additional 1% local tax in Philadelphia and Allegheny County, in its answer updated March 23, 2026 24. No typical combined local rate for the state was found, so a lodging study takes the county's own rate. Philadelphia levies a city hotel tax of its own 25, which is why a Philadelphia room-night budget takes each layer from the body that collects it. A 2025 guide found no statewide licensing or preemption law for short-term rentals, which leaves them to local zoning and permits, while the state hotel tax still reaches short stays 26.

The tank fund

For a fuel site the Underground Storage Tank Indemnification Fund is the cost and the backstop at once. Its board kept coverage at $1.5 million per tank per occurrence and the deductible at $5,000 per tank per occurrence in its vote of December 11, 2025 27. Remediation payments from the fund topped $28.7 million in 2025, and more than 98% of facilities had paid their capacity fees in full by December 4, 2025 28. Its 2025 allocation report sets the Environmental Cleanup Program at up to $5.5 million a year and the Pollution Prevention Program at up to $2,500 per tank 29. Neither document states the fund's balance or actuarial position, which a gas station lender relying on the coverage may ask the fund for directly.

Data Centers

Data center money pledged in 2025, and the state's 2026 turn on permits, standards and reporting.

On June 9, 2025 the Department of Community and Economic Development announced Amazon's plan to invest at least $20 billion in two data center campuses, one at Salem Township in Luzerne County and one at Falls Township in Bucks County, and called it the largest capital investment in the commonwealth's history 32. At the energy and AI summit of July 15, 2025, DatacenterDynamics recorded further pledges: Blackstone at $25 billion for data centers and energy in northeastern Pennsylvania, with no county named; a PA Data Center Partners and PowerHouse venture at $15 billion for three hyperscale campuses in central Pennsylvania, again with no county; CoreWeave at $6 billion in Lancaster; Capital Power at $3 billion over 10 years for a gas plant upgrade at Shamokin Dam, Snyder County; and Enbridge at $1 billion for pipeline work still to be announced 33. Spotlight PA, writing in July 2026, put the summit's total at $90 billion across data centers, energy and workforce, and called the sums pledges rather than commitments 34.

The state's stance moved in 2026. The Governor's Responsible Infrastructure Standards of February 2026 tie a data center's tax benefits to transparency and environmental standards 35. Spotlight PA reported in March 2026 a projected cost to the state of $2 billion by 2031 for exempting data center equipment from sales tax 36. Under the fiscal 2026-27 budget of July 2026, data centers above 10 MW must report their energy and water use every year 37. In August 2026 Executive Order 2026-05 took data centers off the PA Permit Fast Track, so later proposals no longer qualify for its expedited review 38. On October 6, 2026 a Capitol rally pressed for Senator Katie Muth's bill for a three-year data center moratorium, which had not been enacted; the Governor's office put the data center projects proposed in the state at no fewer than 100, and the session had seen at least 80 data center bills 37.

For a lender, the line between those two years is the line between a number announced and a project under way. A hotel, an apartment block or a supplier that sizes its demand on a campus states which campus, which milestone it has reached and on what date, and treats a pledge without a county as no evidence for any particular market.

Hazards

Declarations that reached Pennsylvania counties in 2026 came from SBA and USDA.

Tropical Storm Debby's 2024 FEMA declaration was still being amended in 2025; the 2026 actions are an SBA disaster declaration and a USDA freeze designation.

FEMA declared DR-4815-PA for Tropical Storm Debby on September 11, 2024, and the Federal Register carried its fourth amendment on September 8, 2025 44. In 2026 the federal actions on the record came from other agencies. SBA's administrative declaration PA-20044, issued September 22, 2026, covers the storms and flash floods of September 2 and 3; it names Clinton and Erie counties as primary and lists Crawford, Warren, Cameron and Potter, then Clearfield, Centre, Lycoming and Union, as the contiguous ones. It set the business physical loan rate at 8.000% for applicants with credit elsewhere and 4.000% for those without 42. On May 26, 2026 USDA's Secretary signed a disaster designation for 17 counties after the freeze of April 19 to 21, 2026 45. Earlier in the year the Governor issued emergency proclamations for a winter storm on January 23, 2026 and for another in February 2026, both on PEMA's list of proclamations 46.

An SBA administrative declaration offers SBA disaster loans; it is not a FEMA major declaration, and a governor's proclamation is a state emergency order rather than a federal declaration of either kind. A hotel or campground study in Erie or Clinton County dates its revenue history around the September 2026 storms, and an orchard, winery or farm market study in a designated county reads its 2026 crop and sales against the April freeze. A hotel or restaurant whose trailing twelve months include January and February 2026 says whether the winter storms closed it, so a lender does not read a storm month as a weak market.

Pennsylvania Questions

What lenders ask about Pennsylvania projects.

Are PJM's capacity prices Pennsylvania prices?

No. PJM reports one set of results for the market it runs, and Pennsylvania is one part of that market. The 2026/27 base auction procured 134,311 MW at an 18.9% reserve margin, figures Utility Dive reported for PJM as a whole 1, and no Pennsylvania line sits inside them. A Pennsylvania budget therefore asks the serving utility what it will charge in each delivery year, cites that answer, and sets the PJM-wide result beside it under the auction's own date. For an operating year after May 2029, no auction on this page gives a price, and whatever the pro forma carries for it is labelled an assumption.

How does a new Pennsylvania restaurant get a liquor license?

Restaurant licenses are limited by county under the Liquor Code, as the City of Philadelphia's fact sheet explains, so a new operator in a county at its limit acquires an existing one: from a holder, or from the PLCB when expired licenses go to auction 12. The PLCB's 16th regular auction offered 20 licenses across 20 counties, with bids due on October 5, 2026 and a minimum bid of $25,000 7. The license cost belongs in the project budget as a purchase, with the county named.

Which August 2025 payroll and jobless figures belong in a Pennsylvania study written in late 2026?

The ones in Labor & Industry's September 18, 2026 release: 6,172,100 nonfarm jobs for August 2025 after the benchmark revision, and a 4.4% unemployment rate for that month 8. The September 2025 release's first count of the same month is superseded and is not quoted beside it. The same release carries the preliminary August 2026 count that the year's change is measured to, so the base and the endpoint of any trend come from one document and one vintage 8. A study names the release date it used, so a reader can tell a revised base from a first print.

Does a Pennsylvania nursing home, surgery center or assisted living residence need a Certificate of Need?

Not for a new facility: the Certificate of Need statute expired on December 18, 1996 14. What survives is a Medicaid condition: contracts for nursing home beds, inpatient psychiatric and rehab units and ICF/MR care go only to facilities that were certified before the lapse 1415. An assisted living residence is licensed instead by the Department of Human Services, which requires a Certificate of Occupancy before it licenses 17.

What did the Pennsylvania Insurance Department turn down in 2025?

Insurers' requests worth $227.9 million in property and casualty premiums; title insurance accounted for $103.6 million of that and personal auto for $91 million, by the Department's own January 2026 count 5. The count covers requests the Department refused; it is not a schedule of rates it approved for commercial buildings, so a commercial budget takes its premium from the carrier's own quote for the building, dated. Where no carrier offers one, the FAIR Plan, Pennsylvania's insurer of last resort under the FAIR Plan Act of 1968, writes basic property insurance for commercial as well as residential property 21.

Where did USDA Rural Development's Pennsylvania money go in fiscal 2025?

USDA's January 2026 release puts housing at $279 million, community investment at $122 million and economic development and agribusiness at $79 million, inside a state total of $480 million spread across about 2,000 investments 10. Business and Industry guarantees are not totalled on their own in that release, and neither is Community Facilities, so a B&I borrower's file cites the program terms rather than a Pennsylvania volume. The release names example loans instead of program totals, which is why the volume question goes to Rural Development's state office in Harrisburg 10.

Does USTIF still pay for a tank release at a Pennsylvania gas station in 2026?

Yes, on terms its board confirmed by vote on December 11, 2025: coverage of $1.5 million per tank per occurrence, against a deductible of $5,000 per tank per occurrence 27. The fund's 2025 annual report shows it paying out, with remediation payments above $28.7 million in 2025, and shows the owners paying in, more than 98% of facilities having settled their capacity fees by December 4, 2025 28. What neither document states is the fund's balance or its actuarial position, so a lender relying on the cover asks the fund for both directly, and the budget carries the deductible as the owner's own cost.
