Georgia Feasibility Studies

Census estimates put Georgia 98,540 residents higher in July 2025 than a year earlier 1. Georgia's nonfarm payrolls slipped to a preliminary 4,985.7 thousand over the year to August 2026 2, and in December 2025 the Public Service Commission certified 9,885 megawatts of new Georgia Power capacity 3. For a Georgia feasibility study those dates sit beside the state's SBA, USDA and SSBCI record, its Certificate of Need and licensing rules, and nine markets from Atlanta to Valdosta.

The Georgia Thesis

A population gain to July 2025, a payroll dip to August 2026.

The headline case for Georgia is still population. The Census Bureau's Vintage 2025 release put the state's one-year gain at 98,540 residents, among the largest numeric increases of any state 1, and net domestic migration added 27,333 people in the same year to July 2025 5. The payroll series covers a later span: seasonally adjusted nonfarm employment stood at 4,993.0 thousand in August 2025 and at a preliminary 4,985.7 thousand in August 2026 2. The two periods do not overlap, so this page gives each figure its own dates and sets neither against the other.

Within the payroll year the public side moved two ways. Federal government employment in Georgia fell by 9,400 over the year to August 2026 while state and local government added 7,700 6. Atlanta's metro payrolls also slipped over the year, though BLS did not judge the change statistically significant 7. Meanwhile the state recorded $26.3 billion of investment tied to economic development projects in fiscal 2025 4, and Georgia Power has won approval to build new generation sized for large loads that are still arriving 3.

Georgia's gain of 98,540 residents in the year to July 2025 came in a Census release whose headline was the historic decline of net international migration nationally 1. The research found no Georgia figure for the international component that year. The domestic stream is documented: 27,333 net arrivals from other states, as the Tennessee State Data Center tabulated the same Census vintage 5.

Georgia ended the payroll year lower: a preliminary 4,985.7 thousand in August 2026 against 4,993.0 thousand in August 2025 2. The Joint Economic Committee's update shows where the change came from on the public side: federal employment down 9,400, state and local government up 7,700 6. The research found no 12-month figures for construction, manufacturing, trade and transportation, leisure, finance or information, and no current list of the ten largest private employers with headcounts.

Announced investment, fiscal 2025 and after

The state's own figure for fiscal 2025 is $26.3 billion of investment and 23,200 private-sector jobs tied to economic development projects, as 11Alive reported the Governor's release 4. Announcements keep coming: in October 2026 the Governor's office announced an investment of nearly $560 million to expand an automotive hybrid-components plant at West Point in Troup County 8, and the Columbus magnet plant described below was announced in September 2025. The research found no single register of the large 2025 to 2026 projects with their status.

Per capita personal income in Georgia was $63,006 in calendar 2024 9, and the median owner-occupied home value in the 2020 to 2024 five-year American Community Survey estimate was $303,300 10.

Capital Access

Georgia's SBA record and its SSBCI allocation.

SBA lending and the state's federal credit allocation are the two channels covered here; USDA's rural programs have their own section below.

SBA lenders approved, for Georgia projects, 15,063 loans under 7(a) in fiscal years 2016 to 2025, net of cancellations, for $11,392.3 million approved in fiscal years 2016 to 2025, net of cancellations, according to SBA's loan-level FOIA data 11. Under 504, debentures for Georgia projects totalled $1,681.6 million approved in fiscal years 2016 to 2025, net of cancellations, in the same files 11. The research could not find a primary SBA ranking of the most active Georgia 7(a) lenders or 504 CDCs for fiscal 2025.

The best available proxy is a broker's tabulation. GoSBA Loans ranks Live Oak Banking Co. first, at $127.2 million of Georgia 7(a) volume in 2025, followed by Newtek Bank at $88.7 million, Huntington National Bank at $59.2 million across 124 loans, Metro City Bank at $55.7 million and Northeast Bank at $50.5 million 12. We cite it as secondary, and with a caution: the same page gives two different statewide totals and two different lender counts, so its rankings are a guide to who is lending, not a dataset to model from 12.

SSBCI at the Department of Community Affairs

Treasury approved Georgia's allocation under the current State Small Business Credit Initiative round on November 4, 2022: $199,616,860, which in the state's rules includes a main capital allocation of $109,140,449, a very small business allocation of $8,678,471 and $53,346,483 for socially and economically disadvantaged individuals, with the balance for incentives and other uses 13.

SBA in Georgia

Where a Georgia SBA file meets the program rules.

SBA's power to ask a Georgia borrower for a study comes from 13 CFR 120.160(b): SBA may require professional appraisals, a survey, or a feasibility study 16. SOP 50 10 8.1 with Technical Policy Updates 15 holds the program tests, and SBA's fee notice covers loans approved October 1, 2026 through September 30, 2027 17; the practice page for SBA 7(a) and 504 works through both.

USDA Rural Development

USDA in Georgia: large announced totals, thin program detail.

USDA's December 2025 release gives Georgia's headline. The program-level numbers a B&I study is benchmarked against were not found.

The December 2025 release put the state's Rural Development total at $906 million and split out $501 million for rural development projects and $405 million for affordable housing 14. What it does not do is break the total down by program, and the research found no fiscal 2025 figure for Business and Industry guarantees, Community Facilities or REAP in Georgia. A study should not fill that gap with a national share or an older year; it should state that the Georgia program figures were not published in a form it could cite.

Our USDA Rural Development practice page covers the Agency's feasibility components, and the poultry processing case shows one built for a B&I guarantee on a rural Georgia site.

Agency, Conventional and EB-5

Agency, conventional and EB-5 capital for Georgia projects.

Neither a HUD multifamily office for Georgia files nor a statewide authority issuing bonds for private projects is named in the research. Our conventional and institutional and EB-5 practice pages cover those programs, and the multifamily case takes a suburban Atlanta apartment project through a bank construction loan and an agency permanent takeout.

Rules by Asset Class

Georgia's state-level rules: certificates, licences, tank coverage and rentals.

Georgia regulates health care entry, senior care, child care and fuel tanks at the state level. Zoning, fees and short-term rental rules are set locally.

Health care and Certificate of Need

Georgia still requires a Certificate of Need before a new, expanded or relocated health care facility can be developed, under the state Certificate of Need statute 18. The Department of Community Health's Certificate of Need rules cover hospitals, nursing facilities and similar institutions, while ambulatory surgery and imaging centers still file annual surveys with the department 19. HB 1339, signed in April 2024 and effective July 1, 2024, exempted some renovations and removed expenditure thresholds 20; an academic review published in 2025 called the changes small, and the research found no repeal in 2025 or 2026 21.

Senior care

Georgia licenses personal care homes and classes larger ones as assisted living communities, with the resident threshold set in the department's assisted living rules 22. The larger homes also need a licensed administrator under its personal care home rules 23. The class determines staffing, building standards and therefore operating cost, so an assisted living study names the licence category before it builds a payroll. The state had 1,636,809 residents aged 65 and over in the 2020 to 2024 five-year American Community Survey estimate 10.

Child care

No child care learning center may operate in Georgia without a licence or temporary permit from DECAL, and the licence applies once a provider cares for more than a small number of children for pay 24. The same DECAL rules set staff ratios by age group 24.

Fuel and convenience

Underground storage tanks in Georgia, those under a gas station among them, are covered by the GUST Trust Fund 25. The research found no figure for the fund's balance or solvency.

Short-term rentals and local rules

HB 399, effective July 1, 2025, bars local governments from imposing registration mandates on residential rental property; Georgia has no statewide short-term rental licensing or preemption statute, so the rules remain local 26.

Costs, Taxes and Insurance

Wages, insurance and the cost lines a Georgia pro forma has to source.

Georgia's statutory wage floor and its FAIR Plan's 2025 filing change are on record; its tax and cost indices were not found.

Georgia's statutory minimum wage is $5.15 per hour, but the federal $7.25 governs most employers, and the tipped cash wage cited for the state is the federal $2.13 27.

The Georgia Underwriting Association, the state's FAIR Plan, is the insurer of last resort for building and personal property cover, commercial risks included 28. A December 1, 2025 directive changed its filing rules so that it pays premium tax directly to the Commissioner's office from tax year 2025 29. HB 606, introduced on February 24, 2025 to let the plan redistribute risk among insurers, had no confirmed final passage in the research 30. The research found no approved commercial property rate changes for 2025 to 2026 and no documented carrier withdrawals.

The research could not source a statewide sales tax rule for construction materials and business equipment, a construction cost index for its cities, and an effective commercial property tax rate or rank.

Power

Georgia Power's certified build-out and the large loads behind it.

The research holds three forecasts of Georgia Power's seven-year capacity need, made in 2022, 2023 and 2025, and one certified figure, from December 2025.

In January 2025 the Public Service Commission approved a rule letting Georgia Power bill its largest new customers for upstream costs and requiring their contracts to be filed with the Commission 31. The utility filed its 2025 Integrated Resource Plan on January 31, 2025 and won approval in July 3. On December 19, 2025 the Commission certified 9,885 megawatts of new Georgia Power capacity, with a construction cost estimate of $16.5 billion in one law firm's account 3; a second source gives a higher figure, and the research left the two unresolved. The utility's seven-year capacity need was projected at 400 megawatts in 2022, 6,600 in 2023 and 8,500 in 2025, in ServerCountry's reading of the Commission's December 2025 fact sheet 32.

Hazards

Storms, a winter emergency and spring wildfires: the 2024 to 2026 record.

The declarations listed below came from a tropical storm, a winter storm and wildfires.

Tropical Storm Debby's major disaster declaration, DR-4821-GA, dated September 24, 2024, had $38,168,736 of FEMA Public Assistance obligated by June 2026 33. USAFacts' tally of FEMA data shows no federal declarations for Georgia in calendar 2025 and a return of declarations in 2026 34. The first came in January 2026, when EM-3642-GA was issued for the severe winter storm of January 22 to 27 35. In April 2026 FEMA approved fire management assistance for the Pineland Road wildfire in Clinch and Echols counties (FM-5629-GA) and the Highway 82 wildfire in Brantley County (FM-5630-GA) 36.

Georgia's hazard evidence on this page is the declaration record alone; the research found no National Risk Index ranking for the state and no quantified exposure measure for any hazard.

Statewide Supply

What Georgia counts statewide, and what it does not.

Lessors, hotel staff and renter households, counted by two federal programs; the state's personal care home rules are under rules above.

County Business Patterns counted 1,939 establishments among Georgia's residential building lessors in 2023 (County Business Patterns), a count of operating businesses rather than of buildings 37, and 43,887 employees in the state's hotel industry in 2023 (County Business Patterns) 37. Georgia's renter households number 1,398,009 in the 2020 to 2024 five-year American Community Survey estimate 10, and across renter-occupied units paying cash rent the median gross rent is $1,393 in the same 2020 to 2024 five-year American Community Survey estimate 10.

Statewide, the research found no count of hotel rooms, storage space, RV parks, convenience stores, child care places, hospitals or surgery centers, and no vacancy or construction figure for apartments or industrial space, nor an occupancy figure for senior housing.

Review Failures

Where Georgia studies go wrong in review.

Georgia's record invites four misreadings: arrivals taken for jobs, announcements for payroll, one Atlanta broker for the market, and demand before the certificate.

  1. Population growth read as job growth

    Georgia's population grew by 98,540 between July 2024 and July 2025 1. Its payroll figures cover a different span, August 2025 to August 2026, when nonfarm employment went from 4,993.0 thousand to a preliminary 4,985.7 thousand 2.

  2. An announcement counted as payroll

    The state tied 23,200 private-sector jobs to its fiscal 2025 economic development projects 4, and the Columbus magnet plant, announced in September 2025, promised more than 520 38. Both figures come from announcements, not payroll counts.

  3. One broker's pipeline treated as fact

    For the second quarter of 2026, Matthews counted 16,243 Atlanta apartment units under construction and MMG counted 13,896 4041.

  4. The Certificate of Need left for later

    Georgia's statute requires a Certificate of Need before a new, expanded or relocated health care facility is developed 18, and the 2024 reform in HB 1339 exempted some renovations and removed expenditure thresholds 20.

Site Tests

Georgia site work begins at the power company and ends at the traffic count.

Five checks, in the order our Georgia studies take them, each tied to a record on this page.

Electric service first

Georgia Power manages large loads through contracts and certification dockets rather than a public queue 32, and since January 2025 it may bill its largest new customers for upstream costs 31. Nobody outside the utility can see how much of a feeder is already spoken for. That is why the first request in a Georgia file goes to the electric provider: a will-serve letter naming the service and who pays to extend it.

The state the parcel stands in

Two metros on this page cross a state line, Augusta into South Carolina and Columbus into Alabama. Near either line the study states which state's rules and agencies govern the parcel, and sorts its competitors by state before treating them as one market, using the South Carolina or Alabama page for the far side.

Rules the state leaves to towns

Georgia has no statewide short-term rental licensing, and HB 399 stops local governments from requiring rental registration while leaving the rest of the rules to them 26. Impact fees are local too. The study takes each rule from the government that will issue the permit, and a health care use first asks whether it needs a Certificate of Need 18.

Program status, from the agencies

The research reached no USDA rural, Opportunity Zone or HUBZone result in any of the nine markets, and in the Albany metro it left the USDA eligibility of Terrell and Worth counties unverified, so a Georgia study puts each question to the agency's own tool and names the tool behind each answer.

Traffic last

The research found no GDOT station count in any of the nine markets.

Atlanta

Atlanta: an airline, hospital systems and two counts of one apartment pipeline.

An Atlanta study has to pick its broker series, explain a flat payroll year in a metro still adding residents, and draw a trade area far smaller than the metro.

Census put the Atlanta-Sandy Springs-Roswell metro at 6,482,182 residents on July 1, 2025, as transcribed from the Vintage 2025 metro table 39; the City of Atlanta held 529,110 42. Most of the market lives outside the city, and a trade area drawn around a downtown address misses it.

The employer base is unusually concentrated in a few large organizations. The Chamber's 2025 survey lists Delta Air Lines at 42,090 full-time equivalents, Piedmont Healthcare at 29,646, Publix Super Markets at 23,660 and Wellstar Health System at 21,020, with Northside ranked second behind Delta; the five largest together counted 148,416 FTEs 43. Emory reports 26,400 employees across Emory Healthcare 44, while the survey lists Emory University at 13,500 FTEs 43: different organizations and measures, so a study should not quote one as the other. Georgia Tech appears at 9,389 FTEs 43.

Payrolls went sideways. BLS counted 3,119.4 thousand nonfarm jobs in August 2025 and a preliminary 3,105.1 thousand in August 2026, a change of 14.3 thousand, or 0.5%, that BLS did not find statistically significant 7. A study should present it as flat, not as a decline or a gain.

Apartments carry the most evidence and the most disagreement. Matthews put vacancy at 5.86% in the second quarter of 2026 40; MMG reported stabilized occupancy of 90.5% and an effective rent of $1,570, down 1.0% from a year earlier 41. The two measure different things, and they also disagree on the pipeline: 16,243 units under construction by Matthews' count, 13,896 by MMG's 4041. JLL counted 12.3 million square feet of industrial space under construction 45. The City of Atlanta sets impact fees by square footage for single-family homes and by building height for multifamily 46. The record holds no hotel occupancy or rate level for the metro. Three worked cases are set in metro Atlanta, the Cartersville gas station in Bartow County among them (see cases).

Augusta

Augusta: Richmond County at the core, with a South Carolina side.

A two-state metro with a strong 2025 jobs reading and almost no published supply data.

The Augusta-Richmond County metro is a Georgia and South Carolina metro in the Census tables 47. Census put the metro at 641,231 residents on July 1, 2025, as transcribed by Wikipedia 47. Richmond County, the core, held 206,559 in 2025, which the research reads as essentially unchanged from 2020 48; a study should therefore set its trade area on the side of the metro where the site sits rather than on the core county's trend. Where a trade area crosses the state line, the South Carolina page carries the rules and program context for the Aiken side.

BLS counted 253.0 thousand nonfarm jobs in the metro in July 2026, a preliminary figure 49. An Augusta University economist ranked Augusta the fastest-growing Georgia metro for jobs in 2025 50; a study should cite the BLS series and describe the ranking as one analyst's reading.

Columbus, Georgia

Columbus, Georgia: Muscogee County and a metro that reaches into Alabama.

This is Columbus, Georgia, not Columbus, Ohio: a Georgia and Alabama metro where a new plant meets a population that has been slipping.

Columbus here means Columbus, Georgia, in Muscogee County 38. Its metro reaches into Alabama, as the St. Louis Fed's series name, Columbus, GA-AL, shows 51, so a trade area that crosses the state line needs the Alabama page for the rules on that side.

The demand base has been shrinking slowly. The St. Louis Fed's Census series puts the metro at 326,575 residents in 2021 and 324,830 in 2025 51. Payrolls moved the same way: the Georgia Department of Labor counted 123.1 thousand nonfarm jobs in August 2025 and 122.5 thousand in August 2026 52. Neither figure describes a collapse, but neither supports a pro forma that assumes growth.

The largest new demand signal is industrial. In September 2025 the Governor announced a rare-earth magnet plant in Muscogee County, about $223 million of investment and more than 520 jobs, in a facility of 130,000 square feet planned at Muscogee Technology Park 38.

Macon

Macon: the Macon-Bibb County metro of Middle Georgia.

A Middle Georgia metro whose published tables blend Macon with the Warner Robins metro next door.

The Macon-Bibb County metro's population is published two ways for the same date: 238,533 on July 1, 2025 in Wikipedia's transcription of the Census metro list 47 and 238,553 on its Macon metro page 53. The larger Macon-Warner Robins combined area, which adds the Warner Robins metro, held 446,644 residents in 2025 against 425,416 in 2020 53; the Warner Robins section gives that metro's own figures.

The Georgia Department of Labor, as Middle Georgia CEO reprinted it, counted 101,500 nonfarm jobs in the Macon metro in March 2026 54. The headline spoke of gains, but the same release's year-over-year job change was slightly negative 54, and a study should quote the job count rather than the headline.

The only employer list the research found is a broker's August 2023 report for all of Middle Georgia. It lists Robins Air Force Base at 24,500 employees, Houston Healthcare at 2,475 and Atrium Health Navicent at 637 beds (licensed) 55; because the list covers all of Middle Georgia, a study checks each employer's location against the metro boundary before counting it. A study for Macon should rebuild the list from current sources and keep the two metros apart.

Athens

Athens: the Athens-Clarke County metro and its university.

The job evidence is secondary.

Census put the Athens-Clarke County metro at 224,148 residents on July 1, 2025, as transcribed by Wikipedia 47.

The job evidence is a substitute. An Augusta University economist's reading of state data counted 700 jobs added in Athens in the first half of 2026 50. In a university market the class most exposed to that thin record is student housing, which needs its own count of beds and pre-leasing by property, because no population series measures it.

Albany

Albany: Dougherty, Lee, Terrell and Worth counties.

A southwest Georgia metro where the demand question is decline and the employer record is a decade old.

The 2020 Census counted 148,922 residents in the Albany metro 56, and the research found no 2025 metro figure. The city's own count shows the direction: 69,647 residents in 2020 and 66,907 in 2025 57.

Jobs point the same way. An Augusta University economist ranked Albany among the largest percentage job losses in the state in the first half of 2026, without a count 50. The employer record is old: the latest list the research found dates from 2016 and names the Marine Corps Logistics Base Albany among the leading employers 58.

Valdosta

Valdosta: a metro read from secondary sources.

A south Georgia metro with a substitute job figure and no employer headcount.

Jobs are read from substitutes. An Augusta University economist's reading of state data counted 600 jobs added in Valdosta in the first half of 2026, the largest percentage gain statewide 50. The record names no Valdosta employer with a headcount.

Warner Robins

Warner Robins: Houston County and Robins Air Force Base.

A Middle Georgia metro resting on one Air Force installation whose workforce a study has to test first.

The Warner Robins metro had 191,614 residents in 2020 and 208,091 in 2025 by Wikipedia's reading of Census tables 53. Houston County went from 163,633 to 178,214 over the same years 59, and the City of Warner Robins from 80,308 to 87,761 60. BLS counted a preliminary 78.1 thousand nonfarm jobs in the metro in April 2026 61.

Robins Air Force Base is the anchor, and its numbers have to be read carefully. Military OneSource, citing the base's FY2025 economic impact statement, gives a total workforce of 21,506: 14,154 appropriated-fund civilians, 4,976 military personnel and 2,376 others 62. The broker list filed under Macon shows a higher figure from August 2023, a different year and measure. A study should use the FY2025 statement and note that most of the workforce is civilian.

Worked Cases

Where Georgia's six worked cases sit.

The worked cases are representative examples: public, dated market evidence, a hypothetical site and sponsor, and a published model.

Metro Atlanta carries three. The Cartersville gas station in unincorporated Bartow County tests a 7(a) start-up in the trade area of I-75 Exit 296, where national travel centers already serve the interchange and the store has to win on commuter trips rather than trucks. The drive-thru quick-service restaurant sits on a suburban commuter corridor in the metro's exurban edge and tests a franchised start-up under SBA 7(a), from trade-area demand to a defensible unit volume. The multifamily community in a fast-growing Atlanta suburb is worked through a bank construction loan and an agency permanent takeout, so it tests absorption and capture rather than an SBA coverage floor.

Two cases sit on the interstate corridors that run toward the coast. The truck stop and travel center is a ground-up project on the I-16 corridor between Macon and the Port of Savannah, underwritten to SBA 504, where diesel and freight volumes carry the revenue case. The select-service hotel is a new flagged property on an interstate and tourism corridor between metro Atlanta and the coast, also under SBA 504, and tests demand segmentation against a competitive set. Rural Georgia carries the sixth: the poultry processing plant, a new inspected plant financed with a USDA Business and Industry guarantee, where grower supply and buyer demand carry more weight than local population.

No location in Savannah, Augusta, Columbus, Macon, Athens, Albany, Valdosta or Warner Robins is named by a published case. The Savannah page points to the truck stop and multifamily cases; for the other markets the corridor and rural cases above are the closest comparables.

Georgia Questions

Georgia feasibility study questions.

Which SBA lenders are active in Georgia?

A broker's tabulation, GoSBA Loans, ranks Live Oak Banking Co. first in Georgia 7(a) volume for 2025 at $127.2 million, followed by Newtek Bank at $88.7 million, Huntington National Bank at $59.2 million across 124 loans, Metro City Bank at $55.7 million and Northeast Bank at $50.5 million 12. The same page gives two statewide totals and two lender counts, so it shows who is lending, not a dataset to model from 12. State credit sits beside it: Georgia's SSBCI allocation of $199,616,860, approved on November 4, 2022, is administered by the Department of Community Affairs 13.

What USDA figures does the research hold for Georgia?

One dated release. USDA's December 2025 release gives Georgia $906 million of Rural Development investment, $501 million of it for rural development projects and $405 million for affordable housing, without naming the fiscal year 14; the USDA section sets out what the release leaves out.

Did Georgia add jobs between August 2025 and August 2026?

Not on the statewide count. Nonfarm employment was 4,993.0 thousand in August 2025 and a preliminary 4,985.7 thousand in August 2026 2; federal jobs fell by 9,400 while state and local government added 7,700 6. The Atlanta metro's change over the year was not statistically significant 7.

What did Georgia's Public Service Commission decide on large electric loads in 2025?

Two things. In January 2025 it approved a rule letting Georgia Power bill its largest new customers for upstream costs and requiring their contracts to be filed with the Commission 31, and in December 2025 it certified 9,885 megawatts of new Georgia Power capacity 3. Large loads are managed through contracts and dockets rather than a public queue 32, which is why our Georgia site tests begin with the electric provider.

Is Columbus on this page Columbus, Georgia?

Yes. Our Columbus section covers Columbus, Georgia, in Muscogee County 38, and its two-state metro, which the St. Louis Fed's Census series names Columbus, GA-AL and puts at 324,830 residents in 2025 against 326,575 in 2021 51, and the Governor announced a rare-earth magnet plant there in September 2025 with more than 520 jobs 38. Columbus, Ohio is a different market and is not covered on this page.

When does a Georgia health care or senior housing project need a Certificate of Need?

Before it is built, if it is a new, expanded or relocated health care facility under the state Certificate of Need statute 18. The Department of Community Health's rules cover hospitals, nursing facilities and similar institutions, while surgery and imaging centers file annual surveys 19. The 2024 reform in HB 1339 exempted some renovations and removed expenditure thresholds 20, and the research found no later repeal 21. Personal care homes and assisted living communities are licensed rather than certified, so their study names the licence class first 22.

Why does this page cover Macon and Warner Robins in separate sections when their figures overlap?

Because they are two metros and the published tables blend them. The Macon-Warner Robins combined area held 446,644 residents in 2025 against 425,416 in 2020 53, and the only Middle Georgia employer list in the research, a broker's August 2023 report, puts Robins Air Force Base at 24,500 employees 55, while the base's FY2025 economic impact statement gives a total workforce of 21,506 62. The Macon and Warner Robins sections keep each metro's own population and job figures apart.
