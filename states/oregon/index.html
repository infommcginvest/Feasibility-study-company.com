Oregon's Year

A slow-growing state whose payrolls shrank while its laws and regulators reset power, wage and lodging costs.

Oregon's record for 2025 and 2026 has a demand half and a cost half. On the demand side the payroll count fell, and manufacturing was among the sectors that lost jobs 3. On the cost side, statutes and regulators changed several lines a pro forma carries, for power to very large loads, wages, lodging and property insurance, and each change is dated in its own section below.

Population barely moved at the state level, and it did not move evenly. Central Oregon's Deschutes and Crook counties kept adding residents in 2025; Coos and Curry counties on the south coast held fewer people in 2025 than in 2020.

The eight market sections below show how far apart the regions sit. The Portland metro and Lane County had fewer payroll jobs in August 2026 than a year before, and so did Crook County; the Bend metro's adjusted count rose while its unadjusted count fell. The south coast's counts are below 2020, and in the Columbia Basin the record is one of data-center land, power and permanent jobs rather than of residents.

Capital data are the thinnest part of the record. SBA's loan-level files, as the register reads them, give Oregon's 7(a) and 504 counts; the state's own credit programs are Business Oregon's SSBCI allocation and the revenue bonds the state issues for industry.

Intel

The Hillsboro News-Times, describing Intel as the state's biggest for-profit employer, put its Oregon staff at about 20,000 in December 2024 2. Its 2025 cuts arrived as separate notices. KLCC reported on July 8, 2025 a notice covering 529 Oregon positions 5. On July 14 the Forest Grove News-Times reported a notice of 2,392 across four Washington County campuses: 1,521 at Ronler Acres, 519 at Jones Farm, 192 at Aloha and 160 at Hawthorn Farm 16; the two July reports give notices of different sizes, and the research does not reconcile them. By November 13 OPB counted more than 3,100 Oregon positions cut during the year and wrote that the company, at around 23,000 Oregon workers at its peak, was now closer to around 18,000 1. No current ranked list of Oregon's largest private employers was found; the one other named count in the research, 23,100 Portland-area employees for Providence Health & Services, comes from a 2023 list relayed by Axios 17.

Power and Data Centers

A rate class of their own for data centers: what the POWER Act changed on Portland General Electric's system.

The Legislature passed the POWER Act, HB 3546, on June 5, 2025. It put data centers and crypto mining above its size threshold in a separate rate class with long-term contracts 8.

Portland General Electric's large-load tariff followed. The Public Utility Commission approved it in May 2026 and it took effect on June 10, 2026: customers in the class pay all of the distribution upgrades they require and a minimum generation and transmission demand charge set on most of their contracted capacity, and their place in the queue depends on zero-emission capacity being available 9.

On September 2, 2026 the governor's office reported the first results, from the commission's biennial report to the Legislature: PGE's residential rates had fallen 1.3% on average and its data-center rates had risen 29%, across approximately 858,000 residential customers and roughly 105,000 commercial and industrial ones 4. The same report gives data-center load growth of approximately 36% a year since 2021 and projects approximately 14% a year through 2030; a review of Pacific Power was expected to finish later in 2026 4.

How many data centers

The Oregon Capital Chronicle reported on September 28, 2026 that an ECOnorthwest and University of Virginia analysis counts roughly 111 data centers in Oregon, and that a higher, unattributed estimate also appears because a data center is hard to define 18. Blackridge Research & Consulting's 2026 list counts 26 upcoming Oregon projects totalling about 1.3 GW and about $39.9 billion of construction spending; the research marks the list secondary and unverified 19.

Portland, Oregon

Portland, Oregon: the five Oregon counties of a two-state metro, on state payrolls, county counts and broker reports.

This section covers Portland, Oregon, and the Oregon counties of the Census Bureau's Portland-Vancouver-Hillsboro metro: Clackamas, Columbia, Multnomah, Washington and Yamhill. The metro also takes in Clark and Skamania counties in Washington; the Washington page's Vancouver section covers Clark County. The Employment Department's and the brokers' Portland figures below describe the metro as each publisher draws it, which the research does not set out county by county.

People

Portland State's preliminary estimates for July 1, 2025 put Multnomah County at 805,583 residents, Washington County at 618,037 and Clackamas County at 432,473 6. The City of Portland held 640,623 residents, 1,221 more than a year before, by OPB's report of December 30, 2025 22. No count for Columbia or Yamhill County was found.

Payrolls

The department's Portland release of September 22, 2026 counted 1,234,800 nonfarm jobs in August 2026 and 1,236,500 in August 2025, not seasonally adjusted, a change it gives as 1,700 fewer; on its seasonally adjusted series the metro had 12,200 fewer jobs 25. Manufacturing held 112,100 jobs, 4,300 fewer than a year before, and health care and social assistance 187,800, up 5,200; hospitals alone employed 34,600 25. Nursing and residential care employed 36,000, 1,700 more than a year earlier; no senior housing occupancy reading for the metro was found 25.

Apartments

Kidder Mathews, on CoStar data for the second quarter of 2026, put Portland apartment vacancy at 7.1%, the asking rent at $1,656 a unit a month and the number of units under construction at 4,215 26. Colliers' report for the same quarter counts 3,400 units under construction, a different count from Kidder Mathews', and gives an effective rent of $1,750, down 2.25% on the year, with more than 25% of landlords offering concessions 27. Yardi Matrix counted 5,725 units delivered in 2025 28.

The owners' survey that HFO prepared for Multifamily NW, covering about 29,300 units, reported 6.25% vacancy in spring 2026 against 5.85% in spring 2025 29; Multifamily NW's own page for the same report cites 5.86% and 4.81% 30. The two do not agree, and a study names the one it relies on. In the same survey's Beaverton submarket, in Washington County, vacancy was 6.7% and effective rents were 3.1% lower than a year before, with 178 units absorbed and 285 delivered 29.

Hotels and warehouses

Downtown Portland hotels filled about 62% of their rooms in 2025, about 10 points below 2019, and earned $13 less per available room than in 2019, OPB's Think Out Loud reported on May 29, 2026 from Portland Business Journal and CoStar figures 31. No hotel rate, revenue or pipeline figure for the metro as a whole was found.

For warehouses, Cushman & Wakefield's Industrial MarketBeat for the second quarter of 2026, as WARE CRE reproduces it, gives Portland industrial vacancy of 6.5%, with 3,433,962 square feet under construction and asking rents of $10.89 per square foot per year, triple net 32.

Hillsboro

Hillsboro, a Washington County city inside the Portland metro: Intel's notices and a supplier's closure.

Hillsboro is a city in Washington County, inside the Portland metro, and this section carries only figures the research ties to the city itself; Washington County's population is in the Portland section above.

City officials told the Hillsboro News-Times in August 2025 that 78% of Oregon's semiconductor workers are employed in Hillsboro 33. Intel's notice of November 2025 for 669 more layoffs named its Hillsboro and Aloha campuses 1, and in the same month the Hillsboro News-Times reported that Edwards Vacuum would lay off 128 workers and close its Hillsboro production unit in 2026 2.

The city's own employer list in the research is undated and predates Intel's 2024 and 2025 cuts, so no Hillsboro headcount is printed here, and no Hillsboro permit count, rent survey, hotel reading or local rule was found. A Hillsboro study that rests on semiconductor demand collects current headcounts from Intel and its suppliers, the city's permit log and a rent survey of its own competing properties.

Salem, Oregon

Salem, Oregon, in Marion and Polk counties: one county count, a household jobs series and three apartment readings.

Portland State put Marion County at 352,162 residents on July 1, 2025 6; no count for Polk County, the metro's other county, was found. State government and Salem Health are the two employers the research names, and it holds no dated headcount for either.

No payroll series for the Salem metro past August 2025 was found. The nearest reading is household employment, which rose 2.3% between September 2024 and September 2025 in the Employment Department's December 2025 release 34.

Apartments

TMG Northwest, on CoStar data for the third quarter of 2025, gave Salem apartment vacancy of 5.5%, with 558 units delivered and 853 absorbed over the twelve months to that quarter and asking rents up 0.6% 35. Its July 2025 report puts the market's average asking rent at $1,397 a unit and the four- and five-star average at $1,718, with 766 units under construction 36. At a Salem forum in February 2026, vacancy including units still leasing up was put at about 5.9% with a forecast of about 5%, and speakers described a longer permitting clock, the Salem Business Journal reported on February 12 37. The two vacancy figures come from different definitions and months.

No hotel, storage, industrial or senior housing reading was found for the metro, and no local impact fee or utility capacity record.

Eugene-Springfield

Eugene-Springfield and Lane County: a public payroll, a flat job count and the spring 2025 apartment survey.

Lane County is the whole Eugene-Springfield metro. Portland State put it at 385,201 residents on July 1, 2025 6.

Government at all levels employed 28,200 people in the county in September 2025, by the Employment Department's December release 38. The department's September 2026 release puts the county's unadjusted nonfarm count at 159,700 for August 2026, against 160,100 a year earlier, and its seasonally adjusted change at 1,400 fewer jobs, or 1% 39. Lookout Eugene-Springfield reported 164,600 Lane County jobs in December 2025, 1,800 below the pre-pandemic peak, in a February 2026 report on a job total that has stayed flat since 2020 while the industries behind it changed 40.

Multifamily NW's spring 2025 survey put Eugene-Springfield apartment vacancy at 3.56%, against 4.3% in the survey before it 41; no rent or construction count for the metro was found.

For lenders, the one named record is Summit Bank of Eugene's announcement of November 2022 that the SBA Portland District had named it top community bank lender under both 7(a) and 504 for fiscal 2022 42; no later ranking was found.

Bend and Central Oregon

Bend, Deschutes and Jefferson counties: two population series, payrolls that turn on seasonal adjustment, and a hotel year.

The Employment Department has published the Bend metro as Crook, Deschutes and Jefferson counties since April 2025, with the three-county series carried back to 2001 43. Crook County's own figures are in the Prineville section below.

Population

For Deschutes County the Census Bureau's Vintage 2025 estimate is 213,072 residents on July 1, 2025; the county's April 2020 estimates base was 198,261 44. Portland State's preliminary estimate for the same date is 213,886, a gain of 2,784, or 1.32%, over the year 6. Portland State put Jefferson County at 25,678 residents, up 0.53% 6.

Payrolls

Seasonally adjusted, the metro's nonfarm payrolls rose by 590 jobs between August 2025 and August 2026; before adjustment they fell by 1,810, and private employment by 2,710, the Employment Department's Central Oregon release of September 22, 2026 reports 45. In Deschutes County, leisure and hospitality held 1,270 fewer jobs than a year before on the unadjusted count 45.

Hotels and room tax

For Visit Bend's fiscal year 2024-25, as the Bend Bulletin reported it on November 26, 2025, Bend hotels averaged $141.81 a night, about $2 less than a year before, with occupancy up 3% and the occupancy level itself not published 7. The City of Bend collected $14.5 million of transient room tax that year, 1.4% more than the year before, and Deschutes County $12.7 million 7.

Bend's room tax is 10.4% on top of the state lodging tax and Deschutes County's is 8%, KTVZ reported on March 21, 2025. In the 2025 session HB 3556 proposed letting new or increased lodging tax revenue pay for public safety and infrastructure; the city backed it, and Visit Bend and Visit Central Oregon objected 46.

No employer list, apartment rent or construction count was found for the metro.

Prineville and Crook County

Prineville and Crook County: the state's fastest five-year growth on a payroll that shrank.

Crook County grew faster than any other Oregon county over the five years to July 1, 2025 in the Census Bureau's Vintage 2025 estimates, adding 2,825 residents, or 11.4%, to reach 27,564, KTVZ reported on March 26, 2026 47. Portland State's preliminary estimate for July 1, 2025 is 26,760, a gain of 271 residents, or 1.02%, over the year 6.

The payroll ran the other way. Seasonally adjusted, the county lost 270 jobs between August 2025 and August 2026, a fall of 3.7%, with construction down 130 jobs, manufacturing down 60 and information down 60 45. The research found no source that names a cause for those losses.

No employer headcount, supply reading or local rule was found for the county, and its residential permits sit inside the Bend metro's monthly series, so the competitive set and the current rules for a Prineville site come from the study's own survey and from the city and county.

Oregon Coast

The Oregon Coast, Clatsop, Tillamook, Lincoln, Coos and Curry counties: south coast losses and county caps on vacation rentals.

On the south coast, Coos County held 65,124 residents on July 1, 2025 in Portland State's certified estimates, 156 fewer than in 2020, and Curry County 23,375, 137 fewer than in 2020 and 283 fewer than a year before, the Employment Department reported on January 6, 2026 21. Household employment rose 2.0% in each of the two counties between September 2024 and September 2025 34. No Portland State count for Clatsop or Tillamook County was found, and no 2026 payroll reading for any coast county.

Vacation rental limits

Each county writes its own short-term rental rules. Lincoln County's Order 03-25-115 of March 19, 2025 amended its license caps in Regions 6 and 7 13, and the county has stopped issuing new licenses, even east of US-101 where the caps would allow them, until all legal appeals conclude 12. KLCC counted 469 licensed short-term rentals in unincorporated Lincoln County in December 2024, against 502 in February 2023 48.

Clatsop County caps short-term rental units in its unincorporated areas under Ordinance 24-19, set operating standards for its Coastal Residential zone under Ordinance 25-10, and limits how often a unit may be rented in the AC-RCR zone 49. Tillamook County's 2023 amendments to Ordinance 84 allow license caps by subarea, by an Oregon REALTORS summary 50.

For a lodging project or a rental conversion on the coast, these county rules govern the vacation rentals it competes with or becomes. The research found no hotel occupancy, apartment or employer figure for any of the five counties.

Southern and Eastern Oregon

Southern and eastern Oregon: Jackson, Josephine, Klamath, Umatilla, Morrow and Gilliam counties, health care employers and Columbia Basin data centers.

This section covers six counties in two groups: Jackson and Josephine in the south, with Klamath, and Umatilla, Morrow and Gilliam in the Columbia Basin. No Klamath or Umatilla County population count was found.

Jackson and Josephine counties

Portland State put Jackson County at 222,159 residents on July 1, 2025 6. The Employment Department's 2025 Rogue Valley estimates put Jackson 0.3% below its 2020 level, and Josephine County at 89,686, 1,218 more than in 2020, a gain of 1.4% 51.

The Grants Pass Daily Courier reported on April 26, 2026 that Asante, with more than 6,000 employees, is the Rogue Valley's largest employer, with about 1,300 of them at Three Rivers Medical Center in Grants Pass; in Josephine County the next private employers it lists are Options for Southern Oregon, with 416 employees, and AllCare Health, with 396 52. Federal jobs numbered 1,950 in Jackson County in 2024, at an average wage of $87,866, and 304 in Josephine County 53.

Jackson County payrolls in August 2026 were 0.2% lower than a year before, with private education and health up 1,400 jobs, retail down 710 and manufacturing down 390, by a guest column of October 1, 2026 that cites the Employment Department 54. Unemployment stood at 5.1% in Jackson County and 7.2% in Josephine, a department economist told KOBI-TV in February 2026 55. The Bureau of Labor Statistics' preliminary count for the Medford metro, which is Jackson County, was 90,600 nonfarm jobs in August 2026, 0.7% fewer than a year before 56, and the Grants Pass metro, Josephine County, held 26,700 seasonally adjusted payroll jobs in July 2026, with no twelve-month change found 57.

Umatilla, Morrow and Gilliam counties

The Umatilla Electric Cooperative's load has grown by more than 500% over the last decade, and the cooperative has had to buy more gas- and coal-fired power on the wholesale market, the Oregon Capital Chronicle reported on September 28, 2026; the same report puts about 20% of Oregon's data centers in Umatilla County 18. Copia's proposed Charlestown data center and battery storage project, on 106 acres of Bureau of Land Management land in Umatilla County, would be the first Oregon data center on federal public land; its draft plan was submitted on May 8, 2026, with no dollar figure disclosed 18.

In Hermiston, a local official estimated to OPB in August 2026 that AWS's permanent operations jobs in the Umatilla region number about 2,000 to 2,400, about 200 a campus, while the more than 1,000 jobs of the former Umatilla Chemical Depot are gone; the city has annexed about 800 to 810 acres for future campuses 58. Near Arlington in Gilliam County, AWS bought 376 acres for $10 million and 30 acres for $3 million, DatacenterDynamics reported on February 17, 2025, with no build timeline 59.

No apartment, hotel or storage reading was found for any of the six counties, and no local rule.

Business Oregon and the state's bonds

Business Oregon's SSBCI allocation, approved in August 2022, is $83.5 million: a main allocation of $52.1 million, $4.1 million for very small businesses, $17.8 million for socially and economically disadvantaged individuals and a performance-based $9.5 million on top of it 63. Its programs are venture capital and venture debt, loan participation, loan guarantees with private lenders for lines of credit, construction and term loans, a Capital Access Program and technical assistance 63.

The Oregon Business Development Commission issues industrial development revenue bonds under ORS 285B.320 to 285B.371, with the State Treasurer's approval; $518.9 million of their principal was outstanding on June 30, 2022, by the Legislature's measure analysis for the 2023 session 64.

USDA and other channels

USDA Rural Development announced on December 16, 2025 its guarantee of a $25 million Business and Industry loan from Native American Bank for the Blue Mountain grain mill in Umatilla County 65. SBA Districts

Two SBA districts cover Oregon: Portland's, which crosses into Washington, and Boise's in the east.

SBA's Portland District Office serves Oregon's counties other than the easternmost ones, together with counties in southwest Washington; the easternmost Oregon counties belong to the agency's Boise District, and the Portland District page does not name them 66. The federal text on studies is 13 CFR 120.160(b): SBA may require professional appraisals, a survey, or a feasibility study 67. How an Oregon file meets the 7(a) and 504 tests is on our SBA 7(a) and 504 lending page.

Taxes, Wages and Insurance

Oregon's cost lines for 2026 and 2027: no sales tax, a lodging tax, three wage tiers and the FAIR Plan's limits.

Oregon levies no general sales tax, so construction materials and equipment carry none, a 2026 secondary summary notes 68. Lodging is taxed instead. The state transient lodging tax is 1.5% for stays ending by December 31, 2026 and 2.75% for stays ending on or after January 1, 2027, the change the 2026 Legislature made 10; local rates come on top, as Bend's does 46. Travel Oregon's report of January 2020 put the average local rate across the jurisdictions that levied one at 7.5% 69.

Wages

The Bureau of Labor and Industries announced in April 2026 the rates that run from July 1, 2026 to June 30, 2027: $16.80 an hour in the Portland metro, $15.55 as the standard rate and $14.55 in nonurban counties, after a 3.3% inflation adjustment from $16.30, $15.05 and $14.05 11. A payroll line takes the tier of the subject's own location.

Property insurance

The Oregon FAIR Plan Association is the insurer of last resort. It writes commercial property up to $1,000,000, a limit raised from $700,000 on May 1, 2023, and dwellings and farms up to $600,000 70. In the 2026 wildfire season the Division of Financial Regulation ordered insurers on July 31 not to cancel or non-renew policies in affected ZIP codes, and on August 28 extended the order to September 29 7172.

The 2025 Legislature's insurance bills include SB 82, which bars insurers from using the state wildfire map to rate or non-renew a policy, HB 2563 on an insured's right to an explanation at renewal, and SB 829, a study of a state reinsurance program that takes in affordable and multifamily housing 73.

Licensing and Local Rules

Rules by asset class in Oregon: a narrow certificate of need, three child care classes, fixed liquor fees and private tank insurance.

Hospitals and long-term care

Oregon's certificate of need reaches new hospitals and new skilled nursing or intermediate care facilities under ORS 442.315 15. The Oregon Health Authority's rules add long-term care bed increases above the division's threshold, the rebuilding or relocation of a long-term care facility, and health maintenance organization facilities 74, and ambulatory surgical centers are outside the requirement by statute 14. HB 4139 of the 2024 session would have taken new psychiatric, rehabilitation and substance-use hospitals and new long-term care facilities out of the program; its enactment was not found 75. Long-term care facility licenses come from the Department of Human Services and other health facility licenses from the Oregon Health Authority 14; the license classes for assisted living and memory care were not found.

Child care

The Department of Early Learning and Care licenses child care in three classes: Registered Family; Certified Family, in a single-family dwelling, for up to 16 children; and Certified Center, whose capacity is set by space and staffing. Every applicant's drinking and food-preparation water must be tested for lead before a license is issued 76.

Liquor and fuel

The Oregon Liquor and Cannabis Commission's annual fees, on the schedule revised January 1, 2024, are $800 for full on-premises sales, $400 for limited on-premises, $200 for off-premises and $400 for a nonprofit club 77. No price for buying an existing license from its holder was found.

Oregon has no state financial-assurance fund for underground storage tanks; DEQ requires continuous proof of financial responsibility, set out in its pollution liability endorsement 78. A DEQ coordinator told Beyond Toxics in October 2023 that about 90% of the state's more than 1,600 tank facilities used private insurance 79.

Building codes and rentals

The 2025 Oregon Structural and Mechanical Specialty Codes took effect statewide on October 1, 2025 and became mandatory on April 1, 2026, by a Portland permit guide 80. No state license governs short-term rentals; counties and cities set the rules, as the coast section shows.

Fire and Storms

A wildfire hazard map Oregon issued and repealed in 2025, and two winter storm disasters.

Oregon issued a statewide wildfire hazard map in January 2025 and repealed it the same year: the House passed SB 83 on June 24, 2025 and the governor signed it in July, voiding the Department of Forestry's hazard-zone orders and removing the defensible-space, building-code and seller-disclosure mandates tied to the map 8182.

Two major disasters were declared for winter storms. DR-4881-OR, declared on July 22, 2025, covers the severe storms, flooding, landslides and mudslides of March 13 to 20, 2025; Josephine County was added for Public Assistance on August 28 83. DR-4907-OR, declared on April 7, 2026, covers the storms, straight-line winds, flooding and landslides of December 15 to 21, 2025, with Public Assistance in Clackamas, Hood River, Lane, Lincoln, Linn, Polk, Tillamook, Union and Yamhill counties and, from May 28, 2026, Clatsop and Wasco 8485. No FEMA fire management declaration for 2026 was found.

Oregon Questions

Oregon feasibility study questions.

What did Bend hotels charge in fiscal 2024-25?

Visit Bend's figures for that fiscal year, which the Bend Bulletin reported on November 26, 2025, give an average daily rate of $141.81, about $2 below the prior year, and occupancy 3% higher; city room tax receipts reached $14.5 million 7.

What does a data center on Portland General Electric's system pay under the POWER Act?

HB 3546, passed on June 5, 2025, gave data centers and crypto mining over the act's size threshold their own rate class, with long-term contracts 8. PGE's large-load tariff, approved by the Public Utility Commission in May 2026 and in effect from June 10, 2026, makes those customers bear the full cost of the distribution upgrades they need, sets their minimum demand charges for generation and transmission on most of the capacity they contract, and conditions their queue position on zero-emission capacity 9.

When does Oregon's state lodging tax go up?

On January 1, 2027. Stays that end on or before December 31, 2026 pay the state rate of 1.5%, and stays that end on or after January 1, 2027 pay 2.75%, the rate the 2026 Legislature set 10.

Does Oregon have one minimum wage or several?

Three. A payroll budget uses the tier its location falls in: for July 1, 2026 to June 30, 2027 the Bureau of Labor and Industries set $15.55 an hour as the standard minimum, $16.80 for the Portland metro and $14.55 for nonurban counties, as the Cascade Employers Association reported on April 24, 2026 11.

Is Lincoln County issuing new vacation rental licenses?

No, not while appeals are pending. The county is issuing no new short-term rental licenses, even east of US-101 where its caps would allow them, until every legal appeal concludes 12, and its Order 03-25-115 of March 19, 2025 amended the caps in Regions 6 and 7 13.

Who licenses a new nursing facility in Oregon, and does it need a certificate of need?

The Department of Human Services licenses long-term care facilities, and the Oregon Health Authority licenses the other health facilities under ORS chapter 441 14. A new skilled nursing or intermediate care facility also needs a certificate of need under ORS 442.315, as a new hospital does 15, and the Health Authority's rules extend that review to bed increases at long-term care facilities above the division's threshold and to a facility's rebuilding or relocation 74; ambulatory surgical centers sit outside the requirement 14.
