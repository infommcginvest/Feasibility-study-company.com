Population

Colorado's 2025 count: births and arrivals from abroad on one side, residents leaving for other states on the other.

Feasibility Study Company writes independent feasibility studies to lender standard for Colorado projects: for the owners and developers who propose them and for the SBA, USDA Rural Development, EB-5 and bank lenders who decide whether to fund them. As a Colorado feasibility study company it starts each engagement from the state's own dated record, and the first entry in that record is where the latest year's population change came from.

On January 27, 2026 the State Demography Office summarized the Census Bureau's Vintage 2025 figures. Colorado held 6,012,561 residents on July 1, 2025 1. In the twelve months to June 30, 2025, net domestic migration was negative: 12,100 more people left Colorado for other states than arrived from them. Net international migration was +15,356, and the office gives total net migration as +3,256 1. Births numbered 65,380 in that year, up 4.6% on the year before and the most since 2017 1.

That split is the reason this page opens on population rather than on payrolls or prices. In the latest Census year the flow between Colorado and the other states ran outward, and migration added to the count only through arrivals from abroad. An absorption schedule that leans on in-movers from other states is resting on a component that was negative in the year a reviewer will check.

The components also point to different products. A child care center or a pediatric practice draws directly on births, and the 65,380 count 1 is the state figure its study cites. An apartment project that depends on newly formed households from abroad needs evidence of where those households settle, and the research behind this page found no Vintage 2025 county population figures for Denver, El Paso, Larimer, Weld, Mesa, Pueblo, Boulder or the resort counties. A study takes its county's own estimate and components from the Census county tables before it states a trade-area population.

The market sections below therefore lean on employment and wages by county, which BLS publishes quarterly, and on listing and permit series, rather than on county population. For a household-driven project the order of evidence is: the state components above, the county job and wage record in the market section, and then the site's own trade area.

Payrolls, Output and Capital

Two job counts that move in opposite directions, wages that rose, and a state budget with less room.

BLS's Mountain-Plains office reports 2,973.8 thousand nonfarm payroll jobs in Colorado for September 2026, seasonally adjusted and preliminary, up 0.4% from a year earlier 12. Its household survey for the same month counts 3,047.4 thousand employed residents and 131.9 thousand unemployed 12. BLS's quarterly census of employers, released September 4, 2026, gives a different reading: 2,848,514 jobs in March 2026 at employers covered by unemployment insurance, down 0.7% over the year, at an average weekly wage of $1,774 in the first quarter, up 5.4%, both preliminary 2.

The two job counts are separate series, and a study keeps them apart. The payroll figure is a monthly survey estimate, revised as the quarterly census arrives; the covered count is an administrative tally of employers' insurance filings, published months later and for an earlier month. The covered count also carries wages, and there the reading is plain: average pay up 5.4% on a year earlier 2 while covered jobs fell. A pro forma that holds labor cost flat from a 2024 base understates the line. Each market section prints its county's own weekly wage from the same release, so a labor budget can be set at county level.

Two sectors matter most for the property types this site covers. In September 2026 professional and business services employed 491.5 thousand people in Colorado and leisure and hospitality 359.1 thousand, both seasonally adjusted 1314. The first drives office and flex space; the second staffs hotels, restaurants, ski operations and the attractions that bring visitors. The research read only the levels; the twelve-month change for each sector comes from the BLS series itself before a study relies on a trend.

Output is reported quarterly through BEA's GDP by state release of September 30, 2026. The second-quarter 2026 figures, at seasonally adjusted annual rates, are in the table below 1516. Visitors

Record travel spending in 2025, more visitors and fewer tourism jobs.

The Colorado Tourism Office's research for 2025, released on July 28, 2026 and prepared by Dean Runyan Associates and Longwoods International, put travel spending in the state at $29.2 billion, a record and up about 2% on 2024, as KJCT reported the next day 3. SnowBrains' account of the same research gives 96.8 million visitors, up 1.4%, and $1.91 billion of state and local tax revenue generated by tourism, up 1.9% 7. It also gives tourism-supported jobs at 187,860, down 0.6% 7. Worker earnings from tourism were $10.5 billion in the same account 7.

The figures arrive through news reports because the Tourism Office's own research page refused the connection when this page was prepared. They are secondary readings of a primary study, and a lender's reviewer may ask for the Office's report itself; a study cites both.

Three points carry into a hotel, short-term rental, campground or restaurant study. Spending and visitor counts both rose in 2025, by about 2% and 1.4% in the two reports, so a revenue model has both a volume and a spend figure to cite. Tourism jobs fell while spending rose, which bears on how an operator staffs a property and at what wage, and the county wage figures in the resort section show what a mountain payroll costs. And the spending is concentrated: SnowBrains puts the Denver metro's travel spending at about $14.2 billion, 48.5% of the statewide total in the article's own reckoning 7. Denver's own market record is on the Denver feasibility page; the rest of the state's visitor economy is spread across the mountain counties, the Pikes Peak region and the Western Slope.

A skier-visit count for the 2025 to 2026 season from a named publisher was not found; the estimates in circulation do not name their source and are not printed here. Statewide hotel occupancy, average daily rate and revenue per available room for 2025 or 2026 were not found either. A lodging study in Colorado sources its competitive set's performance from a paid STR report or the operator's own records, and states which.

Statewide Supply

Listings, a price index and the state's main airport: what the statewide series hold.

Realtor.com's series, read through FRED, put the median listing price for Colorado at $564,000 in September 2026, with 34,082 active listings 2021. FHFA's all-transactions house price index for the state stood at 853.19 for the second quarter of 2026, against a base of 100 in the first quarter of 1980 22. These are owner-market series. They tell a for-sale developer what buyers are being asked to pay and how much choice they have, and they tell a rental developer what the ownership alternative costs a household; neither is a rent or a vacancy rate.

Denver International Airport handled 82,427,962 passengers in 2025, a record, of whom 4,867,484 were international passengers, also a record and 5.9% of the total, as Aviation Industry News reported the airport's release 23. The airport serves the whole state, not only the metro: for a mountain lodging project it is a gateway for guests who fly in, and its record sits on this page for that reason. Passenger counts for Colorado Springs, Grand Junction, Eagle County, Aspen and the Yampa Valley airports were not found.

No statewide total was found for industrial, office, multifamily or retail space, for self-storage, for senior housing or licensed beds, or for hotel rooms. Broker reports in Colorado are written by metro, and the market sections print them where the research found a current one: CBRE and Matthews for Colorado Springs and Northern Colorado. Broker figures for the Denver metro are read on the Denver page. For Boulder, Greeley, Pueblo, Grand Junction and the resort counties no current broker report was found, and a study for one of those markets builds its supply count from county assessor records.

Rules by Asset Class

Since 2024 the General Assembly has written housing rules that local codes must follow.

Between April 2024 and March 2026 Colorado enacted a run of laws that override parts of municipal zoning for housing. They change what can be built on a site, how much parking it must carry and how fast it can be approved, and so they change the inputs of a residential or mixed-use pro forma before the market is read at all. Each law below is cited to its General Assembly page; where the research recorded only the dates, the page says so and the study reads the act.

Apartments and mixed use near transit

HB24-1313, signed and effective on May 13, 2024, sets an average zoned density goal of 40 dwelling units per acre for transit-related areas in eligible communities, requires transit centers to allow a net density of at least 15 units per acre, and applies to places with a population of at least 4,000 24. It moved $35 million from the general fund to a transit-oriented communities infrastructure fund from July 1, 2024 24. The goal reports on affordability, displacement and housing opportunity are due December 31, 2026 24. For a site near a rail station or a high-frequency bus stop in a covered community, the zoning a study assumes is the zoning the city adopts to meet that goal, and a study dates its entitlement assumption to the adopted code, not to the statute.

HB24-1304, signed May 10, 2024 and effective August 7, 2024, prohibits covered local governments from enforcing minimum parking requirements in areas within one-quarter mile of certain transit stops from June 30, 2025 25. A project in such an area sets its parking ratio on demand and on the lender's view of it, and the study shows the ratio's effect on rent, absorption and construction cost.

Small sites, accessory units and the HOME Act

HB24-1152, signed May 13, 2024, requires municipalities of 1,000 or more residents to allow a single accessory dwelling unit from June 30, 2025, and funded a fee reduction and encouragement grant program with $5 million 26. HB24-1007, signed April 15, 2024 and effective July 1, 2024, prohibits residential occupancy limits 27. Together they bear on build-to-rent and small infill projects whose rent model counts bedrooms rather than households.

HB26-1001, the HOME Act, was signed and took effect on March 25, 2026. It applies to qualifying properties of up to 5 acres, bars subject jurisdictions from refusing residential development below 3 stories or 38 feet on height grounds, and requires them to allow qualifying residential development by administrative approval after December 31, 2027; a jurisdiction actively updating its code has until June 30, 2028 6. For a sponsor with a qualifying parcel, the approval path, and so the carrying period before construction, depends on which side of those dates the application falls.

Affordable housing: acquisitions, planning and Proposition 123

HB24-1175, signed May 30, 2024 and effective August 7, 2024, gives local governments rights of first refusal on existing affordable housing of 5 or more units, rights that end December 31, 2029 28. A buyer of an existing income-restricted property reads the notice and offer periods into the acquisition timeline, and the lender's commitment allows for them. SB24-174, signed May 30, 2024, requires a statewide housing needs assessment by November 30, 2027 and local housing action plans by January 1, 2028, and moved $10.5 million from the local government severance tax fund into a technical assistance fund for that planning 29.

The Division of Housing reported on August 26, 2026 that 2,000 homes had been approved in communities fully compliant with Proposition 123, and that the current commitment cycle ends December 31, 2026; the same update discussed a draft Qualified Allocation Plan for 2027 to 2028 30. A tax credit project in Colorado states which allocation plan it was scored under and whether its jurisdiction's Proposition 123 commitment is current.

SB26-001, the workforce housing and housing tax credit law, was signed March 25, 2026 and took effect August 12, 2026 31. HB25-1272, on construction defects and middle market housing, was signed May 12, 2025 and took effect August 6, 2025 32. The research recorded only the dates of both laws; their terms, the credit's size and the defect-claim changes that bear on condominium financing, are read from the acts themselves before a study relies on them.

Hotels, commercial property and the property tax

The property tax law of the 2024 extraordinary session, HB24B-1001, signed September 4, 2024, sets the assessment rate for commercial and agricultural property at 25% for tax year 2026, with most other nonresidential and personal property at 26%, against 27% for most nonresidential property in 2025; from 2027 most nonresidential property is assessed at 25% 33. For tax year 2024 it set lodging property at 27.9%, less a deduction from actual value, and it limits growth in local government property tax revenue to 5.25% a year of the reassessment cycle from 2025 33. A hotel, shopping center or industrial building budgets its tax line from the rate for its class and year and the county's mill levy, not from the prior owner's bill.

What was not found

No statewide short-term rental statute enacted in 2025 or 2026 was confirmed on a primary page; search listings indicate that the proposals found were not enacted, which no primary page confirmed. The state sales, lodging and corporate income tax rates could not be read from a dated Department of Revenue page. Changes to certificate of need or liquor licensing rules were not found. A study for a short-term rental, a bar or restaurant, or a health facility reads the county's and the city's own ordinances and the state agency's current rule for the site, and dates each.

Insurance and Costs

Hail and wildfire priced into Colorado home premiums, and a state enterprise that will fund roofs from a fee on insurers.

The Colorado Division of Insurance published a report in February 2026 on what hail and wildfire add to homeowners premiums. As The Aspen Times reported it on February 11, 2026, hail accounted for 26% to 54% of a homeowners premium across the counties sampled and wildfire for 0.9% to 24.6%, from data supplied by 20 carriers, 80% of market 34. The report estimated average annual savings of $82 to $387 per year from hail mitigation and $3 to $25 per year from wildfire mitigation 34.

Those are homeowners figures. A commercial property is insured on its own form and priced from a quote, and the Division's own report page and an average Colorado premium from a primary page were not available when this page was prepared. What the report establishes for a commercial study is that hail is a priced risk in every sampled county, not only wildfire, so roof specification and the deductible for wind and hail are insurance terms a study asks the sponsor's broker to quote alongside fire.

SB26-155, signed June 4, 2026 and effective August 12, 2026, creates an enterprise to widen access to homeowners insurance. From 2027 it charges insurers an annual fee of 0.5% of prior-year multiperil homeowners premiums, caps fee revenue at $100 million over the enterprise's first five years, and directs at least 85% of it to hail and windstorm retrofit grants 35. For a for-sale developer the grants may lower a buyer's cost of ownership once the program runs; for a rental or commercial owner the law adds nothing directly.

Construction cost indices for Colorado or Denver from Rider Levett Bucknall, Cumming or Turner were not found. A budget is priced from the contractor's bid and a dated cost index the lender accepts. Labor cost is better documented: each market section prints its county's first-quarter 2026 average weekly wage and each metro section its metro's May 2025 mean hourly wage, which together set a starting point for an operator's payroll.

Hazards

FEMA turned down the 2025 fires, then funded recovery from the 2026 ones.

The sequence matters for a site in a burned or flooded county. KUNC reported on April 14, 2026 that Colorado would not receive FEMA aid for the 2025 Lee and Elk fires and mudslides in Rio Blanco County or for flooding in western Colorado: FEMA first denied the request in December 2025 and denied the appeal 9. The article put damage to roads and other infrastructure from the western flooding at almost $14 million 9.

The 2026 fire and flood season went differently. On September 15, 2026 FEMA announced more than $90 million of approvals for Colorado communities, of which more than $28 million was Public Assistance and more than $62 million came through the Hazard Mitigation Grant Program, including a Front Range wildfire mitigation project 36. On September 28, 2026 it approved more than $784,000 of initial Public Assistance funding to help the state manage the program after the Aspen Acres Fire and other wildfires and flooding 8. The Boulder, Colorado Springs and Greeley sections name the local projects in the September 15 release.

Acres burned and structures lost in the 2026 Aspen Acres and Gold Mountain fires, the state hazard mitigation plan's figures and 2026 hail loss totals were not found. A Colorado study therefore reads hazard at the parcel: the flood map panel and any letter of map revision, the insurer's wildfire score for the address, and, for a site in a county named in a 2026 release, whether a public mitigation project upstream or nearby is funded and when it is scheduled. Public money that reaches a county repairs roads and drainage; it does not insure a private building.

Colorado Springs

Colorado Springs and El Paso County: fewer covered jobs, more industrial absorption and a busy half for apartments.

BLS counted 301,647 covered jobs there in March 2026, down 1.2% over the year, at an average weekly wage of $1,437 in the first quarter, up 5.0% 2. The metro's mean hourly wage across all occupations was $34.03 in May 2025 37. The county rows are El Paso's alone; the wage survey, the listing series and the broker reports below are published for the Colorado Springs metro, which takes in more than El Paso County, and are labelled as metro figures.

Housing supply comes first in this market. Census data put new private housing authorized by building permits in El Paso County at 5,707 in 2025 38. In September 2026 the metro's median listing price was $484,950 with 4,266 active listings 3940. CBRE's multifamily figures for the first half of 2026 show an average rent of $1,457 per unit, up 2.3% from year-end 2025 but down 2.6% from a year earlier, and net absorption of 1,319 units in the half against 172 units in the second half of 2025 41. Occupancy was 94.4% and completions 369 units in the half 41. Sales picked up: multifamily sales volume reached $82.5 million in the half, from $22.9 million in the second half of 2025 41. An apartment study in Colorado Springs reads that recovery in demand against rents that were still below a year earlier, and models concessions accordingly.

Industrial space carries the fullest broker record. CBRE reports 357,000 sq. ft. of net absorption in the first half of 2026, after 61,000 sq. ft. in the prior half, with 290,000 sq. ft. under construction at midyear after deliveries of more than 550,000 sq. ft. in the half; the average direct asking rent was $10.74 per sq. ft. NNN, down 1.4% since year-end 2025 4. Matthews reads a narrower universe, industrial and flex properties of 5,000 to 200,000 square feet, from CoStar data: its second-quarter 2026 average sale price was $194/SF, an all-time market peak, with 391,462 SF under construction and no construction starts in the quarter 5. The two reports measure different sets of buildings, so their construction totals are not one series; a study for a small-bay or flex project cites Matthews for its product and CBRE for the market as a whole.

Office rents fell. CBRE puts the average direct asking rent at $17.67 per sq. ft. NNN in the first half of 2026, down 4.3%, with Class A at $21.14 42. Total office vacancy was 10.4% and net absorption -8,000 sq. ft. in the half, and the last office project was delivered in the second half of 2021 42. A medical office or owner-user office purchase here underwrites rent from the asking figure and vacancy from the building's own leases.

Visit Colorado Springs counted 25.6 million visitors to the Pikes Peak region in 2025 and $3.1 billion of visitor spending 43; lodging occupancy and average rate were not printed. Under FEMA's September 15, 2026 release the city has more than $8 million of mitigation funding for Cottonwood Creek floodplain restoration and more than $2.6 million of Public Assistance for drainage and embankment repairs 36. A site along Cottonwood Creek dates its flood assumption to the restoration's schedule.

BEA's 2023 GDP for the metro, in a FRED series now marked discontinued, is 53,092.641 (millions of dollars) 44; it describes a year three years past and a study uses it only as a scale reference. Employment and announced projects at the military installations, airport passenger counts, retail and self-storage broker reports, and permit counts from the Pikes Peak Regional Building Department were not found.

Fort Collins and Northern Colorado

Fort Collins-Loveland and Larimer County: a wage jump in early 2026, and broker figures for a wider north.

Larimer County's average weekly wage was $1,627 in the first quarter of 2026, up 11.5% over the year, the largest wage increase among the nine large Colorado counties in BLS's release 2. Its covered employment was 167,827 in March 2026, down 0.4% 2. The Fort Collins-Loveland metro's mean hourly wage was $33.95 in May 2025 45. A preliminary jump of that size is checked against the revised release before a study builds a payroll budget on it, because one quarter's change in a county average can reflect a few large employers.

Housing: building permits in Larimer County authorized 2,436 new private units in 2025 46, and the Fort Collins metro's median listing price was $567,500 in September 2026 47. A current multifamily report for Fort Collins was not found; the only broker apartment report located was from 2024 and is not used.

The commercial figures below are published for Northern Colorado, a region that covers the Fort Collins, Loveland and Greeley submarkets, and they are labelled that way. CBRE's office figures for the first half of 2026 give a direct average asking rate of $16.81 per sq. ft. NNN, flat on the prior period, at a total vacancy of 8.1%, with no completions in the half 48. Its industrial figures give net absorption of 189,000 sq. ft. excluding one large new distribution facility, which by itself supplied two-thirds of the vacancy decline since year-end 2025, with approximately 80,000 sq. ft. under construction 49. Matthews' small-bay reading for the first quarter of 2026 gives an asking rent of $14.30/SF, up 2.0% over the year, with 446,965 SF under construction and a vacancy of 9.5% 50.

The industrial reading carries a caution a lender will see. One building moved the regional vacancy rate, so a study for a flex or small-bay project uses the absorption figure that excludes it and Matthews' small-bay series for its own product, not the headline vacancy change. BEA's 2023 GDP for the Fort Collins metro, 26,974.178 (millions of dollars) in a FRED series now marked discontinued 51, is a scale reference only. Permit and certificate of occupancy reports from Fort Collins, Loveland and Larimer County, hotel and tourism figures and airport passenger counts were not found.

Boulder

Boulder County: high wages, high listing prices and a cluster of FEMA mitigation projects.

Boulder County's average weekly wage was $2,107 in the first quarter of 2026, up 6.6% over the year, and its covered employment 185,013 in March 2026, down 1.0% 2. The Boulder metro's mean hourly wage was $44.12 in May 2025 52. The county is not one of the five on the Denver page; its labor market and its prices are read here.

Ownership costs match the wages. The Boulder metro's median listing price was $769,975 in September 2026, with 1,452 active listings 5354. Census permit data counted 2,001 new private housing units authorized in Boulder County in 2025 55. A median of that level says little about the depth of demand at any one price, so a for-sale study in Boulder tests absorption at its own price point, and a rental study sets its rents against the county's wages rather than against the listing median.

Hazard work is concentrated here. FEMA's September 15, 2026 release lists, under the Hazard Mitigation Grant Program, more than $13.9 million for Longmont's Resilient St. Vrain project, more than $14 million for Coal Creek channel restoration and flood control in Erie, more than $2.4 million for the Prince Lake No. 1 Dam and more than $1.7 million for wildfire risk reduction in Superior, and more than $1 million of Public Assistance for Boulder County roads, culverts, embankments, park buildings and parking lots 36. A site along the St. Vrain or Coal Creek reads its flood exposure against the project's completion date, not against today's map alone.

BEA's 2023 GDP for the Boulder metro was 37,662.897 (millions of dollars), in a FRED series now marked discontinued 56. No 2026 broker report for Boulder office, industrial, apartments or retail was found, and neither were city or county permit reports, local zoning, height or impact fee changes with effective dates, or a dated public account of the Marshall Fire rebuild. A Boulder study builds its supply count from the city's and the county's own records.

Greeley

Greeley and Weld County: wages up, jobs slightly down and a steady flow of housing permits.

Weld County had 122,224 covered jobs in March 2026, down 0.3% over the year, at an average weekly wage of $1,446 in the first quarter, up 5.3% 2. The Greeley metro's mean hourly wage was $32.11 in May 2025 57. The Greeley metro is Weld County alone, which runs north to Wyoming and east to Nebraska; a site near either line may draw customers from across it, and the figures here stop at the Colorado border.

Building permits in Weld County authorized 2,993 new private housing units in 2025 58, against 2,436 in Larimer County 46. In September 2026 the Greeley metro's median listing price was $499,999, with 1,608 active listings 59. A for-sale or build-to-rent study in Weld reads its pricing against that median and against the new supply the permits imply, because buyers choosing between new and existing homes see both.

The Northern Colorado broker figures in the Fort Collins section include Greeley submarkets, but they are regional and are not printed again here. Greeley-specific office, industrial and retail reports were not found, nor were Weld County oil and gas production data, meatpacking or agribusiness plant announcements with job counts, or Greeley zoning and impact fee changes. FEMA's September 15, 2026 release lists more than $1.8 million for stormwater drainage in Kersey, a Weld County town 36. BEA's 2023 GDP for the Greeley metro was 28,181.144 (millions of dollars), in a FRED series now marked discontinued 60.

Grand Junction

Grand Junction and Mesa County: a Western Slope county on the Utah line, read on wages and a short listing count.

Mesa County had 66,300 covered jobs in March 2026 and an average weekly wage of $1,178 in the first quarter, with no over-the-year change published for the county 2. The Grand Junction metro's mean hourly wage was $30.08 in May 2025 64. In September 2026 the metro had 988 listings active 65. Mesa County runs to the Utah line. With 988 listings active 65, the for-sale market is thin enough that a small new subdivision can change local supply, and a study counts the competing lots and homes directly.

The western flooding of 2025 that FEMA declined to fund damaged roads and infrastructure on this side of the state 9. Broker reports, Grand Junction and Mesa County permit reports, Grand Junction Regional Airport passenger counts, energy, agriculture and tourism employer figures with dates, and local rule changes were not found.

Mountain Resort Counties

Summit, Eagle, Pitkin and Routt counties: payrolls set by the ski season and few new homes.

The four resort counties are read together because their projects share a pattern: visitor-driven demand, staff who need somewhere to live, and insurance that prices wildfire as well as hail. BLS's first-quarter 2026 county file, which covers the ski season, counts 35,814 covered jobs in Eagle County in March at an average weekly wage of $1,316, 19,181 in Pitkin County at $1,623 and 23,889 in Summit County 2. Routt County's average weekly wage was $1,248 2, and Summit's was $1,127 with Routt's covered employment at 17,128 2. No over-the-year change is published for these counties.

Those wages are a winter quarter's. A hotel, restaurant or lodge budget in a resort county carries a payroll that swings with the season, so a study sets labor cost by month, not from one quarter's average. Housing for the workers is the other question. Census data put new private housing authorized in 2025 at 318 units in Summit County and 223 units in Eagle County 1011. A project that needs staff in those counties shows where they will live.

The Division of Housing's update of August 26, 2026 reported a petition from the Town of Avon, in Eagle County, for rural resort income limits under C.R.S. 29-32-105.5 (2), with public comment from August 12 to September 11, 2026 30. Limits of that kind set which households a deed-restricted or Proposition 123 unit may serve, and a workforce housing study in a resort county reads the limits in force for its town.

Insurance is the third. KUNC, reporting the Division of Insurance's February 2026 report from the Steamboat Pilot, put wildfire at 21.9% of homeowners premium costs in Routt County and hail at 30.6% 67. For a lodge, cabin or condominium project the wildfire premium is quoted for the address, and the defensible space and construction class the insurer requires go into the budget.

Resort-county hotel occupancy, average rate and lodging tax collections, Vail Resorts and Aspen Skiing season results, airport passengers for Eagle, Aspen and the Yampa Valley, county short-term rental ordinances, deed restriction rules with effective dates and county listing prices were not found. A resort study sources lodging performance from a paid report or the operator, and short-term rental rules from the county and town codes for the site.

SBA 7(a) and 504

The governing procedure is SOP 50 10 8.1, effective October 1, 2026 68. Under it, a standard 7(a) loan has to clear a debt service coverage of 1.15x, and a 504 borrower in business two years or less, or buying special-purpose property, puts in 15% equity, rising to 20% when it is both 68. For studies, the governing text is 13 CFR 120.160(b): SBA may require professional appraisals, a survey, or a feasibility study 69. A new resort-county lodge, a Colorado Springs flex building for a startup operator or a child care center in Weld County is the kind of project where a lender asks for one. For those tests, see SBA 7(a) and 504 lending.

One Colorado lender's year shows the scale of community lending. The Colorado Enterprise Fund, a CDFI, reported deploying $18,085,922 to small businesses in its fiscal year ended September 30, 2025, including 46 loans, $7,829,700 under SBA 7(a), and more than $442 million to over 9,800 businesses since 1976 70. These are one lender's figures, not a state total.

USDA Rural Development

Whether a Colorado site can use USDA's business and community programs is set by the program's eligibility map for the exact address, and it is checked there rather than assumed from a county name; a town in a metro county can qualify and a resort town in a rural county may not. Business and Industry, Community Facilities and REAP activity in Colorado for fiscal 2025 was not found. How the programs test a project is on the USDA Rural Development page.

A bank or life company loan on a Colorado apartment, industrial or hotel asset is underwritten on the broker and county figures in the market sections, and on the legal changes in the rules section, which a credit committee will ask about by name: the parking prohibition near transit, the HOME Act's approval dates, the assessment rate for the asset's class. Colorado Questions

Seven things a sponsor asks before commissioning a study in Colorado.

Is Colorado still gaining residents from other states?

Not in the latest Census year. Between July 2024 and June 2025, 12,100 more people left Colorado for other states than came from them; net international migration was +15,356 and total net migration +3,256, in the State Demography Office's summary of Vintage 2025 1. A demand model for housing, child care or neighborhood retail reads those components, not only the 6,012,561 statewide total 1.

Which loan programs does the study serve, and what starts a Colorado engagement?

SBA 7(a) and 504 lending, USDA Rural Development programs, EB-5 and conventional bank or life company loans; for each the study is independent and lender-grade. An engagement starts from a description of the project, the county or market section where the site sits, and the capital source the sponsor is approaching, and the reply names the framework the study will use, the deliverable that program calls for and what follows.

What did the Colorado Springs industrial market do in the first half of 2026?

CBRE counted 357,000 sq. ft. of net absorption in the half year, 290,000 sq. ft. under construction at midyear and an average direct asking rent of $10.74 per sq. ft. NNN 4. Matthews, reading only properties of 5,000 to 200,000 square feet from CoStar data, put the second-quarter average sale price at $194/SF, a market peak 5. The two describe different sets of buildings and are cited separately.

By when must a qualifying Colorado parcel get administrative approval under the HOME Act?

Signed and effective on March 25, 2026, it covers qualifying properties of up to 5 acres, bars subject jurisdictions from refusing residential development under 3 stories or 38 feet on height grounds, and requires administrative approval of qualifying residential development after December 31, 2027, or June 30, 2028 where a jurisdiction is updating its code 6. A study for a qualifying parcel dates its entitlement path against those two deadlines.

What does Colorado's record 2025 tourism year mean for a hotel or restaurant study?

Two figures that moved apart. Travel spending reached $29.2 billion in 2025, a record 3, and visitors 96.8 million, up 1.4%, yet tourism-supported jobs were 187,860, down 0.6%, in the Colorado Tourism Office research SnowBrains reported 7. A hotel or restaurant study reads the spending and the staffing figures together: the first sizes revenue, the second says what a visitor-serving payroll looked like in the record year.

Is federal disaster money reaching Colorado sites after the 2026 fires and floods?

Some is moving. On September 28, 2026 FEMA approved more than $784,000 of initial Public Assistance funding for the state to manage the program after the Aspen Acres Fire and other wildfires and flooding 8. That money runs the program; it is not aid to a private site. For the 2025 Lee and Elk fires FEMA refused aid and then denied the appeal 9.

What job and housing figures exist for the mountain resort counties?

BLS's first-quarter 2026 county file counts 35,814 covered jobs in Eagle County at an average of $1,316 a week, 19,181 in Pitkin County at $1,623 and 23,889 in Summit County 2. Census permit data put 2025 authorizations at 318 units in Summit County and 223 units in Eagle County 1011.
