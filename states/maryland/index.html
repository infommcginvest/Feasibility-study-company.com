Maryland Feasibility Studies

Federal employees filed 10,256 initial unemployment insurance claims in Maryland between January 19, 2025 and September 12, 2026, in the count Towson University's Regional Economic Studies Institute drew from Maryland Department of Labor data and published on September 18, 2026 1. Commerce's 2025.2 profiles put the National Institutes of Health first and Naval Support Activity Bethesda second in Montgomery County, NASA Goddard and Joint Base Andrews near the top of Prince George's list, and Fort Meade on Anne Arundel's 234. Those profiles count staff for 2023 to 2025 and the claims run to September 2026, so where a project's customers come from one of those campuses, its headcount reaches the Maryland feasibility study with the survey years attached, and the claims count is the later record.

Federal Campuses

Several of Maryland's county employer lists begin with a federal agency or base.

The National Institutes of Health is first on Montgomery County's employer list in Commerce's 2025.2 profile 2, Fort Detrick is first on Frederick County's 10 and Naval Air Station Patuxent River first on St. Mary's 18. Prince George's list carries NASA Goddard, Joint Base Andrews and the Census Bureau 3, Anne Arundel's carries Fort Meade and the Naval Academy 4, and Charles County's carries Naval Support Facility Indian Head 19.

The dates are what a reviewer checks. The profiles count staff for 2023 to 2024 in Montgomery, Prince George's and Frederick, for 2023 to 2025 in Anne Arundel and for 2024 to 2025 in Charles and St. Mary's 231041918. Federal employees' jobless claims in the state are counted from January 19, 2025 1. A headcount from a profile therefore dates from before the claims period or from its first months, and the claims series is the dated record that runs after it.

Baltimore runs on a different anchor. The city's largest employers are the Johns Hopkins hospital system and university and the University of Maryland Medical System 20, and the November 2025 study of Hopkins' economic impact named federal funding cuts as a risk to it 21.

Statewide Economy

Who arrived, who left and who lost a federal job: Maryland's statewide counts.

Towson University's Regional Economic Studies Institute counted 10,256 initial unemployment claims filed by federal employees in Maryland from January 19, 2025 to September 12, 2026, working from Maryland Department of Labor data, in its report of September 18, 2026 1. The same report has federal government jobs in the state 600 lower in August 2026 than in July and the August unemployment rate at 4.1% 1.

Across all employers, BLS's seasonally adjusted count stood at 2,806,200 nonfarm jobs in August 2026, a preliminary figure, and at 2,811,700 in August 2025, a change of -5,500 over the twelve months 22. No twelve-month change by sector was found for the state.

The Census Bureau's Vintage 2025 estimate puts Maryland at 6,265,347 residents on July 1, 2025, beside an estimates base of 6,181,640 for April 1, 2020 and a 2020 Census count of 6,177,224; QuickFacts shows the change from the base as +1.4% 5. The Department of Planning's international migration table for the same vintage has net arrivals from abroad of 47,375 in the year to June 30, 2024, 20,503 in the year to June 30, 2025 and 159,113 from April 2020 to June 2025 6. Its domestic table, issued in January 2026, has net domestic migration of -18,367 and -12,127 in those two years and -134,770 over the whole span 23.

Maryland had 696,710 small businesses in the June 2025 state profile of SBA's Office of Advocacy, employing 1.2 million people, 47.9% of the state's employees 24. Johns Hopkins reported 56,491 jobs in Maryland for fiscal 2022 in an impact report published in April 2023 25, and a study reported in November 2025 put the institution's economic impact on the state at $40 billion 21.

Montgomery County

Montgomery County: NIH, the Navy at Bethesda and a permit count that fell in 2025.

One Maryland county of the Washington metro, read without the District, Virginia or West Virginia.

Commerce's 2025.2 profile of the county lists the National Institutes of Health with 17,535 employees and Naval Support Activity Bethesda with 10,204 for 2023 to 2024, then the FDA at 8,500, Giant Food at 4,354 and Adventist HealthCare at 3,675 2. GEICO's 3,505 employees and the 3,500 each at Marriott International and AstraZeneca come from the same profile, which also counts 2,070 staff at Holy Cross Hospital, 1,887 at Suburban Hospital and an enrollment of 19,972 students at Montgomery College, and names the Universities at Shady Grove among the county's anchors 2.

The profile puts federal jobs in the county at 48,586 in 2024, beside covered employment of 462,298 2. WorkSource Montgomery's overview of September 2025, on Lightcast's modelled series, gives the county 548,863 jobs in 2019 and 535,343 in 2024 27. A twelve-month payroll change for the county alone was not found.

Planning's Vintage 2025 table counts 1,074,582 residents on July 1, 2025 against a 2020 estimates base of 1,062,079 11. Builders took permits for 1,411 housing units in 2025 after 3,656 in 2024, on the Census Bureau's permit survey as FRED carries it 28. The county profile reprints the 2020 counts of its largest places: Germantown 91,249, Silver Spring 81,015, Gaithersburg 69,657, Bethesda 68,056 and Rockville 67,117 2.

For fiscal 2025 the Department of Legislative Services lists Montgomery's impact tax on a single-family detached house at $26,084 for schools and $30,190 for transportation at the maximum, a red-area transportation rate of $9,663, and nonresidential rates of $7.85 to $24.60 a square foot for retail and $4.45 to $13.85 for industrial space 17. The real property tax rate in the profile is $0.6700 per $100 2, and since July 1, 2026 the county minimum wage has three tiers: $18.00 an hour at 51 or more employees, $16.50 at 11 to 50 and $15.95 at 10 or fewer 29.

Montgomery's median gross rent, for renters paying cash, stood at $2,068 over the 2020 to 2024 American Community Survey 30. SBA loans here go through the Washington Metropolitan Area District Office rather than the Baltimore office 7.

It lists Woodmore Towne Centre, National Harbor and Towne Square at Suitland Federal Center, the last under construction 3.

Planning's table counts 970,374 residents on July 1, 2025 and 967,231 in the 2020 base, a change of +0.3% 11. Permits covered 2,090 housing units in 2025 and 1,473 in 2024 31. The median owner-occupied home in Prince George's was valued at $426,000 over the 2020 to 2024 ACS survey years 30.

The county charges school and public safety surcharges. Outside the Beltway they were $20,500 and $9,680 in fiscal 2025 17 and $20,972 and $9,903 in fiscal 2026 32; inside the Beltway and near transit the fiscal 2025 rates were $11,950 and $3,229 33. The county minimum wage has been $15.30 an hour since July 1, 2026 29.

Capital Access

SBA lending in Maryland splits at the Montgomery and Prince George's county lines.

Two SBA district offices divide the state. The Baltimore District Office covers Baltimore City and all of Maryland's other counties apart from Montgomery and Prince George's, which fall to the Washington Metropolitan Area District Office 7. A Baltimore District total is therefore not a Maryland total.

M&T Bank ranked first in the Baltimore District for fiscal 2025, which ended September 30, 2025, with 427 loans under 7(a) for $50.7 million, by its own release of November 19, 2025 8. For fiscal 2024 an M&T release relayed by Meadowlands Media gave 344 loans and $40.1 million in the same district 45. The Baltimore Business Journal's ranking, as Hoodline relayed it in May 2026, put SBA lending in Greater Baltimore in calendar 2025 at more than $237 million across 77 lenders, with M&T at the head 46.

Statewide, SBABroker.ai's tabulation of fiscal 2025 through July 22, 2025, published on July 25, 2025, counts 1,219 loans under 7(a) for $405,883,100 in Maryland 47. By dollars its lender list runs M&T at 447 loans and $52,415,900, Newtek Bank at 71 and $28,692,000, Northeast Bank at 147 and $26,278,600, Live Oak Banking Company at 26 and $25,203,000 and Readycap Lending at 66 and $22,229,300 47. It is a partial year from a secondary source, on a cut-off of its own.

For 504, SBA Loan Index's count from fiscal 2010 to March 31, 2026 gives Business Finance Group 554 Maryland loans, Chesapeake Business Finance Corp. 154 loans and Prince George's Financial Services Corp. 27 48; an official ranking of development companies for fiscal 2025 was not found. SBA's own loan-level files hold 8,050 loans under 7(a) and 311 loans under 504 for Maryland projects approved in fiscal 2016 through 2025, net of cancellations, read at June 30, 2026 37.

State credit runs through the Department of Commerce. Its financing authority, MSBDFA, offers contract financing, a long-term guaranty, surety bonding, equity participation and SSBCI programs, managed by Meridian Management Group, and the DECADE Act of 2026 (HB 898 and SB 388) restructured it from July 1, 2026 as the Maryland Small Business Development Financing Fund 49. The same Commerce menu lists Advantage Maryland, a Child Care Capital Support Revolving Loan Fund, a New Start Microloan, the Strategic Closing Fund and a Cannabis Business Assistance Fund among its other credit programs 49. One aggregator's 2026 page prints two different calendar 2025 totals for Maryland's 7(a) lending, so neither appears here.

SBA Rules

The two SBA texts a Maryland 7(a) or 504 file is read against.

For an SBA loan, the agency's authority to ask for a study is 13 CFR 120.160(b): SBA may require professional appraisals, a survey, or a feasibility study 50, and the program tests sit in SOP 50 10 8.1 with Technical Policy Updates 51. Those tests are the same in every state, and our SBA 7(a) and 504 practice page explains them; in Maryland the variable is which district office a county answers to.

For owner-occupied housing the Tax Foundation's 2026 table gives Maryland an effective property tax rate of 0.92%, 21st among the states, on 2024 data 59, and its 2026 competitiveness index places the state 36th on property tax 56. No statewide effective rate for commercial property was found.

The Maryland Joint Insurance Association writes homeowners, dwelling and commercial property cover for risks that cannot obtain it in the voluntary market, the Maryland Insurance Administration's advisory explains 60. Briefing papers given to the House Environment and Transportation Committee in 2025 describe mobile-home owners in a resort area who could not sell their homes, since a buyer's lender needed cover the buyer could not get, and an update to the association's policy that allows dwelling policies 61. No approved commercial property rate change or carrier withdrawal for 2025 or 2026 was found.

Rules by Asset Class

Maryland's 2025 assisted living rules, two Certificate of Need bills and a closed tank fund.

Maryland replaced its assisted living regulations in 2025. The new COMAR 10.07.14 was published as final in the Maryland Register on April 18 and took effect on April 28 13. It licenses three levels, Level 1 for low, Level 2 for moderate and Level 3 for high care needs, and a program may serve residents at its licensed level or below 14. The Office of Health Care Quality's FAQ of October 21, 2025 sets no minimum number of residents for a licence and asks for 60 days' notice of a licence change in programs of 49 or fewer beds and 75 days' in programs of 50 or more 15. No separate memory care or Alzheimer's unit category was found.

Certificate of Need, administered by the Maryland Health Care Commission under Health-General section 19-120, reaches the building or establishment of a new health care facility, its relocation, a change in its type, scope of service or bed capacity, and capital spending above the review threshold; the 2025 fiscal note on HB 735 lists home health, hospice and freestanding ambulatory surgical facilities among the establishments it covers, and nursing home, residential treatment, psychiatric and rehabilitation services among the expansions 62. Two 2026 bills would narrow it: HB 632 would exempt psychiatric services 63, and HB 498, the successor to a 2025 bill the Commission supported, would replace full review for ASAM Level 3.7 intermediate care facilities with a 45-day notice and Commission approval 64. Enactment of either was not found.

The state's Oil Contaminated Site Environmental Cleanup Fund no longer pays commercial tank claims, whose statutory application deadline was December 31, 2007, and its residual residential heating oil program is listed as closed; the fiscal 2024 annual report, published on April 2, 2025, records 354 residential applications reimbursed that year 16. The fiscal 2025 report followed on February 12, 2026 65. The cleanup funds are paid for by an oil transfer fee of 5.0 cents a barrel since July 1, 2021, down from 8.0 cents 66.

Hazards

Westernport, Luke and Barton in May 2025: the damage FEMA validated and the declaration it did not grant.

More than 6 inches of rain fell in just over a day on May 13 and 14, 2025, flooding Westernport, Luke and Barton, as the Governor's request for a presidential declaration of June 11, 2025 records 69. FEMA validated more than $33.7 million of damage from the Allegany and Garrett flooding 70. The state asked for a major disaster declaration on June 13, 2025, was refused on July 22, appealed on August 19 and was told on October 22, 2025 that the appeal had failed, so no FEMA Public Assistance came 7071.

The Public Assistance thresholds in MDEM's appeal release were $321,460 for Allegany County and $11,674,953 statewide 71. The State Disaster Recovery Fund sent $459,375 to Allegany County, the Maryland Association of Counties reported on August 19, 2025 72. No federal major disaster declaration granted to Maryland in 2025 or 2026 was found.

Maryland Questions

Questions a Maryland loan committee raises, with the record that answers each.

Which risk did the November 2025 study of Johns Hopkins' economic impact name?

Federal funding cuts. The study, reported in the Maryland Daily Record on November 12, 2025, put Johns Hopkins' economic impact on the state at $40 billion and on Baltimore City at $19.4 billion 21. The Johns Hopkins Hospital and Health System and Johns Hopkins University are the first two employers on the city's 2025.2 Commerce profile 20, and the institution's own report of April 2023 counted 56,491 Hopkins jobs in Maryland for fiscal 2022 25.

Did the BGE zone hit PJM's capacity price cap for 2025/26?

Yes. For the 2025/26 delivery year the zone's price was its cap, $466.35 per megawatt-day, where 2024/25 had cleared at $73.00; both figures are in the May 2025 report that Synapse Energy Economics wrote with the District of Columbia's People's Counsel, whose RTO-wide 2025/26 price of $269.92 is PJM's and not Maryland's 9. The 2026/27 and 2027/28 auctions also cleared at their cap, $329.17 and $333.44, both PJM-wide prices 54, and on March 20, 2026 the Governor's Office backed PJM's request to FERC to keep the price collar through 2028/29 and 2029/30 55.

Is a federal headcount in a Commerce county profile current for a 2026 study?

It carries the profile's survey years, not 2026. Frederick County's 2025.2 profile gives Fort Detrick 10,776 employees for 2023 to 2024 10, while the federal employee jobless claims Towson's RESI counted began on January 19, 2025 1. A study that leans on an agency's payroll quotes both and says which year each describes.

Which Frederick County housing permits does the county's own 2025 report leave out?

Those its municipalities issue. The Annual Planning Report 2025 counts 371 units for 2025, permits the county issued itself, 21% below 2024 12, while the Census Bureau's countywide total for 2024 was 1,562 units 39; no countywide 2025 count was found. The county's population reached 302,883 on July 1, 2025 from a 2020 base of 271,710, +11.5%, in Planning's Vintage 2025 table 11.

Does a Maryland assisted living program need a minimum number of residents to be licensed?

No. The Health Department's Office of Health Care Quality says so in its FAQ of October 21, 2025 on the new COMAR 10.07.14; it requires notice before a licence changes, 60 days at 49 or fewer beds and 75 days at 50 or more 15. The regulation's final text appeared in the Maryland Register on April 18, 2025 and it has applied since April 28 13; it licenses Level 1, Level 2 and Level 3 care, and a licensed program may take residents at its level or lower 14. No separate memory care category was found.

When did Maryland's Oil Contaminated Site Environmental Cleanup Fund close to commercial tank claims?

At its statutory deadline of December 31, 2007; the residual program for home heating oil tanks is listed as closed too. MDE's report for fiscal 2024, dated April 2, 2025, puts residential reimbursements that year at 354 applications 16, and its fiscal 2025 report is dated February 12, 2026 65. The fund's income is an oil transfer fee, 5.0 cents a barrel since July 1, 2021, reduced from 8.0 cents 66.

Do Baltimore City and the Lower Eastern Shore charge a county impact fee?

No. The Department of Legislative Services' fiscal 2025 schedules list none for Baltimore City or for the Lower Eastern Shore counties 17. In the same report, Montgomery's highest single-family detached impact tax has a schools part of $26,084 and a transportation part of $30,190 17.
