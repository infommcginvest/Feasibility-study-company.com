Wisconsin Feasibility Studies

Two local bodies weigh heavily in a Wisconsin feasibility study. The county carries the demand case, because the 5,972,787 residents counted on July 1, 2025 3 split into counties losing people and counties gaining them; the village, city or county board carries the rules, and in 2025 and 2026 such boards turned away, paused or sent to referendum data-center campuses, beside the at least $36.3 billion of projects CROWE counts as running, being built or committed as of July 2026 2. The study then names the county that supplies the customers and the board that sets the terms, and carries the date of each one's latest decision.

The Wisconsin Read

A Wisconsin file is decided in its county and dated by its town hall.

The state total grew slowly in 2025 while its counties split, and several of the decisions that moved projects in 2025 and 2026 were made by a village, a city or a county, each with a date a study has to respect.

The July 1, 2025 estimate counted 5,972,787 Wisconsin residents 3, but no lender underwrites that total. The county figures further down run from a loss in Milwaukee County to steady growth in Dane County, so the population and household evidence in a Wisconsin study belongs to the counties its trade area covers, with the Census vintage named.

The make-up of growth changed as well. Net moves in from other states rose to 6,984 in the year to July 1, 2025, from 5,828, while net arrivals from abroad dropped to 7,260 from 19,395 1. The research does not say which counties lost those arrivals, one more reason a county's own series, not the state's rate scaled down, carries the demand case.

The statewide force is electricity. CROWE, the UW-Madison economics center, counts at least $36.3 billion of data-center projects that were running, being built or committed, giving each project's status as of July 2026 2, and its report carries the Public Service Commission's draft forecast tracing roughly 72% of the expected growth in statewide peak demand through 2032 to three very large data centers 2. Through utility rates, part of the cost of serving that load may reach customers far from any campus, so the costs section treats power as a sensitivity. The campuses themselves were decided locally: the village of DeForest turned one away, Janesville set a referendum, and Marathon County paused new ones 26.

Statewide Economy

Payrolls, households and income: what the statewide figures can carry.

Wisconsin's payrolls grew between August 2025 and August 2026, with construction and services up and trade down. That split decides which demand cases a statewide figure can support.

Preliminary seasonally adjusted figures show payrolls 19,400 higher than a year before and 11,800 higher than in July, with unemployment at 3.2% 4. Both are statewide totals, and the sections that follow narrow them to sectors and markets.

Construction was the main gain 4. It came while the data-center and utility work described below was under way, though the research does not tie the hiring to particular projects. For a project breaking ground in 2026 or 2027 it bears on bid prices and schedule, and the development budget should carry a contingency sized to it.

Trade moved the other way: of its loss, 7,500 jobs were in retail and 3,800 in wholesale 4. Neither that loss nor the 2024 per capita personal income BEA reports for Wisconsin, $67,755 7, says what a particular trade area spends, since the income figure averages every county from Milwaukee to Vilas. A retail center, grocery or convenience store study rests on its own rooftops, traffic and competitors.

The service side is stronger. Health care and social assistance added 7,800 jobs, and accommodation and food services added 8,100 4. The health care and social assistance gain supports clinic, outpatient and senior housing demand in the places it happens; the hospitality gain is a hiring signal that restaurant and lodging cases can cite.

It ranks revenue, not staff, and the research found no ranked headcount list, so a demand case resting on one company's payroll needs a count from that company.

Capital Access

SBA counts, a Madison development company and the state's own credit tools.

The SBA totals below come from three different releases, none of them an official FY2025 count. Beside them sit one Madison development company and a set of state programs.

The newest SBA-reported statewide totals in the research are for FY2024: 1,099 7(a) loans for $524.9M and 173 504 loans for $188.3M, inside 1,342 loans across all programs that included 70 microloans 5. The FY2023 counts were 964 7(a) loans for $482.1M and 183 504 loans for $174.9M 10. For FY2025 the research found no SBA total: a lender's award release puts SBA-backed lending in Wisconsin above $757 million 11, and a secondary count built from FOIA records gives 1,128 7(a) approvals worth $551.1M in calendar 2025 12.

A longer window comes from SBA's loan-level file. Taking approvals in the ten federal fiscal years that ended in September 2025, and leaving out loans later cancelled, it holds 10,364 Wisconsin 7(a) loans and $1,692.6 million of 504 approvals 13. A study that wants to show SBA lending as routine in Wisconsin should cite these ten-year counts rather than any single year.

GoSBA's calendar 2025 tally, which ranks lenders by dollars, puts Old National Bank first with $59.6M on 54 loans; U.S. Bank follows at $28.1M on 116 loans, then Byline Bank ($27.2M on 26 loans), Huntington ($25.1M on 73) and Live Oak ($23.2M on 24) 12. The same tally names hotels and motels as the most-funded 7(a) industry in Wisconsin in 2025 12.

WBD, Inc., a certified development company based in Madison, made 152 of the state's 183 504 loans in FY2023, for $139,309,000 10, and its own FY2025 annual report counts 184 approvals for $190,993,000 14. SBA's FY2023 story names Business Lending Partners of Racine as a second CDC 10.

Treasury approved up to $79.1M of SSBCI 2.0 money for Wisconsin, spread over seven programs in capital access, collateral support, loan participation, a debt and equity hybrid and venture equity 15. WHEDA's announcement split it into a $50M venture fund (the Wisconsin Investment Fund), an $8M WEDC Technology Development Loan, $6M for WEDC's Capital Catalyst and $15M of WHEDA credit support for CDFI microloans 16. In FY2025 Capital Catalyst had $3,600,000 available, $1,250,000 of it from WEDC and $2,350,000 from SSBCI 17.

The Public Finance Authority, created under Wis. Stat. s. 66.0304, issues tax-exempt, taxable and tax-credit conduit bonds 18. In April 2026 The Bond Buyer reported that the authority's asset-ownership program held 9 credits with roughly $1.2B of debt outstanding, a figure the research did not verify elsewhere 19. The paper's own headline calls the program troubled, so a lender looking at a deal financed through the authority should test the study's demand case harder rather than take the conduit's name as comfort.

SBA 7(a) and 504

Lodging, funeral homes and the Wisconsin SBA file.

Hotels and motels topped one tally of Wisconsin's 2025 7(a) dollars, and in FY2023 one Madison development company made most of the state's CDC debenture loans. Those facts decide which SBA tests this section takes up.

Given WBD's share of the FY2023 504 count shown above, a Wisconsin 504 study will often be read by that one development company as well as by the bank holding the first lien.

The coverage test depends on what is being bought. A tavern is not on SBA's example list of special purpose properties, so its buyer meets the change-of-ownership floor of 1.25x for an initial acquisition on the business's past earnings, under the SOP's 7(a) change-of-ownership rules 20. Hotels and motels, and funeral homes with crematoriums, are on that list 20. A buyer who will own and occupy one of them, at a price fully supported by the appraised value, can meet the same floor on projections within 2 years 20, so for a hotel, motel or funeral home purchase the study's forecast can carry the coverage test.

Building one instead moves the weight to equity. A 504 borrower with two years of operating history or less that puts up a special purpose building, a Vilas County motel or a waterpark wing near Lake Delton, contributes 20% 20, and the study must show the cushion lasting through the slow months of a seasonal lake or resort trade.

Wisconsin's own seasoned loans diverge by program: of loans approved in fiscal 2011 through 2020, 5.0% of 7(a) loans and 1.1% of 504 loans ended in charge-off 13. The rates count loans, so a small equipment loan and a large hotel loan weigh the same; they say how often Wisconsin SBA credits failed, not what share of a lender's dollars it should expect to lose on the next one.

Applications SBA receives from October 1, 2026 fall under SOP 50 10 8.1 20. Whether a given file needs a study at all goes back to 13 CFR 120.160(b), under which SBA may ask for one 21; our research note on that paragraph quotes it in full. Fees, the other equity tiers and the remaining coverage tests are general program rules, gathered on the 7(a) and 504 practice page.

SBA's newest Wisconsin award list in the research covers FY2021, when U.S. Bank made the most 7(a) loans and Byline Bank the most 7(a) dollars 22; Ixonia Bank took the 2025 Wisconsin SBA volume lender award 11. USDA Rural Development

USDA Business and Industry for Northwoods and Dells projects.

Lake resorts, campgrounds and small-town food plants are the Wisconsin projects on this page that fit the guarantee's rural purpose, and the research holds no Wisconsin figures for the program.

For a Northwoods resort, a Dells campground or a food processor in a small town, the Business and Industry guarantee from Rural Development offers a federal alternative to SBA, open only where the parcel counts as rural. A study prepared for one channel can serve the other only if it already carries that parcel status, which is the first open question in the verification list that closes the page.

What the research could not supply is any Wisconsin record of the program: no state office details, no FY2025 Business and Industry obligations and nothing on Community Facilities or REAP activity. The research note applying USDA's five study components lists what such a study contains, the Rural Development deadline calendar tracks the agency's dates, and the USDA practice page describes the program in general.

Agency, Bonds and EB-5

Agency, bond and EB-5 capital on Wisconsin projects.

Agency lenders meet two very different apartment markets on this page; the bond channel runs through WHEDA and a conduit authority; EB-5 was outside the research.

Wisconsin's two large apartment markets argue in opposite directions for a Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac or construction lender. Milwaukee's broker readings put the metro close to full while Dane County's pipeline is still delivering, so a Milwaukee file has to defend its rents against local incomes and a Madison file its lease-up schedule. The research did not identify HUD's multifamily office for the state or any agency loan volume, which leaves the market study as the main local evidence in an agency file; the practice page on agency and bank construction studies describes what those lenders read.

On the bond side, WHEDA allocates part of the state's private activity bond volume cap 23, and WEDC administers state money such as the Capital Catalyst and Technology Development Loan programs 16; WEDC also certifies the data centers that qualify for the equipment exemption discussed under costs 2. The Public Finance Authority described under capital access is a third route, open to projects in any state.

No Wisconsin EB-5 evidence was gathered, so this page cannot say whether a site sits in a targeted employment area or name a regional center. The EB-5 practice page and the note on counting EB-5 jobs cover the method a sponsor's study would follow.

Rules by Asset Class

State rules that change a Wisconsin pro forma, asset by asset.

The statutes below set limits a pro forma has to respect. Rules the research did not capture are listed among the open questions at the end of the page.

Nursing homes and senior care

Wisconsin has no formal Certificate of Need program, but it keeps a statewide nursing-home bed limit under s. 150.31 and moratoria on new hospital, psychiatric and chemical-dependency, and nursing-home beds 24. A new nursing home needs state approval regardless of cost 25. CR 25-072, a Department of Health Services rule proposed in 2025 and 2026 and published in the 2026 Administrative Register, would repeal DHS 122.05(1)(c), the provision that keeps beds from closed homes from being redistributed 26; a study that counts on such beds should state where the rule stands on its own date. The licensing categories and bed thresholds for assisted living or memory care (DHS 83, 88 and 89) were not in the research; a study in that segment quotes the current rule text itself.

Restaurants, taverns and hotels with a bar

Class B liquor licenses are capped by a population quota 27. A restaurant or hotel study that counts alcohol revenue should show where its license will come from under the quota and run the revenue without alcohol as a sensitivity case.

Room taxes and whole-home rentals

Wisconsin levies no room tax of its own. A lodging bill carries sales tax at the state and county level plus any room tax the municipality sets under s. 66.0615 28. Under s. 66.1014 a local government may not prohibit rentals of seven consecutive days or more; for rentals of 7 to 29 days it may set a cap, but the cap cannot allow fewer than 180 days of such rentals in any 365. An operator renting more than ten nights a year needs a DATCP tourist rooming house license. A 2025 bill to repeal the core of that preemption, SB 1080, was not enacted 29. Lodging and campground studies should therefore count whole-home rentals of a week or longer in the competitive census as supply that local rules cannot remove.

Wages

Wisconsin's minimum is $7.25 an hour, unchanged since July 24, 2009, and s. 104.001 keeps cities from setting a higher one; the tipped cash wage is $2.33, and workers under 20 may be paid an opportunity wage of $5.90 for their first 90 days 30. Because of the preemption the legal floor is identical in Madison and in Vilas County, so what separates a staffing budget in one from the other is the market wage, which a hotel, restaurant, child care center or assisted living study takes from local postings or the operator's own payroll.

Fuel stations and dry cleaners

PECFA, the state petroleum cleanup fund, stopped taking new claims on June 30, 2020; no state fund reimburses new tank releases, and the dry cleaner fund sunsets on June 30, 2032 31. Environmental risk on a fuel station or c-store purchase therefore stays private, and the study should report tank age and condition, the Phase I findings and the premium for private pollution cover.

Costs, Power and Insurance

Electricity, exemptions and the residual market: costs a Wisconsin lender will test.

Data-center demand has turned utility rates and state tax policy into live questions for projects that use ordinary amounts of power.

Wisconsin's industrial electricity price averaged 8.54 cents per kWh in 2024, according to EIA data cited by CROWE 2. WEC Energy Group's capital plan for 2026 to 2030 is $37.5B 2. The draft state forecast puts peak demand at more than 20 GW by 2032 2.

The PSC approved a We Energies tariff on April 24, 2026 under which customers of 100 MW or more pay the full cost of dedicated generation, commit for at least fifteen years and post financial guarantees 2. On May 7, 2026 it approved Alliant's rates for the Meta campus at Beaver Dam, which carry a ten-year minimum, and called for a standard rate to govern the data-center customers that follow 32. Even so, CROWE reports transmission cost tied to data centers, $63M in 2027 and nearly $100M in 2028, as potentially borne by existing customers 2. A hotel, grocery, cold storage or manufacturing project served by We Energies or Alliant therefore has reason to carry a utility sensitivity rather than hold electricity flat across its projection.

State tax policy is moving with the campuses. A Legislative Fiscal Bureau memo of March 18, 2026, cited by CROWE, projected that the sales-tax exemption for equipment in WEDC-certified data centers would forgo $1.5B of state sales tax during construction and $369M per year once four certified projects operate, about 4.7% of state sales-tax collections 2. The Assembly passed a cost-shifting bill, AB 840, in January 2026, but it was not enacted, and the 2025 to 2026 session passed no data-center bills 33.

The August 2025 flood, in the next section, is reason enough to price insurance from a current quote rather than from an older budget. A commercial risk that standard carriers decline can go to the Wisconsin Insurance Plan, the state's residual market, whose lines include commercial property as well as homeowners and dwelling cover. Its producer guidelines, revised in January 2025, set $350,000 as the most it writes on a homeowners dwelling 34, a limit that matters where a borrower's house is pledged as collateral rather than for the business property itself.

Hazards

The August 2025 flood: what the record shows and what it leaves out.

The research documents one recent Wisconsin disaster in detail, the August 2025 storms around Milwaukee.

The Governor's request for a federal declaration recorded 14.55 inches of rain in Milwaukee County in the 24 hours of August 9, 2025 35. FEMA declared DR-4892 on September 11, 2025 for the storms of August 9 to 12, with Individual Assistance only, for Milwaukee, Waukesha and Washington counties 36.

A study whose site lies in or near the declared counties should state the parcel's flood zone and show flood cover in the operating budget. SBA's disaster notice added Dodge, Fond du Lac, Jefferson, Ozaukee, Racine, Sheboygan and Walworth as contiguous counties for economic injury loans 37, so for lending purposes the flood reaches well past the three declared counties.

No other Wisconsin hazard is measured in the research: it has no National Risk Index profile and no wind, seismic or wildfire exposure for the state, and it could not confirm whether storms in April 2026 led to a declaration. For a site outside the flood counties, the study supplies the hazard evidence itself, from the flood map panel and the site's own claims history.

Statewide Supply

Statewide supply: an industrial reading and a funeral home count.

The research found two supply measures above the market level, one regional and one statewide. Everything else this page says about supply comes from the market sections.

The one regional supply benchmark the research found is industrial. On CARW figures, Southeastern Wisconsin's industrial vacancy was 5.3% in Q2 2026 38. The region takes in the Milwaukee and Kenosha-Racine markets and more, so the figure frames a study rather than serving as a comparable.

County Business Patterns counted 543 funeral home establishments in Wisconsin in the 2023 data year 39. Funeral demand follows deaths in a service area, so a study in the segment turns on market share and the area's death count rather than on population growth.

For every other class this page touches, from Dells campgrounds to Milwaukee senior housing, the research holds no reading above the market level. The market sections show which places have local figures; where none exist, as for fuel stations, campgrounds, senior housing and storage in all seven markets, the operators' own rates and occupancy, gathered and dated for the study, are all the supply evidence a study will have.

Data-Center Sites

Three data-center towns outside the seven markets.

Beaver Dam, Janesville and Menomonie sit outside the market counties covered below. Each shows a different stage of the cycle a local study has to account for.

Beaver Dam, in Dodge County, is the furthest along. Meta's $1B campus there, sized at 220 MW, was under construction in July 2026, with $200M of ATC transmission work tied to it 2. For lodging, RV and rental housing studies in and around Beaver Dam, the construction workforce is real demand, but it is temporary, and the permanent case has to stand without it.

Janesville, in Rock County, shows the political stage. A Viridian project valued at $8B was pending in July 2026, and the city scheduled a referendum for November 3, 2026 on any project above $450M at the site 2. Menomonie, in Dunn County just west of the Eau Claire market, shows the pause: negotiations on a $1.6B Balloonist project were on hold in July 2026 2. Any study near a campus in Wisconsin, in these three towns or inside the markets below, should record the campus's standing on the date the study is signed and show its demand case both with the campus and without it.

Milwaukee Metro

Milwaukee: a shrinking core county and a full rental market.

Milwaukee, Ozaukee, Washington and Waukesha counties, where the core county lost residents while metro apartments kept filling.

This page's Milwaukee market is the four-county Milwaukee-Waukesha metro. Milwaukee County counted 924,216 people at July 1, 2025, against 939,489 in the April 2020 census, and Census QuickFacts puts its change from the 2020 estimates base at -1.6% 44. It still permitted 1,046 housing units in 2025 44.

The Milwaukee-Waukesha metro's June 2026 payroll was 3,100 jobs below June 2025 on the unadjusted count and 4,900 below on the seasonally adjusted series 45. Visit Milwaukee names the health systems (Advocate Aurora Health Care, Froedtert Health and Ascension Wisconsin), Quad/Graphics and the Medical College of Wisconsin as the largest employers, on an undated list 46.

Apartments point the other way. Marcus & Millichap put metro multifamily vacancy at 2.6% in Q3 2026, down 70 basis points on the year 47; Colliers counted 96.3% occupancy in Q1 2026 on 168,168 units, at an average effective rent of $1,709 48. One broker reports vacancy and the other occupancy, for different quarters, and the study should identify the series that carries its case. Both favor new rental product, though rents still need testing against submarket incomes, and with no construction count in the research, the open question for a new building is what else will deliver beside it.

Madison and Dane County

Madison: steady growth and a pipeline that has to be absorbed.

Dane County, where builders answered population growth with enough apartments that lease-up is now the question.

This page reads the Madison market through Dane County alone, and two official series give the county different sizes. Census's Vintage 2025 estimate is 590,375 for July 1, 2025, against 561,504 in the 2020 census, a change QuickFacts gives as +5.1% 53, while the state Department of Administration figure behind the county's projections is 611,149 for 2025 54. A Madison study should choose one, use it for base and projection alike, and show the other so a reviewer can see what the choice is worth.

Dane County permitted 6,629 residential units in 2025 53.

Payrolls did not keep pace. Between June 2025 and June 2026 the Madison metro lost 5,400 nonfarm jobs before seasonal adjustment and 2,600 after it 45. Before Closing

Seven Wisconsin gaps a study should close before credit.

Seven things the research behind this page could not establish, each with the office or dataset a study can take the answer from.

  1. USDA, Opportunity Zone and HUBZone maps

    No market on this page was screened against the USDA rural map, the Opportunity Zone tracts or the HUBZone map. For a Northwoods or Dells site the USDA result decides whether Business and Industry is open at all, so the study prints each map's result for the parcel, dated.

  2. Population vintage

    The state's change since April 2020 was not retrieved (Census table NST-EST2025), and the county figures on this page are Census Vintage 2025 except where a Department of Administration figure is also given. Every population line in a study should name its publisher and vintage.

  3. The count on the road outside

    The research retrieved no WisDOT count anywhere in the state. A fuel, quick-service or car wash study takes its capture rate from the traffic passing its entrance, so it needs the WisDOT figure for that segment and its year.

  4. Room nights and rates

    Not one of the seven markets has a published occupancy, rate or RevPAR series in the research, and the only room inventory is Milwaukee's. A lodging study should buy a competitive set report, read with our note on what an STR benchmark measures and what it leaves out, and list the rooms opened since 2025, from The Marc to the Dells resorts.

  5. Licenses that cap capacity

    Child care classes and ratios under DCF 250 and 251, DATCP's campground and RV park licensing, and any car wash water rule were all outside what the research captured. Each sets how many children, sites or cars a project can serve, so the study quotes the current code.

  6. Costs the research could not price

    No construction cost index, effective commercial property tax rate, statewide room-tax average, impact fee range, commercial insurance rate filing or carrier withdrawal record was found. The study takes the levy from the assessor, hard costs from bids, fees from the municipality and the premium from a dated quote.

  7. Power, water and sewer headroom

    The research found no MISO interconnection queue for Wisconsin, no water allocation or sewer moratorium record, and no standard data-center rate yet in place after the PSC called for one in May 2026. A food plant, car wash or other heavy user shows, from the utilities themselves, that its connection and discharge fit each system.

Wisconsin Questions

Wisconsin feasibility study questions.

What does the lender decide first on a Wisconsin hotel or resort SBA file?

Whether the projection carries the coverage test or only supports it, and that turns on whether the resort is bought or built. SBA lists hotels and motels among its special purpose properties 20; for an owner-occupied purchase at a price the appraisal supports in full, the projection can be the coverage evidence. For a new build the weight moves to equity, and the cushion surviving the first off-season is what the study proves. Either way the file sits in the most-funded Wisconsin 7(a) industry of 2025, hotels and motels 12, so the ramp, the seasonal low and the first year's coverage belong on one schedule.

Should a Wisconsin lodging pro forma treat nearby whole-home rentals as permanent competition?

Split them by length of stay, because the state protects only part of that market. A rental lasting seven consecutive days or longer cannot be prohibited by a Wisconsin municipality under s. 66.1014, and the 2025 repeal bill, SB 1080, was not enacted 29; shorter stays remain the town's to restrict. So the competitive set is split: weekly and longer rentals are supply the pro forma cannot assume away, while nightly rentals stay in the count until the ordinance standing when the study is dated removes them. Madison's license reaches only the host's primary residence 59; elsewhere the study quotes the town's own code.

Can a Wisconsin skilled nursing study count beds from a home that has closed?

Not in the base case while the rule that would allow it is still a proposal. Wisconsin caps nursing-home beds statewide under s. 150.31 24, and CR 25-072, which the 2026 Administrative Register carries as a proposal, would repeal the provision that keeps a closed home's beds from being redistributed 26. A base case built on beds the sponsor already holds, with a second case that adds redistributed beds if the rule is adopted, sizes the credit on the first and records the upside on the second.

How large a power-cost sensitivity should a Wisconsin pro forma carry?

Large enough that the lender can see the rate at which coverage breaks, not a flat percentage uplift. The base is the serving utility's own tariff, rather than any statewide average: the EIA figure CROWE cites, 8.54 cents per kWh for Wisconsin industrial customers in 2024 2, does not say how much of the transmission cost that CROWE says existing customers could potentially bear, $63M in 2027 2, will land on a given utility's ordinary accounts. The study names the utility, starts from its current rate and raises electricity in steps until coverage reaches the program's floor, and reports that breakeven beside the large-load terms the PSC set in 2026.

How long a lease-up should a lender fund on a Dane County apartment loan?

As many months as the buildings delivering beside it take to fill, which the study sets from a dated pipeline rather than from the sponsor's curve. CoStar counted 5,837 Madison metro units under construction in January 2026, after 2,802 net units were added during 2025 55, so the interest reserve is sized by the opening quarter of each of those projects rather than by the subject's own schedule. The question is whether the reserve and the stabilization date follow that delivery calendar or the sponsor's lease-up assumption; a study that lists the competing buildings by opening quarter gives the credit officer a reserve it can defend.

Which Wisconsin sites need a flood exhibit after the August 2025 storms?

Wider than the declaration. DR-4892 covered Milwaukee, Washington and Waukesha counties after the August 2025 storms 36, SBA's notice opened economic injury loans to the contiguous counties 37. Across those counties and their neighbours the decision is whether cover is priced from a quote issued after the storm, and whether a residual-market policy, which the Wisconsin Insurance Plan writes for commercial property 34, is accepted as the collateral's cover or sent back to the admitted market.
