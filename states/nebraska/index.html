Nebraska Feasibility Studies

Nebraska counted 2,018,006 residents on July 1, 2025 1, with two migration streams running in opposite directions, net domestic migration of -366 2 against net international migration of +6,599 21, both for the year from July 2024 to July 2025, so a feasibility study here begins by saying which stream the trade area draws on and what demand looks like if that stream slows. The credit side is narrow: NEDCO's 504 book stands at 710 loans and $535.9 million for FY2010 to FY2026 5, and 5.4% of the Nebraska 7(a) loans approved in fiscal 2011 through 2020 ended in charge-off against 1.4% of its 504 loans 9, a state loss record the projections have to clear.

The Nebraska Thesis

One state total, two migration streams, and a credit channel that runs through Omaha and Lincoln.

A Nebraska demand case starts from a state total that hides two streams moving in opposite directions. Residents kept leaving for other states 2, and arrivals from abroad more than replaced them, with net international migration of +6,599 between July 2024 and July 2025 21. Over five years the arithmetic still came out ahead: from an April 2020 estimates base of 1,961,980 the state reached 2,018,006 by July 1, 2025, up +56,026 people, or +2.9% 1. A trade area whose households came from the international stream is exposed to a change in that stream; one built on longtime residents is exposed to the domestic outflow. A study has to say which kind of trade area it is describing.

The map follows the same split. Douglas County and the Lincoln metro gained people, as did a few manufacturing towns in the east, while Scotts Bluff County in the Panhandle lost residents 91, and the July 2026 payroll counts of two of the six smaller markets on this page came in below July 2025 80. A town's own record, not the state's, is the starting point.

Credit is more centralized than population. One SBA district office, in Omaha, covers all counties 8, and Rural Development runs its Nebraska programs from a state office in Lincoln 102.

The one-year gain of about +12,500 people, or +0.6%, came despite net domestic migration of -366, smaller than the -1,498 of the year before but still an outflow 2. The inflow from abroad carried the total; the research found the 2025 figure only in secondary reporting, so a study should confirm it against the Census components file.

Where a town's recent growth arrived with the hiring of one meat plant or factory, that exposure is local, not statewide. Apartments, storage, starter retail and child care in such a town should not carry forward the 2023 to 2024 arrival pace. The report should say which Census release its population rests on, show demand under the smaller 2025 inflow as well, and name the employer whose staffing brings the newcomers.

Payrolls barely moved: the adjusted August 2026 reading of 1,062.7 thousand sat just above the 1,060.9 thousand of August 2025, +1.8 thousand or +0.2% 3. The Joint Economic Committee's private-sector reading is stronger, +2,900 jobs over twelve months and +2,300 in August alone 22, but it is a different release and should be quoted as such. The Department of Labor's unadjusted count set a record of 1,073,234 in June 2026, +946 on the year 4. The adjusted series shows the trend; the unadjusted June count includes seasonal hiring, so its record describes that month rather than a direction.

Hyperscale campuses and the power they draw

Data centers account for several of the large private dollar figures in the research. In March 2026 Google and Tenaska were reported to be proposing a data center paired with its own gas plant in Gage, Lancaster, Cass or Otoe county, with no dollar figure disclosed 23.

That campus was reported as needing more power than all of Lincoln 23. Grid capacity aside (check 6 below), a smaller project near one of these campuses may compete with it for trades and hotel rooms while it is being built.

SBA's own loan-level files give a steadier ten-year view. Counting approvals for fiscal 2016 through fiscal 2025 and netting out cancellations, Nebraska borrowers took 2,940 7(a) loans worth $1,184.7 million 9 and 371 504 loans 9. Losses split sharply by program. Among Nebraska loans from the fiscal 2011 to 2020 approval years, old enough to have seasoned, 5.4% of 7(a) loans, counted by number, ended in charge-off, compared with 1.4% of 504 loans 9. That gap is Nebraska's own record, so a study behind a 7(a) loan should test its projections against the higher state loss rate rather than a national average.

The lender table shows how few names hold the volume: the broker ranks by dollars, Union Bank and Trust heads its top five and also made the most loans of those five, and national lenders such as Live Oak booked fewer, larger loans 24. On the 504 side NEDCO, the Nebraska Economic Development Corporation, carries the program 5. It is worth asking at the outset whether any of them has lent in the subject's town.

Nebraska-run credit: SSBCI and NIFA

Through SSBCI, the federal small-business credit program, Nebraska was approved in October 2022 for up to $64.0 million 26. The research found no state guarantee or capital access program outside it.

For rental housing the state's channel is the Nebraska Investment Finance Authority, which issues tax-exempt bonds and allocates housing tax credits 13. Its February 2026 board book shows a conditional allocation of 2026 volume cap not to exceed $13,175,986 for a 4% tax credit multifamily bond deal 27. A multifamily study that expects bond financing should confirm the cap round and allocation date.

SBA in Nebraska

SBA in Nebraska: the two tests its typical projects meet.

Most Nebraska files this page describes meet either the 504 contribution tiers or the coverage floor for buying a business.

Whether a Nebraska file needs a study is decided loan by loan: the federal rule lets SBA require one 7, and our research note on that question sets out what the wording does and does not oblige.

The first test is the 504 owner-occupied building, NEDCO's core product. Where the borrower has been in business two years or less, or the property counts as limited or special purpose, the borrower's contribution rises to 15%, and to 20% when both apply 28; a young manufacturer in Norfolk or Columbus, or a single-use building off the Kearney interchange, can land in those tiers, and the study has to show where that cash comes from. The second is an initial acquisition under the change-of-ownership rules, such as a newcomer buying a retiring owner's small-town business, where the coverage floor of 1.25x is measured on historical earnings 28; in a county that is losing residents, the study's job is to show that the demand behind those earnings will last after the sale. The rest of the rule set is on the SBA practice area page.

USDA Rural Development

USDA in Nebraska: no current state totals.

Recent Nebraska obligation totals were not found.

The most recent state total the research could find is $269.5 million of obligations across all programs in FY2022 29; FY2025 totals, and Business and Industry obligations for Nebraska in particular, were not found. A study can say the program is available, but not how deep the Nebraska pipeline is.

Smaller programs show where the money has gone. USDA announced $15.1 million for 3 Community Facilities investments in rural Nebraska in January 2025 30, and REAP, USDA's rural energy program, made 233 awards worth $16.5 million from 2021 to January 2025 31. REAP grant applications were closed in 2026 101. A processor, a rural hotel or a small-town grocery whose loan is larger than an SBA lender wants to carry can look at the B&I guarantee instead, provided the site qualifies.

Whether it qualifies is a question about the parcel, which only a dated map run settles (check 2 below). The USDA practice area page covers the program itself, and another research note walks through the report components the agency looks for.

Conventional, HUD and EB-5

Bank, bond, agency and EB-5 capital: what the Nebraska evidence covers.

A manufacturer has a state bond route: NIFA issues tax-exempt industrial development bonds for land, buildings and other property 13. Housing on agency or HUD terms has less to stand on. The research reached no HUD or agency loan totals for Nebraska and found no HUD multifamily office with jurisdiction over the state, so the market study behind a HUD or Fannie Mae file here rests on the county record and a supply census the analyst compiles. Bank, bond and agency credit in general is the subject of our conventional and institutional practice.

An investor's counsel will ask first about the targeted employment area, and this page cannot answer that: the Nebraska research did not look at EB-5 projects or area designations. The EB-5 page covers the program, and a note on job counting explains how the jobs are tallied.

Senior housing and health care

Nebraska's certificate of need statute reaches only long-term care and rehabilitation beds 10, and for those beds it works as a moratorium with limited exceptions. Existing skilled nursing licences can therefore carry scarcity value in an acquisition, and new senior capacity arrives as assisted living, which needs only a licence 11. Memory care runs inside an assisted living licence with an optional endorsement 12. With no certificate of need standing in a new operator's way, the supply section has to count projects in planning as well as those open. See our method for assisted living with memory care.

Child care

Above the Home II cap a center licence applies 3233; the research gives the smaller Home I cap without a source address, so the table leaves it out. Centers staff infants at 1:4 34. The infant ratio drives cost, so a study should model each room separately and show the toddler and preschool rooms carrying the infants, rather than averaging tuition. The child care method sets out the classroom-level model.

Restaurants, bars and liquor

Nebraska has no quota on liquor licences, and licences are reported as non-transferable, with a new licence issued when a licensed business is bought, so no secondary market gives one a value 37. A licence is cheap and obtainable, so a study should not value it as an asset and should assume a competitor can open nearby; the local hearing belongs in the opening schedule.

Fuel and convenience sites

Nebraska still runs a state petroleum release fund under the Petroleum Release Remedial Action Act, and it reimburses releases reported on or before June 30, 2028 35. Since inception the fund has collected more than $375 million and spent about $294.5 million; in FY2025 it reimbursed $3.2 million across 130 sites 39. The fund is a real but time-limited backstop: a buyer holding a site past the 2028 reporting date should price private pollution cover. For the 2023 data year, County Business Patterns puts Nebraska's fuel and convenience establishments at 1,030 40; a claim that an Interstate 80 exit or a county highway is underserved should start from that statewide count and then narrow to the trade area. Our gas station method explains why a per-capita count alone does not settle demand.

Hotels and short-term rentals

The research identified no state statute pre-empting local short-term rental rules; operators need permits and collect tax 14. The lodging taxes under costs below matter more to a hotel study.

Manufacturing and construction

Machinery and equipment qualify for the sales tax exemption when used more than 50% in manufacturing 36. Building materials, however, are taxable; there is no general exemption for materials annexed to a manufacturing building 41. A plant or warehouse budget should carry that tax as a hard cost.

Lodging taxes

Cities add their own occupation taxes. No typical combined rate is published, and the city layer differs from one market section to the next, so the room-tax line in a hotel pro forma is assembled city by city, with its date.

Environmental and disposal fees

Two 2025 to 2026 statutes changed operating costs. LB247, approved March 17, 2025, raised the solid-waste disposal fee from $1.00 to $2.34 45, a line to check in any operating budget that pays for waste haulage. LB815, signed April 14, 2026, moved the petroleum release fee from dyed to on-road diesel at 0.6 cents per gallon 46. For a truck stop the diesel change is a margin item.

Three cost lines could not be documented at all, property tax, building cost and insurance premium; check 5 below says what a report should put in their place.

Statewide Supply

Nebraska's households are counted; the research found no count of its competitors.

Census and BEA describe who lives in Nebraska. No statewide record in the research shows what has been built to serve them.

Demand can be sized from federal tables. In the Census Bureau's 2020 to 2024 five-year estimates Nebraska has 797,847 households, 266,969 of them renters 51, and 330,805 people aged 65 or older 51. For 2024 the Bureau of Economic Analysis reports per capita personal income of $72,701 52. In a farm state that number carries proprietors' farm income and government transfers along with wages, so a store or dining study should not read it as spending power without saying why; the survey's household income is the closer measure.

Supply is where the record stops. Apart from broker reports for Omaha and Lincoln, nothing in the research tracks competing apartments, rooms, storage units or care beds in Nebraska, so for a Kearney hotel, Norfolk storage or a Grand Island assisted living building the competitor list is primary work (check 3 below).

Projects that move demand

Project Health, the University of Nebraska Medical Center and Nebraska Medicine expansion in Douglas County, broke ground in April 2025 58. For medical office, apartments and hotels near the campus, it is the project whose schedule a study should track. Google's northwest Omaha data center, announced at $750 million on a 378-acre campus, was under construction in January 2025 59. The new Omaha Central Library cost $158 million and opened on April 19, 2026 60. Along Highway 50 in Sarpy County, Google and Meta campuses have turned farmland into a data center corridor 61.

The Gretna Good Life District at Nebraska Crossing, in Sarpy County, was presented as a $3.2 billion project 62. Once its developer warned the state in early 2025 that the plan could not proceed as drawn, the Destination Nebraska Act set out a new route for it 53; in January 2026 a large travel center was reported to be coming to the site near Interstate 80 and Highway 31, with no opening date 62. A study near the interchange should count only what has permits and financing on its date.

Apartments: deliveries outran demand in 2025

By CBRE's count, 2025 brought 3,793 new apartment units to Omaha, and fourth-quarter vacancy stood at 8.3%, a rise of +270 bps from a year before 54. Asking rent of $1,288 per unit slipped -0.4% from the third quarter yet sat +4.3% above the prior year, while the pipeline held 3,218 units across 21 properties 54. Berkadia's mid-year report, on RealPage data, put second-quarter 2025 occupancy at 97.1% and effective rent at $1,305 55. The research treats the two as conflicting measures and leaves them unreconciled, and the study must name the one it relies on. An Omaha apartment study should model lease-up on the 2025 absorption record, include first-year concessions, and show the loan works if stabilization slips. The multifamily method explains the absorption test.

Industrial: tight and still building

Colliers' first-quarter 2026 report put industrial vacancy at 2.0%, with 943 thousand square feet under construction 56. Vacancy that low supports the resale case for an owner-occupied 504 warehouse; a study should still check how much of the space under construction is preleased.

Hotels, taxes and short-term rentals

Hotel performance data were not found; an Omaha hotel study needs a paid STR report for its competitive set. The city had no short-term rental registry in July 2026; a registration proposal was pending 63.

Lincoln's July 2026 nonfarm count was a preliminary 199.0 thousand 65. A downtown convention center rebuild, across from the Cornhusker Hotel, was announced in March 2025, with no cost in the research 100.

Water 2.0 and what it means for large users

All of Lincoln's water now comes from Platte River wells near Ashland, and the wellfield is considered adequate for about 25 years 66. The city's second source, Water 2.0, will draw from the Missouri River at a program cost of about $1 billion to $1.39 billion, with a treatment plant by 2035 and full completion by 2048 66. Construction started in August 2025 on a $36 million main along 98th Street paid for with federal recovery funds 66. For a heavy water user in Lincoln, the wellfield's remaining life is the context for the connection question in check 6 below.

Impact fees are a hard cost

Lincoln publishes its impact fees, so the budget can carry real numbers rather than an allowance: the street fee varies by housing type and the water fee by meter size 64. A study should take fees from the schedule in force at permit and size the meter from the engineer's plans.

Supply: apartments soft, industrial and retail tight

Lee and Associates put Lincoln's apartment vacancy at 7.7%, asking rent at $1,203 per unit and the pipeline at 465 units under construction 67; the report is labelled the fourth quarter of 2025, though its text dates the vacancy reading to early 2026. CoStar reported 7.4% in September 2025 and called it a multidecade high, against an average of 5.1% from 2015 to 2019 68. The pipeline is the encouraging part: the report sets the 465 units under way against a 10-year annual average of 1,187 67, so if absorption holds, vacancy should ease as fewer new buildings arrive. A Lincoln apartment study should show that path explicitly and not assume the 2015 to 2019 vacancy returns on its own.

Lee and Associates' mid-2025 industrial report gives vacancy of 2.7% on an inventory of 33.47 million square feet 69, and its early-2025 retail report also shows low vacancy, with the former Sears at Gateway Mall being redeveloped into about 100,000 square feet of tenant space 70.

Grand Island

Grand Island: new medical capacity, a rebuilt highway and a housing push.

Hall, Howard and Merrick counties. Payrolls are flat, but public and medical investment has been steady.

Grand Island's home county, Hall, grew +1.2% between the 2020 census (62,895) and July 1, 2025 (63,633) 71; the metro counted 77,278 in July 2024 72. Nonfarm employment in the area was 40,187 in March 2026, a preliminary count up +74 jobs or +0.2% on the year 73. JBS USA's Grand Island beef plant appears among the state's largest private sites in the same undated federal database that lists Union Pacific 97. With payrolls this flat, the job base cannot be assumed to grow.

Nebraska Medicine announced in May 2025 a 66,000 square foot Grand Island Health Center, with groundbreaking in July 2025 and a build of about 20 months 74. CHI Health St. Francis expanded its emergency department 75. If patients come from well beyond the city, the two projects can carry demand for medical office space, senior beds and visitor nights, but that reach has to be shown: a senior or medical office study should define its trade area past Hall County and map the drive times.

The city has pushed hard on housing. In 2025 it won $1.2 million from Nebraska's workforce housing fund for rural towns 75. Road work has reshaped the corridors. The four-lane US-30 expressway west of town, a $47.8 million project, was scheduled to open fully in early November 2025, according to an October 31 report 76; the second phase of South Locust Street, a $9,044,222 contract from February 2025, replaces water mains and century-old pipes 77; and a $4.3 million Claude Road project began in 2025 78. A retail or fuel study should show the site's access after the work.

Since May 1, 2026 Grand Island has charged a 4% occupation tax on hotel rooms, up from 2%, on top of Hall County's 4% lodging tax 16. A hotel study with a 2025 operating history has to restate it at the new rate. State and federal grants for a rail park were reported without a date and are not used here; a study for an industrial site there should obtain the award letters.

Kearney

Kearney: an Interstate 80 meetings market, with a data center on the table.

Buffalo and Kearney counties. A small market that has invested heavily in visitor demand.

Buffalo County reached 51,172 people by July 2025, +2.2% over its 2020 census count of 50,084 79. The area's payrolls rose by +248 between July 2025 and July 2026, to a preliminary 30,540 from 30,292 80.

Kearney has deliberately built visitor demand. The SportsPlex, a 209,000 square foot facility costing $46 million, opened in April 2025, financed in part by $17 million of state sales tax turned back to the city 81. The Forward Kearney visitor and economic development center, a $5.3 million building, broke ground in October 2023 82. Those facilities can make weekend tournaments and meetings the core segment of a lodging or dining study, which should then show the room nights each event fills from the operators' calendars.

The data center question is newer. In August 2026 the Kearney Planning Commission recommended a conditional use permit for Project Horizon at the Tech oNE Crossing site, with projected city revenue of $11-13 million a year at build-out 83. A study written now should treat it as a possibility with a date to watch, not as demand.

The city raised its hotel occupation tax from 2% to 3% from November 1, 2025 17. Buffalo County's lodging tax was 4% in 2019 84, and a study should confirm the current rate. Buffalo County was named in NIFA's February 2026 discussion of homeownership help after the Lexington closure 27, a sign that some displaced workers may look for housing here.

North Platte

North Platte: a new beef plant, and a county population that may be shrinking.

Lincoln and Logan counties. Lincoln County here is the North Platte market, not the city of Lincoln.

North Platte sets a large investment record against a population count that may be falling. Yet one secondary source puts Lincoln County at 33,303 in July 2025, -4.0% from 2020, a change the research flags for checking against the Census base 86. Jobs moved the other way: the preliminary July 2026 count of 17,629 was +479 above the 17,150 of July 2025 80.

The anchor of the new investment is Sustainable Beef, a cattle-producer-owned plant near the Interstate 80 Newberry Access interchange, built on a former city sewer lagoon 87, with Walmart as a minority partner 88. It processed its first cattle in May 2025 with 200 jobs, has a capacity of 1,500 head per day 89, and plans 950 jobs at full capacity 85. Its cost was estimated at nearly $300 million in 2021; the final cost was not found 87. Great Plains Health, the second-largest employer after Union Pacific 8890, completed a $30 million primary care center and a $6.8 million neurosurgery center in 2025 90.

The constraint is labor and housing. A plant that adds several hundred jobs in a county whose population may be falling has to draw workers from elsewhere, and they need somewhere to live. That supports workforce rental housing, but the file should prove the plant's hiring by date and headcount, and show that the units built since 2020 have filled.

Hotel taxes are known only for 2019: a city occupation tax of 2% and a Lincoln County lodging tax of 4% 84; the current rates were not found. Lincoln County was among the five counties of the June 2026 DR-4920 wildfire declaration 47, a fact the study's own hazard section should carry.

Scottsbluff

Scottsbluff: the Panhandle's service center, on a thin public record.

Scotts Bluff and Banner counties, near the Wyoming and Colorado lines.

Scotts Bluff County's population fell -1.4% from the 2020 census to July 2025, from 36,084 to 35,586 91. Nonfarm employment fell to a preliminary 16,078 in July 2026 from 16,415 a year earlier, a loss of -337 jobs 80. The economy rests on Regional West Medical Center, which heads the local development corporation's undated employer list 99, and on the city's role as the Panhandle's service center.

The published record here is thin. Employer counts, the city's local incentive fund awards and the county lodging tax all appear in the research only without dates, so none is used. The research found no supply data for any asset class, no traffic counts and no current hotel tax rate. A Scottsbluff study therefore rests on fieldwork: interviews with the hospital and the joint Scottsbluff and Gering industrial park, a current lodging tax rate from the county treasurer, and its own list of competitors.

A study should explain whether the payroll loss sits in one employer or across the market and size demand on the current population. For a clinic building or senior housing, the hospital's Panhandle referral area can carry demand the local population cannot, if the study documents it.

Norfolk

Norfolk: steel, new industrial land and steady payroll growth.

Madison, Pierce and Stanton counties in northeast Nebraska.

The Norfolk micropolitan area payrolls added +410 over the latest twelve months, reaching a preliminary 25,627 in July 2026 (25,217 a year before) 80. The base is manufacturing and regional retail.

The city is preparing for more industry. The Bradford Business Park was financed by local banks: Midwest, Elkhorn Valley, First National and Frontier 93. Norfolk's housing program received two state workforce housing awards, $628,356 in 2024 and $1,000,000 in 2025 92. Local bank participation is useful evidence in a credit file.

One open question sits in the operating budget: a city occupation tax was proposed subject to a public vote, but no outcome appears in the research. Until the city confirms the rate, a Norfolk lodging or dining pro forma should show it both ways. Nor does the research hold room counts, occupancy or road counts for Norfolk, so a new hotel or restaurant has to be argued from field counts along the corridors that will serve the new industrial land.

Columbus, Nebraska

Columbus, Nebraska: a medical-device town that is adding residents.

Platte and Colfax counties, in east-central Nebraska.

The Columbus in this section is Platte County's seat, not the Ohio capital. Platte County's July 2025 estimate is 35,649 94.

The economy turns on one employer. BD has a Columbus workforce of more than 2,000, and its August 2025 announcement added a $35 million expansion of pre-filled flush syringe production with about 50 new jobs, after a $60 million Columbus-East expansion that opened in 2021 19. BD did not say when the new work would start or finish 19. The expansion is likely but undated, and the study should test how much the trade area depends on one plant: a market where one employer dominates is strong while that employer grows and exposed if it consolidates.

Payrolls slipped in the latest year: 24,885 in July 2025 and 24,800 (preliminary) in July 2026, a change of -85 80. Accommodation and food services fell to 1,668 jobs from 1,745 80, a warning for any hotel or restaurant study that assumes hospitality demand grows with population. With the county lodging tax known only from an undated listing, and no room supply, road counts or local development rules in the research, a Columbus hospitality study has to establish its own rate, competitors and access before it can argue that BD's growth will reach the dining room or the front desk.

Lender Verification

Six checks before a Nebraska report goes to credit.

Six gaps in the Nebraska evidence, each a question to put to the analyst before the credit memo is written.

  1. Lender activity near the site

    SBA's own Nebraska totals for FY2024 and FY2025, and USDA's FY2025 B&I obligations for the state, were beyond the research, and the private tallies on this page disagree. The question that matters to a credit memo is local anyway: whether any lender has closed an SBA or B&I loan in the subject's county, which the federal loan records answer by borrower location.

  2. The parcel's program status

    No rural eligibility, Opportunity Zone or HUBZone result was pulled for any of the eight markets. Look for each map run on the parcel itself, with its date.

  3. A dated competitor list

    Outside the two eastern cities the report's own list of competitors is the only supply evidence there is, whether the subject is an Interstate 80 hotel, a Panhandle care home or a farm-town fuel stop. Each entry should carry a name, a size and an opening date, and the list should say when it was compiled.

  4. Room tax in the smaller towns

    The North Platte rates date from 2019, the Scottsbluff and Columbus county rates carry no date, and Norfolk's proposed occupation tax has no outcome on record. The pro forma should use the rates the city clerk and county treasurer confirm on the study date.

  5. Levy, building cost and premium

    The state-level research turned up no effective commercial rate of property tax, no construction cost index and no insurance rate filings. Expect the parcel's own levy from the county, a bid-based construction budget and a quote that prices hail, wind and, in the western counties, grass fire.

  6. Wires, water and road counts

    Nothing in the research covers the interconnection queue, water allocations or sewer headroom, and no state road count appears for any of the eight markets. For a data hall, a food plant or a car wash this is the gap that can stop the project, so before closing the file should hold a dated letter from each provider confirming it can serve the connection the plans call for, along with the latest count for the road at the entrance.

Nebraska Questions

Nebraska feasibility study questions.

Can an Omaha apartment feasibility study assume a quick lease-up?

The lender settles that by asking which market reading the lease-up schedule rests on, because the two in print disagree. Berkadia, on RealPage data, put mid-2025 occupancy at 97.1% 55; CBRE's fourth-quarter 2025 vacancy reading was 8.3% 54, and the research gives no county line for either publisher, so the Iowa side may be inside the count. A study that picks the tighter reading owes an explanation of why the subject will lease ahead of the buildings that opened in 2025; one that picks the softer reading has to show the loan covered with concessions in year one and stabilization arriving late. Either way the choice is written down.

Is there a state approval step before a Nebraska senior housing project is built?

Only for nursing home and rehabilitation beds, so the answer depends on what the building is. Assisted living, where new senior capacity in Nebraska arrives, needs a licence rather than a certificate 11, and memory care runs inside that licence by endorsement 12. What a lender takes from this is that nothing stops a competitor from opening nearby: the supply count has to list buildings still in planning beside those open, and demand has to hold after they fill. An existing nursing home licence is the opposite case, since the certificate of need statute covers those beds 10 and an acquisition can price that scarcity.

How should a 2026 study in central Nebraska treat the Lexington closure?

As a line drawn through the record, with every demand figure labelled before or after it. The lender wants three things shown: a population count dated after the plant stopped, a model with the plant payroll out of it, and coverage that survives if the households it supported leave the region. The reach is wider than Dawson County, where the plant sits: NIFA's board took up homeownership help for Custer and Buffalo as well 27, so a housing study in any of the three inherits the question. The scale is set by the notice, 3,212 jobs with the plant closing on or about January 20, 2026 6.

Which part of the room tax is most likely to be wrong in a Nebraska hotel pro forma?

The city layer: the state rates are published and a county sets its own rate under a cap, while a city's occupation tax can change between the study date and the opening. The lender's test runs backwards from the operating history. Where the comparable set or the subject's own 2025 results were earned under an old city rate, their net room revenue is overstated for a hotel opening later, so coverage is recomputed at the rate the opening date will carry. The county cap is statutory 15; the city's figure comes from the clerk, in writing.

Which Columbus does this page cover, Nebraska's or Ohio's?

Nebraska's: the Platte County seat, in east-central Nebraska; Columbus, Ohio is in the Ohio page's own section. The lender's test is concentration, not growth. BD, one medical-device employer, keeps more than 2,000 people there 19, so a hotel, restaurant or apartment study must show demand with that plant held flat and the trade area as it looks if production consolidates elsewhere. The $35 million expansion BD announced in August 2025 carries no start or finish date 19, which keeps it out of the base case. The micropolitan area grew over 2020 to 2025 18, but the town's payroll count fell in the latest year 80.

Why does this page leave out the Omaha metro population and payroll figures?

Because a metro total would count Iowa households against a Nebraska loan. Part of the federal metro lies across the river, so any metro headcount or payroll figure mixes two states; this page works from the Nebraska counties alone, starting with Douglas County's 606,460 residents at July 1, 2025 20. Broker figures face the same test. NAI NP Dodge labels its quarterly report as Omaha and Council Bluffs 96 and is left out for that reason; for the CBRE, Colliers and Berkadia reports, the research does not record where the line falls. A comparable is admitted once its counties are known; the Iowa page is the start for Council Bluffs.
