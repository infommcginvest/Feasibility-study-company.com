South Dakota in Brief

A visitor season that funds the sales tax, a harvest forecast for October 2026 and a payroll with few people out of work.

Feasibility Study Company prepares feasibility studies for South Dakota projects. The clients are owners and developers, and the readers are the SBA lenders, USDA Rural Development and banks that finance them. What follows on this page is the record such a study draws on, starting with the two sources of income that set South Dakota's year.

The first is visitors. The Department of Tourism counted 14.97 million visitors to and within the state in 2025, spending $5.16 billion, supporting 59,145 jobs and generating $406.1 million in state and local taxes; tourism accounted for 16.5% of total state sales tax collections 1. A state that collects that share of its sales tax is a state where hotels, campgrounds, restaurants and fuel stops are underwritten against a calendar, and the summer of 2026 gave the calendar a mixed reading: SDDOT put the ten-day Sturgis vehicle count under its five-year average 5.

The second is the crop. USDA's National Agricultural Statistics Service forecast on October 9, 2026 a South Dakota corn crop of 881,840 thousand bushels and a soybean crop of 226,800 thousand bushels 2. Grain elevators, input dealers, equipment shops, ethanol and processing plants and the main streets of the farm counties take their volume from those numbers, and a lender reads a project in those trades against the forecast for the crop year it opens in.

Between the two seasons sits a payroll with very little slack. In September 2026 the Labor Market Information Center counted 484,700 people in the civilian labor force and 475,100 employed, both seasonally adjusted and preliminary 6, and BLS's release of October 20, 2026 reports South Dakota's September jobless rate as the lowest of any state 14. A hotel that needs a full housekeeping staff or a plant that needs a second shift is tested first on whether it can hire, and second on what it will pay to do so.

The page then turns to costs, the rules the 2025 and 2026 sessions wrote, the hazards that brought federal declarations, statewide supply and a dated list of events. Six markets follow, Sioux Falls and the Black Hills first, then Aberdeen, Brookings, Watertown, and Mitchell with Pierre. The lending programs and the asset classes close the page.

Visitors and Gaming

Where the 2025 visitor money went, what the 2026 rally counted and what Deadwood paid the state.

The Department of Tourism's release of January 21, 2026 is the statewide anchor for any lodging or hospitality study. It reports 14.97 million visitors for 2025, $5.16 billion of visitor spending, 59,145 jobs supported by tourism and $406.1 million in state and local taxes from the industry, and puts tourism at 16.5% of total state sales tax collections 1. The release gives its own year-over-year changes, and they are small; a hotel pro forma that assumes a growth rate cites the release for it rather than building one.

Visitor spending lands on a narrow payroll. FRED, relaying BLS, shows 55.7 thousand leisure and hospitality jobs in South Dakota in September 2026, seasonally adjusted 15. The state's 2025 QCEW report gives the sector's average annual pay as $24,183 8. That pay figure is an annual average across seasonal and part-time positions, and a study for a year-round hotel states its own hourly wage and staffing schedule rather than dividing it out of the annual number.

The Sturgis Motorcycle Rally

The rally in Meade County is counted by two agencies. SDDOT's release of August 17, 2026 counts 448,166 vehicles entering Sturgis at its count sites over the ten days, and states that the count ran below the 2021 to 2025 five-year average 5. Its first three days, Friday to Sunday, brought 168,482 vehicles, which Dakota News Now, relaying SDDOT, reported as down from the same period of 2025 16.

The Department of Revenue counts money. Its release of September 1, 2026 estimates $1,404,408 of tax collections from the 2026 rally, which it reports as a decrease from 2025 4. The Rapid City section prints the department's figure for the southern Black Hills, where collections rose. For an RV park, campground, bar or seasonal retail project in the northern hills, the rally is a share of the year that a lender stress-tests on its own line: the pro forma shows the year with a weak rally, not only an average one.

Deadwood gaming

Gaming in Deadwood, in Lawrence County, is taxed on adjusted gross revenue under SDCL 42-7B-28, and the Commission on Gaming's report for fiscal 2025 shows $2,315,908.04 of excess Deadwood proceeds deposited in the State general fund under the chapter's excess-proceeds section 17. A hotel, restaurant or entertainment project in Deadwood reads that report for the gaming trend behind its demand. The gaming handle by property and a fiscal 2026 report were not available, so a study takes the year's figures from the commission when they are posted.

What the visitor record does not give

No dated public page gave South Dakota's hotel occupancy, room rate or RevPAR for either year, and the Department of Tourism's county spending tables could not be read. A lodging study in South Dakota therefore buys a competitive set report for its own trade area and states that it did. Lodging tax rates and collections by month were not found either; the rally release names a state tourism tax among the collections without setting out its rate here, and the rate in force on the opening date is taken from the Department of Revenue.

Crops and Land

The October 2026 crop forecast and two readings of what an acre of South Dakota cropland is worth.

The crop numbers that matter most to a South Dakota farm-service project are in NASS's Crop Production release of October 9, 2026. It forecasts, as of October 1, corn for grain at 881,840 thousand bushels and soybeans at 226,800 thousand bushels for South Dakota 2. Its crop comments put the state's sunflower yield at 1,959 pounds per acre, below the previous year's record, and single out South Dakota's soybean pod count as showing the greatest decrease among the states surveyed 2.

Three kinds of project carry that forecast into their revenue line. A grain storage or handling facility sizes throughput on bushels, a fertilizer, seed or chemical dealer on planted acres and farm income, and a processing plant on a contracted supply. The October figure is a forecast, and NASS revises it; a study dated after January cites the annual summary that replaces it.

Cropland values

Land is the collateral behind most farm lending and the cost line in most rural site budgets, and two surveys price it differently. The USDA NASS Land Values 2026 Summary, as DTN Progressive Farmer reported it on July 31, 2026, gives South Dakota cropland a value of $4,770 per acre 18. The SDSU Extension Farm Real Estate Survey, as Pro Farmer reported it on June 17, 2026, gives non-irrigated cropland of average productivity $6,268 per acre 19. The two use different methods and land definitions. A study that prices a rural parcel names the survey it relies on, or uses a sale of comparable land in the county, and does not average the two.

A NASS cash rent figure for 2026, the cattle inventory and the state's ethanol capacity were not found on a dated page that could be opened. A livestock or ethanol project sources those from NASS's and EIA's own releases at the date of the study.

Payrolls and Output

Jobs by sector, pay by sector, output for 2025 and the ten-year projections a staffing plan is set against.

The population base is small and counted two ways. FRED relays the Census Bureau's Vintage 2025 estimate of 935.094 thousand South Dakota residents on July 1, 2025 20. The Labor Market Information Center's December 2025 bulletin names Minnesota as the largest origin of residents moving in from other states, in the Census Bureau's 2022 survey 21. County estimates for 2025 could not be retrieved from the Census Bureau, so the market sections use the bulletin's 2024 city figures.

Payroll jobs

The Labor Market Information Center counted 470,500 nonfarm wage and salary jobs in September 2026, seasonally adjusted and preliminary, beside the 484,700 labor force and 475,100 employed residents in the opening section 6. Its April 2026 bulletin gives the 2025 annual average, not seasonally adjusted, as 469,400 nonfarm workers 22.

BLS's monthly release of October 20, 2026 gives the sector split for September 2026, seasonally adjusted and preliminary: 81.6 thousand jobs in education and health services, 80.6 thousand in government and 35.9 thousand in professional and business services 14. Health care and public employment carry a large part of the state's payroll, which matters for a clinic, a senior housing community or an office building whose tenants are agencies.

Pay

The state's 2025 QCEW report gives statewide average annual pay of $60,577 for covered workers 8. Construction paid an average of $71,093 and leisure and hospitality $24,183, and manufacturing employed an average of 44,302 covered workers 8; for September 2026 FRED, relaying BLS, shows 43.8 thousand manufacturing jobs, seasonally adjusted 23. BLS's first-quarter 2026 county release puts the statewide average weekly wage at $1,211, a preliminary figure 3. A construction budget uses the construction figure, not the all-industry one, and a plant's labor line uses its own trade's wage.

Output

FRED relays BEA's 2025 figures for South Dakota: GDP of 82,001.9 million dollars and real GDP of 59,865.2 million chained 2017 dollars 2425. Quarterly growth rates for 2026 were reported in the press but are not printed here; a study that relies on them takes them from BEA's release.

Projections

The Labor Market Information Center's July 2026 bulletin projects 562,027 jobs in total across all industries in 2034, a count that includes self-employed, unpaid family and farm workers and is not a wage and salary figure 26. Its October 2026 bulletin projects 63,399 total openings a year across all occupations from 2024 to 2034, adding growth, people leaving the labor force and workers changing occupations 7. Openings measure turnover as well as growth: a restaurant or a plant hires into that churn, and a staffing plan says which occupations it competes for.

Costs and Insurance

Labor and land carry dated prices; building costs and insurance premiums have to be quoted for the site.

Three cost lines in a South Dakota budget can be tied to a dated public figure. Construction labor is the first: the statewide average annual pay in construction was $71,093 in 2025 8. The second is the operating wage, read by sector from the same report and from BLS's first-quarter 2026 weekly wage 83. The third is land, priced on the NASS and SDSU surveys in the crops section or, better, on a county sale.

Taxes are the fourth line, and they changed. HB 1137 lowered the state sales tax rate from July 1, 2023, and its sunset clause repeals the reduction on June 30, 2027 unless the Legislature acts 28; no 2026 action on the sunset was confirmed on an opened page. A pro forma whose first full year falls after mid-2027 states which rate it assumes on taxable sales. On property tax, the Governor signed SB 245, creating the Homeowner Property Tax Reduction Fund, with a group of other property tax bills on March 12, 2026 29, and SB 96 lets a county adopt an optional sales tax to offset property tax on owner-occupied homes, as soon as the 2027 pay year if adopted in time 30. Both measures are aimed at homeowners; a commercial owner's tax line is read from the county's levy, and a retailer in a county that adopts the option collects the added tax at the register.

Three other costs have no public South Dakota figure behind them. A construction cost index for Sioux Falls, Rapid City or any other city in the state was not found; neither were property insurance rate filings with the Division of Insurance or a measure of how available commercial cover is. Prevailing wage requirements and a minimum wage citation on a dated page were not found either. A study therefore prices hard costs from a contractor's bid for the site, and insurance from a dated quote, and says so.

Rules by Asset Class

What the 2025 and 2026 sessions changed for data centers, housing, building codes and pipelines.

Data centers

In March 2026 the Governor signed a law setting rules for new data centers above a peak electrical demand threshold, after a push for incentives failed, as KOTA reported from South Dakota Searchlight on March 25, 2026 31. The law sets conditions rather than incentives. A data center or large-load project here is read under it, and power is documented by the utility's written commitment to serve the site. In Sioux Falls the city council approved a rezoning and preliminary subdivision plan for the Gemini data center site in early January 2026 32; in Rapid City a data center at the Black Hills Industrial Center was moving forward in July 2026, with construction not expected before late 2026 33.

Housing and lodging

SB 3 limits which residential improvements counties, municipalities and townships may require permits for; SB 129 updates the state's references to the 2024 standard building codes; SB 204 revises the criteria for loans from the housing infrastructure fund; and HB 1260 authorizes municipal property tax rebate programs. Dakota News Now listed all four among the bills signed by March 28, 2026 34. For a subdivision or apartment project, the building code edition and the city's rebate program, if it adopts one, belong in the entitlement memo.

South Dakota Housing's income limits for the Governor's House program, effective June 15, 2026, rest on a State Median Income of $103,400 35. Its Give Me Five downpayment assistance program launches on November 2, 2026 as a forgivable second loan for buyers who have not owned a home recently 36. A developer of entry-level homes reads both, since they define who can buy.

A statewide short-term rental statute did not appear among the 2026 bills signed, and rental rules come from each city's ordinance. A rule on lodging licensing or campground licensing was not found on a dated state page.

Land use and zoning

SB 91 clarifies how a petition reaches a county commission to change a comprehensive plan or zoning ordinance 34. For a project on unincorporated land outside a city, a common case for a travel center, campground or processing plant in South Dakota, that bill sets the route to a rezoning.

Pipelines and banks

The state banned eminent domain for carbon pipelines in March 2025, and in May 2026 Summit described a new pipeline route that does not include South Dakota and affects counties in Iowa 37. An ethanol plant whose plan counted on shipping captured carbon by pipeline revisits that assumption. SB 18 of 2026 repealed bad-debt income modifications for the bank franchise tax 34, which matters to the banks that lend here more than to a borrower.

Health care, liquor and child care

A certificate of need program for health facilities was not confirmed on an opened South Dakota page, and liquor licence rule changes and child care licensing changes for 2025 or 2026 were not found. A senior housing, restaurant or child care study takes the rule from the licensing agency on the filing date.

Hazards

Wind in December 2025, storms and floods in mid-2026, and a drought declaration with an injury loan deadline in 2027.

Two federal major disaster declarations cover 2025 and 2026 weather. DR-4903-SD answers the wind event of December 17 and 18, 2025, with Public Assistance for Custer, Fall River and Pennington counties, as Safeguard Properties relayed FEMA's alert on April 7, 2026 38. DR-4938-SD answers storms, tornadoes, high winds and floods between June 28 and July 3, 2026, with Public Assistance for Beadle, Bennett, Buffalo, Day, Hand, Hughes, Hyde and Stanley counties and tribal lands, relayed on September 1, 2026 39. FEMA's own pages could not be opened, and the declaration dates themselves are not printed in the relays.

SBA has acted three times. In a release of June 5, 2026, after a drought declaration, SBA opened economic injury loans to small businesses and private nonprofits in a list of South Dakota counties that includes Lawrence, Lincoln, Meade, Minnehaha and Pennington, with applications due February 1, 2027 40. Flooding and excessive moisture from July 21 to August 19, 2025 opened injury loans in Brown, Day, Marshall and Roberts counties 41. Storms and flooding of June 2 to 4, 2026 on Oglala Sioux Tribe lands brought a presidential declaration on September 1 and SBA loans there, with injury loans also open in Pennington, Custer, Fall River, Bennett, Jackson, Mellette, Oglala Lakota and Todd counties 42. In July 2026 KOTA reported more than half of the state in drought 33.

For a study the drought and the storms work through two lines. A farm-service business in a drought county carries weaker customer income into its forecast for the next crop year, and a building in a wind and hail county carries the insurer's quote, not a regional average. A FEMA flood zone count, a state hazard mitigation plan figure and wildfire acreage for the Black Hills were not found, so the flood map panel for the parcel and the carrier's own wildfire assessment stand in for them.

Statewide Supply

Permits, listings, a price index and the housing money South Dakota awarded in December 2025.

The statewide housing series come through FRED. The Census Bureau's permit series shows 471 new private housing units authorized in South Dakota in September 2026, not seasonally adjusted 43. Realtor.com counted 3,581 active home listings in the state in September 2026 44, and FHFA's all-transactions house price index read 642.50 for 2026's second quarter, indexed to 1980:Q1 at 100 45. One month of permits is a single reading in a seasonal series, and a study for a subdivision reads the county's annual permits, which the market sections give.

The affordable housing pipeline is set by South Dakota Housing's award rounds. On December 10, 2025 the authority announced $59.4 million awarded to developments, including $4.19 million of Housing Tax Credits 13. A tax credit project's feasibility study is written to the authority's market study rules for its round, and the round's awards tell the reviewer what else is coming into the same city.

Need is measured too. The statewide point-in-time count of January 27, 2026 identified 1,154 people experiencing homelessness, a count South Dakota Housing described as lower than in previous years 46. A supportive housing or shelter project cites that count, and the count for its own continuum of care where one is published.

For the commercial asset classes there is no statewide inventory: no count of hotel rooms, self-storage space, senior housing beds or licensed child care slots was found on a dated page. The Sioux Falls section carries the only broker readings in the state's record; outside Sioux Falls, competing supply is tallied from city permit records and a canvass of nearby competitors.

Sioux Falls

Sioux Falls: a billion-dollar permit year, apartment vacancy read twice a year and record months at the airport.

Sioux Falls is read here on Minnehaha and Lincoln counties and the city itself. Two population figures are on record and they measure different things. The Labor Market Information Center's December 2025 bulletin tables the Census Bureau's 2024 estimate of 209,300 for the city 21. The city's own figure of 224,676, quoted with its 2025 year-end permit report, is a city estimate, not a Census figure, as South Dakota Public Broadcasting reported it on January 14, 2026 52. A study cites the one it uses with its year and publisher.

Employment and pay

Minnehaha County had 139,770 covered workers in 2025 at average annual pay of $66,906, in the state's QCEW report 1053. BLS's first-quarter 2026 county release puts the county's average weekly wage at $1,369, a preliminary figure 3. The metro-wide figures in the same QCEW report, labelled here as including Rock County, Minnesota, are 177,999 workers, average annual pay of $66,768 and 34,900 workers in health care and social assistance 9. The health care count bears on medical office, senior housing and hotel demand from patients and visiting families.

Permits

The city's 2025 year-end permit report, dated January 14, 2026, values building permits at $1,327,437,573 across 5,615 permits 48. New commercial buildings were valued at $711.6 million, which the city's table says includes apartments, and commercial additions, renovations and remodels at $314.2 million 48. On the housing side the city issued 357 permits for new single-family dwellings, which SDPB described as the lowest in ten years, and permitted 403 town house units 4852. The Census Bureau's permit series, as FRED carries it, counts 2,044 units authorized in Minnehaha County and 232 units in Lincoln County in 2025 5455.

The record also names the big projects behind the commercial number. SiouxFalls.Business reported on February 16, 2026 that Smithfield Foods plans a new processing plant at Foundation Park, with construction planned to begin in 2027 and operations as early as late 2028, subject to permits and board approval, and its century-old site to be redeveloped 50. The Board of Economic Development approved a Reinvestment Payment Program grant application from Smithfield Packaged Meats on June 10, 2026 51. A project that depends on the plant's workers or its suppliers cites both dates and the conditions on them.

Industrial and land

Bender Commercial Real Estate Services' annual outlook, as SiouxFalls.Business reported it on February 19, 2026, counts 680,000 sq ft of net industrial absorption in 2025, a record $168 million of industrial sales volume and 1,120 acres of unimproved land sold, the second-strongest year on record 56. Those are a broker's readings for its own market area. An industrial or flex project uses them for direction, and its rent and vacancy for the subject's size and submarket come from the broker's own report or a survey.

Apartments

The South Dakota Multi-Housing Association surveys Sioux Falls apartments twice a year. Its January 2026 survey, as SiouxFalls.Business reported it on March 16, 2026, found overall vacancy of 6.62%, occupancy of 93.38% and market-rate vacancy of 6.41% among the properties surveyed, and the report's headline expected more construction ahead 11. Its July 2025 survey had market-rate vacancy at 5.43% and an average one-bedroom rent of $1,002, excluding properties in lease-up 12. An apartment study treats them as readings, not a trend line, and surveys the competitors around its site.

Homes

Realtor.com's Sioux Falls area, published on its own metro boundary, had a median listing price of $362,200 and 1,469 active listings in September 2026 5758. FHFA's all-transactions index for the Sioux Falls metro area read 365.85 for 2026's second quarter, indexed to 1995:Q1 59.

Air traffic and output

Sioux Falls Regional Airport's 2025 statistics count 754,686 passengers enplaned, 46,696,248 lbs of freight enplaned and deplaned and 18,506 air carrier operations 60. The airport authority's minutes of March 25, 2026 record 62,556 passengers enplaned in February 2026, a record for the month 61, and federal money for the terminal project was approved in February 2026 49. Business travel through the airport is one measure of demand for a hotel near it. BEA's last GDP figure for the Sioux Falls metro area is for 2023, 30,084.975 million dollars, in a series FRED marks as discontinued 62; it shows the metro's size, not its current output.

Not found for Sioux Falls: hotel occupancy and rate, self-storage and senior housing market figures, cap rates, traffic counts on the arterials and an impact fee schedule. A study here buys the hotel or storage data for its trade area and reads the fee from the city at application.

Rapid City

Rapid City and the Black Hills: rally money that moved south, a record airport year and a base taking on a new bomber.

The Black Hills market here is Pennington, Meade and Lawrence counties, all inside South Dakota. Rapid City had a 2024 population of 79,900 in the Census Bureau's estimates as the center's December 2025 bulletin tables them 21.

Employment and pay

The Rapid City metro area had 74,312 covered workers in 2025 at average annual pay of $59,377 63. Pennington County alone had 62,445 covered workers, at average annual pay of $59,768 1053. Ellsworth Air Force Base, beside Rapid City, is taking on the B-21 bomber, and South Dakota News Watch reported on the expansion and its support in the Legislature in February 2024 64; the base's own releases on the program were closed to the research. Which programs finance South Dakota projects, and what each asks a feasibility study to show.
