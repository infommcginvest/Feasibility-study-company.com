Missouri Feasibility Studies

A Missouri feasibility study is written at the county, because the state's growth is people moving in rather than people being born: net domestic migration added about 14,000 residents and net international migration about 12,700 in the year to July 2025 4, with births barely ahead of deaths. Where they settle is a county question the statewide totals cannot answer, so the study locates the customers' county and the businesses now holding them before it sizes the loan, whether the file is SBA 7(a) or 504, USDA or bank credit.

The Missouri Thesis

Missouri now grows only by arrivals, and a demand case should name them.

Between July 2024 and July 2025 births beat deaths in Missouri by just 177, against about 12,700 people added by net international migration and about 14,000 by net domestic migration, in the Census figures USAFacts compiles 4. Over 2020 to 2025, University of Missouri Extension finds deaths ahead of births by more than 12,000, with about 70,000 international and about 57,000 domestic migrants making up the difference and more, for a gain of about 115,000 3. International arrivals were the larger of the two flows over those five years 3. Any projection of household growth in Missouri is therefore a projection that people keep moving in, and a childcare, apartment or senior housing model that carries a county's recent trend forward should say which flow it relies on and show coverage in a year when that flow thins.

The totals that result are small. In the Vintage 2025 estimates Missouri had 6,270,541 residents on July 1, 2025 1, 1.9% above its April 2020 estimates base of 6,154,913 2. At that pace a new hotel, storage facility, car wash or restaurant in a county without growth of its own fills its book by winning customers from operators already trading, and the study has to name who loses them and explain why they switch.

Payrolls are firmer ground than population: MERIC put nonfarm employment at 3,015,700 in August 2026 and unemployment at 3.5% 5. SBA's files hold thousands of 7(a) approvals for Missouri borrowers over the past decade 10, and state agencies are still placing a federal small business credit allocation 1314. What a lender cannot take off the shelf is county proof, meaning where the arrivals settle and who already serves them, and the seven market sections below hold most of what exists.

Statewide Economy

Population, migration and payrolls, read from the latest releases.

Missouri's demand base grows slowly and depends on migration. Its payrolls swung through 2026 before finishing the summer well ahead of a year earlier.

The newest Census vintage also shows the pace easing. Extension's analysis has Missouri growing 0.4% over the twelve months to July 2025, a gain of more than 27,000 people, after 0.6% in the twelve months before 3. The previous vintage had put the July 2024 population at 6,245,466 2; that figure and the Vintage 2025 total come from different releases and are not subtracted here, and a study that compares two years should take both from one vintage. An analysis from 2024 reused at the older rate overstates household growth; a study should state its county estimate's vintage and keep county and trade-area growth apart.

Payrolls moved more than population. MERIC's August 2026 report shows 2,100 jobs added in the month and 34,100 over the year, with unemployment down from 4.0% in August 2025 5. The path there was uneven: the over-the-year change was +1,500 in February 2026, fell to -8,400 in March, and recovered to +18,700 in June and +29,400 in July 5. A study should cite the latest month and show the twelve-month path.

Mining, logging and construction grew in the June release, and so did leisure and hospitality 6; a hotel or restaurant study can cite the second as background, but its case rests on hiring inside its own trade area. A project whose customers work at a plant or a federal installation should name the employer, show its recent headcount from a dated source, and test coverage if that payroll shrinks again.

Statewide survey counts are most easily misread in senior housing. The 2020 to 2024 American Community Survey counts 1,107,743 Missourians aged 65 or older 8, but one building draws from a drive-time ring cut by age band and income, so the statewide pool says nothing about the depth of its own market. The same survey gives Missouri 2,508,227 households, with the median household earning $70,702 and the median renter paying $1,033 in gross rent 8; a rent roll set above its county's own median should name the renters who will pay it. BEA puts Missouri's 2024 per capita personal income at $64,920 9; it comes from another agency and another method, and belongs in a study as its own series rather than beside the survey's household income.

Capital Access

Who lends in Missouri, and what the record says about risk.

Missouri has a deep SBA channel, a federal credit allocation run through state agencies, and bond programs that suit manufacturers better than retailers.

Two counts of Missouri SBA lending circulate, and only one is SBA's. The broker GoSBA Loans, working from SBA data, reports 1,201 Missouri 7(a) loans worth $714.2 million from 156 active lenders in calendar 2025 12, but the same page prints a smaller count and total elsewhere, and the research flags it as internally inconsistent. The federal loan-level files, net of cancellations, hold 8,698 7(a) approvals and 891 504 approvals for Missouri projects between FY2016 and FY2025 10, and a single year's volume belongs to SBA's FY2025 lender activity reports 46. The broker's page still names the banks writing Missouri 7(a) loans.

The federal files also show how Missouri loans ended. Of the loans approved in FY2011 to FY2020, cohorts old enough to have run their course, 7.5% of the 7(a) loans were charged off, counted loan by loan, against 1.6% of the 504 loans 10. A start-up 7(a) request joins the first of those records, and its projection is read against it, so its demand case should rest on evidence gathered independently of the borrower.

Which SBA office reads the file depends on the county. A Greene, Jasper, Taney or Camden County project goes to the Springfield branch of the Kansas City District 18. On the 504 side, SBA's roster of certified development companies includes EDC Loan Corporation in Kansas City and the Economic Development Corp. of Jefferson County 45; the research found no ranking of Missouri CDCs by activity.

The state's own credit programs

Under SSBCI 2.0, Missouri was allocated $95 million, approved on October 11, 2022, with a first tranche of nearly $27 million and about $40 million reserved for businesses owned by socially and economically disadvantaged individuals 13. A second tranche of more than $33 million reached the Missouri Technology Corporation on December 9, 2025 14. By then the state's IDEA Fund had deployed more than $30 million across more than 100 companies, including about $13 million invested directly in 34 firms, and the IgniteMO loan participation program had placed over $10 million 14. IgniteMO, run with Justine PETERSEN since September 2024, buys participations in loans to socially and economically disadvantaged borrowers 15, and a study for such a borrower should model the participation as its own tranche.

Bond finance suits a narrower set of projects. The Missouri Development Finance Board's tax-exempt program excludes commercial, non-manufacturing, real estate, retail and service projects except refundings; its taxable program is open to retail, commercial and industrial borrowers, but every issue needs a letter of credit or other enhancement 16. Cities and counties issue Chapter 100 revenue bonds, and Chapter 349 corporations can issue bonds too 17. Where a private company repays a Chapter 100 issue, the state's guide says bids are not required and wage rates are not in effect 17, so the study prices the contract the borrower signed, not a public-works rate.

SBA in Missouri

The SBA tests Missouri's lake and highway projects run into.

Whether a Missouri file pays the upfront 7(a) fee can turn on its county alone, and a young operator's fuel stop or wash tunnel meets the highest 504 contribution.

Start with the county. In FY2027 a 7(a) loan of $700,000 or less to a business located in a rural area pays no upfront guaranty fee, a waiver category the notice names 69, and the SOP defines a rural area as a county that the U.S. Census Bureau has defined as at least 30 percent rural 20. A diner in the Ozarks or a fuel stop in the Bootheel may qualify on its address alone, so the study should quote the county's published rural share and set the fee line in sources and uses from it.

Then the building. Many projects on this page are start-ups in one-use buildings: fuel stations, travel centers and wash tunnels. The SOP's examples of special purpose property include both car washes and gas stations 20. Where the operating company is two years old or younger, the building is one of those special purpose types and the loan is a 504, the borrower puts in 20% of the project's cost, by SOP 50 10 8.1 20. A strong market finding does not lower that contribution; it decides whether the lender believes the projection the contribution sits behind.

Coverage floors, loan limits and the other contribution levels are set out in the 7(a) and 504 practice. On the study itself, 13 CFR 120.160(b) provides that SBA may require one 19, a discretion rather than a rule, and the note on the feasibility study myth reads the clause word by word.

USDA, HUD, EB-5 and Conventional

USDA, HUD and EB-5 in Missouri: a site test, a tenant law and a tract designation.

Rural status opens two federal guarantees by two different tests, a 2025 state law reaches voucher rent rolls, and EB-5 lay outside the research.

A rural Missouri project may have two federal guarantees open to it, and they test rurality differently: SBA's fee waiver reads the county, while USDA's B&I guarantee reads the agency's eligibility map for the site, and the research screened no Missouri market against that map. It also found no Missouri figures for the program, whether FY2025 guarantee obligations or Community Facilities and REAP awards, and could not identify the state office. A sponsor weighing the two should get the site's USDA status, and the agency's recent Missouri guarantees in the asset class, from Rural Development before the study commits to either. What a USDA reviewer expects is laid out in the practice covering Rural Development lending and the note on applying the five USDA components.

For HUD-insured and agency lending, the research found no record of which HUD multifamily office covers Missouri. The state law that matters to such a file is HB 595, which limits local source-of-income and tenant protections from August 28, 2025 30: a rent roll that counts on a local source-of-income ordinance should be underwritten on state law as it now stands. Bank and agency executions in general are covered under conventional and institutional capital.

EB-5 lay outside the research behind this page; a Missouri sponsor raising EB-5 capital should hold the targeted employment area designation in writing, for the exact tract and date.

Rules by Asset Class

A rewritten wage law, care licenses and a tank fund with an end date.

Missouri's 2025 changes came in its wage and leave law. Its licensing rules reach childcare, long-term care and fuel storage, and no state statute enables or preempts local short-term rental rules.

Wages and leave

The state minimum wage rose to $13.75 on January 1, 2025 and, by reports from July and August 2025, was scheduled to rise to $15.00 on January 1, 2026 2829. HB 567, signed July 10, 2025 and effective August 28, 2025, ended the inflation indexing that would have followed from 2027 and repealed the paid sick leave requirement that voters had put in force on May 1, 2025 29. A budget prepared before mid-2025 may carry an indexed wage and sick leave accruals that no longer apply, so labor cost should be rebuilt from the law now in force.

Childcare

Not every competitor holds a license. The 2023 County Business Patterns count 1,463 Missouri childcare establishments with paid employees 11, a tally most home providers fall outside. The childcare asset page sets out the room-by-room method.

Senior care and health facilities

Certificate of Need, administered by the Missouri Health Facilities Review Committee, reaches facilities licensed under chapter 198, long-term care beds in hospitals and long-term care hospitals 21. SB 337, introduced in 2025, would amend the program 82.

DHSS licenses assisted living and residential care facilities under 19 CSR 30-86, and a facility with an Alzheimer's special care unit must file the disclosures set by section 198.500 RSMo and the sections after it 23. The one staffing figure in the research predates that rule's June 30, 2026 update: AHCA/NCAL's 2019 state summary gives residential care a licensed nurse for at least 8 hours a week per 30 residents 22. Nor did the research find a bed threshold separating the two licenses, which a memory care study has to settle with the department.

Fuel storage

Missouri's tank cleanup cover, the Petroleum Storage Tank Insurance Fund, has a fixed end: 2022 legislation moved its sunset from 2025 to December 31, 2030, unless extended again 26, and the fiscal note on it estimated annual collections at about $16.6 million against annual expenses of about $18.8 million 25. A fuel station or truck stop financed past 2030 should name the cover that would replace the fund and carry its cost in operating expenses.

Lodging and short-term rentals

The research found no state statute that either enables or preempts local short-term rental rules 2783, so a hotel or cabin study reads its own city's ordinance and puts nightly rentals in the competitive set wherever that ordinance allows them.

Costs, Taxes and Insurance

Statutory assessment ratios.

A 2025 House fight over short-term rentals showed how much the assessment class matters in Missouri.

Missouri's assessment classes were argued over on the House floor in 2025. HB 1086, which would have taxed short-term rentals as residential property, passed the House on March 6, 2025, its sponsor framing the dispute as the residential ratio against the commercial one 27; the research, drawing on an advocacy group's undated spring 2025 roundup, records no enactment 83. The ratios themselves are statutory. The Lincoln Institute's state profile, dated November 2016, gives 32% of value for commercial real property, 19% for residential and 33.3% for personal property 31. A study of a nightly rental should show which ratio its county assessor applies to comparable properties rather than assume the lower one.

The research extracted no effective commercial tax rate for Missouri, so the tax line runs from the county assessor's market value, through the ratio that fits the use, to the levy of every taxing district that reaches the parcel.

The FAIR Plan inspects every new property 32. It sells sinkhole cover as a standalone policy and excludes earthquake, the insurance department says 33, so earthquake cover comes from the private market. Whether Missouri carriers raised commercial rates or withdrew in 2025 and 2026 is not in the research, so the premium should come from a carrier's written quote for the building at its full insured value, with the Plan named only where it would carry part of the risk.

Power and Large Projects

The New Florence campus, and a tariff written for loads that size.

Google's announced campus at New Florence, an Amazon project the sources place inconsistently, and the tariff Missouri wrote for very large loads all bear on studies outside Kansas City and St. Louis.

On May 20, 2026 the Governor announced that Google would build a $15 billion air-cooled data center campus at New Florence, in Montgomery County, where site work had already begun 42. The Edison Electric Institute's September 2026 list of large load projects also carries a $3 billion Amazon Web Services data center at a location it gives as Warren 43; Montgomery County approved tax abatements in December 2025 for an AWS project after crowded town halls, and reports place it inconsistently 44.

The first question for a study near New Florence is which campus it is counting: Google's is announced and under way, while the AWS site is not settled, so a model should not add the two together. No source in the research gives either campus's construction workforce, how long it stays, or its operating headcount. A motel, RV park or truck stop nearby should reach its coverage floor on demand that does not depend on the build years, and show any crew demand as a separate line that ends.

Missouri's large-load tariffs bear on how long the operating demand lasts. SB 4 (2025) directed the Public Service Commission to price very large loads, and in November 2025 it approved Evergy and Ameren Missouri tariffs for new loads of 75 MW and above 3435. Ameren's carries a minimum contract of 12 years, an optional five-year ramp, required collateral and a minimum monthly bill with the demand charge set at 80% 34. To a nearby motel those terms are a horizon: a campus that has signed such a contract has committed to a minimum term with collateral behind it, so the study should ask whether and when the campus signed before it carries operating staff into later loan years.

Hazards

Tornadoes, floods and the 2025 declarations.

Missouri's 2025 federal disaster declarations all fell between May and July and included the St. Louis tornado. 2026 has added more.

FEMA counts five presidential disaster declarations for Missouri in 2025, all between May and July, and more than $185 million of federal support across them by May 13, 2026 36; USAFacts counts six 4, so a study should cite FEMA's own list. DR-4877-MO was declared June 9, 2025 for Scott County, St. Louis County and the City of St. Louis after the EF3 tornado of May 16 37. The state added a $100 million special session appropriation for tornado relief, signed June 14, 2025 40.

Earlier storms had their own. Two major disaster declarations for the storms of March 14 to 15 and March 30 to April 8, 2025 were approved by May 23 39, and DR-4876-MO followed on June 9 38. DR-4885-MO, declared July 22, 2025 for the storms and flooding of May 23 to 26, provided Public Assistance only to Dade, Douglas, Ozark, Vernon and Webster counties 38. USAFacts records three federal declarations in Missouri in 2026 through September 3, including a June 30 major disaster for storms, tornadoes and flooding 4.

The recovery money is what a pro forma can misread. Household grants, Public Assistance and the state appropriation pay for rebuilding over several years, and the crews and displaced families they bring may fill rooms and tables in St. Louis City and County or Scott County for a time, so a study there should carry that demand as its own line with an end date. A Missouri file adds two hazard records the research left unpulled, the site's FEMA flood designation and the county's risk scores, and dates both.

Statewide Supply

Why this page publishes almost no statewide supply number.

Apart from the register's establishment counts, the only statewide supply count the research found for Missouri is an old senior care inventory. Everything else on the competitive side is built site by site.

That inventory is HHS's residential care compendium of 2007, which counted 502 residential care facilities with 15,661 units and 114 assisted living facilities with 5,505 units in Missouri 41. Nearly two decades of openings and closures make it useless as a supply figure. A senior housing study counts its competitors from the department's current licensure list and from site visits.

Past the compendium, the research found no statewide Missouri count of apartments, storage space, hotel rooms, industrial availability, senior occupancy, RV sites, licensed childcare slots, hospitals or surgery centers. None of the seven market sections has a hotel occupancy, storage, senior occupancy or RV park figure either, and no MoDOT traffic count was retrieved anywhere. Competitive supply in a Missouri report is therefore a table the study compiles, listing each named competitor with its size, its distance from the site, a dated rate or occupancy reading and where the entry came from, whether a licensure list, a registration roll, a site visit or a phone survey. The traffic past the site is a count the study orders.

Kansas City, Missouri side

Kansas City: data center campuses, a warehouse wave and a growing core city.

Missouri's nine Kansas City counties. Some broker figures below cover the whole two-state metro, and the section says where.

From the Missouri side of State Line Road, the metro takes in Jackson, Clay, Platte, Cass, Bates, Caldwell, Clinton, Lafayette and Ray counties. Johnson and Wyandotte counties have their own section on the Kansas page, and the metro's remaining Kansas counties fall on neither page. Most population, payroll and broker vacancy series for Kansas City count both states together, so a study on this side of the line builds from Missouri county data and from broker submarkets inside Missouri.

The core city is growing

Unlike St. Louis, Kansas City's core city added people: 521,220 residents in the July 2025 estimate, against 508,012 in the April 1, 2020 estimates base, a rise of 2.6% 51. A city-sited project here can show resident growth; it still has to show that the growth reached its own neighborhood, using tract estimates rather than the citywide figure.

Data centers in the Northland and beyond

Google's campus known as Project Mica sits at I-435 and US-169 in the Northland, and Yardi Matrix reported it in March 2026 as a $10 billion investment on 500 acres 70. In Liberty, in Clay County, Metrobloks announced a $1.4 billion data center on March 24, 2026 71. Lambda announced an AI cloud site in Kansas City in October 2025 holding more than 10,000 NVIDIA GPUs, with no dollar figure published 73. In Bates County, Clearway closed $1.5 billion of financing and began building the 650 MW Swan Energy Center solar project in October 2026, according to the trade publication PV Tech 72.

None of the four announcements is a headcount, and Clearway's is a generating plant rather than a load. A Northland lodging, apartment or restaurant study should take a campus's staff and contractors from the operator's statements or its incentive filings, never from its capital cost.

Warehouses

The industrial record here is a list of very large single buildings. Lee & Associates recorded a 912,000 SF Google lease at Carefree Industrial Park in the second quarter of 2026 74. Newmark Zimmer listed speculative buildings under construction that quarter including Building IV at Raymore Commerce Center, at 577,500 SF, and HMBC Logistics VII in the Hunt Midwest Business Center, at 505,440 SF 75. The broker reports cover both states and the research places none of the three, and each address needs confirming before it counts as Missouri supply. Americold opened its import and export cold-chain hub in Kansas City in August 2025 80. An industrial study must list the speculative buildings under way in its own submarket, with delivery dates and pre-leasing, rather than cite a metro vacancy rate that blends in Kansas buildings.

Apartments and the Northland submarket

Northmarq's first-quarter 2026 report shows the Platte County submarket with asking rents up 2.4% over the year and vacancy down 40 bps 79. It is the one Missouri-side apartment reading in the research; the brokers' metro totals take in Kansas submarkets and are left off this page. A multifamily study for Clay or Platte County should pair it with a survey of nearby lease-ups. KCUR reported in July 2026 that Kansas City had eased some energy-efficiency building requirements after developers said they raised costs 59, which a construction budget prepared earlier may not reflect.

Short-term rentals and lodging fees

Kansas City writes its own short-term rental rules. Ordinances passed in May 2023 and effective June 15, 2023 barred non-resident short-term rentals from residential zones and set a $200 registration fee for non-resident operators, indexed to inflation 76. In November 2025 the council re-enacted the rental chapter with a 90-day major-event registration at $50 77, part of its preparation for World Cup visitors 81. Rooms carry two $1.50 per room-night license fees, the second approved by voters on April 4, 2023, and the city has extended the fee to short-term rental units 78. A hotel study should carry those fees, and the city's registration list doubles as a roster of the nightly rentals competing for the same guests.

St. Louis, Missouri side

St. Louis: a defense campus, two innovation districts and a smaller city.

The independent city and the six Missouri counties around it. The metro's Illinois counties across the Mississippi are not part of this section.

On the Missouri bank, St. Louis County and the independent City of St. Louis form the core, with St. Charles, Jefferson, Franklin, Lincoln and Warren counties around them; the Illinois counties belong with Illinois. Broker reports for St. Louis draw the market as each firm chooses, sometimes with Illinois submarkets, so a Missouri-side study should ask for its own county's submarket data rather than quote a metro headline.

The city and its counties

The independent city fell to 278,144 residents by July 2025, down from 301,349 in the Census Bureau's April 2020 estimates base, a loss of 7.7% 51. That decline is the first thing a reviewer will look for in a city-sited study. A project inside the city cannot lean on its resident base, so its demand has to come from jobs, visitors, students or patients, or from a neighborhood that is gaining while the city as a whole loses.

The May 2025 tornado is part of the city's recent record. FEMA put $147 million into St. Louis City and County across the 2025 declarations, and more than $90 million in Public Assistance went to projects stemming from the tornado alone, by May 2026 36. A study in the damaged parts of the city or county should map the damage area and the recovery status of competitors in it.

Boeing and the north county corridor

Boeing employed more than 16,000 people across north St. Louis County, St. Charles County and the city in March 2025, when it won the Air Force's F-47 fighter contract 47. In June 2025 Construction Dive reported that Boeing had hired Jacobs for a $1.8 billion campus expansion at Lambert covering 1.1M SF 48; Boeing does not officially tie the facility to the F-47. The aircraft assembly building occupies a site near I-170, Airport Road and James S. McDonnell Boulevard, according to an undated KSDK report 49, and by July 2026 its roof, exterior panels and hangar doors were in place, with steel started on the central utility plant 50.

A campus of that size feeds hotels, restaurants, fuel stations, apartments and suppliers along the north county corridor, during construction and once production starts, with phases running from 2026 to 2030 48. It is also one employer and one program, so a study that depends on it should cite a dated headcount and test coverage at lower employment.

Cortex and 39 North

The region has two innovation districts.

Neither district has a federal geography, and the research found no population, permit or employment-change series for either. A study near them should build demand from tenant rosters and dated project announcements, not district totals.

Lenders

GoSBA's ranking of 2025 7(a) lending in the city, drawn from SBA data, puts Midwest Regional Bank first by dollars: seven loans totalling $11.3M. Next by dollars came 29 loans worth $6.1M from U.S. Bank and three worth $5.9M from Harvest Small Business Finance 52.

Springfield

Springfield: a health care hub in a growing metro.

The Springfield metro is Christian, Dallas, Greene, Polk and Webster counties. The city's own count barely moved.

Reporting Census figures, the Springfield Business Journal put the metro at 500,694 for July 2025, against 475,435 in April 2020, growth of 5.3% 53; the latest single year added 0.87% 55. The city itself barely moved, at 169,847 in the 2025 estimate and 169,143 in the 2020 count 54. A site elsewhere in the metro can point to the metro's growth; inside the city limits the case rests on hospital payrolls and the patients and visitors the city draws.

The metro counted 234.8k nonfarm jobs in February 2026 (preliminary), according to BLS 56; the research has no over-the-year change for that month, which the study should take from BLS directly. Health care leads the employer lists, with CoxHealth and Mercy running the two big hospital systems, and Springfield Public Schools and Missouri State University are also named among the leading employers by the local relocation site Live in Springfield MO 84.

SBA's Kansas City District runs its Springfield branch from here 18. A city ordinance in place since 2019 caps short-term rentals at 1 STR per 8 houses per block face, and the council reviewed enforcement in 2024 without agreeing on changes, the Springfield News-Leader reported on April 14, 2024 57. The cap helps a hotel study and constrains a rental-home plan, and because the council has already reopened the question once, a study that relies on it should cite the ordinance as of a stated date.

Columbia

Columbia: the university and its hospitals set the demand.

The Columbia metro is Boone, Howard and Cooper counties. Its biggest Boone County employers are mostly public or institutional.

Columbia's largest employers are almost all public or institutional. Columbia REDI's 2025 list, using first-quarter 2024 full-time benefited counts for Boone County, puts the University of Missouri at 9,998, MU Health Care at 8,103, Columbia Public Schools at 3,014, Veterans United Home Loans at 2,847 and the Harry S. Truman Memorial Veterans' Hospital at 2,074 58. Four of the five depend on state or federal budgets or on enrollment. A study here should treat the university's enrollment and the state's appropriation as demand drivers, track both, and test what a hiring freeze would do to the projection.

The city of Columbia counted 130,851 residents in July 2025 54, and the metro grew 0.41% in the year before that 55. Payrolls went the other way, falling 0.51% from December 2024 to December 2025 55, the latest twelve-month change the research found. Permits fell in one month: Missouri Business Alert's report of Census data counts 101 residential permits for Columbia in May 2026, down 17.9% from May 2025 59; the research leaves open whether that is the city or the metro, and permits or units. A Columbia study should date each of these readings to the month and say which one its demand line follows.

For a hotel, revenue should be modeled by month and coverage proven across twelve months.

Jefferson City, Cape Girardeau and Joplin

Three small metros, three separate demand cases.

Three metros far apart, grouped here because the record on each is short. The Cape Girardeau and Joplin metros cross state lines, and this section covers their Missouri counties only.

Jefferson City

The Jefferson City metro takes in Callaway, Cole, Moniteau and Osage counties. City Population's compilation of Census estimates puts Jefferson City at 42,489 in July 2025, below its 2020 census count of 43,254 60. The city is the seat of state government, yet the research found no headcount for the state workforce or any other employer there, so a lodging or office study takes the public payroll from the state's own records and tests coverage with it held flat.

Cape Girardeau

Cape Girardeau's metro pairs Cape Girardeau and Bollinger counties in Missouri with Alexander County, Illinois, which this page leaves out. The research found no city population estimate, permit count or employment series for Cape Girardeau, so a study there starts from Southeast Missouri State University's enrollment and the Census county tables.

Joplin

Joplin, in the southwest corner where Missouri meets Kansas and Oklahoma, had 53,930 residents in July 2025, as the research read the figure from a Census QuickFacts comparison chart and flagged it for checking before use 61. Jasper and Newton are the Missouri counties of its metro; the one employment series found covers the Kansas side as well and is left out. A secondary encyclopedia entry names Mercy's Joplin hospital among the system's largest complexes 85.

St. Joseph

St. Joseph: meat processing, animal health and a hospital campus.

Andrew, Buchanan and DeKalb counties. The metro's fourth county, Doniphan, lies across the river in Kansas and is left out.

Food processing, animal health and health care carry St. Joseph, with the Air National Guard's 139th Airlift Wing as a military anchor; the local development office's employer list names Mosaic Life Care, Triumph Foods, the school district, the airlift wing, Boehringer Ingelheim Animal Health and Missouri Western State University 64, but its headcounts carry no date, so this page leaves them out. The comparison chart that gives Joplin its count puts St. Joseph at 71,001 in July 2025, with the same caveat to check it against census.gov 61.

Two projects show where the economy is adding capacity. Triumph Foods announced an expansion in December 2023 expected to create 88 new jobs with Missouri Works support 62, and Mosaic opened its Plaza 4 outpatient facility in 2026, according to a Wikipedia entry 63. A meat plant expansion supports workforce housing, fuel, food service and trucking demand; an outpatient building supports medical office and nearby lodging for patients and families.

A trade area drawn across the Missouri River into Kansas should say where its customers live and which state's rules apply to the competitors.

Branson and the Lake of the Ozarks

Branson and the Lake of the Ozarks: two tourism regions, two permit booms.

Branson sits in Taney and Stone counties. The Lake of the Ozarks is a separate region, in Camden and Miller counties and beyond.

Branson: Taney and Stone counties

The figures here are for Taney and Stone counties, not for the City of Branson. Builders in Taney County, home to Branson, pulled permits for 863 housing units in 2025, up from 323 in 2024 65. Stone County permitted 212 in 2025 against 195 the year before 66. New housing in Taney means more year-round households, or more short-term rentals competing with hotels, and the study should find out which before it counts the permits as demand.

A Branson-area study should model revenue by month. City tourism tax collections are useful evidence for a site inside the city; a site in unincorporated Taney or Stone County needs county-level evidence instead. The Missouri restaurant case under SBA 7(a) works through the method a restaurant here would follow, from covers and average check to loan coverage, and the restaurant asset page explains it in general terms.

The Lake of the Ozarks: Camden and Miller counties

The Lake of the Ozarks is its own market, well north of Branson. Permits in Camden County covered 419 housing units in 2025, up from 272 in 2024 67; Miller County permitted 86 against 88 68. The research does not say who is buying Camden's new housing, and that mix decides how much of it is year-round demand for marinas and restaurants. Morgan County, also on the lake, has no permit figure in the research. A study at the lake should model revenue by month and count private docks and nightly rentals among the competition. The marina method and the one for boat and RV storage are written in general terms, with inland-lake benchmarks rather than Missouri ones.

What to Verify

What a Missouri lender should check before relying on a study.

Five points the research could not settle, each able to move a number or a date in the credit memo. The study should answer each from a named, dated record.

  1. Licenses and approvals still open

    Whether SB 337 changed Certificate of Need 82, what the residential care staffing rule requires after its June 2026 update, a childcare center's inspections before opening, and any local liquor quota are unsettled in the research. Each can move an opening date; the study should cite the agency's current answer and its date.

  2. Employer headcounts with a date

    The research holds no statewide ranking of private employers, no ranked list for Kansas City, nothing for St. Louis beyond Boeing, and Springfield and St. Joseph lists without dates. A demand case built on an employer should take its headcount from a dated record, such as a filing, an incentive agreement or a release, and say how old it is.

  3. Utility capacity below the large-load line

    Missouri's large-load tariffs reach only the biggest users, and for everyone else the research found no interconnection queue, statewide water allocation limit or sewer moratorium at all; that silence settles nothing. For a wash tunnel or a food plant, the study quotes the connection terms each utility has put in writing, and when.

  4. Hard costs with no Missouri benchmark

    The research found no construction cost index for Missouri cities, nothing on how sales tax applies to building materials and equipment, no impact fee range and no general prevailing wage rule beyond the Chapter 100 guidance. Contractor bids and the city's fee schedule stand in for them.

  5. Permit counts by metro

    No official 2025 permit total was found for any of the seven metros. A study that cites housing growth should pull the Census building permits survey for the counties in its own trade area.

Missouri Questions

Missouri feasibility study questions.

What demand case is left for a Missouri project when its county has stopped growing?

The decision in the file is whether a capture case is accepted in place of a growth case, and on what evidence. Missouri's own growth slowed to 0.4% over the year to July 2025, against 0.6% twelve months before 3; a county without even that much is a market in which every customer already belongs to someone. The evidence is then a dated reading of each named operator's rate, occupancy or waiting list, a reason for the switch, whether price, distance, the age of the stock or a closure, and coverage shown on that share alone, with no growth line in the model at all.

Which month's Missouri jobs figure belongs in a 2026 study?

The decision is the as-of date of the demand evidence, because 2026 gave two different states depending on the month. MERIC's payroll change from a year earlier read -8,400 in March 2026 and +29,400 by July 5, so a file built on the March release described a state losing jobs and one built on July's a state adding them, with nothing changed at the site. The study should print the data month beside every statewide series, pull the latest release again before the file closes, and show coverage on both readings, so the loan does not rest on which month the research happened to be done.

When does payroll start in a Missouri childcare feasibility study?

At the first room's first hire; the decision is whether loan proceeds and sponsor cash carry that payroll while rooms are licensed but not full. Missouri's staffing ratios fix how many staff each room needs by the children's ages 24, and July and August 2025 reports had the wage floor scheduled to reach $15.00 an hour on January 1, 2026 2829, so a half-empty infant room costs what the rule says, not what the budget says. The study should show payroll month by month from first hire to first tuition, run an infant-heavy mix and a slower fill, and mark the month coverage is first reached.

Which months of 2025 can a study in tornado-struck St. Louis use as its base year?

The decision is where the base year is cut, because 2025 in the damaged parts of St. Louis City and County is two periods. The EF3 tornado struck on May 16 and DR-4877-MO was declared on June 9, 2025 37; readings taken before the storm describe a competitive set the storm then changed, and readings taken after it describe a market with rebuilding under way. The study should say on which side of May 16 each comparable's rate or occupancy reading was taken, choose a base period the file states and defends, and show coverage on demand that would remain once rebuilding ends, with recovery-period demand kept out of the base.

Is an IgniteMO participation counted as equity in a Missouri feasibility study?

No. A participation is part of the loan, not of the project's equity, so the study shows coverage on the whole loan. Since September 2024 IgniteMO has bought loan participations aimed at socially and economically disadvantaged owners 15, and the Missouri Technology Corporation received the second SSBCI tranche, more than $33 million, on December 9, 2025 14. The study then keeps the demand case the size it would be without the participation, shows the combined debt service, and dates the program terms it relies on.
