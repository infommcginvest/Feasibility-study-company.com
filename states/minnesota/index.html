Minnesota Feasibility Studies

A Minnesota feasibility study is judged on one trade area rather than on the state, whose 5,830,405 residents on July 1, 2025 1 and +38,374 private sector jobs over the year to August 2026 2 only set the backdrop. A lender reads the file for who employs the site's households, what competes with it today and what its insurer will quote, with the average homeowners premium at $3,987 by June 2026 4. Many Minnesota project rules are local, so the site's city and county matter alongside its market: Eagan's one-year pause on new large data centers, adopted in February 2026 18, stops at Eagan's line, and a hotel's tax stack or a bar's license quota can change at the next city limit.

The Minnesota Thesis

Where a Minnesota site sits decides which rules apply.

Two kinds of rule bear on a Minnesota project, and they sort by geography. The state holds the gate on new hospital beds, which only the Legislature can open, and the 2026 session enacted such an exception 9. A 2025 statute directs the Public Utilities Commission to create a rate class for very large electric loads by December 15, 2026 16. Those rules apply wherever the site is.

Much of the rest is local. A bar's license comes from its city or county, inside a cap that statute ties to population 39; a short-term rental answers to whatever its city or county has adopted, with no state license above it 45; a hotel's tax bill depends on the local layers its city has added 14; and Eagan in February 2026 and Minneapolis in May 2026 adopted pauses of their own on large data centers, while statewide moratorium bills died on May 18, 2026 171855.

For a sponsor weighing two or three sites, jurisdiction therefore enters the comparison early. Sites a short drive apart can sit under different license quotas, different lodging tax stacks and different policies on large power loads, so a study that compares locations names the city and county of each and the rules each imposes, beside its trade area. The demand base under those rules is large and growing slowly: the Vintage 2025 estimate from the Census Bureau counts 5,830,405 residents on July 1, 2025, against 5,706,733 at the April 2020 base 1.

The population story is one of slowing arrivals. Minnesota added about 33,000 residents in the year to July 2025, growth of +0.6%, after a total increase of 44,400 the year before 2425. Arrivals from abroad fell, with net international migration at +12,486 against 33,400 a year earlier 2425, while domestic migration turned positive at +8,300 25, the first net gain from other states this decade by the Star Tribune's reading 24. Two consequences follow for a demand model. Arrivals from abroad should be carried at the 2025 level, not the 2024 one. And the turn in domestic movement, small as it is, is new: a study that counts on households moving in from other states should show where in Minnesota they are settling, because the statewide estimate does not say.

Jobs are the firmer foundation, and the table shows the gains gathering in institutions. Education and health services added the most jobs and government also grew 2122, the sectors of the hospital systems, the university and the state agencies that fill the employer table below. Information and financial activities show the steepest percentage losses in the table 2123. In Minnesota, then, hiring around a hospital campus or a public institution is borne out by the statewide series, while a demand case that rests on a bank, an insurer or a media firm runs against it and needs that employer's own figures.

The incomes behind that demand are solid. The 2020-2024 American Community Survey counts 2,307,955 Minnesota households and puts their median income at $89,062 26; for 2024, per capita personal income from the Bureau of Economic Analysis came to $75,603 27. The ACS labor force of 3,119,042 26 is the pool every employer competes for, which is why wage assumptions matter as much as demand in a labor-intensive project. The same ACS release counts 992,385 residents aged 65 and over 26, the base from which any senior housing or memory care study builds its age- and income-qualified pool before narrowing to a primary market area.

Capital Access

Where Minnesota credit comes from, and what it can and cannot replace.

SBA volume grew in FY2025, the state layers its own guarantee and participation programs on top, and USDA's largest recent Minnesota money went to energy rather than business lending.

The SBA's Minnesota District Office 28 reported 1,822 approved 7(a) and 504 loans in FY2025 3. Of those, 238 were 504 loans supporting total project financing exceeding $249 million 3. Together the two programs approved more than $936 million that year 3, up from $846 million in FY2024 3, so SBA credit in Minnesota expanded in the year the current program rules were being rewritten. The research found no separate FY2025 count of 7(a) loans for the district and no ranking of its most active lenders; the SBA's FY2025 year-end 7(a) and 504 activity reports are where a study should take a 7(a) count from, and the awards below are SBA Minnesota's own recognitions.

Between FY2016 and FY2025, net of cancellations, lenders approved 14,475 7(a) loans worth $5,611.1 million for Minnesota projects 29. Because the federal file is loan by loan, with an industry code and a county on each record, a Minnesota study can look for earlier SBA financings of the same kind of business in the same part of the state and cite them as precedent.

CDCs carry the 504 side. A regional development company in the southeast of the state, the Southeastern Minnesota 504 Development Corporation, has 267 loans worth $175.1 million on record from FY2010 to March 2026 by one secondary tally of SBA data 30. In the SBA's own 2025 recognitions, Midwest Business Finance Corporation was named for 504 loan volume for the eleventh year running and AMPLIO for job creation, with U.S. Bank and Frandsen Bank & Trust recognized among lenders by loan count and the Entrepreneur Fund as microlender of the year 31. Those are statewide awards for the year to June 30, 2025, not market rankings.

The state's own credit layer

Minnesota's allocation under the federal SSBCI program is run by DEED. The second round can reach $97 million and arrives in tranches; the first round, in 2010, brought $15 million 32. What they change is who holds the loss: the guarantee puts the state behind part of a bank loan, and a participation makes DEED a holder of a share of the note. Neither adds a customer, so the market chapter of a Minnesota study carries the same weight with the state involved as without it.

USDA money in Minnesota

USDA Rural Development works from a St. Paul state office 34. Its largest recent commitment was not business lending: in January 2025 the agency announced $825.3 million in New ERA clean energy awards for 9 projects in rural Minnesota, of which $794 million went to Great River Energy for 1,275 MW of renewable procurement across Minnesota and North Dakota 35. The latest dated Community Facilities announcement the research found was $3.8 million in January 2022 36. REAP applications funded under the Inflation Reduction Act were paused on December 19, 2024 37. For Business and Industry guarantees the research found no FY2025 Minnesota total; Rural Development's state fiscal-year reports are the place a B&I study should ask for one. The energy awards change the backdrop of cooperative service territories without adding business credit.

Rules by Asset Class

Where Minnesota law decides first: beds, liquor licenses and fuel sites.

Several Minnesota asset classes are governed by statute before they are governed by demand: moratoria on hospitals and nursing home beds, liquor license quotas tied to population, and a petroleum cleanup fund with its own balance sheet.

Health care and senior housing

Minnesota repealed certificate of need in 1984 and replaced it, for hospitals, with a construction moratorium under Minn. Stat. 144.551 8. New hospital beds therefore need a statutory exception, and in 2026 the Legislature enacted one in Laws 2026, chapter 91 9. A second moratorium, in Minn. Stat. 144A.071, governs the certification of nursing home beds, with exceptions through 144A.073 10. For a lender, a skilled nursing or hospital study in Minnesota begins with the approval pathway. A demand analysis that shows unmet need is necessary but not sufficient, because the beds cannot be licensed without the exception.

Assisted living is different. Since August 1, 2021 Minnesota has issued two licenses, one for an assisted living facility and one for an assisted living facility with dementia care 38. A memory care study should name the license the building is designed to hold and budget for that category rather than a blend of the two.

Restaurants, bars and liquor

Liquor licenses in Minnesota are issued by cities and counties, with fees set locally, but statute caps how many a city may issue 39. A study for a restaurant or bar should confirm that a license is available, or name the license being transferred, before it projects alcohol sales.

Gas stations and convenience stores

Any acquisition of an older fuel site starts with the environmental file. The Petrofund has no sunset date 40. Its board reported an unencumbered balance of $17,227,238 as of August 22, 2025, after paying $4,657,120 on 272 claims in FY2025 41, and it imposed a fee from September 1 to December 31, 2025 40. For a lender the fund is a backstop, not a guarantee: the study should identify whether the site has an open release, whether a claim is pending and what the borrower's share of eligible cost would be.

Labor and lodging

Minnesota's minimum wage applies to all employers at $11.41 an hour from January 1, 2026, with a 90-day training wage of $9.31 for workers under 20 42. The rate was $11.13 in 2025 and is indexed annually with a cap of 5% 43; one law firm reports the 2027 rate at $11.87 44. New meal and rest break rules took effect on the same January 2026 date 43. A labor-intensive business, whether a restaurant, a child care center or an assisted living building, should model wages at the published indexed rate for each projection year rather than holding them flat. There is no statewide short-term rental license, so rules come from each city or county 45.

Costs and Insurance

Property tax, insurance and construction tax: the cost lines reviewers question.

Minnesota classifies commercial property for heavier taxation, its homeowners insurance costs rose faster than any state's, and its sales tax treats construction materials in a way that changes the budget.

Minnesota taxes commercial property more heavily than homesteads through its classification system. The Minnesota Center for Fiscal Excellence puts the commercial classification ratio at 1.844 for taxes payable in 2023 46. In plain terms, a commercial building pays tax on a larger share of its value than a house of the same value in the same jurisdiction. A study should therefore build the property tax line from the actual levy and classification for the parcel, not from a rule-of-thumb percentage of cost, and should check whether the parcel sits in a city whose levy is rising fastest. The Brainerd Lakes section below shows how much those levies can differ inside one county.

For insurance, the research found published Minnesota rate data only for homeowners coverage, where the average premium reached $3,987 by June 2026 4 after the steep 2025 increase. The FAIR Plan, created under Minn. Stat. 65A.31 to 65A.42, writes dwellings, homeowners, commercial and farm property as the insurer of last resort 13. Because the research found no published change in commercial property rates, no count of FAIR Plan policies and no record of carrier withdrawals, a study should replace any inflation-based insurance assumption with a quote for the subject, dated within the study period.

Construction budgets have a Minnesota-specific tax feature. Contractors pay sales tax on the materials they incorporate into real property, and no tax is charged on the construction contract itself 47. The capital equipment exemption covers equipment and the special-purpose buildings and foundations that hold it, not general building materials 48. The exemption applies up front, at purchase 48, so a budget should carry qualifying equipment without sales tax and general building materials with it. For hard costs the study needs the contractor's bid and a second opinion on it; the research holds no state cost index to check either against.

FEMA declared DR-4894 for the Leech Lake Band of Ojibwe on October 22, 2025, and authorized fire management assistance for the Munger Shaw Fire on May 14, 2025; the prior major declaration, DR-4797, followed the June 2024 flooding 50. A site-level study should close that gap with the parcel's flood map panel, the county risk profile and, for a lakeshore or river site, the shoreland and floodplain ordinance, before the insurance quote is obtained.

Statewide Supply

Two statewide counts frame Minnesota supply; the competitor survey does the rest.

The ACS tenure split and the County Business Patterns establishment counts cover the whole state on one definition. For vacancy, pipeline or occupancy in any major property class, the research found little.

Housing tenure is the one supply-side split this page can give for the whole state. The 2020-2024 ACS counts 641,268 renter-occupied homes in Minnesota against 1,666,687 owner-occupied ones 26. Renters are a minority of Minnesota households, so a rental project outside the larger centers should measure its own renter pool rather than assume one. The ACS median rent for the same period is a lagged, all-units figure that includes older and subsidized stock; a multifamily study should set it beside asking rents on new buildings and explain the spread rather than let one stand in for the other.

For businesses, County Business Patterns for 2023 counts 10,016 restaurant establishments in Minnesota 52, and the same release covers child care centers, hotels and campgrounds. An establishment is a firm at a location, not a seat, a room or a licensed slot. The count can bound a competitive set; it cannot size one.

Sizing it falls to the study's own survey, and Minnesota's licensing records give that survey a starting list for two classes: the state's child care licensing lookup for centers and family homes, and the Department of Health's licensed establishment lists for campgrounds and RV parks. Every competitor found should carry its size, rate and occupancy and the date it was gathered. The market sections below show how much a study can borrow from published local data. The Twin Cities have broker and vendor reports and CoStar hotel figures, Moorhead's city report counts units in progress, and several regional markets have little published local data. No statewide series for apartment vacancy, hotel pipeline, self-storage, senior housing occupancy or licensed child care capacity appears in the research.

2025 to 2026

What changed in Minnesota between early 2025 and autumn 2026.

Two dated threads run through the period: the state's answer to data center power demand, and SBA's rule changes.

Data centers and large electric loads

A 2025 law, Minn. Stat. 216B.1623, set up a large-load electric customer class for loads of at least 100 MW, with the Public Utilities Commission due to establish the class by December 15, 2026 16. The same law ended the sales tax exemption on data center electricity after June 30, 2025, while keeping the equipment exemption 1653. Xcel filed a large-load tariff in July 2025, and the PUC required Dakota Electric to file one in December 2025 54. Statewide moratorium bills died when the Legislature adjourned on May 18, 2026 55, after Eagan had adopted its own pause in February and before Minneapolis adopted one on May 21 1718, both covered in the Twin Cities section. Until the PUC rules on the class, the Xcel and Dakota Electric tariff dockets are where the price and timing of serving big loads will be settled, and no published document in the research shows an ordinary borrower in those territories how its own service upgrade will be charged.

SBA rule changes

A Minnesota lender's own summary notes that the 2025 SOP took effect on June 1, 2025, that the credit-score prescreen was retired on March 1, 2026, and that from July 4, 2026 an outstanding 7(a) balance no longer reduces a borrower's 504 availability 56. Of the provisions in force under SOP 50 10 8.1 with Technical Policy Updates 57, two bear on the Minnesota projects this page discusses most. A start-up borrowing on a standard 7(a) loan has to show its projections reaching 1.15x coverage within the period the SOP allows, while a small 7(a) loan has a coverage test of its own. A 504 borrower whose business is two years old or less and whose building is a limited or special purpose property carries the 20% equity tier 57. Minnesota files meet the remaining rules too; they are covered by the SBA 7(a) and 504 practice area. A study written under the earlier SOP should be rechecked against the current one before closing.

Minneapolis-St. Paul

The Twin Cities: tight industrial, recovering apartments, lagging hotels.

The metro spans two states, but only its Minnesota counties are treated here; Pierce and St. Croix counties in Wisconsin fall outside this page.

As the federal (OMB) metro delineation used by the site's market register draws it, the Minnesota side of Minneapolis-St. Paul is Anoka, Carver, Chisago, Dakota, Hennepin, Isanti, Le Sueur, Mille Lacs, Ramsey, Scott, Sherburne, Washington and Wright counties. Wisconsin has its own state page. DEED reports metro payrolls only for the whole two-state metro, Pierce and St. Croix counties included, so this section does not carry that series; a Twin Cities study should build its employment case from Minnesota county data. The broker and CoStar market figures below describe the Twin Cities as each firm draws the market, which the research does not split by state. No 2025 estimate for the whole metro turned up in the research. Hennepin County, its largest, was estimated at 1,284,784 in July 2025 against 1,281,565 at the 2020 Census 58, and the State Demographic Center put the City of Minneapolis at 435,246 in 2025 59. The core county is barely growing, so a study of a core site needs to show demand from existing residents and workers, not from growth.

Residential construction tells the same story. The Twin Cities area permitted 8,611 housing units on 6,387 permits in 2024, with single-family building the strongest segment 60; the research found no 2025 count. On the employment side, the named projects are medical devices and logistics: Boston Scientific's Maple Grove expansion was reported as advancing in May 2026 at $131M, 52,500 sq ft and 440 jobs 61, and an Amazon build-to-suit of 3.6M sq ft was under construction in the second quarter 62.

The apartment market is absorbing well and building less. Strong first-half absorption and a moderate pipeline support a lease-up assumption for a well-located new building; what neither Yardi nor the brokers publish is a vacancy rate, so the lease-up pace in a study has to rest on the buildings it will actually compete with. The two brokers' industrial vacancy readings differ, and a study should cite both and say which submarket definition it follows; Colliers flags the South Central and Southwest submarkets and bulk warehouse product as the places where new supply is pressing on vacancy 62. Most of the industrial pipeline is build-to-suit, which is good news for a speculative small-bay project and a warning for a large speculative box. Retail is a story of two markets: suburban centers are tight while urban storefronts carry far higher vacancy, a gap that should drive the rent and lease-up assumptions for any urban mixed-use ground floor.

Hotels are the weak segment. CoStar reported January 2026 occupancy of 50.6% (+17.5%) and RevPAR of $63.01 (+25.9%) 67, but attributed part of the gain to federal-agent activity and protests, a one-off driver. In November 2025 the market's 51.9% occupancy ranked last among the 25 largest U.S. markets 68. A hotel study here should not annualize the January jump; it should start from the 2025 base and show what has to happen for the gap to close.

Local taxes and data center pauses

A $1 million commercial property in Minneapolis paid an effective rate of 2.36% in 2024, a bill of $28,264, and 2.19% without the classification effect 69. Minneapolis may levy combined lodging taxes up to 13% on hotels of 50 or more rooms, by a 2019 authorization 70. Eagan adopted the state's first one-year moratorium on new large data centers in February 2026 18, and Minneapolis followed in May with a six-month pause, through November 21, 2026, on facilities over 350,000 sq ft 17. Neither stops an ordinary commercial project, but both signal that utility capacity and land use around large loads are contested in the core metro.

Rochester

Rochester: a construction boom on a flat payroll.

The Rochester metro (Olmsted, Dodge, Fillmore and Wabasha counties) is built around one institution, and the evidence splits cleanly into what that institution is building and what everyone else is.

Mayo Clinic's downtown program, "Bold. Forward. Unbound.", is budgeted at $5B across five buildings of about 2.4 million square feet, with all five sites under active construction in January 2026 and completion planned for 2030 71. Two nine-story clinical buildings account for about 1.8 million square feet 72. A retail or restaurant study near the campus has to say how the building sites move foot traffic past the site.

The construction figures are large. Through October 2025 the city recorded permit valuation of $1.06B, of which $423.5M was commercial and $113.1M residential, and around 400 new housing units including 158 single-family permits 73. Yet the metro's payrolls rose by just +43 jobs (0.0%) in the year to August 2026 15. That combination is the core underwriting issue in Rochester. Permit value measures money spent on buildings, not people added to payrolls, and the clinical and support staff who would rent apartments and fill restaurants follow the openings scheduled through 2030. The demand ramp in a study should follow the opening dates of named buildings and show how the project performs if one of them slips by a year.

Olmsted County was estimated at 166,731 residents in July 2025, against 162,847 in 2020 74. The city has required permits for residential re-roofing, re-siding and window replacement since May 1, 2025 75. The research found no measure of Rochester hotel occupancy or apartment vacancy, so a lodging study there needs operating data from comparable properties and an apartment study a survey of the buildings it will compete with.

Duluth

Duluth: two health systems, a lake and a heavy lodging tax.

Duluth is read here through St. Louis and Carlton counties, its Minnesota side in the OMB delineation the site's register uses; Douglas County and Superior, in Wisconsin, are not part of this page.

Duluth's economy rests on health care and tourism, and both are visible in the evidence. Essentia Health, the city's largest employer, commissioned a Northspan analysis that credits its Twin Ports operations with more than $4.5B in economic output and more than 14,800 direct and indirect jobs 76. It is a self-published impact study, but it frames the scale of the anchor. Aspirus committed at least $300M over eight years to strategic projects at St. Luke's when the two systems merged in 2024 77. Medical office, senior housing and workforce rentals near either campus have a documented employer behind them.

The broader economy is hard to read from published series: DEED's payroll count covers the two-state Duluth-Superior metro, Douglas County included, so this page does not use it. The Duluth evidence the research found centers on the hospitals and the lodging trade; for a warehouse, storage or retail file in the city it found no local benchmark, and the study has to supply its own.

Lodging is the best-measured sector. The city's tax receipts rebounded: the 3% hotel and motel tax brought in $212,756 (+18.5%) on January 2026 sales, against $179,492 a year earlier, and the additional 2.5% tax on larger properties $140,438 (+12.0%) 78. Monthly tax receipts are an imperfect proxy, but they are public, timely and specific to the city, and a hotel study should chart them against its projected revenue path.

St. Cloud

St. Cloud: a health anchor that restructured.

The St. Cloud metro covers Benton and Stearns counties. It lost more jobs than any other Minnesota metro over the latest year.

St. Cloud's population grew to 205,854 in July 2025, by a secondary restatement of Census estimates 79. Its payrolls went the other way: down 876 jobs, or -0.8%, from August 2025 to August 2026 15. Much of the explanation is one employer. CentraCare, at 7,541 employees in August 2025 by a chamber figure that KNSI reported 80, while the chamber's own undated list gives a smaller count for the hospital and clinics alone 81, announced that month that it would cut 535 positions across 44 locations, about 375 in administrative and support roles and about 160 in patient care 80. Not all of those locations are in the metro, but the restructuring of the dominant health system is the event any St. Cloud study must address.

The rest of the employer base is public and industrial: the chamber's list names the State of Minnesota (through St. Cloud State, the technical college and corrections), the St. Cloud VA Health Care System, the area school district and a bus manufacturer, though its counts are undated 81. That is a stable but slow-growing base.

Two further features bear on financing. St. Cloud Financial Credit Union was SBA Minnesota's 2025 credit union lender of the year 31, so a lender based in the market has recent SBA activity. And the City of St. Joseph lists small-issue industrial development bonds for manufacturers among its incentives, subject to a state allocation 82, which gives a plant expansion a financing route alongside 504.

Mankato

Mankato: the fastest payroll growth in Minnesota.

The Mankato metro covers Blue Earth and Nicollet counties. Moorhead is a separate market in the Red River Valley, with its own passage below.

Mankato metro payrolls grew by +2,194 (+3.9%) in the year ending August 2026, the fastest pace of any Minnesota metro 15. The employer base behind that growth spans several industries. Manufacturing, health care, higher education and state institutions do not all move together, which makes the base more resilient than a single-anchor market.

Set against that growth, the published record is thin and dated. Neither county has, in the research, a 2025 population estimate, a permit count or a figure for any property type. The best job growth in the state is therefore a claim a Mankato study has to connect to its own site: where the new hires live, which properties they already fill, and what rents and rates those properties achieve.

Iron Range

The Iron Range: one industry, idled and restarted.

The Iron Range is measured here by St. Louis and Itasca counties. Because St. Louis County also contains Duluth, county totals overstate the Range.

The Range's credit story is the taconite cycle. Hibbing Taconite employed more than 700 people before it was idled 83. In March 2025 Cleveland-Cliffs filed WARN notices covering 597 workers at Minorca and Hibbing Taconite combined, 342 at Minorca near Virginia and 255 at Hibbing Taconite 19. In September 2026, Mesabi Metallics began mining at Nashwauk in Itasca County, the first new taconite mine in Minnesota in 50 years, on a $2.5B project supported by a $770M Export-Import Bank loan 20.

For a lender the two events do not net out cleanly. Idled plants remove payroll from specific towns, while a new mine adds construction and then operating jobs in another; a convenience store in Virginia and a hotel in Grand Rapids face different versions of the same cycle. DEED keeps no metro payroll series for the Range, and the research turned up no local population, permit or space figures; borrowing Duluth's numbers would fold a health care and tourism economy into a mining one. The evidence a Range study can stand on is the status of each mine that feeds its trade area, documented from company filings and news reports, and a downside case in which the nearest operation idles again.

Itasca County is one of the counties named in the SBA's October 2025 administrative disaster declaration 51; the hazards section above covers what that means for a borrower's existing debt.

Brainerd Lakes

Brainerd Lakes: a county built on seasonal property.

Crow Wing County is the unit of measure for the Brainerd Lakes market: its tax roll, its levy decisions and the lakes area's tourism budget are the published evidence.

Crow Wing County's tax base shows what kind of market this is. Its taxable market value for taxes payable in 2026 is $21,286,099,053, of which 48.23% is residential and 37.12% seasonal property 84. A county where seasonal cabins and lake homes carry that share of value has a demand profile unlike any metro: population and spending swell in summer and on weekends, and many of the households that support restaurants, marinas, fitness and retail are not counted as residents. A study here has to model seasonal demand explicitly, month by month, and should not divide annual sales by year-round population to judge a market's depth.

New construction added $260,310,600 of value, +1.22%, to the 2026 roll 84. Levies are rising quickly: the county set a +6.49% increase for 2026 84 and a preliminary +4.99% for 2027, while the same September 2026 report put the City of Brainerd's levy change at +9.99%, without restating the levy year 85. Property tax is a meaningful operating cost for any owner-occupied business, and a study should take the levy of the subject's own city and school district rather than a county average. Because no 2025 series the research reached measures the county's population, jobs or building permits, the tax roll is the best public record of where money is going in this market.

Tourism marketing was itself contested. After a dispute over lodging tax payments, the destination marketing organization rebranded for 2026 as Visit Brainerd Baxter, and the City of Brainerd's contribution to its budget is $20,000 86. For a hotel or campground study, that history means the marketing budget behind the destination is not guaranteed year to year.

Rosemount

Rosemount: the state's first hyperscale data center.

Rosemount sits inside the Twin Cities metro, so the broker and vendor readings in the Minneapolis-St. Paul section are the closest published market benchmarks for it.

Meta chose Rosemount in 2024 for an $800M data center of 715,000 sq ft on 280 acres at UMore Park, with about 100 operational jobs and at least 1,000 construction workers at peak 87. The company had bought the land from the University of Minnesota for $39.7M, and the city council approved the project unanimously in December 2023, when it was valued at about $700M 88. In July 2026 the building was nearly complete, with the city expecting completion later that summer 89. The site sits near Highway 52 and County Road 42 90.

Once it opens, a data center generates little local demand: an operating staff of the size Meta announced supports little housing or retail. The building phase was the opposite: a peak crew of that size fills extended-stay rooms, rented houses and lunch counters for as long as it is on site, and that business ends with the work. A hotel or apartment study that cites the data center has to date the demand it claims to the construction schedule, which is now closing out. On utilities, an interviewee told InForum that data centers pay for the upgrades that serve them rather than passing the cost to existing Xcel customers 89, which matters for any business on the same circuits.

Local housing evidence is thin and old: Rosemount issued 27 permits for 32 units in December 2023 91. The research found nothing newer for the city, not even a population estimate, so a Rosemount file takes its market benchmarks from the Twin Cities section and earns its site case through fieldwork in the south metro.

Lender Checklist

Gaps a Minnesota credit officer should see closed.

The research behind this page came up empty on the points below. A sound study fills each one from a source it names, or tells the lender the gap is still open.

  1. A competitor survey with a date on it

    Outside the Twin Cities, published local supply figures are rare in the research, and no statewide occupancy or pipeline series turned up for any major class. The lender should see each competing property in the primary market area named in the study, with the month the survey was taken.

  2. The right employment series

    The research found DEED metro payroll changes for Minneapolis-St. Paul, Rochester, Duluth, St. Cloud and Mankato (the first and third span two states), and none for the Iron Range, Brainerd Lakes or Moorhead's side of Fargo. A study outside those five metros should use county Quarterly Census of Employment and Wages data and say so, and should cite employer headcounts with the list and date they come from.

  3. A dated insurance quote and actual levies

    No commercial property insurance rate change was found in the research, and the county and city levies on this page moved by several points in a single year. The lender should see a dated premium quote for the building being financed and the current levy of each taxing district over the parcel.

  4. Utility capacity in writing

    The large-load class is still pending, two cities have paused large data centers, and no MISO or Minnesota queue data surfaced in the research. The study should carry the serving utility's written statement of the capacity available at the site, dated in 2026.

  5. Environmental history for fuel sites

    For any older gas station or convenience store, the MPCA release record, the status of any Petrofund claim and whether the site is among the leaded-gasoline sites under re-evaluation.

Minnesota Questions

Minnesota feasibility study questions.

What should a Twin Cities feasibility study show that a statewide figure cannot?

The ground each benchmark was drawn on, so a credit officer can decide whether to accept it. A broker's vacancy reading describes the metro as that firm maps it, so the study should name the submarket the subject competes in before borrowing a rent or an absorption pace. Retail shows the stakes: suburban centers stood 5.5% vacant in Q2 2026 while urban storefronts stood at 13.3% 65, so an urban ground floor needs urban comparables. A metro payroll change, which DEED reports with the Wisconsin counties inside, does not carry a Minnesota site; the study goes back for county data.

Can a new nursing home or hospital be built in Minnesota?

Only through a door the state opens, so the lender's first decision is whether that door is open. Assisted living runs on a state license in two categories, and a memory care building applies for the dementia care license. Hospital beds sit behind a construction moratorium to which only the Legislature can grant an exception, as it did in 2026 89, and certified nursing home beds behind a moratorium of their own with exceptions granted through a defined process 10. A credit officer should make the exception or the license a condition that precedes the study, and read the market chapter as sizing a building that can be licensed.

What does the rise in Minnesota home insurance mean for a commercial project's premium line?

A warning, not a benchmark. The Minnesota rate series the research found cover homeowners only: the average premium rose 34% in 2025 11 and stood at $3,987 by June 2026 4. For commercial property the research found no rate change in print. Insurers may file and use rates without prior approval from Commerce 12, and the FAIR Plan is the last-resort carrier for commercial property 13. The premium line should be a quote for the subject from the borrower's agent, dated inside the study period.

Which Minnesota markets does this page cover?

Eight markets, each on the Minnesota side of its boundary. Where a metro crosses into Wisconsin the section stops at the state line: the Twin Cities section works from thirteen Minnesota counties and leaves its two Wisconsin counties out, and the Duluth section reads St. Louis and Carlton counties without Superior; Wisconsin has a state page of its own. Rochester, St. Cloud, Mankato, the Iron Range, Brainerd Lakes and Rosemount complete the eight, and a closing passage takes up Moorhead and Lake County. For a site in none of them, benchmarks come from county records and the study's own dated competitor survey.

How much lodging tax should a Minnesota hotel study carry?

Whatever the subject's city stacks, listed one layer at a time; the lender's decision is whether the pro forma room rate is net of all of it. A rate carried over from a city with a lighter stack overstates what guests will pay, and a coverage test built on it inherits the error. Show the average daily rate net of tax, name each layer with the authority that imposes it, and benchmark only against properties quoted the same way.
