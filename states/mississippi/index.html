The Mississippi Record

A statewide count that barely moves, counties that pull apart, and capital that arrives one named site at a time.

The state's Census and payroll levels, the county change rows behind them, and the project announcements of 2025 and 2026.

Mississippi's population has held nearly still since the 2020 base: the Census Bureau's Vintage 2025 estimate for July 1, 2025 is 2,954,160 Mississippians against an estimates base of 2,961,264 for April 1, 2020, and QuickFacts gives the change as -0.2% 9. Payrolls are counted monthly: the Department of Employment Security's preliminary figure for August 2026 is 1,195,400 nonfarm jobs, not seasonally adjusted, which it puts 2,800 above August 2025 10.

Inside that flat total the counties move in opposite directions: QuickFacts' Vintage 2025 change rows for April 2020 to July 2025 read +6.8% for DeSoto County 13 and for Lafayette County 21, +6.6% for Madison County 22 and +4.1% for Harrison County 23; they read -7.0% for Hinds County 24, -3.6% for Lauderdale County 25, -2.6% for Lowndes County 26 and -10.0% for Washington County 27.

The capital record of the same years is a list of named sites. The Development Authority announced on January 8, 2026 that xAI would make a corporate investment of more than $20 billion in a Southaven data center 28, which SuperTalk Mississippi reported as the largest private investment ever made in Mississippi 29. In April 2026 Amazon Web Services raised its plans for the state to $25 billion 30, and the Authority's review of calendar 2025 counted more than $21 billion of capital investment, as its director told Mississippi Today 31.

Rules by Asset Class

A state-run liquor wholesaler, Certificate of Need amended in February 2026, and child care licensed by the Health Department.

Alcohol first, then health facilities, child care and fuel tanks.

Alcohol: one wholesaler, and its 2026 backlog

Mississippi is a control state. ABC, a division of the Department of Revenue, is the sole wholesaler of spirits and wine and issues the state's alcoholic beverage permits, each sold at one combined price that takes in the state privilege tax, the local tax and an application fee 1. Its warehouse at Gluckstadt is state-owned and has been operated by Ruan Transport since March 2023; early in 2026 new software there turned out to be incompatible with the conveyor system 40. Mississippi Today counted the backlog at 170,000 cases on April 7, 2026, reported that the Legislature had failed to pass an emergency measure letting businesses buy straight from distributors, and reported borrowing of $95 million authorized for a new warehouse 2. In March it had described a new warehouse under construction as a $55 million project with double the current capacity 40; WLBT reported in June that the new warehouse was expected to be operational in 2027, and that by May 24 the backlog was just under 106,000 cases 41. On October 12, 2026 a federal judge denied the operator's motion to dismiss the class action over the shortage 3.

A retailer's permit does not pass with a sale: a new owner files a fresh application, and a temporary retailer's permit can cover the changeover 42. Since July 1, 2025 a direct wine shipper permit lets a resident receive up to 12 nine-liter cases a year, by Park Street's account 33. No numeric quota on retail permits, and no price for a permit changing hands, was found.

Certificate of Need after HB 3

Governor Reeves signed HB 3 on February 4, 2026. It confines UMMC's academic exemption from Certificate of Need to the Jackson area and asks the State Department of Health to study exempting small hospitals for dialysis and adult psychiatric services, reporting by December 1, 2026 443. Mississippi Today reported a capital threshold of $10 million for clinical improvements under the new law 44. The Center for Mississippi Health Policy's June 2026 report lists psychiatric residential treatment facilities, nursing facilities and long-term care hospitals in specific counties among the facilities the bill reaches, beside two other 2026 bills: HB 41 as introduced, to take ICF/IID facilities, intermediate care for people with intellectual disabilities, out of the program, and HB 1622, passed by the House, to exempt rural hospitals within a 5-mile radius of their main campus 4546. The statute's full list of reviewable facility types was not found in the sources reviewed, and neither were licensing categories or thresholds for assisted living and memory care.

Child care

Child care centers are licensed by the State Department of Health under the Mississippi Child Care Licensing Law, and a center's indoor capacity is set from its square footage on the Department's capacity worksheet 47. The regulation of January 2020 sets the licence fee by capacity: $97.50 up to 12 children, $195.00 for 13 to 30, $260.00 for 31 to 50 and $390.00 for 51 to 100, with higher tiers above that 48. In 2023 County Business Patterns counted 808 child day care establishments with paid employees in Mississippi 49.

Fuel tanks

The Mississippi Groundwater Protection Trust Fund reimburses tank owners who are in substantial compliance, meaning registration, leak detection, corrosion protection, spill and overfill protection and fees paid, and it asks for the Department of Environmental Quality's written approval before work begins 50. The Commission on Environmental Quality's agenda of May 26, 2022 held the tank fee at $150 a tank and recorded SB 2158's authority to draw up to $1,000,000 a year from the fund for program administration 51; a balance for 2025 or 2026 was not found.

Several rules a project here might expect were looked for and not located: water use rules for car washes, licensing for campgrounds and RV parks, a state law on short-term rentals, a 2026 state wage schedule and a state lodging tax rate. Each is a question for the agency or city at the site.

Costs, Taxes and Insurance

A contractor's tax in place of sales tax on materials, and a coastal wind pool priced to a statutory loss standard.

How a Mississippi build is taxed, what equipment purchases pay, and who insures the coast.

Mississippi taxes non-residential construction through a contractor's tax on the contract, collected in place of sales tax on the component materials, which the contractor buys under a Material Purchase Certificate 16. Contracts above a set value must be prepaid or bonded with the Department of Revenue. Residential work is excluded, but apartment and condominium construction carries the contractor's tax 16.

Furniture, fixtures and equipment bought for a project pay the general sales tax rate, while manufacturing machinery and repair parts pay a reduced rate and raw materials used in manufacturing are exempt 54. Purchases financed with Mississippi Business Finance Corporation industrial revenue bonds, and certified data center computing equipment, can qualify for exemption 5455. No published effective tax rate on commercial property was found for the state, nor an approved commercial property rate change or a carrier withdrawal dated 2025 or 2026.

On the coast, wind and hail cover comes from the Mississippi Windstorm Underwriting Association where the voluntary market does not write it. The statute requires the association's rates to be at least adequate to buy reinsurance for the 100-year probable maximum loss, a standard that lengthens by 5 years every 2 years 17. The Insurance Department's December 2023 bulletin recorded a flat 50.0% increase in the association's commercial property rates from February 1, 2024, and an overall -6.0% loss cost change for AAIS commercial property filings in the voluntary market from April 1, 2024 18. Residential fire and extended coverage has its own residual market, the Mississippi Residential Property Insurance Underwriting Association 56.

Mississippi's SSBCI programs, MBFC bond financing and the December 2025 grant round.

Treasury's second SSBCI allocation, the state's bond issuer and the Governor's grants.

Mississippi's allocation in the second round of SSBCI is up to $86 million, approved in July 2023 and active in 2025, across four programs: loan participation, a loan guarantee and two equity or venture programs 11. The Mississippi CDFI Small Business Loan Fund takes $45 million of it for loan participations through non-depository CDFIs, three of which MDA approved in April 2024 85. InvestMS for Startups, run with Innovate Mississippi, holds $11 million for pre-seed to Series A rounds and matches up to $1 million on deals of up to $3 million 11. The guarantee program is approving loans: at its May 27, 2025 meeting the MDA board approved a Small Business Loan Guaranty of up to $150,000 for a Madison County borrower through Hope FCU 86. Treasury's August 2017 list shows $13.2 million from the first SSBCI round going to MDA 87; whether a capital access program ran in 2025 or 2026 was not found.

The Mississippi Business Finance Corporation issues taxable and tax-exempt industrial development revenue bonds for investment-grade credits. A bond-financed project can receive a property tax exemption for a term of years and a sales and use tax exemption on bond-financed purchases, and MDA allocates the private activity volume cap 55. HB 964 of the 2025 session reenacted the MBFC economic development bond sections 88.

On December 11, 2025 the Governor announced more than $100 million of MDA, ARC, DRA, RESTORE, GOMESA and SCRC grants for site development, infrastructure and workforce, among them $20,891,932 from ARC and $3,918,949 from SCRC 20.

SBA

One SBA district for the whole state, with offices in Jackson and Gulfport.

The district office, ten approval years of 7(a) loans, and the rule behind a study request.

SBA's Mississippi District Office serves the entire state from offices in Jackson and Gulfport, under District Director Robert Coffey 89. SBA's loan-level FOIA data count 3,210 loans under 7(a) for Mississippi projects approved between fiscal 2016 and fiscal 2025, after cancellations are removed, with loan status as of June 30, 2026 66.

The FY2025 year-end activity reports, issued on November 13, 2025 with data to September 30, 2025, were not read into this page, so it prints no single-year state total 90; the district's own lender report is published only as an interactive dashboard 91. Coleman Report's ranking for fiscal 2023 put Peoples Bank first among Mississippi 7(a) lenders, with 61 loans for $25,154,700 92.

The rule under which SBA can call for a feasibility study is 13 CFR 120.160(b): SBA may require professional appraisals, a survey, or a feasibility study 93. Loan tests come from SOP 50 10 8.1 with Technical Policy Updates 94, which our SBA 7(a) and 504 practice page walks through.

Mississippi Questions

Six Mississippi questions, answered from the sources this page lists.

Does a Mississippi retailer's alcohol permit transfer when the business is sold?

No. The buyer files a fresh application with ABC, and a temporary permit covers the changeover 42. The Department of Revenue's ABC division issues each permit at one combined price covering state privilege tax, local tax and the application fee 1. The sources set no numeric cap on retail permits and give no going price for one that changes hands.

Is the Mississippi liquor warehouse backlog clearing?

By WLBT's June 2026 report, the backlog was just under 106,000 cases by May 24, 2026, and WLBT put the new warehouse's opening in 2027 41. Mississippi Today had described that warehouse in March as a $55 million build with twice the capacity of the current one 40, and in April reported $95 million of borrowing authorized for it 2.

Which Mississippi metros get a monthly payroll count from the state?

Three. MDES's State & Metro Trends carries Gulfport-Biloxi, Hattiesburg and Jackson: the July 2026 edition puts the eight-county Jackson metro at 301,300 nonfarm jobs and the four-county Gulfport-Biloxi metro at 173,700, both preliminary 14, and the June 2026 edition puts the three-county Hattiesburg metro at 67,300 15. No payroll series was found for DeSoto County, Lafayette County, Lauderdale County, the Golden Triangle, the Tupelo area or the Delta.

Who writes wind cover on the Mississippi coast?

The Mississippi Windstorm Underwriting Association, for coastal property the voluntary market will not write. By statute its rates must at least fund reinsurance for a 100-year probable maximum loss 17, and its commercial property rates went up a flat 50.0% on February 1, 2024, under a filing listed in the Insurance Department's December 2023 bulletin 18. Residential fire and extended coverage has a residual market of its own, the Mississippi Residential Property Insurance Underwriting Association 56.

How is a Mississippi child care center's licensed capacity set, and what does the licence cost?

The State Department of Health licenses centers and fixes a center's indoor capacity from the building's square footage on its capacity worksheet 47. Its January 2020 regulation scales the licence fee to that capacity: $97.50 at a capacity of 12 or fewer children, $195.00 from 13 to 30, $260.00 from 31 to 50, $390.00 from 51 to 100, and more above that 48. No statewide count of licensed child care capacity was found.

Is the Mississippi coast in any of the four 2025 and 2026 disaster declarations?

The lists for DR-4874 (the 30 SBA loan counties of the March 2025 storms) 552, DR-4899 (the January 2026 winter storm) 6 and DR-4922 (the May 2026 storms) 753 name none of Hancock, Harrison or Jackson County, and none of DeSoto, Lafayette or Lowndes; the sources carry no county list for DR-4930, the August 2026 declaration for the remnants of Tropical Storm Arthur 8. Inland, Lamar County sits in both the first declaration's loan areas and the third's Individual Assistance list 57, and Hinds and Madison in the winter storm's 6.
