Utah in 2025 and 2026

Growth from births, payrolls that picked up after March, and four statutes that reach the pro forma.

Utah's labor market strengthened through mid-2026. The Department of Workforce Services counted 10,400 more nonfarm jobs in March 2026 than in March 2025 14, and by August the gain over twelve months was 20,600 5.

Four statutes now reach Utah operating budgets: HB 456 on lodging taxes, HB 48 on wildfire maps and fees, SB 132 on service to very large electric loads, and HB 18 on fuel tanks.

The eight market sections read each market on its own counties. Three of them, Park City, Moab and the Uinta Basin, have no published multifamily, storage or industrial series at all, so their sections rest on county counts, park and lodging records and local rules.

Residents and Households

Births ahead of moves in 2025, by the state's committee and by the Census Bureau, and what the survey says of income, rent and age.

The Utah Population Committee, which publishes its estimates with the Kem C. Gardner Policy Institute, released its 2025 figures in December 2025: 3,551,150 residents on July 1, 2025, 44,351 more than a year before, a gain of 1.3%, with net migration of 19,233 1. In the institute's news release of December 17, 2025, its senior demographer Emily Harris described natural change as the primary driver of growth for the first time this decade 1.

The Census Bureau's Vintage 2025 count is lower than the committee's 3,551,150: 3,538,904 on July 1, 2025, against 3,502,983 a year earlier, growth of 1.0% 2. The bureau's release of January 27, 2026 says Utah grew mainly from natural change as net international migration, the largest contributor the year before, slowed 2. The bureau's own split of the year into domestic and international migration was not retrieved, so this page gives no component figure from it.

Property Cover

Utah's 2025 nonrenewals, a FAIR Plan bill that failed, and the wildfire map insurers must now reference.

Insurers declined to renew 4.45% of Utah homeowners policies in 2025, the highest rate in the country, against 1.73% in 2024, KUER and the Mountain West News Bureau reported on September 17, 2026 from NAIC data 3. Those are residential figures. No Utah rate approvals or carrier withdrawals for commercial property were found, so the homeowners series is the nearest public measure of the market in which a commercial policy is bought.

The 2026 General Session took up a backstop and let it go. HB 562 would have created a FAIR Plan writing residential and commercial property; its enacting clause was struck and the bill was filed on March 6, 2026 417. Utah therefore has no insurer of last resort for property of either kind.

The wildland-urban interface map

HB 48, passed in 2025, created a state wildland-urban interface risk map that insurers must reference. When the Division of Forestry, Fire and State Lands released the map in December 2025 it counted approximately 60,000 structures inside the high-risk boundary, the 95th percentile of risk statewide 8. The law's fee for 2026 and 2027 is $20 to $100 per structure, set by the square footage of taxable structures, and it becomes risk-based from 2028; an insurer must also explain on request a wildfire-driven increase of 20% or more, or a nonrenewal 8.

Fire and Flood

The Cottonwood Fire of July 2026: an SBA declaration that names a Nevada county, then Utah's first major disaster since 2023.

The Cottonwood Fire and the flooding and mudslides that followed it, from July 13 to 24, 2026, produced the state's first major disaster declaration since the 2023 floods. DR-4948-UT, declared on October 7, 2026 and announced by FEMA the next day, provides Public Assistance only, in Beaver and Piute counties, with possible eligible damages of at least $47 million; it brings no Individual Assistance 18.

SBA had acted first. Its administrative declaration of September 9, 2026, published in the Federal Register on September 14, names Beaver County as the primary county and Garfield, Iron, Millard, Piute and Sevier counties as contiguous, together with Lincoln County in Nevada 19.

The 2025 fire season had already drawn three federal Fire Management Assistance declarations: for the Forsyth Fire on June 20, the Deer Creek Fire on July 12 and the Monroe Canyon Fire on July 16 20.

Earthquake risk on the Wasatch Fault is widely cited, but no measure of it reached this page: the research found no FEMA National Risk Index ranking and no quantified seismic, wind or flood exposure for the state. For wildfire, the state map in the property cover section gives a parcel its reading.

Payrolls and Employers

Where Utah's job gains came from in the year to August 2026, who employs the most, and the projects EDCUtah counted in fiscal 2026.

The Department of Workforce Services' summary of September 18, 2026 counted 1,788,800 nonfarm jobs in August 2026, 20,600 or 1.2% more than a year earlier, with private employers up 23,600, or 1.6% 5. Professional and business services added 8,500 jobs, education and health 4,900, and leisure and hospitality 4,000; information lost 2,300 and natural resources 200 5. Unemployment stood at 3.5%, against 4.1% for the nation 5.

The year started slowly. The department's release of May 6, 2026 put March payrolls 10,400 jobs, or 0.6%, above March 2025, with leisure and hospitality 2,800 jobs lower and manufacturing 900 lower 14. For a hotel or restaurant study the sector moved from a loss of 2,800 jobs on the year in March to a gain of 4,000 in August, both on the department's count 145.

Announced projects

EDCUtah, the state's public-private economic development organization, reported 12 project wins in its fiscal year 2026, from July 2025 to June 2026, carrying $20.2 billion of planned capital spending and 6,909 new jobs 6. Three of the wins it names are Creekstone Energy's Project Flute in Millard County, at $17 billion of capital spending and 106 jobs; Trigg Minerals' tungsten and antimony project in Garfield and Iron counties, at $287 million and 400 jobs; and Stadler Rail in Salt Lake County, at $189.4 million and 250 jobs 6.

Separately, the Governor's Office of Economic Opportunity announced on March 12, 2026 an incentive for Western Governors University's commitment in Salt Lake County, about $2.5 billion over 20 years with 5,072 jobs 22.

Power and Large Loads

A separate path to power for very large loads since March 2025, and the Stratos proposal in Box Elder County.

SB 132, enacted in March 2025, gives a customer expecting a load of 100 MW or more within five years its own route to service. Rocky Mountain Power has 90 days to evaluate the request, and where serving it would need major upgrades the customer may contract for alternative supply 13.

At the Public Service Commission, the Division of Public Utilities' investigation of the utility's line-extension policy for large loads, Docket 24-035-43, was deferred on August 19, 2025 pending rulemaking Docket 25-R318-01 23. No count of projects waiting in the interconnection queue was found, and the research found no sewer moratorium.

Stratos

The Military Installation Development Authority approved the Stratos project, a data center campus in Box Elder County planned at 9 GW. Its value at full build, more than $100 billion, is a developer and press projection rather than committed capital 24. After bipartisan objections over its water and power, the Christian Science Monitor reported on September 1, 2026, the site had been cut from 40,000 to 20,000 acres, and the project had requested water rights of 13,000 acre-feet a year 25. The same authority finances Deer Valley's East Village, as the Park City section sets out.

Provo-Orem

Provo-Orem and Utah County: two universities on the employer list, the county's 2025 count and an industrial corridor built on speculation.

The committee put Utah County at 765,515 residents on July 1, 2025, against 664,258 in its 2020 base 1. Builders in the county were permitted 6,733 residential units in 2025, 3,254 of them single-family, after 6,646 in 2024, by the Gardner Institute's State of the State's Housing Market report of September 2026 26.

Covered employment in the county reached 305,583 in December 2025, 2.1% higher than a year before, in the Bureau of Labor Statistics' county release of June 11, 2026 27. On the Workforce Services list, whose bands are statewide headcounts, Brigham Young University sits at 15,000 to 19,999, the Alpine School District at 7,000 to 9,999, Utah Valley University at 5,000 to 6,999, the Nebo School District at 4,000 to 4,999 and Vivint at 2,000 to 2,999 21. No county apartment vacancy, hotel pipeline, storage, retail or senior housing reading was found, and no Utah County impact fee schedule, rental ordinance or hotel tax rate.

Industrial space is the county's best-documented class. Cushman & Wakefield's Utah County report for the first quarter of 2026 gives vacancy of 6.8%, up from 6.2% in the fourth quarter of 2025 28, and Team Utah Commercial's 2026 report names the Lehi to Spanish Fork stretch of I-15 as the Utah submarket with the most speculative industrial construction 29. Northmarq's report of July 30, 2026 lists Orem among the apartment submarkets leading vacancy compression 30. The Gardner Institute's tourism report says the county's hotel occupancy, daily rate and revenue per available room all rose in 2025, without publishing the levels 7.

St. George

St. George and Washington County: growth carried by migration, a water district's 2026 fees and a hospitality year.

Washington County held 209,129 residents on July 1, 2025 in the committee's estimate, against 182,111 in 2020, and the committee attributes 87% of the county's 2025 growth to migration 1. The county's 2025 permits covered 2,888 residential units, 1,737 single-family, against 3,066 in 2024 26.

Covered employment was 91,421 in December 2025, 2.3% more than a year before and the largest gain of the state's five large counties in the BLS release 27. The Workforce Services list places the Washington County School District at 3,000 to 3,999 and Utah Tech University at 2,000 to 2,999 21; the county's next three employers were not found.

Water

The Washington County Water Conservancy District approved higher impact fees with its 2026 budget in December 2025, and the Salt Lake Tribune put the district's supply at about 86,000 acre-feet a year; the new fee amounts were not found 31. A month earlier the district adopted its 2025 Water Shortage Contingency Plan with its municipal partners 32.

Visitors and rentals

Leisure and hospitality taxable sales in the county reached $1.2 billion in 2025, and its transient room tax collections showed one of the three largest gains in absolute terms, the Gardner Institute reports 7. No hotel occupancy, rate or pipeline figure for the county was found. Unincorporated Washington County limits short-term rentals by owner occupancy and size under rules adopted in 2021, and Springdale, the Zion gateway town, paused new transient lodging, KUER reported on February 25, 2022 33.

Salt Lake City

Salt Lake City, Salt Lake and Tooele counties: the airport, a full hotel market, apartments, three industrial readings and a 2026 rental licence.

The register's Salt Lake City market is Salt Lake and Tooele counties, the Census Bureau's Salt Lake City-Murray metro. The county counts below are Salt Lake County's, the brokers' readings follow each broker's own definition of the Salt Lake market, and no Tooele County figure was found.

Salt Lake County had 1,240,937 residents on July 1, 2025, against 1,188,213 in the 2020 base 1. It permitted 7,523 residential units in 2025, 4,752 of them apartments, after 4,093 in 2024 26. The BLS county release gives covered employment of 821,026 for December 2025, 2.0% above a year earlier 27. Northmarq counted 12,000 jobs added in the Salt Lake metro over the twelve months to the second quarter of 2026 30.

The University of Utah, Intermountain Health Care and the State of Utah each sit in the department's 20,000-and-over band, the Granite and Jordan school districts at 7,000 to 9,999 each, and the VA hospital at 3,000 to 3,999 21. Salt Lake City International Airport handled 28.2 million passengers in 2025, 0.7% fewer than in 2024, and completed its Phase 3 early in 2026 7.

Hotels and the convention district

Salt Lake County hotels averaged 69.6% occupancy in 2025, the highest of any Utah county; downtown Salt Lake City hotels ran at 72.1%, at an average daily rate of $176, with revenue per available room up 3.5% 7. About 1,600 rooms were added in the county between 2019 and 2025, a 7.9% increase 7. Hotels in three county districts pay a 2.0% assessment, in place from 2023 to 2028, which raised $8.3 million in 2025, with $9.5 million expected for 2026 7.

Downtown, the sports and convention district around the Delta Center, the Salt Palace and Japantown is funded by a 0.5% sales tax, and on April 29, 2025 a 6.5-acre Salt Palace parcel was sold to Smith Entertainment Group for $55.4 million 7.

Apartments

Northmarq's first-quarter 2026 reading of metro apartment vacancy was 7.3% 34. Its July report forecasts 7.1% and an average rent of about $1,600 a month for the end of 2026; both are forecasts, not readings 30. Marcus & Millichap counts more than 900 units delivered downtown, at the Astra, Luma and Worthington buildings, between 2024 and the first quarter of 2026 35. The county's apartment rent level and construction pipeline were not found.

Industrial space

For the second quarter of 2026 three brokers give three vacancy rates: Colliers 4.79% direct vacancy for Salt Lake County, CBRE 7.2% and Savills 8.8% for Salt Lake City 363738. Each broker defines the market and its vacancy in its own way, so a study chooses one series and holds to it for every quarter it cites. Savills also records an occupancy gain of 0.3 million square feet in the Northwest submarket 38.

Short-term rentals

Salt Lake City's first short-term rental licence took effect on July 1, 2026: $198 a licence plus $342 a unit, a two-night minimum stay, a cap of 200 nights a year, one licence in a building of up to 10 units and a 10% cap in larger buildings, and fines of $1,000 for each seven days of violation, Axios Salt Lake City reported 39. No county impact fee, moratorium, room tax rate or water and sewer record was found.

Ogden-Clearfield

Ogden-Clearfield, Davis, Morgan and Weber counties: Hill Air Force Base and the Falcon Hill campus.

The register's Ogden-Clearfield market is the three counties in the heading, the Census Bureau's Ogden metro; the research also reads Box Elder County into the market, but no Box Elder figure appears in this section, and Morgan County has none either.

Defense employers anchor it. On the Workforce Services list, Hill Air Force Base, the Davis County School District and Northrop Grumman, counted across its Utah sites, each sit at 10,000 to 14,999, the U.S. Department of the Treasury at 7,000 to 9,999, the Weber County School District at 4,000 to 4,999 and Weber State University at 3,000 to 3,999 21.

At Falcon Hill, the Defense Department's largest commercial enhanced-use-lease program, all 1.3 million square feet of available space was under lease on 550 acres in September 2025, with about 5,500 people at work there by the estimate of the base's leasing program chief, Utah Business reported on September 12, 2025; approvals there come from the Air Force and the Defense Department rather than from the cities, and Draper Labs began building on the site in April 2025 40. Northrop Grumman broke ground on July 14, 2026 on the Legacy Building at its Roy Innovation Center, which takes the campus to six buildings and more than 1.1 million square feet, with room for more than 5,000 employees by 2028; the company directly employs more than 11,000 Utahns and did not disclose the investment 41.

Davis County permitted 1,862 residential units in 2025 and Weber County 1,824 26. Covered employment in December 2025 was 143,004 in Davis County, 0.6% lower than a year before, and 123,226 in Weber County, 0.1% lower 27. No year-over-year payroll change for the Ogden metro was found.

Cushman & Wakefield's northern Utah industrial report puts Davis and Weber vacancy at 3.1% in the first quarter of 2026, against 2.3% a year earlier 42. The Northern Utah Economic Alliance's August 2026 profile names the I-15 and US-89 axis, where a Syracuse site of more than 300 acres lies, the West Davis Corridor and the Ogden Express bus rapid transit line as the area's development axes 43. In the tourism report, Weber County's hotel occupancy rose in 2025 and Davis County's fell, and Weber had fewer rooms in 2025 than in 2019; the levels are not published 7.

Logan

Logan and Cache County: Utah State University, a food plant's expansion and a county with no published supply series.

This section is Cache County alone, the county the register assigns to Logan. The committee put it at 145,865 residents on July 1, 2025, against 133,743 in 2020 1, and builders were permitted 910 units in 2025, after 904 units in 2024 26. Covered employment was 64,416 in December 2025 27; the county's twelve-month nonfarm change was not found.

The Workforce Services list places the Utah State University system, counted statewide, at 10,000 to 14,999 and the Cache School District at 3,000 to 3,999 21. Logan City's 2021 profile ranks Icon Health & Fitness, the school district, Conservice and Intermountain Health Care second to fifth, without headcounts 44.

In February 2026 the Governor's Office of Economic Opportunity approved a rural tax credit of 25% for Schreiber Foods' $165 million expansion in Logan, which is to add 145 jobs over eight years 45.

Cache County's leisure and hospitality businesses reported $0.3 billion of taxable sales in 2025, and its transient room tax receipts were among the largest declines in the state 7. Northmarq's mid-2026 report names Logan as a market for Class C apartment trades 30; no apartment, hotel, storage or industrial figure for the county was found, and no corridor traffic count.

Park City

Park City and Summit County, and Deer Valley's East Village in Wasatch County: a resort market on county records.

No research figure belongs to Park City itself. This section reads Summit County, the register's Park City market, and Wasatch County where the research reports Deer Valley's East Village, whose hotels compete with Park City's while its permits and room taxes are counted in Wasatch County.

Summit County held 43,303 residents on July 1, 2025, against 42,394 in 2020, and two more than a year before, by the committee's estimate 1. It permitted 486 units in 2025, after 284 units in 2024, while Wasatch County permitted 959 units 26. Covered employment in Summit County was 35,525 in December 2025 27; no list of its largest employers, no twelve-month payroll change and no apartment, storage, industrial or retail reading was found.

Lodging

The Park City Chamber's figures for May to October 2025, as KPCW reported them on October 29, 2025, give Summit County lodging an average occupancy of 35.7%, up 5.2%, at an average daily rate of $350, down 3.1% 46. Between 2020 and 2026 the county's hotel supply grew 22.7% and its short-term rental supply 29.4%, and its annual occupancy fell by one of the largest margins in the state, the Gardner Institute reports 7. Wasatch County hotels averaged $483 a night in 2025, at 46.8% occupancy, the lowest of any county 7.

Enjoy Park City's January 2026 update lists the East Village hotels: a Grand Hyatt open since November 2024, a Canopy by Hilton of 180 keys due in summer 2026, a Four Seasons of 134 rooms in 2027 and a five-star hotel of 132 rooms on Lot 5 47. The East Village Express Gondola opened in February 2026, and Deer Valley's expansion takes the resort from about 2,050 to 4,300 acres with 10 new lifts 7.

Public infrastructure bonds

KPCW reported on September 25, 2026 that the Military Installation Development Authority and its districts had bonded about $1.1 billion in Wasatch County since 2020 48. TownLift's May 2026 report gives a narrower scope, about $650 million of authority bonds for East Village alone, and records the Mountain Village Public Infrastructure District's tentative budget for fiscal 2026, approved on May 13, 2025: $274 million, down from $324 million the year before, including more than $230 million of project costs and $35 million of debt service 49.

Sundance

The Sundance Film Festival holds its last Utah edition in 2026 and moves to Boulder, Colorado, for 2027 to 2036. The Gardner Institute puts the festival's 2025 contribution at $196.1 million of Utah GDP and 2,697 jobs, a statewide measure, not Summit County's 7.

Uinta Basin

USBCI

Utah's allocation under the federal State Small Business Credit Initiative is $69 million, under an agreement of November 2022 with tranches through 2030; the Governor's Office of Economic Opportunity administers it as the Utah Small Business Credit Initiative 58. Its active programs are a Capital Access Program and a Loan Participation Program, with First Utah Bank and Evans Bank among the participating lenders, and the office published fourth-quarter 2025 results on February 17, 2026 59. No other state loan guarantee program was found.

Bonds and special-purpose issuers

Utah has no general-purpose development finance authority. Tax-exempt financing for private projects comes through the Private Activity Bond Review Board's allocation of volume cap and through special-purpose issuers; on the board's agenda of July 10, 2024, the Utah Housing Corporation's single-family allotment request was $179,431,035 60. The Utah Inland Port Authority finances through public infrastructure district bonds and an infrastructure bank that lends to private entities at a margin above the state's AAA rate 61. The Military Installation Development Authority's bonds for Deer Valley are in the Park City section.

Fiscal 2025 Utah totals for the agency's guaranteed business loans, REAP and Community Facilities were not found. SBA District

Salt Lake City's SBA district office, and the rule under which SBA can ask for a study.

Utah's SBA district office is in Salt Lake City and is the agency's only district office in the state; no table of the counties it covers was found 64. Licensing and Local Rules

A nursing bed moratorium in place of certificate of need, liquor quotas that loosen through 2031, a July 2026 child care rule and new tank fees.

Health care and senior housing

Utah has had no certificate of need program since it expired in 1984. What remains is a Medicaid certification moratorium on nursing facility beds under Utah Code 26B-3-310 to 26B-3-313, with exceptions by application under rule R414-514, last amended on February 7, 2024 66. Assisted living is licensed under rule R432-270 in two types, Type I for help with activities of daily living and Type II for substantial help and aging in place, and Type II may operate secure memory care units, in the description of a federal compendium from about 2007 67. Current memory care licensing detail was not found.

Child care

The Department of Health and Human Services' Office of Licensing licenses child care centers under R381-100, whose amended text took effect on July 7, 2026 68. The rule sets staff ratios and maximum group sizes by age band, from infants to children of five and older 68.

Liquor

Liquor licences are allotted by population quota, and a 2024 law loosens the quotas over seven years from July 1, 2024: full-service restaurants move from one licence per 4,467 residents to one per 3,167, and bars from one per 10,200 to one per 7,264 11. The Department of Alcoholic Beverage Services awarded 53 bar licences and 112 full-service restaurant licences in fiscal 2025 12, and after the commission's November 2025 meeting 27 bar licences remained available, with the other types in what Gastronomic SLC called relatively healthy supply 69. No price for buying a licence from a holder was found.

Fuel tanks

Tank owners register with the Department of Environmental Quality's Petroleum Storage Tank Fund. From July 1, 2025 registration costs $150 a tank on the fund or $300 off it; a fund fee set by throughput and an environmental assurance fee charged per gallon come on top 70. HB 18, signed on February 27, 2025 and in effect from May 7, 2025, aligns the aboveground and underground tank rules and allows limited transfers from the fund to orphan-site cleanup 71.

Rentals

The short-term rental rules this page can date are local ones, in the Salt Lake City, St. George and Moab sections.

Taxes and Wages

Utah's lodging tax after HB 456, and a wage floor set at the federal minimum.

HB 456 of the 2025 session raised the state transient room tax from 0.32% to 1.07% 72 and the county cap from 4.25% to 4.5%, effective July 1, 2025 73. On October 1, 2025, 18 counties moved to the higher county rate 72. A hotel, motel or short-term rental budget therefore takes the state rate, its own county's rate from that date and any municipal room tax.

Utah's minimum wage is the federal minimum, by the Labor Commission's wage claim page 74.

Utah Questions

Utah feasibility study questions.

Why did Bryce Canyon's visit count drop so sharply in 2025?

The park's visits were counted by a new method in 2025. The Gardner Institute's tourism report gives Bryce Canyon National Park 1,967,367 visits that year, 21.2% fewer than in 2024, a comparison it marks as made on the new counting method 7. A lodging study near the park compares 2025 with earlier years on one counting method only.

What does HB 48 charge a Utah property owner for wildfire?

A wildland-urban interface fee of $20 to $100 per structure in 2026 and 2027, graded by the size of the taxable buildings, and a risk-based fee from 2028. The state map that draws the boundary puts approximately 60,000 structures in its high-risk area 8.

Where is a HUD multifamily loan for a Utah project processed?

In Colorado. Utah's HUD field office, in Salt Lake City, is part of Region 8, and the Denver Multifamily Hub does the multifamily production and asset management work for Utah projects 910.

How quickly are Utah's liquor licence quotas loosening?

Over seven years from July 1, 2024. A full-service restaurant quota of one licence per 4,467 residents moves to one per 3,167, and the bar quota from one per 10,200 to one per 7,264 11. The state's fiscal 2025 awards were 53 for bars and 112 for full-service restaurants 12.

Which Utah electric customers get their own service pathway under SB 132?

Those whose expected load is 100 MW or higher within five years, a data center among them. Rocky Mountain Power evaluates such a request within 90 days, and a load that would need major upgrades to serve can be met instead by a contract for alternative supply 13.

Which Utah counties had the busiest and the emptiest hotels in 2025?

Salt Lake County's hotels ran at 69.6% occupancy, the state high, and Wasatch County's at 46.8%, the state low, although Wasatch hotels charged $483 a night on average 7.
