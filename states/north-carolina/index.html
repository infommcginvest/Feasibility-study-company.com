The North Carolina Thesis

Migrants, SBA loans and a permit law upheld in court and still under appeal.

The state took in 131,000 net migrants in the year to July 2025, third among the states, and its population rose by 146,000 over the same twelve months 2; the Census estimates base for April 2020 was 10,441,392 1.

One state law is in court and before the legislature at once. A Wake County three-judge panel upheld the Certificate of Need in December 2025 and the ruling is on appeal 17; for the repeal bills filed in 2025 and SB 1040, heard in May 2026, enactment was not found 131416. For a surgery center or a regulated bed, North Carolina still sets supply by permit before any market does.

  • SBA 504 BEFCOR of Charlotte, first on SBA Ledger's five-year table
  • SSBCI the Rural Center's loan participations

The Census estimates base was 10,441,392 in April 2020 1, and the year to July 2025 added 146,000 residents, the third-largest numeric gain of any state 2. Net migration came to 131,000, third among the states 2. The state budget office then splits the stream: net international migration came to 47,000 in that year, about half what it had been the year before, and international arrivals since 2020 total about 232,000, or 31% of the state's growth over the period 3. For arrivals from other states, the Governor's office ranked North Carolina first that year 2.

Total nonfarm employment stood at 5,112,000 in July 2026, seasonally adjusted, up 51,600 over twelve months, and private employers accounted for 48,400 of that gain 4. Professional and business services added 19,400 jobs and construction added 15,400, while manufacturing lost 9,900 4.

Income sets what that population can pay. The 2020 to 2024 five-year American Community Survey estimate of median household income for North Carolina is $72,388 5, and per capita personal income, BEA's mean over all residents, was $65,634 in calendar 2024 6.

In December 2025 a Wake County three-judge panel upheld the Certificate of Need law, and the challenger took the case to appeal 17. The legislature has the same law in front of it. SB 1040, which would end certificate review for ambulatory surgical facilities and inpatient rehabilitation, was set for a Senate committee hearing in May 2026, after the full-repeal bills SB 370 and HB 455 were filed in 2025; for all three, enactment was not found 131416. Meanwhile the statute in Article 9 of Chapter 131E still reaches new health care facilities. Its covered list, as the 2025 repeal bill reproduces it, runs from hospitals, rehabilitation facilities and nursing homes to adult care homes, kidney disease treatment centers, home health agencies and ambulatory surgical facilities, so a surgery center is among them 13. The 2023 Medicaid expansion law removed certificate review for psychiatric facilities and chemical dependency treatment and exempted certain ambulatory surgical centers in counties of more than 125,000 people, subject to a charity-care requirement 15.

Short-term rental registration is closed to towns and counties by statute: G.S. 160D-1207 bars a local government from requiring registration or a permit to rent residential property, except for a property with verified violations, while zoning rules on rentals remain valid 26.

An RV park is designed to a state wastewater number: the rules set its design flow, in gallons per day, at 100 for each standard RV site and 150 for each park model 25.

DHHS licenses care by size and by rating. The adult care statute names three settings: adult care homes, adult care homes for elderly residents only, and multiunit assisted housing with services 18. A home that advertises dementia care must be licensed as an adult care home with a special care unit, while multiunit assisted housing with services registers each year instead of holding a licence 19. The state's 2024 counts of those homes are under supply 20. A child care licence carries a rating of one to five stars, and only a center rated three stars or more can accept subsidy 21. Since July 2023 a center run in a residence has its own capacity classes, 3 to 12 children where any are of preschool age and 3 to 15 where all are of school age, with staff ratios by age band 22, and rated centers follow a ratio and group-size table with a tighter version for the higher tier 23.

For a fuel station, every tank carries an annual state registration fee of $420, and the state's commercial cleanup fund stands behind leaks: in fiscal 2025 it closed 502 commercial releases, replaced 59 contaminated wells and paid $14,031,105 in reimbursements to responsible parties 24.

Power and Water

A town that stopped approvals over sewer.

On the power side, an intake process for the largest loads; on the water side, one town's moratorium.

Duke's forecast of the capacity its large loads would need was revised upward between its early 2024 and May 2025 vintages, reaching almost 6 GW in the later one 29. In July 2025 the company filed an intake process for the largest new loads with the Utilities Commission 30, and on its February 2026 earnings call Duke said its secured large load starts coming online in late 2027, with a late-stage data center pipeline about double that 31.

That intake process applies to loads over 100 MW, and the July 2025 filing warned of longer timelines in constrained urban areas 30.

On the water side the 2026 record is local. In July 2026 the Town of Green Level adopted a 60-day moratorium on all development approvals over water and sewer capacity, and later extended it by six months 32.

Statewide Supply

Licensed adult care homes, a hotel rate and five federal counts.

The state counted its adult care homes in 2024; the federal series count hotels, storage payrolls, rents, home values and older residents.

In August 2024 the state's fiscal note counted about 582 adult care homes with seven or more beds, about 268 of them with special care units, and about 530 family care homes 20. Those are licensed homes rather than occupied beds, and the record holds no occupancy rate to set against them. The people they serve are counted separately: the 2020 to 2024 five-year American Community Survey estimate counts 1,845,563 residents aged 65 and over 5.

Statewide hotel occupancy was 61.3% in calendar 2025, a figure that reaches this page through Wake County's tourism report, which cites it as a comparator 35; no statewide room count stands beside it. County Business Patterns counted 1,928 establishments among hotels (except casino hotels) and motels in 2023 36, and 1,682 employees among lessors of miniwarehouses and self-storage units in the same 2023 count 36.

Across renter-occupied units paying cash rent, the 2020 to 2024 five-year American Community Survey estimate of median gross rent is $1,228 5, and the median value of owner-occupied homes in the same five-year estimate is $288,900 5.

Review Failures

Four North Carolina records a feasibility study can fail on.

The Certificate of Need, Buncombe's recovery months, Chantal's two federal records and the homeowners settlement.

  1. The Certificate of Need read as settled, either way

    A surgery center or regulated-bed study that treats the law as gone, or as beyond change during lease-up, misreads the court and legislative record set out under rules 17.

  2. Buncombe's recovery months annualized

    Buncombe County hotel results in 2025 carried recovery stays that had fallen away by February 2026 37. A study that annualizes those months overstates the base.

  3. Chantal read from FEMA alone

    FEMA's record for Chantal and SBA's offer different help, as the hazards section sets out, and a Durham or Orange County study that cites only FEMA's leaves out the disaster loans SBA made available there.

  4. The homeowners settlement carried into a commercial budget

    The January 2025 settlement raised homeowners base rates 7.5% in June 2025 and 7.5% in June 2026, and holds new filings to June 1, 2027 28; no commercial rate settlement was found. A hotel or storage budget that applies those homeowners increases has priced insurance from a policy line the building does not buy.

Site Tests

Sewer allocations, state licences and storm declarations, checked against the parcel.

North Carolina's 2025 and 2026 record decides which questions a site has to answer, and which office holds each answer.

The plant behind the sewer line

In 2026 wastewater capacity was decided plant by plant and town by town, as the Brunswick County pause in the Wilmington section shows. Green Level went further and stopped every development approval, not only new sewer connections, while it worked through water and sewer supply 32. The study names the plant that will take the project's flow and carries the utility's dated allocation for it.

A state decision ahead of the town's

Several uses meet a state permit or licence before a local one. A new regulated bed, or a surgery center outside the 2023 exemption, needs a Certificate of Need 1315. An assisted living building is licensed by size class, and the larger memory care buildings change building class with it 19. A child care center's star rating decides whether it can take subsidized children 21. An RV park's wastewater is designed to the state's flow per site 25. For each, the study names the licence or permit, the agency that grants it, and where the application stands on the study date.

The parcel's county is then read against the declarations under hazards, by name where the record lists counties.

Asheville

Asheville: Buncombe County and its metro after Helene.

Buncombe County lost residents in the year to July 2025, and its hotels filled with recovery stays in 2025 and then lost them in early 2026.

Census Vintage 2025 figures, as transcribed in a reference encyclopedia, put the Asheville metro at 422,345 residents in 2025 against a 2020 base of 406,926 38. That gain conceals a turn. The state budget office puts Buncombe County down 1,810 in the year to July 2025 3, and local reporting on the Census release named Asheville as the only North Carolina metro to lose population in that year; the metro was down 634 39.

Lodging shows the distortion most clearly. Recovery workers and federally funded stays filled rooms in 2025; when that demand ended, February 2026 hotel occupancy fell 11 points from the February before 37. By July 2026 Buncombe's hotel RevPAR was $122, a gain led by rate, and vacation rental occupancy was 66.8% 40. Local reporting on the tourism authority's figures in March 2026 described $34.5 million of lodging-tax revenue as recently approved for marketing and tourism-related capital projects 37.

Worked Cases

Four worked cases set in North Carolina.

Composite examples placed by region rather than by town: the coastal plain, a growth corridor, the Piedmont and an unnamed metro.

No published case names a site in Asheville, Fayetteville, the Triad, Greenville, Hickory or Wilmington. The rural case is agricultural: the pork processing plant under USDA B&I tests a new federally inspected harvest and fabrication plant in a rural county of the eastern North Carolina coastal plain, hog country, where the questions are hog supply, independent processing capacity and whether the plant can hold its fabrication margin.

The multifamily case follows a new mid-rise in a high-growth suburban submarket of the Charlotte-Raleigh growth corridor toward an agency permanent loan, and rests its case on absorption and capture in a tightly drawn market area. The assisted living and memory care case places a new community in a suburban submarket on the edge of a major North Carolina Piedmont metro under a HUD Section 232 takeout, where the Certificate of Need position of adult care beds makes the supply read decisive. The medical office and surgery center case, set on a growing suburban health corridor in a major North Carolina metro the case does not name, finances an owner-occupied building with a physician-owned surgery center under SBA 504, and shows a Certificate of Need facility as both a limit on new competitors and a precondition the project must satisfy.

North Carolina Questions

North Carolina feasibility study questions.

Where are the CDCs that lead North Carolina's 504 lending based?

In Charlotte, for two of the top three. SBA Ledger's table for fiscal 2021 to 2025 puts Business Expansion Funding Corp. (BEFCOR), a Charlotte CDC, first at $355M on 312 loans and Carolina Business Capital, also of Charlotte, third at $73.9 million. The one between them is based outside the state: 504 Capital Corporation of Chesapeake, Virginia, at $82.4 million 7.

How does Certificate of Need change a North Carolina health care feasibility study?

It makes the count of competitors a question of who holds a certificate, not only of who could build. New health care facilities still need one under the state statute 13. A 2023 change lets some surgery centers in counties above 125,000 population skip the certificate, on a charity-care condition 15. The court and legislative record on the law is set out under rules.

What does the record show for Asheville's hotels and population after Hurricane Helene?

A recovery peak, then a fall. Recovery stays filled hotels and then ended, and February 2026 occupancy fell from the year before 37; Buncombe County lost 1,810 residents in the year to July 2025 3. RevPAR reached $122 in July 2026 and vacation rental occupancy stood at 66.8% 40. In March 2026, $34.5 million of lodging-tax revenue for marketing and tourism-related capital was reported as approved 37.

What sewer and power capacity limits does the North Carolina record hold?

A town moratorium. Green Level adopted a moratorium on all development approvals in July 2026 over water and sewer 32. On power, Duke expects its secured large load to start coming online in late 2027 31.

What did North Carolina's petroleum tank cleanup fund pay out in fiscal 2025?

$14,031,105 in reimbursements to responsible parties, in a year in which the fund also closed 502 commercial releases and replaced 59 contaminated wells 24. Tank owners pay an annual registration fee of $420 per tank 24. The rules section sets the fund beside the state's other rules by asset class.

Which new plants are in the Triad's record, and how large are they?

Two, one opened and one announced. The opened one is Toyota's battery plant at Liberty, Randolph County: a project of more than 5,100 jobs, with its grand opening in November 2025 46, about 1,850 acres of site 47, production under way 48. The announced one is ProVia's $120 million window plant in Davie County, with a state job development grant approved in August 2026 45. The Triad section gives the populations around them: 805,945 in the Greensboro-High Point metro and 712,206 in Winston-Salem's 49.
