The Arkansas Record

Data center pauses county by county, and a power law that bills customers before plants run.

Three records set Arkansas apart in 2025 and 2026: county land use, the utility bill and the property insurance market.

In 2026 the first questions about a large Arkansas project were asked at county level. The Arkansas Advocate counted six large data center projects proposed in the state and two under construction in August 2026, as counties and cities outside Little Rock moved ahead with moratoriums 9. The state set none of the terms: each pause on this page was adopted by a county or a city, and the dated record gives each its body and its date.

The second change is on the power bill. The Generating Arkansas Jobs Act, Act 373 of 2025, lets a utility recover the cost of building generation before the plant enters service 10, and the utility section sets out how the law works.

The third is cover. The Arkansas Senate's November 2024 review of property insurance counted eight insurers that had stopped selling policies in recent years and a loss ratio of 130% for 2023 11.

Demand is the steadier part of the file. The Census Bureau's Vintage 2025 estimate counted 3,114,791 Arkansans on July 1, 2025; the April 2020 estimates base was 3,011,530 7. The market sections below carry the county counts under that total.

Utility Costs

Act 373's rider on Entergy bills, and siting rules that ask for proof of capacity.

What the 2025 law does, Entergy's estimate of the bill effect, and the local rules that tie a site to its utility.

Act 373 of 2025, the Generating Arkansas Jobs Act, created a strategic investment rider through which a utility recovers generation construction costs before the plant is in service; the Senate listed it in the economic development package the legislature approved that spring 10. It became law on March 18, 2025, and SWEPCO filed for rider eligibility eight days later 4.

The rider reached Entergy Arkansas bills in June 2026. Talk Business & Politics, citing Entergy's filing in March 2026, estimated the gross increase at $5.77 a month, about 4.17%, on a bill of 1,000 kWh, and the net increase at $4.22, about 3%, after a nuclear production tax credit of $1.55 5. Those estimates are for a bill of that size; the research holds no figure for a commercial or industrial tariff.

The rider drew the legislature back in. On September 15, 2026 the Joint Energy Committee questioned Entergy about data center cost recovery; its co-chair said the rider had added $22.81 to his own bill, Entergy's witness answered that the rider moves forward costs ratepayers would eventually pay, and changes were floated for the 2027 session without being enacted 30.

Local siting rules now ask for the utility answer up front. North Little Rock limited data centers to its I-3 zones on September 15, 2026, with a conditional use permit and demonstrated utility capacity as conditions 29. Independence County's five-year pause cites strain on its water supply 1. The research found no published interconnection queue for Arkansas utilities, and its statewide search found no sewer moratorium.

The tax side moved the other way. Act 548 of 2025 lowered the investment a data center needs to qualify for the state sales and use tax exemption from $500 million to $100 million, the Arkansas Democrat-Gazette reported in July 2025 31.

Costs, Taxes and Insurance

Eight insurers out of the property market, a 130% loss ratio, and a notice rule for commercial renewals.

The Senate's market review, one approved filing, the 2025 commercial notice law, and the wage and tax lines.

The Senate's November 2024 review found eight insurers that had stopped selling property policies in recent years, a loss ratio of 130% for 2023 and average rate increases of 15% to 20% that year, with the ten largest insurers holding 75% of the market in 2024; it named wind and hail as the main perils behind property damage 11.

Approved filings show the pressure one company at a time. The Insurance Department's rate-change portal records a homeowners increase of 17.10% for Nationwide Mutual, filed on May 29, 2025 32. That is a personal-lines filing. Approved commercial rate changes for 2025 and 2026 were not found, nor was a commercial property insurer of last resort.

The 2025 answer for commercial cover was notice, not a cap. Act 22 requires at least 60 days of notice to the agent and 30 days to the insured before a commercial renewal rises by 25% or more, effective August 5, 2025, as Mitchell Williams summarised the Insurance Department's Bulletin 9-2025 of June 12, 2025 20. The law changes when an owner learns of a large increase, not how large it may be.

On property tax, the Lincoln Institute's comparison of taxes paid in 2025, published in April 2026, holds commercial effective rates for Little Rock and rural Arkansas, but the research did not extract them 33. No construction cost index for Arkansas cities was found either.

Labor has a fixed floor. The state minimum wage for employers with four or more workers is $11.00 an hour, unchanged since January 1, 2021; the tipped cash wage is $2.63, and no 2027 increase has been enacted, in the Labor Department series FRED updated on January 1, 2026 34.

Capital Access

SSBCI money ADFA could not place on schedule, a bond cap it called short, and SBA's 504 record.

The state finance authority's own books for 2025, and the federal loan files for the 504 program.

The Arkansas Development Finance Authority runs the state's credit programs: SSBCI, a Capital Access Program, bond guaranties and conduit tax-exempt bonds, with reports on each reviewed by its board in July 2025 19. Its FY2025 annual report records SSBCI Tranche 1 at $24,021,112, approved on December 13, 2022, with 80% of it to be obligated before December 13, 2025 for the second tranche to be drawn 35.

The SSBCI programs ADFA lists are loan guarantees, direct loan participations with community development financial institutions, two venture funds and an Arkansas Economic Development Commission loan guaranty for minority- and women-owned businesses 36. The deployment fell behind. A memo of November 19, 2025 in the board's December book called it tremendously hampered and put the amount obligated to CDFI loan participations at $7.16 million 19.

Tax-exempt capacity is the other constraint. In July 2025 ADFA told its board that requests for private activity bonds would exceed the 2026 volume cap, which it put at about $401,500,000 19; the research could not reconcile that figure with the federal formula for 2026. Conduit issues in 2025 included about $28 million of tax-exempt bonds for Providence Academy, approved in July, and $4,130,500 of Multifamily Housing Revenue Bonds, Series 2025, for Sunset Terrace 37. Each is one borrower's financing, shown for the kind of deal ADFA's conduit carries.

Six Bridges Capital Corp., run by ACC Capital in Little Rock, covers Arkansas and the Memphis and Texarkana metros 38, and SBA approved Capital CDC's expansion into the state 39. For approval fiscal years 2016 to 2025, SBA's FOIA files hold 147 loans under 504 for Arkansas projects, net of cancellations, with $139.1 million of debentures, the SBA and development company share of those projects 40.

SBA

Where Arkansas SBA files start: a Little Rock district office and ten approval years of 7(a) data.

The district office, the restaurant class in the loan files, and the rule under which a lender asks for a study.

SBA serves the state from its Arkansas District Office in Little Rock 41. Of the 7(a) approvals for Arkansas projects in SBA's FOIA files for fiscal 2016 through 2025, cancelled loans excluded, 274 loans went to restaurants and other eating places, the class of the state's published case 40.

The official FY2025 approval totals for the state were not readable in the research, so no single-year Arkansas total is printed here. A lender that asks for a feasibility study does so under 13 CFR 120.160(b): SBA may require professional appraisals, a survey, or a feasibility study 42; the tests a loan has to pass come from SOP 50 10 8.1 with Technical Policy Updates 43, and our SBA 7(a) and 504 page explains them.

USDA, HUD and Agency Lending

A REAP round for 21 rural projects, and HUD multifamily run from Fort Worth.

The latest USDA round in the record and the HUD office that handles Arkansas apartments.

USDA Rural Development's round announced on October 18, 2024, counted toward FY2025 and the nearest the research came to a yearly total, awarded more than $6.6 million to 21 rural Arkansas projects under REAP 44. The terms of USDA's guarantees are on our USDA Rural Development page.

HUD handles Arkansas multifamily from its Southwest Region, through the regional center in Fort Worth, with a field office in Little Rock 45, so the regional center for an Arkansas apartment loan insured by HUD is in Texas. Loans outside the federal guarantee programs are the subject of our conventional and institutional lending page.

August 2026 payrolls by sector, and 63 of 75 counties with more deaths than births.

The Division of Workforce Services' August report, the Vintage 2025 components, and incomes in the federal series.

Leisure and hospitality added 6,200 jobs over the year and professional and business services 3,400, while government lost 1,300 and financial activities 1,200 6. Manufacturing held 158,900 jobs, with durable goods up 1,000 and nondurable goods down 800 over the year, and professional and business services held 166,700 6. The table gives the other sectors' August 2026 levels from the same report, which is benchmarked to the first quarter of 2025.

On the seasonally adjusted series, the Arkansas Economist, a secondary source, put twelve-month payroll growth to August 2026 at 7,800 jobs, about 0.6% 46. Unemployment was 3.9% in August 2026, seasonally adjusted, against 4.1% for the nation 6. Because the federal household survey lapsed in October 2025, the report's state underutilization averages rest on eleven months 6.

Little Rock

Little Rock, North Little Rock and Conway: Faulkner, Grant, Lonoke, Perry, Pulaski and Saline counties.

Census counts, payrolls, the convention bureau's year, one brokerage's apartment readings and Pulaski County's data center vote.

The metro held 777,607 residents on July 1, 2025 and 748,989 in 2020, in Talk Business & Politics' report of the Vintage 2025 estimates 52. Pulaski County counted 404,611, Faulkner County 133,979 and Saline County 133,288 in 2025, and Saline's 2020 figure in the same report is 123,830 52. Metroplan's September 2026 report, as FOX16 described it, counted more than 15,000 households added in its region from 2020 to 2025, projected 15,800 housing units by 2030 and named Conway the region's second-largest city; Metroplan's region is its own, not the six-county metro 53.

Metro payrolls were 395,500 in August 2026, not seasonally adjusted, against 391,200 a year earlier 54.

The Little Rock Convention and Visitors Bureau's 2025 annual report credits visitors with $2.19 billion of spending, 12,213 direct jobs and at least $139 million of direct state and local taxes 55, and its facilities drew 480,089 attendees to more than 300 events in 2025 56. Those are the bureau's figures for its city. New rooms appear only under their owners' names: a secondary register of hotel ownership entities records an upper upscale opening in January 2026, an upper midscale opening in March and two upscale openings in April, without the hotels' own names 57. A hotel study in the metro therefore sets its competitive set by name from the ground up.

MMG Real Estate Advisors, a brokerage citing CoStar, put metro apartment occupancy at 88.9% in the first quarter of 2026, down 1.7 percentage points on the year, with an average rent of $1,030 8. Its 2026 forecast counted 574 units delivered in 2025 and projects a rent of $1,039 for 2026, with no completions expected in the year 58; its 2025 forecast had counted 365 units under construction at December 31, 2024 59. One brokerage is the only apartment source the research found for the metro.

Pulaski County is where the data center question turned on a count of votes. The moratorium passed on May 26, 2026 exempted the AVAIO Digital Partners campus near Wrightsville, which Entergy told regulators it expected to start building in June 2026 12; once it was reported void, the county moved toward heavy-industrial zoning for data centers 13. Little Rock adopted siting regulations rather than a pause 9.

City advertising and promotion taxes on lodging run 3% to 4% in Little Rock and 3% in North Little Rock in bnbcalc's 2026 guide, a secondary source 60. SBA's files hold $355.8 million of 7(a) loans for projects in the six counties from approval years 2016 through 2025, cancelled loans excluded 40.

Mississippi County

Mississippi County: steel plants at Osceola, and a home-buying incentive their owners fund.

Projects announced or under way in 2025 and 2026, the county's Census estimates and its housing incentive.

Mississippi County is a steel cluster. A direct reduced iron plant turns iron ore pellets into feedstock for electric-arc furnaces. U.S. Steel announced on April 29, 2026 a direct reduced iron plant at Big River Steel Works in Osceola, which Arkansas Business described in June at $1.9 billion, with about 200 full-time employees, 35 embedded contractor roles and an estimated 2,000 construction jobs at peak, its underground infrastructure already laid 27. It follows the new metals plant U.S. Steel confirmed for Big River in November 2025 23. Hybar's rebar mill, about $700 million of construction and equipment and nearly $1 billion with its solar and battery plant and its Mississippi River port, opened that November, with capacity for more than 700,000 tons of rebar a year 24.

Two processing plants came next: Ferrosource's, at $70 million with operations expected in August 2026 61, and Marubeni-Itochu Steel America's flat-rolled processing plant at $37 million 62. Nucor-Yamato, Nucor Steel Arkansas and Nucor Skyline also operate in the county 63. Each of those amounts describes one plant, not the county's market.

The county counted 37,828 residents in 2025 against 38,174 in 2024, in the Census series FRED tabulates 64. Local officials described an affordable housing crisis driven by an influx of industrial and construction workers, and in May 2025 the steelmakers added more than $800,000 to the county's home-buying incentive, bringing its funding above $6 million; a buyer can receive up to $50,000 toward a new home or $25,000 toward an existing one by living in the house and staying in the job for four years, and the coverage named an average house price of $267,000 18.

South Arkansas

South Arkansas: Union, Columbia and Lafayette counties, and the Smackover lithium project.

Each county's Census counts, the lithium project's filed numbers and Union County's data center pause.

Union County counted 36,926 residents in 2025 against 37,121 in 2024, Columbia County 21,894 against 22,014 and Lafayette County 5,888 against 6,056, in the Census series FRED tabulates 64. Ouachita County, which the research reads with them but this section's market leaves out, counted 21,731 against 21,856 64. No 2020 base for these counties was found.

The South West Arkansas lithium project of Smackover Lithium, a Standard Lithium and Equinor joint venture west of Magnolia, carries a total capital cost of $1.45 billion in its definitive feasibility study of October 14, 2025, with a Department of Energy grant of $225 million and planned output of 22,500 tonnes of lithium carbonate a year; its federal environmental review is complete 65. Trafigura signed a ten-year offtake for 8,000 tonnes a year in March 2026 26, and the final investment decision was targeted for the end of 2026, with first production in 2029 rather than the 2028 first planned 66. The Oil and Gas Commission approved a state royalty of 2.5% for the Reynolds Unit's first phase on May 29, 2025 67. Standard Lithium's annual filing with the SEC ties the investment decision and construction to financing, and places its demonstration plant at the Lanxess South site in El Dorado 68. The project page records LG Energy Solution as a second binding offtake buyer and names its well field and processing contractors 65. Until the decision is taken, the construction workforce a lodging or housing study in these counties might count on is a plan, not a payroll.

Union County's one-year moratorium, adopted unanimously on June 30, 2026 and running from July 10, 2026 to July 9, 2027, pauses data center development while the county studies water, electricity, other utilities, emergency services, property and zoning; ServerCountry, citing local reports, tied it to a proposed campus near El Dorado 2.

Fort Smith

Fort Smith: Sebastian and Crawford counties, the Arkansas side of a metro that reaches into Oklahoma.

County counts, county register values, and the F-35 mission at Ebbing Air National Guard Base.

The Census Bureau's Fort Smith metro is Crawford and Sebastian counties in Arkansas with Sequoyah County, Oklahoma 51, and in 2025 BLS removed Le Flore County, Oklahoma, from the metro it reports 69. The metro's population and payroll series therefore include Oklahoma residents and jobs, and this page does not print them as Arkansas figures. Every figure in this section is for Sebastian or Crawford County alone, the register's included. The research reads the market as Sebastian, Crawford and Franklin counties; Franklin is not in the Census Bureau's metro, and the research holds no figure for it. Sequoyah County is not one of the markets on our Oklahoma page.

Sebastian County counted 130,641 residents in 2025 against 127,807 in 2020, and Crawford County 62,657 against 60,164 52. Median household income in the 2020 to 2024 five-year survey was $57,790 in Sebastian County and $64,132 in Crawford County 48.

The defining project is the F-35 Foreign Military Sales pilot training center at Ebbing Air National Guard Base. The Air Force estimates it at $807 million, about $600 million of federal funds had been appropriated for the build-out since 2021, and BL Harbert's contracts in August 2026 included $38.958 million for a Special Access Program Facility 70. The FY2025 appropriation of $141 million was secured in May 2025 71. About 400 Singapore military personnel were expected at Ebbing, Arkansas Money & Politics reported in August 2026 72, and Lockheed Martin raised its planned local workforce from 110 to more than 230 positions in June 2026 28. Singapore's F-35s were expected late in 2026 and its F-16s in the summer of 2027, the same August report said 72. On the road side, Talk Business & Politics reported in October 2026 that the I-49 bridge over the Arkansas River was ahead of schedule 73.

A sewer consent decree was among Talk Business & Politics' top Fort Smith metro stories of 2024, and its current terms were not found 74. The city's advertising and promotion tax is 3% in bnbcalc's 2026 guide 60.

Jonesboro

Jonesboro: Craighead and Poinsett counties, with a medical school at Arkansas State.

The metro's Census counts and payrolls, the university's new schools and an undated employer list.

The Jonesboro metro is Craighead and Poinsett counties, and the research carries a county count for Craighead alone. The metro counted 139,440 residents on July 1, 2025 and 134,577 in 2020, and Craighead County 116,957 against 111,624 52. Payrolls stood at 64,500 in August 2026 against 63,900 a year earlier, not seasonally adjusted, after a record 65,500 in December 2025 54.

Arkansas State University now hosts the NYIT medical school and has broken ground on the state's first public veterinary school, Talk Business & Politics reported in July 2025 75. Jonesboro Unlimited's list of major employers is headed by St. Bernards Healthcare, Arkansas State University and NEA Baptist Health System, but the list is undated, and this section leaves its headcounts out 76.

Craighead County is one of the 17 counties in the DR-4873 declaration for the April 2025 storms 16. Jonesboro levies a 3% advertising and promotion tax, by the same 2026 guide 60, and no supply figure for any asset class was found for the market.

Hot Springs

Hot Springs: Garland County, and a short-term rental cap that is full.

Garland County's estimates and jobs, the rental licence cap and the listings market.

Census estimates released in March 2026 made Hot Springs the only Arkansas metro to lose residents from 2024 to 2025, Talk Business & Politics reported 52. Garland County, which is the Hot Springs metro, counted 99,695 residents on July 1, 2025 against 100,259 in 2020 52, and payrolls of 40,500 in August 2026, down from 40,800 in August 2025 on the unadjusted count 54.

The city's cap of 400 short-term rental licences in residential zones has been reached, so no new residential licences are issued, while non-residential zones still take applications, under Ordinance 6435 of August 2, 2022 77. Airbtics counted 1,957 short-term rental listings for full-year 2025, with occupancy of 48% and an average daily rate of $191 78.

The five-year survey's median value of owner-occupied homes in Garland County was $194,300 for 2020 to 2024 48.

Pine Bluff

Pine Bluff: Jefferson County, and a casino resort's new hotel.

Jefferson County's Census counts and the Saracen expansion.

Jefferson County counted 62,987 residents in 2025 against 66,825 in 2020 79. Cleveland County, part of the Census Bureau's Pine Bluff micropolitan area 51 though not of this section's market, had 7,224 residents in 2025 64. BLS stopped reporting Pine Bluff as a metro in 2025 69.

Saracen Casino Resort's expansion adds a 13-storey hotel that the sources put at 320 or 321 rooms and an event center of 1,600 seats, a $250 million project announced in August 2024 80; it was scheduled for fall 2025, and a secondary source reported its opening on February 26, 2026 81. Before the expansion the resort employed about 800 people, Yogonet reported in March 2025 82.

Hazards

Two major disasters and two emergencies declared for Arkansas between April 2025 and January 2026.

Each declaration with its dates and counties, and the perils the state's insurance review names.

EM-3627-AR, an emergency declaration for severe storms, tornadoes and flooding, came on April 5, 2025 14. The storms of March 14 and 15 became DR-4865-AR on May 8, 2025, after an initial request was denied on April 11, with Individual Assistance for Greene, Hot Spring, Independence, Izard, Jackson, Lawrence, Randolph, Sharp and Stone counties 15. The storms and flooding of April 2 to 22 became DR-4873-AR on May 21, 2025, covering 17 counties including Pulaski, Craighead, Crittenden and Saline 16.

SBA's deadlines for economic injury disaster loans ran to February 9, 2026 under DR-4865 and February 23, 2026 under DR-4873 83. A winter storm from January 23 to 27, 2026 brought EM-3636-AR on January 24 14. Hot Spring County, on the DR-4865 list, is not Garland County, the county of the city of Hot Springs.

The perils behind those declarations are the ones the Senate's 2024 review named as the main sources of property damage, wind and hail 11. The research did not retrieve a FEMA risk ranking or flood, seismic and wildfire exposure measures for the state.

Rules by Asset Class

A Permit of Approval for long-term care, child care licences that end with a move, and rentals left to the cities.

The state rules a project meets before its market case.

The research found no statewide licensing or tax change in 2025 or 2026 that blocks a conventional commercial asset class; the state rules below shape timing, cost and licensing instead. Arkansas's Certificate of Need takes the form of a Permit of Approval from the Health Services Permit Agency and Commission. It is required for nursing facilities, residential care, assisted living, home health, hospice, psychiatric residential care and intermediate care facilities for individuals with intellectual disabilities, and for nursing home construction or renovation above a cost threshold; the Commission was meeting and issuing permits in 2026 84. Its June 10, 2025 minutes record a permit for 58 Assisted Living Level II units in Fayetteville 85. Bed or unit thresholds for assisted living, and a memory care licence category, were not found.

Child care licensing runs through the Department of Human Services' Division of Child Care and Early Childhood Education under Rule 016.22.20-005, in four classes: child care centers, licensed child care family homes, out-of-school time facilities and registered child care family homes. A licence closes automatically when the location or the operator changes 86, so a purchase or a move needs a new one.

The Petroleum Storage Tank Trust Fund reimburses corrective action for registered tanks whose fees are paid, not retrofits or replacements, and its advisory committee reviewed the fund's finances and reimbursements monthly through 2025 87; the fund's balance was not found.

No state statute governs short-term rentals. HB1445 went to interim study, and HB1790, which would have preempted local rules, died on May 5, 2025 2117. Act 459 of 2025 did preempt local limits on rental application fees and deposits, from August 5, 2025 21, so an apartment budget's fee and deposit lines follow state law rather than a city ordinance.

Lodging pays the state sales tax and a state tourism tax, plus a city advertising and promotion tax; the market sections give the city rates the 2026 guide lists, and the research found no typical combined rate. Liquor licence quotas, costs and transfers were not found.

Arkansas Questions

Questions about Arkansas projects, answered from this record.

What is Arkansas's Permit of Approval, and which projects need one?

It is the state's form of Certificate of Need, issued by the Health Services Permit Agency and Commission. Home health, hospice, assisted living, residential care, nursing facilities, psychiatric residential care and the intermediate care facilities that serve individuals with intellectual disabilities need one, as does building or renovating a nursing home past a cost threshold; through 2026 the Commission met and issued permits 84. Its June 10, 2025 minutes show a Fayetteville permit for 58 units of Assisted Living Level II 85. No bed or unit threshold for assisted living, and no memory care licence category, turned up in the research.

Are Arkansas short-term rental rules set by the state or by the city?

By the city. Arkansas has no short-term rental statute: HB1445 went to interim study, and HB1790, the 2025 bill to preempt local rules, died on May 5, 2025 2117. Hot Springs has issued every licence under its residential-zone cap of 400 and takes applications only for non-residential zones, under Ordinance 6435 of August 2, 2022 77; Airbtics counted 1,957 listings there for full-year 2025, with 48% occupancy and $191 as the average daily rate 78.

What is being built at Ebbing Air National Guard Base in Fort Smith?

An F-35 Foreign Military Sales pilot training center. The Air Force puts it at $807 million, about $600 million of federal money had been appropriated since 2021, and a Special Access Program Facility took $38.958 million of BL Harbert's August 2026 contracts 70; the $141 million FY2025 appropriation was secured in May 2025 71. The base expected about 400 Singapore military personnel, their F-35s late in 2026 and F-16s in summer 2027 72, and Lockheed Martin's planned Fort Smith workforce went from 110 to more than 230 positions in June 2026 28.

Is a new apartment building in Little Rock competing with anything under construction?

MMG Real Estate Advisors, citing CoStar, expects no completions in the metro in 2026, after 574 units were delivered in 2025, with a projected 2026 rent of $1,039 58; at the end of 2024 its 2025 forecast had 365 units under construction 59. First-quarter 2026 occupancy was 88.9%, 1.7 percentage points under a year earlier, and the average rent $1,030 8.

How many Arkansas counties recorded more deaths than births in the year to July 2025?

63 of the 75, by the count of the UA Little Rock institute that works with the State Data Center 47. The state's estimate for July 1, 2025 was 3,114,791 7.

Did Arkansas change its sales tax exemption for data centers?

Yes. Under Act 548 of 2025 a data center qualifies for the state's sales and use tax exemption with an investment of $100 million, down from $500 million, as the Democrat-Gazette reported 31.
