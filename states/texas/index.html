The Texas Thesis

Texas still grows. It no longer grows the way old pro formas assume.

Texas added more people than any other state in the year to July 2025 and passed 31.7 million residents 1. A lender reading that headline could conclude that demand in Texas is a given. It is not, and 2026 is the year the difference started to show in underwriting files. The surge of 2021 to 2023 was carried by people moving in from other states and from abroad, and both of those inflows fell sharply in the latest Census vintage 23. Births still outnumber deaths by a wide margin, and job growth still leads the country 5, but the extra layer of in-migration that let almost any new apartment, storage facility or hotel lease up faster than plan is thinner than it was.

At the same time, Texas has added gates that did not exist when many of the studies now on lenders' desks were written. Large electrical loads queue for interconnection under a 2025 statute and a draft 2026 rule 49. The Governor paused data center interconnection in August 2026 and environmental permits for data center infrastructure in September 5051. Coastal property insurance moved through a residual-market reset, with the FAIR Plan writing commercial association policies for the first time in 2025 43. The July 2025 Hill Country floods produced the state's only documented federal major disaster of the period 53.

None of that makes Texas a weak market. It makes Texas a market where a statewide number misleads. Houston and Dallas-Fort Worth added more people than any other metros in the country 73, while El Paso's metro lost population in the same year 74. Austin's industrial vacancy in mid-2026 was more than double that of Dallas-Fort Worth 7576. A study that averages them describes no real place. We build every Texas study on the metro the site sits in, the capital program the borrower is using, and evidence with a date a reviewer can check. This page sets out the statewide layer: the economy, capital access, rules, costs, hazards and the 2025 to 2026 events that sit under every Texas file. The eight metro pages carry the local evidence, and the worked cases show the method on real Texas towns.

The population figure most studies quote is still impressive: 31,709,821 Texans on July 1, 2025, up from an estimates base of 29,149,498 in April 2020 1. The one-year gain of 391,243 was the largest of any state, but in percentage terms Texas slipped from first to fourth 1. The reason is migration. Net international migration into Texas fell to 167,475, a drop of 48% from 319,569 the year before, as the national figure fell by more than half 2. The domestic stream, which carried much of the 2021 to 2023 absorption story, came in at 67,299, behind North Carolina, and against 222,154 in 2022 3. Texas 2036, reading the state demographer's figures, describes about 184 net domestic arrivals a day, against more than 500 a day two years earlier 4.

What remains is natural increase and jobs. Texas recorded 390,203 births and 232,492 deaths in the year 3; the source's own net figure does not match that subtraction, so we cite the components rather than the net. Employment is the stronger story. Total nonfarm jobs reached 14,469,300 in August 2026 6, and the twelve-month gain of 159,400, or 1.1%, was the largest of any state and 0.7 points ahead of the national rate 5. The August month alone added 5,600 jobs, led by leisure and hospitality at 4,900 and professional and business services at 4,500 8. Two readings temper that. Unemployment sat at 4.4% against 4.1% nationally, and manufacturing employment of 982,800 was slightly below a year earlier 7.

What this means for a Texas demand model

A study written in 2023 could reasonably project household formation from the in-migration of the prior two years. A study written now cannot. Any model that converts population growth into demand, whether apartments, storage units, retail square feet, school seats or rooftops for a convenience store, should be re-based on the Vintage 2025 numbers and should say which vintage it used. Where a metro's growth still leans on in-migration, the study should run a case at the lower migration rate and show the loan still works. Where growth leans on jobs, the study should name the employers and the sectors, because a jobs-led market behaves differently in a downturn from a retirement-led or migration-led one.

The slowdown is also uneven. The large metros in the east of the state still led the nation in numeric growth, while a border metro posted a decline in the same Census release. That is one more reason the useful unit of analysis is the metro, and below it the trade area, not the state.

Texas is served by six SBA district offices, and the spread in their FY2025 dollars is wide: $1.5 billion in the Dallas/Fort Worth district against $37 million in El Paso, by El Paso Inc.'s reading of SBA's regional report 9. Two features of the map matter for a study. First, Austin has no district office of its own. Travis, Williamson and Hays counties sit inside the 55-county San Antonio district 12, so a Central Texas file is reviewed against a San Antonio district portfolio. Second, the district totals mix very different economies. The 71 counties of the West Texas district run from Lubbock to the Permian Basin 68, which means a district-level loan count says little about the market for a hotel or a storage facility in any one of them.

Statewide 7(a) totals are not published by SBA in a form the research could retrieve. A loan broker's tabulation of SBA loan-level data puts Texas at 6,295 7(a) loans and $4,067,228,500 in FY2025 62; we cite it as a secondary figure and expect the SBA loan-level files to replace it. What the evidence does support is that SBA credit in Texas is deep in the eastern metros and thin in the west and along the border, so a study for a borrower in the thinner districts should expect fewer lenders with recent comparable loans and should carry more of its own market evidence. On the 504 side, the debenture goes through a Certified Development Company; the oldest in Texas dates its founding to 1981 by its own account 72, and statewide CDCs underwrite projects in every district, which helps a borrower outside the eastern metros find a 504 partner even where local bank appetite is thin.

The state's own credit program

Texas runs its federal Small Business Credit Initiative money through the Texas Small Business Credit Initiative. The state's allocation totals about $472 million, split between a Capital Access Program of $118 million and a Loan Guarantee Program of $354.1 million 59; the portal's total and its components differ by $0.1 million, so we cite the parts. The Texas Economic Development Bank recorded receipt of the first tranche, $134,488,297, on July 11, 2023 60, and enrolled loans from January 15, 2024 for businesses with 499 or fewer employees 61. The same bank is winding down the original Capital Access Program it administered before the federal money arrived 60. For a feasibility study the program matters in one way: a TSBCI guarantee or loss reserve changes what the lender can accept on collateral, not what the market will pay, so it never substitutes for demand evidence.

USDA money is concentrated in Texas

Texas led every state in USDA Business and Industry guarantees in FY2025, with $289.2 million of obligations, 13.7% of the national total, ahead of New York at $144.6 million and Louisiana at $137.0 million 13. It ranked second in Section 538 rural rental housing guarantees at $33.8 million, behind North Carolina's $66.6 million 14, and far down the list in REAP guaranteed energy loans at $3.3 million, against Illinois's $33.4 million 13. USDA Rural Development's Texas state office is in Temple 63. Those figures say Texas lenders know the B&I program well, which helps a rural borrower, and they also mean Texas files are a large share of what the Agency reviews, which is one reason the study components in 7 CFR Part 5001 get applied closely here.

SBA in Texas

The SBA rules a Texas study is tested against.

Every SBA application received on or after October 1, 2026 is underwritten under the new SOP. These are the rules a Texas feasibility study has to meet.

The governing text is SOP 50 10 8.1 with Technical Policy Updates, effective October 1, 2026 57. It did not create a feasibility study mandate. The regulation is unchanged: 13 CFR 120.160(b): SBA may require professional appraisals, a survey, or a feasibility study 56. Lenders ask for one when they cannot underwrite repayment from history, which in Texas describes a large share of the pipeline: ground-up gas stations and travel centers on new interchanges, express car washes, limited-service hotels and the other special purpose properties SBA lists. Our note on the SBA feasibility study myth sets out the regulation in full, and the SOP 50 10 8.1 note covers what changed this month.

Four rules decide most Texas files. A start-up borrowing under 7(a) must inject 10% of total project cost, and its projections must reach 1.15x coverage within two years. An acquisition is a change of ownership: coverage of 1.25x measured on the seller's historical cash flow, which a study cannot manufacture from projections. Under 504, a new business or a special purpose property needs 15% and a new business in a special purpose property needs 20%, which catches most new Texas car washes, hotels and fuel stations. The 504 debenture is capped at $5 million, and a borrower who takes 7(a) first and 504 second can now reach $10 million combined.

One FY2027 fee rule reaches rural Texas directly. The upfront 7(a) guaranty fee is 0% on loans of $700,000 or less to businesses in a rural area, defined as a county that the U.S. Census Bureau has defined as at least 30 percent rural 58. A study for a small project in a rural county should state the county's Census rural share, because the waiver changes the sources and uses. Our Texas cases test these rules on real towns: the Seguin express car wash and the Terrell truck stop both carry the 20% tier, and the Weatherford dealership shows what happens when a proposal runs into the debenture limit.

Unlike SBA, USDA writes the study requirement into its rule. A Business and Industry guarantee over $1,000,000 to a new business needs a feasibility study prepared by an independent qualified consultant the Agency accepts, and the Agency sets its scope; below that amount the Agency may still ask for one when the lender's analysis is insufficient or the project affects an existing business's cash flow 16. The lender must then write its own evaluation of the study into the credit file 21, and the projections must run at least two years past full capacity or stable operations 16. Our note on the five USDA study components explains how the economic, market, technical, financial and management sections are judged.

The terms improved in FY2026. The guarantee on loans under $5 million went from 80% to 85% 18, with a 3.0% initial fee and a 0.55% annual retention fee 15. The program's money is less certain. USDA obligated $2,111,702,000 of B&I guarantees in FY2025 against an appropriated program level of $1,663,000,000, covering the gap with carried-over and recovered authority 13. For FY2026, the enacted act language provides $1,750,000,000 of loan authority 19 while USDA's own lead-off table shows $1,428,899,083 13, and the two have not been reconciled in any record we found. The FY2027 budget request proposes a program level of zero 13. A Texas borrower whose closing depends on a B&I guarantee late in the fiscal year should know that.

The lender base also changed. On May 12, 2026 USDA revoked the approved-lender status of ten OneRD lenders whose portfolios held about $620 million of delinquent loans, roughly 47% of Rural Development's delinquencies 20. For a study, the practical effect is closer review of the market and management sections by the lenders that remain. Wind and solar projects became ineligible for B&I in August 2025 67, which closes one Texas use of the program. Our USDA cases in Texas, the Elgin meat processing plant, the Bastrop RV park and the Hankamer I-10 travel center, apply these terms to real sites, and the USDA deadline calendar tracks the fiscal-year timing.

Rural rental housing: the Section 538 changes of September 2026

Texas ranked second in Section 538 guaranteed rural rental housing in FY2025 14, so the program's autumn changes reach Texas apartment sponsors in small towns. From September 25, 2026 the maximum loan-to-cost on the continuous guarantee option rose from 70% to 80% of total development cost 69. A fee revision effective September 30 set the standard fees at 65 basis points initial and 35 annual, with a reduced 60 and 25 for workforce housing, preservation of older USDA properties and new construction in a Qualified Opportunity Zone 70. A preservation pilot that began on October 9 lowers the coverage requirement from 1.15 to 1.11 for its first 200 loans 71. Higher leverage and lower coverage both shift weight onto the market study: at 80% of cost, a rent assumption that misses by a few percent leaves little equity cushion, and USDA has itself proposed requiring a market study in complete Section 538 applications for new construction 14.

For senior housing and multifamily in Texas, the agency programs set the bar the study has to clear, and they changed in both directions over the last two years. HUD made refinancing existing Section 232 facilities faster: the Express Lane accepts low-leverage deals with strong trailing coverage and has cut processing from months to weeks 22, and Section 232 firm commitments doubled to a record $8.1 billion in FY2025 24. New construction did not get the same treatment. In HUD's draft Section 232 valuation chapter the market study sits inside the appraisal, written by the same person, ordered and paid for by the lender, with a requirement to map every competing facility in the primary market area, which the draft expects to supply 60% to 80% of demand 6566. For multifamily under the MAP Guide, the market study ages out 120 days after the pre-application and 180 days after the firm commitment, and the market analyst must analyze rents independently of the appraiser 6566.

The credit record explains why reviewers are careful. HUD's Inspector General found 167 of 3,670 Section 232 borrowers in default in June 2024, with more than $1.1 billion of unpaid principal, while the office overseeing those loans shrank from 61 to 38 staff 25. Fannie Mae's seniors housing book carried a serious delinquency rate of 4.09% against 0.63% for its whole multifamily book 28. A Texas senior housing study therefore has to show stabilized occupancy and rents from named competitors, not from a national benchmark. Our Boerne assisted living and memory care case works through a Section 232 new construction file in Kendall County.

On the conventional side, the Federal Housing Finance Agency raised each Enterprise's 2026 multifamily purchase cap to $88 billion 26, so agency appetite for Texas apartments is not the constraint. The constraint is the market: a lender sizing to 1.25x or more on stabilized income still needs a supportable rent and absorption schedule for the specific submarket, which is where the Texas metro pages begin.

EB-5 and Conventional

EB-5 and conventional capital: what the Texas evidence does not yet cover.

Two of the five Texas capital channels are not covered by the dated research behind this page. For EB-5, a Texas project's study has to support the targeted employment area designation at the filing date and produce a job count the investors' petitions can rely on; our note on how EB-5 jobs are counted explains the method, and the EB-5 practice area sets out the deliverable. We do not publish Texas EB-5 volume or TEA counts here because we hold no dated source for them.

For conventional bank, life company and CMBS lending in Texas, the research we hold carries no Texas-specific terms or volumes, so this page does not state any. What a conventional lender asks of a Texas study is the same thing the agency programs ask in more formal language: a defined trade area, a competitive census with named properties, absorption evidence from the current cycle rather than the last one, and a sensitivity analysis that shows where coverage breaks. The conventional and institutional practice area describes that work.

The pattern across asset classes is that Texas leaves supply to the market and to local government. The state sets no quota on liquor licenses; availability follows local wet and dry elections 33. It has not preempted city short-term rental rules, and the online-marketplace bill of the 2025 session cleared a House committee without a recorded final outcome 35. It no longer reimburses petroleum storage tank cleanups: the fund stopped paying owners on September 1, 2012 and now finances only state-led cleanups, so every fuel retailer in Texas carries federal financial responsibility through private coverage 34. For a gas station or travel center study that means the environmental section prices insurance and a Phase I, not a state backstop; our gas station and convenience store page covers the full document list.

Senior housing licensing is the one area where state rules shape the product directly. Texas licenses assisted living as Type A, for residents who can evacuate on their own, or Type B, for residents who may need help or night attendance, and it stopped issuing Type C 29. A memory care unit marketed for Alzheimer's care needs certification and a Type B license 30. The license type sets staffing, and staffing sets the operating budget, so a study that prices a Type A budget for a building that will admit Type B residents understates expenses. The assisted living and memory care page explains the staffing test.

For lodging, the tax structure matters to the revenue line. The state tax is 6% on rooms of $15 a night or more, cities may generally add up to 7%, and venue taxes up to 2% apply in some places, with a combined cap that is generally 17% 3637. Occupancy tax receipts are also one of the few public demand series for a Texas hotel study, because cities report them; our hotel page and the Bastrop hotel case use them that way.

Some rules a lender will ask about are not in any source we could date. The scope of Texas Certificate of Need review, campground and RV park licensing, car wash water recycling rules, the sales tax treatment of construction materials and the primary text of child-care center ratios were all not found in the research behind this page, so a study should confirm each one with the agency for the specific project rather than rely on a summary.

The only statewide property tax figure in the research is for owner-occupied homes: an effective rate of 1.40%, seventh in the country on the Tax Foundation's method, with other compilers putting it at 1.245% or 1.49% 38. A commercial effective rate for Texas was not found. That gap is the reason a Texas study should never apply a statewide rate to a commercial pro forma. The right number is the sum of the taxing units for the specific parcel (county, city, school district, hospital district, community college and any special district), applied to a value the appraisal district is likely to set for a new building of that type. In fast-growing exurban counties the school and special-district rates can dominate, and the value a new building is assessed at in its second year is often higher than the cost the pro forma started from.

Windstorm cover is the second line that moves. On the coast, the Texas Windstorm Insurance Association is the market of last resort. Its 2025 analysis found rates inadequate by 3% for residential and 5% for commercial policies, yet its board directed a 0% filing for 2026 39. A year later the picture had flipped: the 2026 analysis found rates adequate by 9% and 4%, which the association attributed to a 2025 law that reduced required catastrophe funding 40, and its committee recommended no change for 2027 41. The commercial indication came in 9% lower than the 2025 study, with the reinsurance expense ratio down 8.7% 42.

Inland and for properties that cannot place cover privately, the Texas FAIR Plan began writing commercial property owners' association policies in 2025, using ISO loss costs with a loss cost multiplier of 4.865 and a $350 policy fee 43. Its residential changes from September 1, 2026 run from a 2.6% cut for homeowners to a 21.6% increase for dwelling extended coverage 44, a reminder that residual-market pricing can move in double digits in a single year. Residual commercial auto rises 4.9% from November 1, 2026 45, which matters for fleets, dealerships and travel centers. A coastal study should carry a dated quote, and a lender should make the bound policy a condition of closing.

The generation side is crowded too, with 2,008 requests totaling 453,562 megawatts, led by storage and solar 47.

The state's response came in steps. Senate Bill 6, signed in June 2025, set interconnection standards for loads of 75 megawatts or more, and the Public Utility Commission's draft rule of March 2026 proposed a non-refundable fee of $50,000 per megawatt, with a final rule due by year-end 49. On August 3, 2026 the Governor directed an audit and paused data center interconnection; ERCOT then missed its Batch Zero deadline 50. On September 21 the environmental agency paused air and water permits for infrastructure serving data centers while a survey of more than 400 facilities runs to December 51, and the state water board was told to compel water-use reporting by major users 52.

Why it reaches other projects

A hotel, a distribution building or an assisted living community does not need a 75-megawatt interconnection. It does need its utility to commit to serve it, sometimes with a new feeder or substation, and in the same territories where data centers queued for capacity, those commitments now come later and with more conditions. Water is the same. Where a city or water district is being asked to account for large users, a new connection for a car wash, a meat processing plant or a 200-unit apartment building can take longer to confirm. A Texas study should therefore carry will-serve letters for electricity, water and sewer dated after August 2026, or a lender condition that requires them before closing. For a project that is itself a large load, the study must model the interconnection timeline and the draft fee as part of the cost.

Review Failures

How Texas feasibility studies fail review.

The research holds no count of studies rejected for market deficiencies, so we state none. These are the patterns the 2025 to 2026 evidence exposes.

  1. Demand built on the last cycle's migration

    A study that projects absorption from the 2021 to 2023 inflow overstates demand now that international migration is down 48% and domestic migration is at its lowest in more than a decade 24. The fix is the Vintage 2025 base and a lower-migration case.

  2. A statewide or national number where a metro number belongs

    There is no defensible statewide supply figure for most asset classes, and the metros diverge. A study that cites a Texas average for vacancy, rent or occupancy is describing no real market.

  3. Utility capacity assumed, not confirmed

    After the August and September 2026 pauses 5051, a pro forma that assumes power and water arrive on the construction schedule without a dated will-serve letter is an untested assumption a reviewer will flag.

  4. Insurance priced from an average

    Windstorm and residual-market pricing moved in 2025 and 2026 4044. A coastal study without a quote for the subject property gives the lender no basis for the insurance line.

  5. The wrong program's study standard

    A USDA B&I loan over $1,000,000 to a new business needs a study from an independent consultant the Agency accepts 16; a HUD senior housing study must meet the program's independence and age rules 6566. A generic study written for an SBA lender will not pass either review.

  6. Flood and outage exposure left out

    The July 2025 floods reached counties in the Austin metro 54. A study that omits the flood zone and the site's disaster history leaves the lender to find it in due diligence.

  7. Model numbers that do not trace

    Reviewers now check that every input is sourced or labelled as an assumption, and that the debt sizing follows the program's current rules. Our cases publish the inputs and the model so a reviewer can check both. Our note on why feasibility studies fail review covers the national pattern.

The Deliverable

What a Texas feasibility study has to contain.

The statewide layer on this page sits under every file. These are the parts a Texas lender or agency reviewer will look for first.

A Texas study starts with the trade area, drawn from drive times and the actual road network rather than a radius, because a river, a toll road or an interstate frontage pattern can cut a circle in half. Inside that trade area it counts competitors by name and location, each confirmed by the operator's own listing or the county appraisal district record, and it says which of them opened, expanded or closed since the last cycle. It then sets demand from the Vintage 2025 population base, the jobs the metro actually added, and the traffic or visitor counts the specific asset depends on, with every figure dated and linked.

The second part is the capital program. An SBA start-up study shows projections that reach the coverage floor within two years; an SBA acquisition study tests the seller's history; a USDA study for a new business over $1,000,000 follows the five Agency components 16; a HUD senior housing study meets the program's age and independence rules. The same market can support one program's loan and fail another's, so the study names the program it was written for.

The third part is the gate list that is particular to Texas in 2026: utility will-serve letters dated after the August pause, a flood zone from the effective panel, a windstorm or residual-market quote on the coast, and the local permits that decide whether the use can open at all, from a specific use permit for a truck stop to a conditional use for a dealership. Where the evidence could not be found, the study says so and turns the gap into a lender condition instead of filling it with an estimate. Every one of our Texas cases ends with that list, and the model behind each case is published input by input, so a reviewer can change an assumption and see the loan move.

Two Site Tests

The traffic count and the program maps: one method in every Texas metro.

Our Houston, Dallas-Fort Worth, Austin and San Antonio pages send readers here for both checks. The corridors and the towns differ by metro; the way a study proves them does not.

Traffic: the station, its year and its position

The research behind our Texas pages holds almost no station-level traffic counts for the large metros, so a study has to pull its own. TxDOT publishes annual average daily traffic by count station on a statewide map layer 84, a second layer for freeway ramps 122, and count maps that carry vehicle classification where the truck share drives revenue 123. For every count it relies on, the study should report three facts: the station ID, the year the count was taken, and where the station sits against the parcel (mainlanes, frontage road, ramp or cross street). A volume without those three facts is a listing-flyer number, and a reviewer will weigh it as one.

Position usually matters more than volume. A site on a frontage road sees only the traffic that has already exited, and a station on a tolled mainlane counts drivers who cannot reach the parcel without leaving the toll road. On a state frontage road the TxDOT district also reviews driveway spacing before it permits an entrance (our Houston page shows that district\'s application), which can leave a parcel fewer driveways than the broker's plan shows. The traffic section of a study therefore pairs the count with the access the district is likely to approve; each metro page names the corridors and forms that apply there.

Eligibility: rural, Opportunity Zone and HUBZone status belong to the parcel

None of the three program tests can be read off a city name. A USDA Business and Industry guarantee needs a site outside any city or town of more than 50,000 people 18, and USDA's eligibility tool answers the question by address 85; inside the large metros the qualifying land is unincorporated or sits in the small towns at the edge. Where the guarantee exceeds $1,000,000 and the borrower is a new business, the rule calls for a feasibility study by an independent consultant the Agency accepts 86. Opportunity Zone status follows the census tract, so the study matches the tract to the designated list 87. HUBZone status comes from the SBA's map, which changes with tract updates and states when the next one is due 88. An EB-5 raise adds a fourth lookup, the targeted employment area finding for the site, described in the EB-5 section above 89.

Each test belongs at the start of the engagement, when the program is chosen, and each result needs its date, because a map read in the first week can change before closing. Three local items the research found for none of the four large metros (impact fee schedules, utility capacity and the terms of short-term rental ordinances) become written conditions in the same way: a fee estimate from the city, a will-serve letter from the utility or district, and the ordinance as adopted.

McAllen and the Valley: border trade, winter residents and a softening rental market.

A Valley study has to show that demand holds through the crossing cycle and the summer months, and price new supply against a rental market HUD found slightly soft.

Economic base

The McAllen-Edinburg-Mission metro is Hidalgo County alone, and its largest employers are institutions rather than plants. HUD's July 2025 market analysis, which leaves school districts out, lists Doctors Hospital at Renaissance with 6,423 workers, the University of Texas Rio Grande Valley with 5,247, U.S. Customs and Border Protection with 3,700, Hidalgo County with 3,579 and H-E-B with 3,150 77. That payroll does not follow the retail cycle, and a border inspection force of that size shows how much of the economy rides on the crossing.

The crossing is large. In 2024 the Hidalgo port of entry handled $15.3 billion of exports, more than 779,500 trucks entered through it, and Foreign Trade Zone No. 12 counted more than 410 member companies 77. A truck stop, warehouse, cold storage or fleet service project should tie its revenue to those volumes and show debt coverage at lower ones. HUD also records Driscoll Children's Hospital in Edinburg, a $100 million facility, as built and hiring in 2024 to 2025, and a $24.8 million Sam's Club distribution center in Weslaco for 2026; no Valley project above $50 million was found for 2025 to 2026 77.

Growth, seasonality and supply

Census estimates carried in the St. Louis Fed's ALFRED archive put the metro at 921,549 residents on July 1, 2025 78; HUD's own estimate for that date is 927,000 77. The city of McAllen held 150,640 residents 79, so most of the market lives in Edinburg, Mission, Weslaco and smaller towns, and a trade area drawn around McAllen alone misses most of its customers. HUD counted 2,225 for-sale homes and 2,375 rental units under construction on July 1, 2025 77. The research holds no Census-published 2020 base and no verified payroll count, so no five-year or job-growth rate is given for the Valley.

HUD names the Winter Texans, retirees who spend the cold months in the Valley, as a source of seasonal demand, but it gives no count 77. RV parks, senior housing, restaurants and clinics swell in winter and thin out in summer, so a study for any of them has to build revenue month by month, compute coverage on the full year rather than the peak, and cite a dated count of seasonal residents instead of a figure repeated in marketing material.

Apartments are the only property type with published supply evidence. HUD, citing CoStar, measured vacancy at 11.2% in the second quarter of 2025, up from 10.6% a year earlier, with an average rent of $986, down $6, and estimated demand for 5,400 units over three years against the 2,375 under way 77. The pipeline is absorbable, but vacancy rose while it was being built, so new units lease on price. A multifamily study should underwrite concessions and bring those mid-2025 readings current. Hotels, storage, industrial space, retail, senior housing, RV parks and convenience stores have no Valley figure in the research; each needs a competitor census.

The SBA's Lower Rio Grande Valley District made nearly $103 million of 7(a) and 504 loans to more than 180 companies in fiscal 2025, and Lone Star National Bank of McAllen led the district in 7(a) loans by count 80. The district office is in Harlingen, and the partial county list the research found omits Hidalgo, so the assignment should be confirmed 81. In fiscal 2027, 7(a) loans up to $700,000 to rural-area businesses carry an upfront fee of 0%, a rural area being a county that the U.S. Census Bureau has defined as at least 30 percent rural 58; whether Hidalgo, Starr or Willacy County qualifies is a lookup to record. McAllen is above the 50,000-person ceiling for USDA Business and Industry loans 18, while smaller Valley towns can be tested by address in USDA's OneRD tool 85. Opportunity Zone, HUBZone and EB-5 status were not found for any Valley site and must be looked up 878889.

El Paso: Fort Bliss, the border and a data center construction wave.

An El Paso study has to separate the temporary demand of thousands of construction workers from the permanent base the project will live on once they leave.

Economic base

El Paso's base is the Army, the border and public institutions, and the record for each is older than a lender would like. The latest ranked employer list in the research is HUD's 2011 market analysis: 23,500 military and 7,000 civilian workers at Fort Bliss, 5,600 at T&T Staff Management, 3,100 at Tenet Healthcare and 2,550 at the University of Texas at El Paso 96. The cross-border manufacturing and freight trade with Ciudad Juárez could not be measured at all: no current plant, crossing or trade figure was found, so an industrial, logistics or fuel study must pull them from the federal series.

The data center wave

Meta broke ground in far Northeast El Paso in October 2025 on a first phase worth more than $1.5 billion, with about 100 permanent jobs, more than 1,800 construction workers at peak and a site that could scale to one gigawatt 90; local reporting described a 1.2 million square foot building on 1,000 acres 92. On March 26, 2026 Meta raised its commitment to more than $10 billion, with more than 300 permanent jobs and more than 4,000 construction jobs at peak 90. In July 2026 it formed a venture in which funds managed by BlackRock own 80% and Meta 20%, partly funded by a $12.5 billion debt financing; more than 2,300 workers were already on site, and the venture expects capacity to come online from 2028 91.

The public commitments are contested. El Paso Electric's 366-megawatt McCloud gas plant, dedicated to Meta for its first five years, was awaiting state utility commission approval in January 2026 94. The city is reimbursing $12.5 million of road improvements around Stan Roberts Sr. Avenue, near the site 93, and El Paso Water's chief executive said in December 2025 that Meta would not rank among the utility's ten largest users 95. Across the state line in Santa Teresa, Doña Ana County authorized up to $165 billion of industrial revenue bonds for Project Jupiter in September 2025 98; in 2026 that campus was reported 26% complete with 3,686 construction personnel, 466 of them county residents 99, and New Mexico's Supreme Court rejected two challenges on September 17, 2026 100.

The lesson for a lender is the gap between peak and permanent. Two campuses full of crews push up demand for hotels, RV parks, apartments, restaurants and fuel; when construction ends, the permanent payroll is in the hundreds. A study should show revenue with and without construction demand, name the year the crews are expected to leave, and test coverage in the year after. Whether the Governor's August 3, 2026 pause on new ERCOT data-center connections reaches El Paso is not stated 101, so a power-intensive project should confirm its grid and interconnection date in writing.

Lubbock: Texas Tech, the farm economy and a market to be counted.

Growth in Lubbock is steady rather than fast, and the published record holds almost no supply data, so the study has to build its own census and prove share.

Economic base

Lubbock is the service center of the South Plains: a university town, a regional medical hub and the market town for a wide farm economy. HUD's 2022 market analysis, reporting 2018 data, ranks Texas Tech University first with 6,635 employees, with the City of Lubbock at 2,700 and Lubbock County at 1,355 104. The state is adding to the medical base: in June 2024 the Governor announced a 50-bed maximum-security psychiatric unit in Lubbock budgeted at $121 million, roughly $2.4 million a bed 106. Its opening date should be confirmed before a study counts its staff as demand.

Agriculture is the part of the base the research could not quantify; it holds no crop, acreage or farm income figure for the region. Retail, fuel, equipment and processing businesses in the surrounding towns rise and fall with harvests, so their studies should show a weak crop year as well as a strong one. The research holds no Census 2020 base for the metro, no verified payroll count and no reliable city population, so no growth rate beyond that one year is given here. Steady growth suits uses that serve every household, such as medical office, child care, grocery and everyday retail, provided the study shows the subject's own trade area adding residents.

Storage is where a Lubbock study has to do the most work. Corpus Christi: the port, the drought record and windstorm cover.

Port and military demand is real here; the study's harder task is to show the project can secure water and wind insurance at a cost its revenue can carry.

Economic base

Corpus Christi's base is military, municipal and maritime. HUD's 2024 market analysis ranks Naval Air Station Corpus Christi first with 5,000 workers, followed by the City of Corpus Christi at 4,100, the Corpus Christi Army Depot at 2,900 and Corpus Christi Medical Center at 2,000 109. Two of the four are defense installations, whose payroll moves with federal budgets rather than oil prices. The port is the engine: it moved 203.4 million tons through the ship channel in 2025, down 1.5% from 206.5 million in 2024 110. The Texas A&M Real Estate Research Center counted nearly $60 billion of private capital placed in petrochemical and industrial sites near the port from 2012 through 2023, with manufacturing jobs concentrated in San Patricio County ZIP codes and along the ship channel 111.

That capital built plants more than payroll, and it creates demand in bursts. Construction and turnaround crews fill hotels, RV parks and restaurants for months, then leave. A lodging, fuel or service study should tie its demand to named terminal and plant projects with their schedules, and show coverage in the year after each burst ends; tonnage alone is not a demand driver.

Water

No other Texas metro has a documented drought record like this one. The city held Stage 3 restrictions from December 2024 for 596 days, stepping down to Stage 2 on August 4, 2026, when combined reservoir storage stood at 36.9%, with Lake Corpus Christi at 67.8% and Choke Canyon at 24.9% 114. The city moved to Stage 1 on August 27, 2026 115, and by September 25 Lake Corpus Christi held 83.6%, against 15.7% a year earlier, with Mary Rhodes Pipeline repairs complete 116. The rains bought time: in late July the projected date of a Level 1 Water Emergency stood at September 2028, after projections of September 2026 in April and September 2027 in June 117. An April 2026 draft plan for that emergency proposed allocation cuts of 25% to 30% for every customer class, large industrial users included; it was not adopted 118.

The long-term supply is undecided. In May 2026 the Texas Tribune reported that the council had killed its own desalination plan after the price nearly doubled to more than $1.2 billion, and had opened talks with AXE H2O on a 150-million-gallon-a-day plant at about $1.3 billion, with water at $6.50 per 1,000 gallons 119. On September 1, 2026 the council voted 5-3 to reject the Inner Harbor plant again, despite a preliminary guaranteed maximum price of $978.8 million 120. No plant was confirmed as proceeding when the research closed. A car wash, hotel, laundromat, food processor or industrial user should therefore show operations under Stage 1, Stage 3 and the draft cuts, quote the stage rules for its use, and carry a will-serve letter.

Growth, supply and rules

Census estimates carried in FRED put the metro of Aransas, Nueces and San Patricio counties at 451,191 residents in 2025 112; no 2020 base, verified payroll count or reliable city figure was found. The coast is where growth shows: Rockport, in Aransas County, had 11,348 residents in 2025, up 12.7% from 10,068 in 2020, with a median household income of $72,174 in the 2020 to 2024 survey 113. Its RV parks, marinas and seasonal retail sit inside the windstorm catastrophe area and under the same drought rules. The research found no current supply reading for any property type in the metro; HUD's 2024 analysis is the starting source for apartments 109.

All three counties are first-tier coastal counties in TWIA's designated catastrophe area 83, so a pro forma should carry a written windstorm quote, a flood determination and the construction standard the policy requires. The Comptroller reported a 9% city hotel tax for fiscal 2017, with any county or venue tax on top not found 121; impact fees, rezonings and short-term rental rules were not found either.

Lenders and eligibility

Corpus Christi is served by the SBA's Lower Rio Grande Valley District from Harlingen, with a Corpus Christi virtual office, and the partial county list includes Aransas, Nueces and San Patricio 81; no Corpus Christi lender ranking was found. Car washes, hotels and marinas are special purpose property for the SBA, so a 504 loan to an established business buying or building one needs 15% equity, and a new business needs 20% 57; the note on special purpose property risk explains why. The city is above USDA's 50,000-person ceiling, while Rockport is below it, subject to the OneRD address test 8518. Opportunity Zone, HUBZone and EB-5 targeted employment status were not found for any site 878889.

What a lender should verify

  • Operations and coverage at Stage 1, Stage 3 and a 25% to 30% allocation cut 118, with the current Level 1 projection.
  • A written wind quote, a flood determination and the required construction standard in the budgets.
  • The named port or plant projects behind any crew demand, with their schedules.
  • The status of every desalination and supply project at the study date, the current hotel tax and TxDOT counts for the frontage 84.

Texas Questions

Texas feasibility study questions.

When does an SBA loan in Texas need a feasibility study?

Only when the lender asks for one. The regulation, 13 CFR 120.160(b), says SBA may require professional appraisals, a survey or a feasibility study, with no loan size or asset class attached 56. SOP 50 10 8.1, effective October 1, 2026, brought new equity, coverage and valuation rules but left that sentence alone 57. In Texas the request usually arrives with a special purpose property or a business without operating history, because the projections then carry the repayment case. A 7(a) start-up study should reach the coverage floor within two years; a 504 file should show the equity tier for a new business in a special purpose property 57.

When does a USDA loan in Texas require an independent feasibility study?

When a Business and Industry guarantee exceeds $1,000,000 and the borrower is a new business. 7 CFR 5001.306(a)(3)(i) then calls for a study by an independent qualified consultant acceptable to the Agency, with projections running at least two years beyond full capacity or stable operations 16; below that line the Agency can still ask 16. The site, not the borrower, must sit outside any city or town above 50,000 people 18, so the big cities fail and their edges and small towns pass. Texas booked $289.2 million of these guarantees in FY2025, 13.7% of the national total, so its files draw close review 13.

What is the difference between a market study and a feasibility study in Texas?

A market study answers one question: can the trade area absorb the project at the rent, rate or volume assumed? It ends at demand, supply, competitors and absorption. A feasibility study carries that answer into the loan: project cost, sources and uses, debt sized under the program's rules, and coverage in the test year. HUD's draft Section 232 chapter puts the market study inside the lender-ordered appraisal, mapping every competing facility in a primary market area expected to supply 60% to 80% of demand 6566; a MAP Guide multifamily market study expires 120 days after pre-application 6566. USDA's five components fold both into one document 16.

How should a 2026 Texas study handle the migration slowdown?

Start from the Vintage 2025 estimates and say so in the report. The year to July 2025 brought Texas 167,475 net international migrants, down from 319,569 2, and 67,299 net domestic migrants, less than a third of the 222,154 recorded in 2022 and fewer than North Carolina took in 3. Natural increase is now the larger engine: 390,203 births against 232,492 deaths 3. A reviewer will ask which vintage the model used, because a 2023 model extended forward overstates household formation. Where growth now rests on jobs rather than arrivals, name the employers: Texas added 159,400 jobs in the year to August 2026, the most of any state 5.

Does the 2026 data center pause affect a Texas project that is not a data center?

Directly, no: the August 3, 2026 order paused ERCOT interconnection for data center loads above 75 megawatts 50, and the September 21 order halted the air and water permits that data center infrastructure needs 51. Indirectly, yes, for anything needing a new feeder, a substation upgrade or a water allocation in the same territory: ERCOT held about 474 gigawatts of large-load requests in June 2026, against the 63 gigawatts queued at the end of 2024 4648. With the audit and the final large-load rule due in December 2026 4951, a file closing earlier needs a will-serve letter dated after the August order, or a closing condition that obtains one.

How should a coastal Texas study price windstorm insurance?

From a dated quote for the subject, because the residual markets have moved in both directions. TWIA held its 2026 rates flat after a 2025 analysis that called commercial rates 5% inadequate 39, then found them 4% adequate in June 2026 and recommended no change for 2027 4041. The FAIR Plan wrote its first commercial property owners' association policies in 2025 at a loss cost multiplier of 4.865 plus a $350 policy fee 43, and its dwelling extended coverage rose 21.6% from September 1, 2026 44. Whether TWIA applies at all depends on the county: Cameron, Nueces and Chambers are in its designated catastrophe area, Hidalgo is not 83.
