Texas · San Antonio

San Antonio Feasibility Studies

A feasibility study for a San Antonio project must show that the loan can live with slow absorption. The metro adds people steadily, 38,402 in the year to July 2025 1, but payrolls grew only 6,100 in the year to August 2026 3, and no major U.S. market had a higher estimated apartment vacancy than San Antonio's 16% in Q2 2026 17. The base is stable, built on military installations, hospitals and new auto plants, so the question is rarely whether demand exists. It is how long the subject takes to reach stabilized occupancy, and whether the loan survives that wait.

Economic Base

Military, medicine and a new generation of South Side plants.

San Antonio's demand base changes slowly, and for a lender that is its main virtue. Joint Base San Antonio, which spans Fort Sam Houston, Lackland and Randolph, is the metro's defining employer. The economic development page the research found gives a JBSA headcount but no date 4, so this page does not quote it; a study that leans on base personnel should obtain the installation's current economic impact figures and name their year. The same gap applies to H-E-B and USAA, whose local headcounts were not found in a primary source.

Health care is the second pillar, and its evidence is about supply and consolidation rather than headcount. Medical office vacancy reached 12.3% in Q2 2026, its third straight quarterly rise 9. In August 2026, Methodist Healthcare took over 18 urgent care centers in San Antonio as part of HCA's purchase of a Texas chain 10. Senior housing keeps adding stock on the northwest side: The Waterford at Shavano Park opened in 2024 with 84 units of assisted living and memory care, joining The Haven and The Laurels in Stone Oak, with 60 and 94 units, which date from 2003 11. Payers are pushing back on the costliest formats: an advocacy group, citing the San Antonio Express-News, reported that one freestanding ER operator accounted for 53% of the city employee plan's ER claims in 2025 and that the plan's average cost per visit rose from $886 to $1,846 39. For a medical office, clinic, freestanding ER or senior housing study, the demand is real but so are the competition and the payer risk, and the study has to show the subject's share of the market.

The upside is manufacturing on the South Side. Toyota announced a $3.6 billion expansion of its San Antonio plant with 2,000 jobs, 2,500,000 square feet of building area, a second assembly line for the Tacoma, a footprint doubled by 2030 and a $20 million Texas Enterprise Fund grant 6; its local workforce of more than 3,700 is expected to reach about 6,000 5. JCB broke ground in June 2024 on a $500 million, 1 million-square-foot plant, with production from 2026 and 1,500 jobs planned over five years 8. IEM's $200 million plant at Brooks was reported in September 2026 with up to 3,000 jobs 7. Those announcements support apartments, quick-service restaurants, fuel and childcare on the South Side, but only as hiring happens. A feasibility study consultant should model those jobs on the employers' own schedules and show the project still covers its debt if hiring lags.

Freight and power round out the picture. Love's opened a San Antonio travel stop on April 16, 2026 with 70 spaces for trucks 12, and Cushman & Wakefield's 2025 guide put San Antonio's data center construction cost at $9.3 million per MW, the lowest of 19 U.S. markets against an average of $11.7 million 13.

Growth

Steady population gains, the slowest payroll growth of the big four.

The San Antonio-New Braunfels MSA counted 2,813,140 residents on July 1, 2025, up 10.0% from a 2020 base of 2,558,389, and added 38,402 people in the year 1. The City of San Antonio reached 1,548,422 after a gain of 14,359, the third-largest numeric increase of any U.S. city 2. Unlike the suburban-led growth of Houston, a meaningful share of San Antonio's gain lands inside its own large city limits, where the city's code and utility rules apply.

Payrolls are the soft spot. Nonfarm employment rose from 1,185.6 thousand in August 2025 to 1,191.7 thousand in August 2026, a gain of 6,100 or 0.5% (preliminary, not seasonally adjusted) 3, the weakest of the four large Texas metros. Population is growing faster than jobs, which favors necessity retail, health care and housing over projects that depend on new business travel or office demand. It also means a demand model built on job growth will be modest unless the South Side hiring arrives on time.

Builders have already pulled back. HBWeekly's tracking universe counted just over 6,300 residential permits for 2025, down 18%, with Bexar County at 4,048, down 21% 14. CultureMap, citing Construction Coverage and Census data, reported 14,857 units for 2024 15; the two are different universes and should not be chained. Multifamily permits fell to 1,126 units in the 12 months to June 2026, against a peak of 13,248 16. That collapse in new starts is the precondition for the apartment recovery described below.

Corridors

I-35, I-10 and the loops: where a San Antonio study finds its traffic.

San Antonio's growth rings follow Loop 410 and Loop 1604, with I-35 running northeast toward New Braunfels and Austin, I-10 northwest toward Boerne and east toward Seguin, and US 281 north into Comal County. The South Side plant cluster around Toyota, JCB and Brooks is the newest employment node, and the research names it as the metro's main corridor story, without traffic counts. The I-35 and I-10 corridors between San Antonio and its satellite cities are where fuel, travel, storage and quick-service projects compete.

No San Antonio corridor has a TxDOT count in the research. The study should pull the station number, the year of the count and its position from TxDOT's AADT layer 36 and, where trucks matter, the vehicle classification counts on TxDOT's count maps 37. On the loops, the study must also show how a frontage-road site is reached: an exit ramp upstream, a turnaround, or a cross street. A loop-frontage parcel can sit beside heavy traffic and still be hard to enter.

Supply by Asset Class

An apartment reset in progress, steady hotels and crowded storage.

Apartments: highest vacancy, smallest pipeline

Apartments.com estimated San Antonio's vacancy at 16% in Q2 2026, the highest of the major U.S. multifamily markets 17. RealPage data put the annual rent change at minus 5.8% in the year to Q2 2026, the largest decline of any U.S. metro 18, and RealPage expects slight further declines over the next four quarters 19. The supply side has already reset: the 2026 pipeline is about 800 units, the slowest since 2011 20, against permits that peaked at 13,248 16.

That is a slower, steadier version of the correction under way in Austin: less dramatic swings, but a longer road back. A market study for a San Antonio apartment loan should assume today's rents, a lease-up long enough to absorb the competing vacant units, and no rent growth until the vacancy rate falls. The absorption evidence matters more than the pipeline, because almost nothing new is coming (see absorption forensics and multifamily feasibility).

Hotels: soft, but not volatile

A broker's reading of CoStar data, limited to economy through upper-midscale hotels, shows 59.3% occupancy, an $89.48 average daily rate and $53.06 RevPAR in the year ending January 2025, with 560 rooms under construction against an inventory of about 23,000 21. RevPAR then fell 3.7% in Q1 2025, 3.3% in Q2 and 3.4% in Q3 22. Those are steady, moderate declines, not the shocks seen in Austin or Houston. A study for a limited-service or extended-stay hotel should show which demand segment it serves (base visitors, medical travel, leisure on the River Walk or plant contractors) and test break-even against the current trend; our note on break-even occupancy and hotel page show how.

Storage, car washes and the rest

Storage for RVs and boats is oversupplied. Yardi Matrix reports that deliveries over the 36 months to September 2025 equaled 47.9% of San Antonio's inventory, the most of any market it tracks, against 15.0% nationally; six new facilities added about half again to supply, and rent growth was 0% 23. A Class A boat and RV storage facility opened in New Braunfels on March 7, 2024 24. A new RV and boat storage project must count those competitors and model rents flat. Garage condominiums add a third layer: one San Antonio project markets 65 units in 30-by-50 and 30-by-30 foot plans 40. Express car washes keep coming: BlueWave filed a 4,696-square-foot San Antonio location with an estimated cost of $1.25 million in 2026 25, one more reason an express car wash study needs a full competitor census. Industrial, retail, office, self-storage per capita and convenience-store counts were not found and must come from the study's own census (see self-storage and assisted living).

Local Rules

A parking band, an aquifer and county rules on the Hill Country edge.

Parking minimums and maximums

San Antonio regulates parking from both sides, which is unusual among large Texas cities. The Unified Development Code sets minimum and maximum ratios: for general retail, at least 1 space per 300 square feet and no more than 1 per 200; for a hotel, at least 0.8 per room plus 1 per 800 square feet of meeting and restaurant space, and no more than 1 per room plus 1 per 400; for apartments, at least 1.5 per unit in residential districts with a cap of 2, and 1 per unit in nonresidential districts with a cap of 1.9; for warehousing, between 1 per 5,000 and 1 per 350 square feet; for offices, between 1 per 300 and 1 per 140 square feet; for a car wash, at least 1 per 500 square feet including bays and tunnels, and at most 1 per 375 square feet; for assisted living, 0.3 per bed plus 1 per employee 26. The downtown district is exempt, the IDZ-1 infill district has no minimums, and IDZ-2 and IDZ-3 sites may cut parking by half 27. Contrast Dallas-Fort Worth, where Dallas and Fort Worth have largely dropped minimums. In San Antonio a site plan that exceeds the maximum needs relief, so a study's parking count must fall inside the band. The ratios come from a code mirror current to 2018 and should be checked against the city's official code.

Edwards Aquifer and Hill Country counties

Bexar, Comal and Medina counties fall under the state's Edwards Aquifer protection rules, and construction over the recharge or contributing zone waits on a plan the state has approved 28. In Comal County, every septic system in the recharge zone, and the system for any RV park, requires engineered planning 29; its updated subdivision regulations took effect on June 11, 2026 30. Farther out, Bandera County charges an infrastructure development plan fee of $250 plus $10 per space for rental communities such as RV parks, sets subdivision lots of at least 10 acres where a lot relies on a well and septic, sets finished floors at least 1 foot above base flood elevation and lies in a state-declared priority groundwater management area 31. Utility hookups are a real line item at the edge of the metro: Comal County's minutes of March 6, 2025 record $1,392 for a single RV-style electric hookup 41. Those rules set the site count and the cost of water, wastewater and power, which is often the binding constraint on a Hill Country project.

Hotel tax and what was not found

The Texas Municipal League's 2018 guide lists San Antonio's combined hotel tax at 16.75% 32; a study must confirm the current state, city, county and venue rates, and the statewide limits are set out on our Texas page. San Antonio's short-term rental ordinance, impact fees, water and sewer capacity charges and any recent moratorium are absent from the research. Each becomes a lender condition, met with the city's or utility's written figures.

Lenders

A different 7(a) roster from the rest of Texas.

A ranking site, GoSBA Loans, compiled calendar 2025 SBA data for San Antonio proper; it puts Huntington National Bank first by dollars with $12.4 million on 31 loans, Live Oak Banking second with $11.0 million on 9 and Newtek Bank third with $8.9 million on 23 33. Across the city, 7(a) lending totaled $167.2 million to 285 businesses 33. The leaders are national SBA platforms rather than the Texas regional banks that top the Houston list, which matters to a sponsor: national lenders bring program depth and standardized credit boxes, so the feasibility study has to fit their templates.

The SBA San Antonio District lent $742 million in fiscal 2025 (the reported total appears to stop after three quarters), third among the six Texas districts 34. Its territory runs to 55 counties, including Bexar, Comal, Travis, Williamson, Hays, Webb, Kerr and Tom Green 35, so the district total includes the Austin area and Laredo and should not be read as San Antonio lending. The research did not locate the SBA's own fiscal 2025 lender ranking for the district. A 7(a) start-up needs 10% equity and projected coverage of 1.15x under SOP 50 10 8.1, and the SBA 7(a) and 504 page sets out the other program rules. Senior housing often goes to HUD's Section 232 program or to bank and agency lenders (see conventional and institutional lending); an EB-5 offering here must carry a dated targeted employment area finding for its site.

Program Eligibility

Checking rural, Opportunity Zone and HUBZone status around San Antonio.

The research holds no rural, Opportunity Zone or HUBZone determination for any parcel around San Antonio. The tests are parcel-level and change with maps and census updates, so the study should run each one at the start.

  • USDA Rural Development. Business and Industry guarantees go only to sites outside cities and towns above 50,000 people 38. San Antonio, New Braunfels and the inner suburbs are out; small towns in Wilson, Atascosa, Medina and Bandera counties are where a B&I option is worth testing against USDA's eligibility map; our USDA Rural Development page explains the program.
  • Opportunity Zones. Look up the tract among the designated tracts in force at closing.
  • HUBZone. Run the address through the SBA's HUBZone map when the application is prepared.
Worked Cases

Worked cases in the San Antonio region.

Two worked cases sit in the San Antonio region, one on each side of the metro. The express car wash case in Seguin, Guadalupe County tests a tunnel wash on the I-10 and SH 46 corridor against a crowded local field, under SBA 504 with the special-purpose equity tier. The assisted living and memory care case in Boerne, Kendall County shows how a HUD Section 232 loan is sized for a new community northwest of San Antonio, where rates are high and competing stock keeps opening. Both test the claims a feasibility study company must support in this metro: real demand, real competition and a lease-up that sets the loan.

Due Diligence

San Antonio checks for the credit file.

  1. The lease-up period

    An absorption schedule built from recent comparable lease-ups, long enough for today's vacancy, with no early rent growth.

  2. Dated military figures

    A current, dated JBSA personnel or economic impact figure for any project that depends on the bases.

  3. Plant hiring on schedule

    Toyota, JCB or IEM jobs counted only as the employers report them hired, with a late-hiring case.

  4. Health care competition

    For medical office, urgent care and senior housing, the rising vacancy and the operators consolidating the market.

  5. Parking inside the band

    A site plan that meets the UDC minimum without exceeding the maximum, checked against the official code.

  6. Aquifer and water

    Edwards Aquifer plan status, septic or sewer approval, and groundwater limits in the Hill Country counties.

  7. Traffic and access

    A TxDOT station and year, and the exact route from the loop or interstate to the driveway.

  8. Current hotel tax

    Today's combined rate in place of the 2018 figure.

  9. Rural, OZ and HUBZone status

    Each program tested on the agency's map for the exact parcel.

San Antonio Questions

Questions lenders ask about San Antonio projects.

What does a feasibility study in San Antonio have to prove?

How long the lease-up runs, and that the loan outlasts it. Census counted 2,813,140 MSA residents in July 2025, 10.0% above the 2020 base 1, and the city added 14,359 people, the third-largest gain of any U.S. city 2, yet nonfarm employment grew only 0.5% in the year to August 2026 3. HBWeekly's 2025 permit count fell 18% to just over 6,300 14. Joint Base San Antonio is the anchor, but the only headcount the research found carries no date 4, so a base-dependent project needs the installation's own figure. A feasibility study company in San Antonio builds the absorption schedule from recent comparable lease-ups, not from the metro total.

Is San Antonio's apartment market past its worst?

Supply has turned; rents have not. Only 1,126 multifamily units were permitted in the year to June 2026, down from a 13,248 peak 16, and the 2026 delivery pipeline of about 800 units is the thinnest since 2011 20. Yet Apartments.com's Q2 2026 estimate of 16% vacancy was the highest among major U.S. markets 17, and RealPage recorded a 5.8% rent decline in the year to Q2 2026, the steepest of any U.S. metro 18, with a little more erosion forecast 19. The pro forma that survives review starts at current rents, stretches the lease-up to fit the vacant stock nearby, and leaves rent flat until the vacancy number moves.

Is RV and boat storage oversupplied in San Antonio?

Yes, by the widest margin of any market Yardi Matrix tracks. Deliveries in the three years to September 2025 came to 47.9% of the metro's inventory, more than three times the 15.0% national share, and rents did not grow at all 23. New Braunfels gained a Class A boat and RV facility on March 7, 2024 24. Car washes are crowding in too: BlueWave's 2026 filing for a 4,696-square-foot San Antonio wash carries an estimated cost of $1.25 million 25. A market study for San Antonio storage or a car wash lists each competitor by name and holds rents flat.

Do the Toyota, JCB and IEM plants change demand on San Antonio's South Side?

Over time, and only as the hiring lands. Toyota's $3.6 billion expansion adds 2,000 jobs, a second Tacoma line and 2,500,000 square feet, backed by a $20 million Texas Enterprise Fund grant 6; the local workforce of more than 3,700 is to grow toward about 6,000 5. JCB's $500 million plant, started in June 2024, plans 1,500 jobs over five years with production from 2026 8, and IEM's $200 million Brooks plant, reported in September 2026, could reach 3,000 jobs 7. A feasibility study consultant in San Antonio counts those workers on the employers' own hiring schedules and proves coverage if hiring runs a year behind.

What parking does San Antonio require?

A band with a floor and a ceiling. Under the Unified Development Code, general retail sits between 1 space per 300 and 1 per 200 square feet, a hotel between 0.8 and 1 space per room plus an allowance for meeting and restaurant space, and a car wash between 1 per 500 and 1 per 375 square feet with bays and tunnels counted 26. The downtown district is exempt, IDZ-1 infill zoning carries no minimum, and IDZ-2 and IDZ-3 allow a 50% reduction 27. A plan above the maximum needs relief, and the ratios, from a code mirror current to 2018, should be verified against the official code.

Who are the most active SBA lenders in San Antonio?

National SBA shops, not the Texas regional banks seen at the top in Houston. GoSBA Loans' compilation of calendar 2025 SBA data for the city ranks Huntington National Bank first by dollars ($12.4 million, 31 loans), then Live Oak Banking ($11.0 million, 9 loans) and Newtek Bank ($8.9 million, 23 loans), inside citywide 7(a) lending of $167.2 million to 285 businesses 33. The SBA's San Antonio District reported $742 million for fiscal 2025, a total that seems to stop at the third quarter 34, but its 55 counties take in Austin and Laredo 35, so the district figure is not a San Antonio figure.

How is the San Antonio hotel market performing?

Softly, without the swings seen in Austin or Houston. A broker's summary of CoStar data for the economy-to-upper-midscale segment put the year to January 2025 at 59.3% occupancy, an $89.48 rate and $53.06 RevPAR, and counted 560 rooms being built against roughly 23,000 open 21. The three quarters that followed each lost RevPAR: 3.7%, 3.3% and 3.4% in Q1, Q2 and Q3 of 2025 22. A hotel study here has to say which travelers fill the rooms, then test break-even against that drift. The only tax figure in hand, the Texas Municipal League's 2018 combined rate of 16.75% 32, is a prompt to confirm today's stack, not an input.

Underwriting a San Antonio project? Start with the absorption schedule.

We build the ramp from the subject's own competitive set and the hiring that is actually on payroll, then size the loan to survive the lease-up. Tell us where the site is and what you plan to build.
