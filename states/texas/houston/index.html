Texas · Houston

Houston Feasibility Studies

A feasibility study for a Houston project has to prove that the subject's own trade area shares in the metro's growth, because the metro averages hide wide splits. The metro added 126,720 residents in the year to July 2025, the most of any U.S. metro 1, and payrolls rose 44,700 in the year to August 2026 5. Yet hotels ran at 58.9% occupancy in 2025 16 and apartment vacancy read 11.1% to 11.9% on the two broadest counts in mid-2026 1011. In an unzoned city, the study must also show that drainage, floodplain and deed restrictions leave a buildable site.

Economic Base

Hospitals, freight and government carry the payroll.

Houston's image is oil and gas, but its largest payrolls belong to hospitals, a retailer's logistics network and local government. On Harris County's 2025 list, Memorial Hermann ranks first with 31,425 employees, Amazon third with 24,000, the City of Houston fourth with 22,170 and Houston Methodist fifth with 22,015 6. UT Health adds 16,150, putting three Texas Medical Center systems among the county's ten largest employers 6. The second name on that list is illegible in the copy the research extracted, so we leave it out rather than guess. The University of Houston reported 6,986 employees on the 2024 list 7.

For a lender, the hospital cluster is the steadiest demand source in the metro. It fills medical office suites, sends patients' families to hotels near the Medical Center and the suburban campuses, and supports workforce rentals within commuting range. The research records Memorial Hermann expansion in Cypress, Pearland and Lake Houston, but without budgets or opening dates 8. A study that leans on one of those campuses must document the project itself: permit, budget, opening date, beds and staff.

Energy and the port are the evidence gap. The research holds no current headcount for Port Houston, the Ship Channel industries or the energy sector, and no Houston project over $50 million with a confirmed 2025 or 2026 budget. Where a subject's demand depends on that economy (an east-side hotel that books plant crews, a truck service near the Ship Channel, a warehouse leased to an oilfield supplier), the study must source the segment directly, from BLS industry employment for the metro and Port Houston's own statistics, and then run the pro forma with that segment cut back. We do not estimate a number we cannot cite.

Growth

The fastest-growing U.S. metro by headcount, mostly outside city limits.

The Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands MSA added 126,720 residents between July 2024 and July 2025, the largest numeric gain of any U.S. metro 1. The prior Census vintage counted 7,796,182 residents on July 1, 2024, up 9.0% from a 2020 base of 7,150,233 2. The research did not retrieve the exact 2025 total; a secondary report puts it slightly above 7.9 million 4, and a study should replace that with the Census table before it sizes a trade area. The City of Houston reached 2,397,315 residents, a gain of 11,515 in the year 3. Most of the metro's growth therefore landed outside city limits, in unincorporated county land and suburban cities, where the approval path changes.

The job count is the stronger evidence. Nonfarm payrolls rose from 3,450.6 thousand in August 2025 to 3,495.3 thousand in August 2026, a gain of 44,700 or 1.3% (preliminary, not seasonally adjusted) 5, the only 12-month change among the large Texas metros that BLS marks as statistically significant. A lender can lean on that more than on a headline population count, because it is a payroll survey with a stated error test. It still says nothing about one submarket, so the study must connect the subject to the job centers its customers or tenants actually reach.

Permits point north. HBWeekly's tracking universe counted 35,520 residential permits for the Houston area in 2025, down 10%, with Harris County at about 16,000 and Montgomery County at about 10,720 9. Those are not Census Building Permits Survey counts, and the Census total for 2025 is not in the research, so the two should not be combined into a growth rate. What the split does show is that Montgomery County, home to The Woodlands, Conroe and Magnolia, takes a large share of new rooftops. Retail, childcare and storage projects that live on new households need permit and plat counts drawn from their own trade area, as our note on trade areas explains.

Corridors

I-10, US 290 and the Grand Parkway: where the exurbs meet the freeways.

Houston's suburban growth runs out along its radial freeways and the outer ring that joins them: I-10 west through Katy toward Brookshire, US 290 northwest through Cypress, I-45 north to The Woodlands and Conroe, and SH 99, the Grand Parkway, linking those exurbs on a wide loop. These are the corridors where travel centers, fuel and convenience stores, express car washes, storage and limited-service hotels compete for the same frontage, and where a feasibility study consultant in Houston spends most of the site work.

The research has no TxDOT AADT figure for a Houston corridor. A study must pull the station itself from TxDOT's AADT layer 52, use the ramp layer for an interchange site 53, and report the station ID, the count year and whether the station sits on the mainlanes or the frontage road. A frontage-road site captures only the traffic that exits, and on the tolled stretches of the Grand Parkway the mainlane count overstates what a corner can reach. Listing flyers that quote a count without a station or a year are not evidence.

Access approval comes before traffic. On a state frontage road, TxDOT's Houston District driveway application asks the applicant to show every driveway within 501 feet on either side and every signal within 1,000 feet 54. That spacing review can cut a frontage parcel to one driveway, so a study should model the site plan the district is likely to approve, not the one in the broker's flyer. See our truck stop and travel center and gas station and c-store pages for how access drives volume.

Supply by Asset Class

A shrinking apartment pipeline, a hotel trough and thin data elsewhere.

Apartments: fewer cranes, rents still soft

The Greater Houston Partnership reports 88.1% occupancy in Q2 2026, which is 11.9% vacancy, with average asking rent of $1,368 against $1,380 a year earlier, 13,066 units under construction and 4,719 delivered in the quarter 10. Cushman & Wakefield's stabilized series reads 11.1% vacancy, $1,312 effective rent and 11,756 units under construction, 36.4% fewer than a year earlier 11. Northmarq publishes 7.1% for a different property universe 12. Houston delivered 25,000 apartments in 2024 13, and RealPage forecasts slight rent declines over the four quarters from Q2 2026 14. Build-to-rent adds about 3,000 units under construction metro-wide, 1,200 of them in Spring and Tomball 15.

The read for a lender: a project that delivers after today's pipeline clears should face fewer competing lease-ups than 2025 projects did, but there is no pricing power yet. That is a different problem from Dallas-Fort Worth, where the open question is how much is still being built rather than where rents go. A market study for a Houston apartment project should start from effective rent in its own submarket (see effective rent versus asking rent) and state which vacancy universe it uses. Our multifamily feasibility page sets out the absorption test.

Hotels: 2025 was the trough

Full-year 2025 occupancy fell 8.6% to 58.9%, the steepest decline among the top 25 U.S. markets 16. December 2025 ran at 50% occupancy, a $108 average daily rate and $54 RevPAR 17. By March 2026 RevPAR had climbed to $98.93, up 11.9%, at 69.9% occupancy 18. One strong month after a weak year is not a stabilized year. A hotel study should build from trailing twelve-month data for the subject's competitive set (see reading the STR report) and test break-even occupancy against the 2025 numbers (see the break-even occupancy trap). Hotel pipeline and opening data were not found. Our hotel feasibility page covers the method.

Industrial, retail, medical, senior housing and storage

CBRE names Houston as one of three U.S. leaders for industrial absorption over January to June 2026 20, and Cushman & Wakefield puts the industrial pipeline at 23.0 million square feet 19; industrial vacancy itself was not found. Retail is tighter but softening: Cushman & Wakefield reports 5.8% vacancy with 161 projects and 3.8 million square feet under construction 21, while Matthews reports 5.5% vacancy and net absorption of minus 478,000 square feet in the same quarter 22. Medical office vacancy was 11.7% in H1 2026 23. The metro had 275 urgent care centers 24, and HCA's CareNow took over 8 more in August 2026 25, which matters to any independent clinic study. Senior housing sold for $157,518 per unit over 1Q to 3Q 2025 26, and occupancy of 84.7% in 1Q 2025 was among the lowest of the major markets 27, so an assisted living study must show demand the existing stock is not already absorbing. Data center vacancy was 19.7% in H1 2025, with 15 MW of new capacity scheduled 28.

RV and boat storage is the one storage class with metro data. Yardi Matrix counts 178 properties on 1,252.52 acres, 13 new properties in three years and advertised rent of $5.60 per square foot 29. A Katy and Richmond portfolio of 705 units was 94% occupied when it sold in early 2026 30, and a single Conroe facility registered 319,181 square feet of storage buildings with an estimated cost of $1,470,000 31. A new RV and boat storage project near Lake Conroe or the west side must count those competitors by name. Self-storage per capita, office and convenience-store counts were not found; a study needs its own competitor census, as on our self-storage page.

Local Rules

No zoning, but a full engineering rulebook.

The city and its ETJ

Statewide rules are on our Texas page; this section covers what is particular to Houston and its counties. The City of Houston has no zoning. Its Planning Commission must approve plats that meet Chapter 42 and cannot disapprove one because of objections to the use 32. In the extraterritorial jurisdiction the city reviews plats only; development there is outside the city's permitting and building inspection 33, and Harris County issues site, building, driveway and fire-code permits. Use is therefore rarely the approval risk. Private deed restrictions are, and a study should confirm them from the title commitment before it assumes a use is available.

What remains is engineering that decides how much of a parcel can be built. Chapter 42 sets a 25-foot building line along major thoroughfares 34. A preliminary plat can take up to 30 business days per submittal 35. Harris County takes its detention rate from a design manual the research could not date, so the study should get the current rate from the county engineer. The county fire code, effective January 1, 2025, was amended again from October 1, 2025 37, and Montgomery County moved to the 2024 International Fire Code on May 14, 2025 36. On a small frontage pad, those rules set the footprint and the slab elevation, so the site budget should come from a civil engineer's layout, not a per-acre allowance.

The suburban counties

Waller County on the I-10 corridor has no zoning and controls projects through its development regulations: a policy of zero net increase in stormwater runoff with a 0.55 acre-feet per acre detention minimum, a design rainfall of 16.00 inches for the Katy area, and buildings in the 100-year floodplain raised at least 2 feet above the 500-year elevation 38. Montgomery County's drainage manual, adopted August 26, 2025, also requires zero increase in peak flow 39, and the county asks for buildings within 100 feet of a flood zone to sit at least 2 feet above base flood elevation 40. Conroe has no zoning ordinance 41. In Magnolia, the city paused building permits in December 2022 over water-well capacity 47; no 2025 or 2026 moratorium was found, but the episode is why a study needs a written capacity letter from the city or municipal utility district that serves the site.

Parking, fuel stations, hotels and short-term rentals

Unlike Austin, Houston still sets parking minimums. Sec. 26-492, in its 2014 printing of the 2013 ordinance, requires 4.0 spaces per 1,000 square feet of general retail, 10.0 per 1,000 for a sit-down restaurant, 1.0 per room for a hotel up to 250 rooms and 1.333 per one-bedroom apartment 42. Later amendments were not verified, so the table must be confirmed with the city before a site plan is fixed. A 2026 council request sought to stop new gas stations near homes for 90 days 43; a fuel study should check whether anything was adopted. The Texas Comptroller uses Houston as its example of a 17% combined hotel tax 45, a rate the Texas Municipal League's 2018 guide places among the highest of any major U.S. metro 44. Houston's short-term rental ordinance, No. 2025-322, took effect January 1, 2026 46; its terms were not captured, so an STR-dependent project must read it directly.

Taxes, fees and capacity

The only Houston property tax figure in the research is a 2.337% effective rate on a $1 million commercial property, from 2010-era data 48, which is too old to underwrite. The study must pull current rates for every taxing unit on the parcel, including any municipal utility district. Impact fees and water and sewer capacity were not found for any Houston-area city.

Lenders

Who makes SBA loans in Houston.

The research ranks Houston city SBA 7(a) lenders for calendar 2025 from SBA data compiled by GoSBA Loans, a ranking site, not the SBA 49. Texas Capital Bank led by dollars with $20.0 million on 12 loans; Frost Bank followed with $16.0 million on 13 and Comerica with $16.0 million on 21. Zions led by count with 59 loans. Across the city, 7(a) lending totaled $388.1 million to 613 businesses 49. The pattern matters to a sponsor: the dollar leaders wrote fewer, larger loans and the count leader many small ones, so the right bank depends on loan size and appetite for the asset class.

The SBA Houston District lent $983 million in fiscal 2025, a total that probably covers only three quarters, and ranked second among the six Texas districts 50. Its 32 counties stretch from Harris, Fort Bend and Montgomery to Galveston, Brazoria, Jefferson and Brazos 51, so it reaches Beaumont and Bryan-College Station. The official fiscal 2025 district ranking was not found, and the research names no certified development company active in Houston; a 504 sponsor should get the CDC's own production record. Under SOP 50 10 8.1, a start-up 7(a) borrower brings 10% equity and the projected coverage must reach 1.15x; the SBA 7(a) and 504 practice page covers what a lender asks a study to prove. Larger apartment and hotel loans go to banks, debt funds and agencies (see conventional and institutional lending), and an EB-5 raise needs its own targeted employment area finding for the site.

Eligibility

Rural, Opportunity Zone and HUBZone tests: unverified until mapped.

No Houston parcel has a USDA, Opportunity Zone or HUBZone finding in the research. Each is a parcel test, so the study should run it on the agency's map before the program is chosen, not after.

  • USDA Rural Development. For a Business and Industry guarantee, the site must not be in a city or town above 50,000 residents 55. Houston, Pasadena, Sugar Land and the large suburbs fail. Unincorporated land and small towns at the metro's edge, such as the Chambers County exit used in our travel center case, are where B&I is in play. A B&I guarantee above $1,000,000 to a new business requires a feasibility study by an independent consultant the agency accepts 56. See USDA Rural Development and the five USDA feasibility components.
  • Opportunity Zones. Match the census tract to the designated-tract list in force at closing.
  • HUBZone. Map the parcel in the SBA's HUBZone tool when the loan is submitted.
Worked Cases

A worked case on Houston's I-10 east edge.

Our worked case for the Houston region sits east of the metro, at an I-10 exit in Hankamer, Chambers County. The Hankamer travel center case shows how a fuel, food and truck-parking program is sized from interstate through-traffic when TxDOT has not published a station count at the exit, how an unzoned county approves the use by permit rather than hearing, and how a USDA Business and Industry guarantee shapes the equity and the scope of the feasibility study. It is the template a feasibility study company in Houston should follow for any I-10 or US 290 corridor site where the market is the highway, not the town.

Before Closing

Houston items a lender should confirm.

  1. The vacancy universe

    Which apartment series the study uses (partnership, broker stabilized or a narrower set) and why it fits the subject's class and submarket.

  2. Hotel performance on a full year

    Trailing twelve-month data for the competitive set, with break-even tested against 2025, not against March 2026.

  3. Energy and port exposure

    Where demand depends on plant, port or oilfield activity, sourced sector employment and a downside case with that demand reduced.

  4. Deed restrictions

    A title review confirming the use is allowed, since zoning will not answer the question.

  5. Drainage and floodplain

    The FEMA panel, the county floodplain rule that applies, detention volume and the finished-floor elevation priced into the site budget.

  6. Utility capacity

    A will-serve letter from the city or municipal utility district, and a check for any permit pause.

  7. Counts and driveways

    The TxDOT station number, its year and where it sits, plus the driveway layout the district is likely to permit.

  8. Current local rules

    The parking table, any gas station limits and the short-term rental ordinance as they stand when the loan closes.

  9. Eligibility findings

    Rural, Opportunity Zone and HUBZone results for the exact parcel, taken from the agencies' own maps.

Houston Questions

Questions lenders ask about Houston projects.

What does a feasibility study in Houston, Texas have to prove?

That the trade area, not the metro, carries the demand. The City of Houston grew by 11,515 residents to 2,397,315 in the year to July 2025 3, a small slice of the metro's gain, and HBWeekly placed about 10,720 of the area's 35,520 residential permits in Montgomery County alone 9. Growth that uneven is why a feasibility study company in Houston starts from the drive time, not the MSA. A credit officer will expect the study to tie the project to the employers, permits and plats within reach of the site, name the vacancy series it relied on, and show coverage with that demand reduced.

Does Houston's lack of zoning make a feasibility study easier?

It removes most use-approval risk and none of the site risk. The Planning Commission cannot turn down a plat because neighbors object to the use 32, but Chapter 42 still imposes a 25-foot building line on major thoroughfares 34, a preliminary plat review may run 30 business days per submittal 35, and county drainage rules, such as Waller County's 0.55 acre-feet of detention per acre 38, decide how much of the parcel the pond takes. Private deed restrictions can bar a use outright, and zoning will never answer that question. The study prices those constraints and reads the restrictions off the title commitment.

Which apartment vacancy figure should a Houston market study use?

Name the universe and pick the one that fits the subject. Three series disagree for Q2 2026: the Greater Houston Partnership's 88.1% occupancy implies 11.9% vacancy 10, Cushman & Wakefield's stabilized series read 11.1% 11, and Northmarq reported 7.1% for a narrower property set 12. A lease-up study should test absorption against the broadest count, since a new building competes with everything still leasing, and show the narrower series beside it. Rent is the second check: asking rent slipped to $1,368 from $1,380 a year earlier 10, so the pro forma should carry no rent growth until the 13,066 units under construction have cleared 10.

Has the Houston hotel market recovered from 2025?

It is rebounding, not recovered. Calendar 2025 occupancy came in at 58.9%, down 8.6%, the worst slide of any top-25 U.S. market 16, and December 2025 managed 50% occupancy, $108 in average rate and $54 of RevPAR 17. RevPAR had recovered to $98.93 by March 2026, 11.9% higher, with occupancy at 69.9% 18. One strong spring month does not make a stabilized year. A hotel study should underwrite on trailing twelve-month figures for the subject's competitive set, test break-even occupancy against the 2025 trough, and say plainly that the research found no pipeline or opening data for new Houston rooms.

Which banks make the most SBA 7(a) loans in Houston?

By dollar volume in calendar 2025, Texas Capital Bank, with $20.0 million across 12 loans, then Frost Bank ($16.0 million, 13 loans) and Comerica ($16.0 million, 21 loans); Zions wrote the most loans, 59 49. The ranking is GoSBA Loans' compilation of SBA data for the city, where 7(a) lending reached $388.1 million to 613 businesses 49, not an SBA publication; the SBA's own fiscal 2025 table was not located. A few large-ticket lenders and one high-count lender tell a sponsor to match the bank to the loan size. The Houston District's 32 counties run from Harris to Brazos and Jefferson 51, so district totals reach past the metro.

What should a feasibility study consultant in Houston check on a frontage-road site?

Three things before any sales forecast. The count: the research holds no TxDOT figure for any Houston corridor, so the study must identify the TxDOT station 52 or ramp station 53 nearest the site and say what year it was counted and which lanes it measured. The capture: a frontage parcel sees only the traffic that exits, and where the Grand Parkway is tolled, a mainlane count overstates what a corner lot can draw. The access: the Houston District's driveway permit form 54 wants every existing driveway within 501 feet and every signal within 1,000 feet drawn on the application, and that spacing review can leave a parcel one entrance.

What hotel occupancy tax does a Houston hotel collect?

17% inside the city, by the Texas Comptroller's worked example: 6% state, 7% city and two 2% venue levies 45. The Texas Municipal League's 2018 guide lists the same 17 percent and ranks it among the highest combined big-city rates in the country 44. Room revenue belongs net of that stack, and a site outside the city limits needs its own confirmation, because the city and venue pieces change at the boundary. The short-term rental ordinance, No. 2025-322, has applied in Houston since January 1, 2026 46; the research did not capture its terms, which an STR plan has to read before the pro forma is final.
