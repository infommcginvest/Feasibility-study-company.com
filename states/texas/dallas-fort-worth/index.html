Texas · Dallas-Fort Worth

Dallas-Fort Worth Feasibility Studies

A feasibility study for a Dallas-Fort Worth project has to prove that it can lease up or ramp against the deepest new supply in Texas, not just that the metro is growing. The MSA added 123,557 residents in the year to July 2025 1, yet about 30,200 apartment units were still under construction in Q2 2026 15 and no U.S. market had more hotel projects in its construction pipeline than Dallas 24. The study has to place the subject inside that pipeline, submarket by submarket, and show who absorbs it.

Economic Base

Airlines, health systems, freight and a long list of headquarters.

Dallas-Fort Worth has no single industry that can sink it, and that is the first thing a lender should credit. The Dallas Regional Chamber places American Airlines Group, Texas Health Resources and Walmart in its band of employers with 10,000 or more workers, but it publishes bands rather than ranked headcounts 7. Tarrant County's top-ten list, built from 2023 data of the North Central Texas Council of Governments, names DFW Airport at 14,000 employees, GM's Arlington Assembly plant at 10,512, Naval Air Station Joint Reserve Base Fort Worth at 10,500, BNSF at 4,900 and UT Arlington at 4,383 8. Those counts are three years old, so a study that relies on one of them should ask the employer for a current figure.

The metro is often sold to lenders as a corporate relocation story, and the population and payroll counts below are consistent with that. The research, however, holds no count of relocations, no list of new headquarters and no office absorption figure for 2025 or 2026. A study that leans on a specific move, such as a campus expected to fill a hotel's weekday rooms or to anchor a retail pad, has to document it: the company's announcement, the building permit, any incentive agreement with its job targets and clawbacks, and the move-in date. The office side of the story is also mixed. Dallas had 3,966 apartment units in its office-to-apartment conversion pipeline at the start of 2026 20, which tells a lender that some older office stock is being retired rather than leased (see what office repurposing means for a study).

Freight is the second pillar, and it is better documented. CBRE reports that Dallas-Fort Worth led U.S. industrial absorption in the first half of 2026 together with Houston and Phoenix 12. Cushman & Wakefield puts the Alliance submarket in north Fort Worth first, with 3.9 million square feet absorbed year to date in Q2 2026, ahead of the East Dallas suburbs at 2.4 million, South Dallas at 2.0 million and South Fort Worth at 1.8 million 9. On October 14, 2026 the U.S. Department of Transportation awarded the City of Fort Worth a $5 million INFRA grant for about 100 new truck parking spaces at a Pilot travel center on I-35 13. For a lender, logistics growth pays twice: warehouse jobs, and the truck traffic that travel centers, fuel, truck parking and quick-service restaurants live on.

Health care rounds out the base. UT Southwestern's Texas Behavioral Health Center in Dallas adds 292 beds in 505,000 square feet, with phased opening from summer 2026 14. A feasibility study consultant working near the Medical District should treat that campus as a documented anchor and rumored projects as unconfirmed until they carry a permit.

Growth

The largest metro in Texas, still adding a city's worth of people a year.

The Dallas-Fort Worth-Arlington MSA counted 8,477,157 residents on July 1, 2025, up 11.0% from its 2020 base of 7,638,294 1. It added 123,557 people in the year to July 2025, the second-largest numeric gain of any U.S. metro, just behind Houston 1. Dallas reached 1,329,491 residents and Fort Worth 1,028,117, after a gain of 19,512 that made it the tenth-largest city in the country 2. Unlike Houston, where the central city took only a small share of the metro's gain, Dallas-Fort Worth's growth is spread across dozens of incorporated suburbs, each with its own zoning code, fee schedule and council.

Payrolls grew more slowly. Nonfarm employment rose from 4,313.9 thousand in August 2025 to 4,357.0 thousand in August 2026, a gain of 43,100 or 1.0% (preliminary, not seasonally adjusted) 3. A study that models demand growth above that trend should say why.

Housing permits show where the rooftops are going. The Census series for the MSA ran at a seasonally adjusted rate of 5,104 units in December 2025 4; the full-year 2025 Census total is not in the research. HBWeekly's narrower tracking universe counted about 25,260 permits for 2025, down 5%, led by Collin County at 8,634, Tarrant County at 6,579 (up 65%) and Ellis County at 2,330 5. For calendar 2024, CultureMap, citing Construction Coverage and Census data, ranked the metro first in the nation with 71,788 units 6. These are three different measures and should not be chained into a growth rate. Read together, they say that Collin County's northern suburbs are still the volume leader, that Tarrant County's permit count jumped, and that Ellis County to the south is now a real growth market. Retail, childcare, storage and service projects in those counties should be sized against permits and plats in their own trade area, not against the metro average (see why the trade area is the study).

Corridors

Corridors, freight nodes and the counts a study must find.

Growth in Dallas-Fort Worth follows its radial highways and tollways: US 75 and the Dallas North Tollway north into Collin County, I-35W and SH 114 toward Alliance, I-20 east into Kaufman County and west into Parker and Hood counties, and I-45 and US 287 south into Ellis County. The research does not tie a single traffic count to any of them. The industrial absorption leaders named above are the best available guide to where freight concentrates.

No TxDOT annual average daily traffic count for any Dallas-Fort Worth corridor appears in the research, and a market study must not fill that gap from a listing. Broker flyers on I-20 near Terrell and Hudson Oaks quote counts that either carry no station or cite TxDOT without naming one. The study should pull the station ID, count year and location from TxDOT's AADT layer (data updated January 6, 2025) 48, and use TxDOT's count maps for vehicle classification where a truck share drives revenue 49.

A travel center or truck parking project on I-35W, I-20 or I-45 should count competing spaces within a defined drive time, including the new grant-funded spaces, before it assumes overnight demand. Our truck stop and travel center page sets out that census.

Supply by Asset Class

The deepest pipeline in Texas, class by class.

Apartments: strong absorption, and still more deliveries

Dallas-Fort Worth absorbs apartments at a pace few metros match, and it still has the most under construction. Cushman & Wakefield counted about 10,800 units absorbed in Q2 2026 alone, with stabilized vacancy down 30 basis points in the quarter to 9.6% and about 30,200 units under construction, 21.3% fewer than a year earlier 15. Grapevine was the tightest submarket at 5.5% 15. Apartments.com's all-property projection for the quarter was 12.6% 16, because it counts buildings still leasing up. Dallas delivered 35,000 apartments in 2024 17, more than 4,000 market-rate units in Q2 2026 19, and RealPage expects nearly 19,000 more over the next four quarters, with Fort Worth rent growth forecast below 1% 18. Build-to-rent adds about 3,700 units under construction in Dallas 21.

What a lender should take from this: absorption is real, but a new building's lease-up competes with every other building opening in its submarket over the same months. A market study for a Dallas-Fort Worth apartment project should name those buildings, model concessions and effective rent rather than asking rent (see effective rent versus asking rent), and show the absorption pace of comparable recent lease-ups (see absorption forensics). Cost is the other side of the test: Rider Levett Bucknall put Dallas multifamily hard costs at $190 to $280 per square foot in Q2 2026 22 and Dallas construction cost escalation at 3.52% for the year to Q1 2026 23. Our multifamily feasibility page covers the method.

Industrial: balanced, with a large pipeline

Industrial vacancy sat between 8.1% and 9.3% in Q2 2026 depending on the broker: 8.1% at Cushman & Wakefield 9, 8.8% at Kidder Mathews 11 and 9.3% at JLL, which also counted 31.2 million square feet under development 10. That range is balanced by Texas standards, and it contrasts sharply with Austin, where industrial vacancy runs at roughly twice its long-term norm. The risk here is not vacancy today but the speculative space still to deliver, so a study for an owner-user or small-bay project should identify the competing buildings within its submarket.

Hotels: the biggest pipeline in the country

Dallas carried the largest hotel construction pipeline of any U.S. market in Q2 2026, at 183 projects in Lodging Econometrics data 24. RevPAR slipped 1.5% in Dallas and 0.8% in Fort Worth and Arlington over January to September 2025 25. Short-term rentals add a second layer of supply: Dallas listings grew 34.4% in the year to March 2025 26. A limited-service hotel study here has to list every pipeline project within the competitive set, test break-even occupancy against the new rooms (see the break-even occupancy trap), and treat 2026 World Cup demand as a one-time event, not a stabilized year. See our hotel feasibility page.

Medical, senior housing, storage and retail

Medical office vacancy was 11.4% in Q1 2026, after a peak near 12.0% in late 2025 28, and Dallas had 429,000 square feet of medical outpatient space under construction 29. The metro had 316 urgent care centers, third among U.S. metros 30. Senior housing penetration in Dallas was 13.3% in 2023 31, and transactions averaged $187,375 per unit in the first three quarters of 2025 32; a new assisted living study must show unmet demand after that existing penetration (see assisted living and memory care). RV and boat storage is crowded: Yardi Matrix counts 130 properties on 1,488.91 acres, the largest inventory by acreage in its national sample, with supply up 28.3% over 36 months, 20 new properties in three years, rent growth of 1.9% and advertised rent of $5.69 per square foot 33. Retail recorded one of the largest quarterly vacancy increases in the country in Q2 2026 34; Class A neighborhood centers traded at cap rates of 5.5% to 6.25% in H2 2025 35, and shopping center hard costs ran $210 to $345 per square foot 22. Self-storage supply per capita was not found and must come from a site-level census (see self-storage).

Local Rules

Dozens of cities, dozens of rulebooks.

Dallas-Fort Worth is a patchwork of incorporated suburbs, and the rule that decides a project is usually one suburb's zoning code. Statewide rules are summarized on our Texas page.

Parking reform in the two central cities

On May 14, 2025, Dallas removed parking minimums downtown, within half a mile of light rail and streetcar stations, on historically designated property, for offices and most retail, and for industrial uses unless they sit next to single-family zoning 36. Apartment parking moved from per-bedroom ratios to tiers: none under 20 units, 0.5 spaces per unit for mid-size projects and 1 per unit above 200 units 36. Fort Worth now applies minimums only to uses on residential property or within 250 feet of one- or two-family zoning, and caps parking at 125% of the minimum 37. That is the opposite of San Antonio, which still sets both minimums and maximums for most uses. A lower legal minimum does not lower the parking a tenant, franchisor or lender expects, so the site plan should still show enough spaces for the business plan.

Discretionary approvals in the suburbs

Car washes show how much a suburban council can decide. In Mansfield a car wash needs a Specific Use Permit; the City Council denied one for a 2.6-acre tunnel site on April 13, 2026 38, and the Planning and Zoning Commission had recommended denial 5-0 on another site in November 2024 39. Of the four Mansfield car wash cases the research reviewed from 2024 to 2026, none reached adoption 3839. The application fee is $2,500 plus $100 per acre, capped at $7,500 40. Fort Worth requires drying and vacuum equipment to sit at least 25 feet from residential districts and a conditional use permit within 200 feet 41. A study for an express car wash, fuel station or auto use in these suburbs should report the approval path and the hearing record, and the lender should not fund on a site that has not cleared it.

County rules at the edge

Outside city limits, counties regulate by subdivision and infrastructure standards. Parker County requires RV parks to sit on at least 5.0 acres, caps density at 60 spaces per 5 acres and requires at least 12 feet between units 42, which sets the site count an RV park study can model. In Hood County, eight data-center proposals covering more than 7,600 acres had been filed by June 2026, raising local fights over water and power 43. A data-center project there also faces the statewide pause on new large data-center interconnections that began in August 2026, covered on our Texas page.

Hotel tax, short-term rentals and fees

The Texas Comptroller lists Dallas County's venue tax at 3%, above the usual 2% venue limit 44. The research found no current, primary-source combined hotel tax rate for Dallas or Fort Worth; the only figure it located came from a 2012 council exhibit prepared by another city, which we do not use. A hotel study must confirm the state, city, county and venue rates that apply to the parcel. Short-term rental ordinances, impact fee schedules, water and sewer capacity and any 2025 or 2026 moratoria were not found for Dallas-Fort Worth cities, so each becomes a condition: a written fee estimate and a will-serve letter from the utility.

Lenders

The biggest SBA district in Texas, with a ranking gap.

The SBA's Dallas/Fort Worth District recorded $1.5 billion of lending in fiscal 2025, likely through the third quarter, the largest of the six Texas districts 46. Run from an office in Euless, it spans 72 counties, among them Dallas, Tarrant, Collin and Denton but also Bell, McLennan, Smith and Wichita 47, so its totals reach well past the metro to Waco, Tyler and Wichita Falls. The research did not find the district's official fiscal 2025 ranking of lenders. The latest district release it found is from fiscal 2017, when Wells Fargo led 7(a) lending with $102 million and Greater Texas Capital led certified development company lending with $55 million 45. That is nine years old; it shows who has historically been active, not who is lending today. Among CDCs, the SBA's current list includes Alliance Lending Corp, based in Fort Worth 51.

For a 504 project, the CDC's appetite for special-purpose property matters as much as its size, and the SBA's equity tier rises to 15% when the borrower is a start-up or the building is special purpose, under SOP 50 10 8.1. Our SBA 7(a) and 504 practice explains what an SBA lender looks for in the study. The governing rule is 13 CFR 120.160(b): SBA may require professional appraisals, a survey, or a feasibility study, so whether a study is needed is a credit decision (see the SBA feasibility myth). Larger apartment, hotel and industrial projects go to banks, debt funds and agencies on conventional terms, where cap rates such as the 5.5% to 6.25% range for Class A neighborhood centers set the exit test (see conventional and institutional lending). EB-5 capital needs a targeted employment area finding for the specific site, which the research does not hold for any part of the metro (see EB-5).

Program Eligibility

USDA, Opportunity Zone and HUBZone status: check the parcel.

The research made no USDA, Opportunity Zone or HUBZone determination for any Dallas-Fort Worth location, so none can be assumed, and each must be checked parcel by parcel before the program is chosen.

  • USDA Rural Development. B&I money is limited to sites beyond the limits of any city or town over 50,000 residents 50. Dallas, Fort Worth and the large suburbs fail that test. The smaller county seats and unincorporated land on the edge of the metro, in Kaufman, Parker, Hood, Ellis and Johnson counties, are where eligibility should be tested on USDA's map. See USDA Rural Development.
  • Opportunity Zones. Confirm the census tract against the current designated list; the research holds no Dallas-Fort Worth tract list.
  • HUBZone. Check the parcel on the SBA's HUBZone map at the time of application, because designations change with map updates.
Worked Cases

Worked cases on the Dallas-Fort Worth edge.

Four of our worked cases sit in the Dallas-Fort Worth region, three of them on the I-20 corridor where the metro meets its exurbs. The truck stop case in Terrell, Kaufman County shows how a diesel and truck-parking program at an I-20 exit east of Dallas is sized and financed under SBA 504 when the only traffic figures near the site are broker claims. The RV and boat storage case in Granbury, Hood County tests lake-driven storage demand against the crowded regional storage supply described above, under SBA 504. The franchised dealership case in Weatherford, Parker County shows how a dealership's real estate and franchise relationship are underwritten under SBA 504 on the western growth edge. The multifamily case on the outer ring of a major Texas metro works through absorption, effective rent and the agency takeout coverage test, the three numbers that decide an apartment loan in this pipeline. Together they show what a feasibility study company has to prove where the metro's growth meets open land.

Lender Checklist

What a lender should verify in Dallas-Fort Worth.

  1. The pipeline inside the competitive set

    A list of every apartment, hotel or storage project under construction or permitted within the subject's submarket, with unit or room counts and opening dates, not a metro total.

  2. The vacancy universe

    Whether the study uses the stabilized apartment series or the all-property series, and why that choice fits a building still in lease-up.

  3. Relocation and anchor evidence

    Permits, incentive agreements and move-in dates for any employer the demand case relies on; a press release is not enough.

  4. The use approval

    For car washes, fuel, auto and drive-through uses, the zoning district, the SUP or CUP path, distance rules from residential zoning, and the hearing record. Fund only on an approved use.

  5. Parking under the new rules

    The current parking table for the permitting city, and the parking the operator or franchisor actually requires.

  6. Event revenue

    World Cup or other one-time demand shown separately and excluded from the stabilized year.

  7. Traffic and access

    TxDOT station ID, count year and position relative to the site's frontage road and ramps.

  8. Utilities, fees and power

    A will-serve letter, an impact fee estimate and, for industrial or data-heavy users, confirmation of power availability under the state's interconnection pause.

  9. Program eligibility

    USDA, Opportunity Zone and HUBZone status checked on the agency maps for the exact parcel.

Dallas-Fort Worth Questions

Questions lenders ask about Dallas-Fort Worth projects.

What must a feasibility study in Dallas-Fort Worth prove to a lender?

Two things the metro totals cannot show: that the subject's submarket absorbs its units, and that the suburb will approve the use. Absorption is real, about 10,800 units in Q2 2026 alone, but stabilized vacancy of 9.6% 15 and the all-property projection of 12.6% 16 differ because the second counts buildings still leasing, and RealPage expects nearly 19,000 more Dallas deliveries over the following four quarters 18. Payrolls grew 43,100 in the year to August 2026 3, so a demand curve steeper than that needs a reason. The study then shows the hearing record of the city that permits the site, since every council runs its own code.

How deep is the development pipeline in Dallas-Fort Worth?

Deeper than anywhere else in Texas. Apartments: about 30,200 units under construction in Q2 2026, even after a 21.3% drop from a year earlier, with Grapevine the tightest submarket at 5.5% 15, plus about 3,700 build-to-rent units in Dallas 21. Dallas delivered 35,000 apartments in 2024 17 and over 4,000 market-rate units in the second quarter of 2026 19, and RealPage sees Fort Worth rents growing less than 1% 18. Hotels: 183 projects in Q2 2026, the largest pipeline of any U.S. market 24. The study lists the projects inside the subject's own competitive set, by name and opening date.

Do Dallas and Fort Worth still require parking for new projects?

Far less than before, and the suburbs are another matter. Since May 14, 2025 Dallas has had no minimums downtown, within half a mile of rail, on historic property, for offices, most retail and industrial uses away from single-family zoning; apartments follow size tiers, from none under 20 units to one space per unit past 200 36. Fort Worth now imposes minimums only where a use sits on residential land or within 250 feet of a one- or two-family district, with a ceiling of 125% of the minimum 37. Every suburb keeps its own table, so the study cites the permitting city's code and the operator's own parking standard.

Is an express car wash allowed by right in the Dallas-Fort Worth suburbs?

Often not. Mansfield treats a car wash as a Specific Use Permit case: its council turned down a tunnel wash on 2.6 acres on April 13, 2026 38, its Planning and Zoning Commission had voted 5-0 against another site in November 2024 39, and none of four such applications from 2024 to 2026 was adopted 3839. The SUP fee runs $2,500 plus $100 an acre, to a $7,500 ceiling 40. In Fort Worth, dryers and vacuums must stay 25 feet from a residential district, and a site within 200 feet needs a conditional use permit 41. A feasibility study consultant in Dallas-Fort Worth reports the approval path before the market.

Which SBA lenders are active in Dallas-Fort Worth?

The current official ranking is missing. The SBA's Dallas/Fort Worth District, run from Euless across 72 counties 47, booked $1.5 billion in fiscal 2025, a figure that likely stops at the third quarter and still led the six Texas districts 46, but the research found no fiscal 2025 lender table. The newest district release located is from fiscal 2017: Wells Fargo on top in 7(a) at $102 million, Greater Texas Capital on top among CDCs at $55 million 45. The SBA's current CDC list includes Alliance Lending Corp in Fort Worth 51. Ask each bank and CDC for its recent closings in the asset class.

Will the 2026 World Cup help a Dallas hotel or short-term rental pro forma?

Only for the year it is played, and the study should show it on a separate line. AirDNA forecast 5.5% short-term rental RevPAR growth for Dallas in 2026 on World Cup demand 27, but Dallas listings had already grown 34.4% in the year to March 2025 26, and hotel RevPAR was already down over January to September 2025: 1.5% in Dallas, 0.8% in Fort Worth and Arlington 25. Event demand lands on a market that was softening before it arrived. Stabilize the property without the tournament, label the event uplift non-recurring, and test break-even against the 183-project pipeline 24 rather than a peak month.

Is Dallas-Fort Worth industrial space overbuilt?

Not on the vacancy evidence, but the speculative pipeline is the thing to watch. Three brokers put Q2 2026 vacancy between 8.1% and 9.3%: Cushman & Wakefield lowest 9, Kidder Mathews at 8.8% 11, JLL highest 10. Absorption led the country in the first half of 2026 alongside Houston and Phoenix 12, with Alliance in north Fort Worth absorbing 3.9 million square feet year to date 9. Against that, 31.2 million square feet were under development 10. For a small-bay or owner-user building, the market study should name the competing speculative buildings in its submarket and their delivery dates, because the tenant, not the metro, carries the credit.
