The Oklahoma Thesis

Google's Stillwater campus, data centers at MidAmerica Industrial Park, and a grid the utilities say runs short.

Stillwater confirmed on March 12, 2025 that Google would operate a data center campus there 55, and in August 2025 the company tied it to a statewide investment, with phase one targeted by 2027 56. Google already runs a long-established data center at MidAmerica Industrial Park in Pryor 66, where Lambda announced a data center of its own on September 27, 2026 67.

Oklahoma State's 13,940 Stillwater employees, and the Google campus's final plat of November 13, 2025.

Payne County's estimate, the university and medical center, and the Google campus from confirmation to plat.

Payne County had 83,889 residents on July 1, 2025, against an estimates base of 81,644 in April 2020 58, and Stillwater itself counted 50,046, up from 48,350 59. Oklahoma State University employed 13,940 people in Stillwater, and Stillwater Medical Center 1,320, by the Greater Oklahoma City Chamber's October 2025 list 54. USDA Rural Development's state office is in the city 10.

The new factor is Google. The city confirmed on March 12, 2025 that Google would operate a data center campus of up to $3 billion on 400 acres, with up to 6 buildings of about 300,000 square feet each 55; in August 2025 the company tied it to a statewide investment, with phase one targeted by 2027 and 100 to 150 permanent jobs expected 56. On November 13, 2025 the planning commission approved a final plat of 202 acres in 3 lots, and building permits for Lots 1 and 2 were approved 57. Site Selection also lists Rare Earth LLC's magnet production project in Stillwater among its 2025 announcements 6.

Power and Water

PSO's 11 large-load customers by September 2025, and the terms HB 2992 sets for loads of 75 MW and more.

The Frontier reported in December 2025 that PSO already had 11 new large-load customers under letters of agreement or contract, about 779 MW, as of September 2025, that PSO's projected deficit by 2031 stood at 31%, and that OG&E's 2025 resource plan showed a shortfall of 3,459 MW by 2035 20.

The rules changed in 2026. HB 2992, signed in May, requires new loads of 75 MW or more that connect after July 1, 2026 to sign long-term agreements covering their infrastructure costs 21. OG&E filed a large-load tariff with the Corporation Commission in June 2026 with 100% upfront connection cost, minimum billing and minimum 10-year terms, and the decision was pending 22.

Water is reported campus by campus. The Frontier reported in February 2026, citing a Payne County commissioner, that Google estimates it will eventually use nearly 3 billion gallons of water a year for its data centers in Stillwater; the same report said the city can withdraw up to 50 million gallons per day from Kaw Lake and uses 7 to 14 million, and that a homeowners' association had sued over construction runoff; it reported that MidAmerica Industrial Park's permit from the Oklahoma Water Resources Board allows up to 27.375 billion gallons a year 72. For 2025 and 2026 the research found no statewide water allocation limit or sewer moratorium.

Costs, Taxes and Insurance

Homeowners rates stay file-and-use until a prior-filing law takes effect on July 1, 2027.

Oklahoma's average homeowners premium was $6,133 a year in LendingTree's data, against a national average of $2,801, 2.2x the national figure, and it rose 50.8% from 2019 to 2024 16. Rates are file-and-use, and the Insurance Department has no statutory authority to set or approve homeowners rates outside extraordinary circumstances 16. There is no FAIR plan, so no insurer of last resort stands behind an owner the voluntary market declines, although more than 30 states have one 17. A written department statement in October 2025 counted 113 insurers with active homeowners policies and approximately 60 actively writing business 18; the department's own page in May 2025, as Oklahoma Watch reported it, put the number writing new policies at over 50 77. Losses ran ahead of premiums: in 2023 the top 20 homeowners insurers in Oklahoma paid out $129 in claims for every $100 of premium collected, by the department's May 2025 account as an insurance agency quotes it 78. A law signed May 14, 2026 requires rate changes to be filed 30 days before use in a competitive market and 60 days in a non-competitive one, but it takes effect only on July 1, 2027 19. All of those are homeowners figures; the research did not find commercial property rate filings for 2025 or 2026.

Median household income stood at $65,039 in the 2020 to 2024 five-year American Community Survey estimate 39. On labor cost, State Question 832, which would have raised the minimum wage and then indexed it to prices, failed at the June 16, 2026 election 35, and the minimum in 2026 follows the federal rate, adopted by reference 79.

DR-4866-OK for the March 2025 wildfires, and DR-4950-OK for the July 4, 2026 storms.

The wildfires and winds of March 14 to 21, 2025 became DR-4866-OK, declared May 21, 2025, covering Cleveland, Creek, Lincoln, Logan, Oklahoma, Pawnee and Payne counties 24. SBA set July 21, 2025 as the deadline for physical disaster loans and February 23, 2026 for economic injury loans 25. The storms of July 4, 2026 became DR-4950-OK on October 8, 2026; Cleveland, Nowata, Osage and Washington counties receive Public Assistance under it 26. Wind and hail make up 85% of Oklahoma homeowners claims, the Insurance Commissioner told NonDoc in 2025 18.

Oklahoma City

Oklahoma City: payrolls lower in May 2026 than a year before, and the city's February 2025 home-sharing rules.

Oklahoma County's estimate, the chamber's employer list, apartment readings, the city's hotel tax and its home-sharing rules.

The metro's seven counties are Canadian, Cleveland, Grady, Lincoln, Logan, McClain and Oklahoma. Oklahoma County had 822,125 residents on July 1, 2025 in the Vintage 2025 estimate, a 3.2% rise on its estimates base of 796,550; QuickFacts also gives the April 2020 Census count, 796,292 82. Metro payrolls stood at 714,400 nonfarm jobs in May 2026, against 718,300 in May 2025, both not seasonally adjusted, a fall of 3,900, or 0.5%, in the Employment Security Commission's July 2026 report 83.

The Greater Oklahoma City Chamber's list for the region, updated in October 2025, is headed by the State of Oklahoma with 32,500 employees, then Tinker Air Force Base at 26,000 and INTEGRIS Health at 10,000; Amazon counts 8,000, Hobby Lobby Stores 6,500, Mercy Hospital 6,000, the FAA's Mike Monroney Aeronautical Center 5,150, OU Health Sciences Center and SSM Health 5,000 each, and Boeing 3,740 54.

Colliers' Q2 2026 report counted 346 multifamily units under construction, the 11th consecutive quarterly decline, with 188 units delivered in the quarter and occupancy at 95.5% 85. GlobeSt, reporting Colliers, put the average asking rent at $1,060 a month in Q4 2025, and Colliers' forecast for Q4 2026 at $1,098, a forecast rather than an actual 84. NAI Sullivan Group's Q3 2025 report put the metro's apartment vacancy at 10.7% 80.

The city's hotel tax collections rose by $9,333,442, or 48.2%, in the fiscal year to June 30, 2025, a year in which its rate went from 5.5% to 9.25% 86. Its home-sharing rules changed on February 16, 2025: a host may rent up to 10 nights a month without a special exception, and special exceptions are capped at 10% of the homes on a block 87. In June 2025 the council set the special exception's initial application fee at $1,100, up from $300, as KFOR reported 88.

Rules by Asset Class

Oklahoma's Certificate of Need covers long-term care beds; whether it still covers psychiatric units is disputed.

Health care and senior housing

Oklahoma keeps a Certificate of Need program for part of the health care market. The State Department of Health rule text covers long-term care, meaning nursing facility and ICF/IID beds, psychiatric and chemical-dependency units, and acquisitions of covered facilities 29. The department's application instructions, revised March 5, 2025, set the fee for a psychiatric or chemical-dependency project at 0.75% of capital cost, with a $1,500 minimum and a $10,000 maximum, and for long-term care at a $1,000 minimum with no maximum 76. Whether the psychiatric part still applies is unresolved: a secondary source reports that HB 2330 repealed it in May 2024, while the department's 2025 form still refers to it 30. The department licenses senior and care settings as nursing facilities, assisted living centers, residential care homes, continuum of care facilities, adult day care and specialised Alzheimer's facilities 31. The research found no bed or unit thresholds for assisted living or memory care.

Fuel stations and travel centers

The Corporation Commission runs the federal underground storage tank program and the Petroleum Storage Tank Indemnity Fund under its chapter of rules, OAC 165:27 15. Its FY2025 report shows 1,332 Indemnity Fund claims paid to 431 applicants, $22,689,820.20 paid in total, an average of 8.55 days to pay an initial claim, and $267,891,516.37 of corrective action still needed to close 532 active release cases 14.

Short-term rentals and alcohol

Short-term rentals have no state preemption and no enabling statute. HB 2800 lost its rental language and died in 2025, SB 1519 died in committee 33, and HB 3296, which would have authorised local bans, was only introduced in 2024 34. Retail liquor licensing has no quota system, with fixed fees under the state alcohol code as rewritten after a state question vote, according to a secondary guide 32; the research did not find a record of license transfer prices.

Child care

Three settings need a Human Services license: a family child care home for up to 7 children, a large family child care home for 8 to 12, and a center, for which the rules set no statutory capacity cap; no provider may accept children until the department has given permission 28. Those rules took effect November 1, 2024 28.

Oklahoma Questions

Oklahoma feasibility study questions.

How many petroleum release cases did Oklahoma's Indemnity Fund report still open at the end of FY2025?

532 active cases, with $267,891,516.37 of corrective action still to be funded, in the Corporation Commission's FY2025 claims report, which also records 1,332 claims paid in the year 14. For the buyer or builder of a fuel site, what matters is whether the parcel is one of them: the fund pays for corrective action under the Commission's rules in OAC 165:27 15, so an open case goes into the loan file by number and status, with the reimbursement route named rather than assumed.

Did HB 2330 end Certificate of Need review of psychiatric units in Oklahoma?

Not settled. Rojas Report, a secondary source, reads HB 2330 as repealing the psychiatric and chemical-dependency part of the program in May 2024, yet the State Department of Health's 2025 application form still names those units 30, and long-term care beds stay covered in the rule text either way 29. A behavioral health sponsor plans for both outcomes, with and without a Certificate of Need hearing, and asks the department in writing which applies before the loan is sized.

Is there an insurer of last resort for Oklahoma property owners?

No. Oklahoma has set up no insurer of last resort, so an owner the voluntary market declines has no state-backed policy to fall back on 17. Cover comes from the 113 insurers the Insurance Department counted with active homeowners policies in October 2025, approximately 60 of them actively writing 18, or from the over 50 writing new policies on the department's May 2025 page as Oklahoma Watch reported it 77. The owner's move is to bind the subject's own policy before closing and to carry its wind and hail deductible in the operating budget, since wind and hail made up 85% of Oklahoma homeowners claims by the Commissioner's 2025 account 18.

What water rights do Stillwater and MidAmerica Industrial Park hold?

Stillwater can take up to 50 million gallons a day from Kaw Lake, against the 7 to 14 million it uses, in The Frontier's report of February 2026, which also recorded a homeowners' association suit over construction runoff 72. MidAmerica Industrial Park holds an Oklahoma Water Resources Board permit for up to 27.375 billion gallons a year 72. No statewide allocation limit or sewer moratorium turned up for 2025 or 2026, so the question is local: an owner in either market asks the city or the park for the volume its project needs, in writing and dated.

How much did USDA Rural Development put into Oklahoma housing in FY2026?

$235,496,957, in the agency's release of October 8, 2026 as the Oklahoma Farm Report reprinted it; $61,759,754 went to small rural businesses and to oil and gas enterprises in the same year 9. An owner weighing a USDA guarantee also notes that the Rural Energy for America Program's Oklahoma page showed the program closed when the research read it 74, so a 2026 budget does not carry a REAP award until the window reopens.

How many licensed child care places did Oklahoma count in FY2024?

112,781 in centers and 15,325 in homes, down from 116,644 and 17,948 in FY2023, with 2,053 more places classed as exempt in FY2024, in the Department of Human Services' licensing data 27. A child care sponsor in Oklahoma counts the licensed places inside its own trade area, not statewide, and times its opening to the rule that no provider may take children before the department gives permission, in force since November 1, 2024 28.

What does the record show for Love County, around the Thackerville casino?

Love County's July 1, 2025 estimate was 10,780 residents, against a 2020 base of 10,145 70, and the Employment Security Commission put its unemployment rate at 7.0% in May 2026, in its July 2026 report 83. South of Thackerville, beside I-35 and the Texas line, stands the Chickasaw Nation's Border Casino 71. A travel center, hotel or store owner there shows the project working on the county's own households and payroll first, and treats casino visitors as a second case, documented on its own.
