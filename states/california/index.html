The California Thesis

Two fires in Los Angeles County in January 2025, and the insurance decisions dated after them.

On January 8, 2025 a major disaster, DR-4856-CA, was declared for wildfires and straight-line winds in Los Angeles County, for an incident period that ran from January 7 to 31 10. SBA's matching disaster declaration named Los Angeles as the primary county and Kern, Orange, San Bernardino and Ventura as contiguous ones 11. When the Department of Finance published its January 2026 estimates, it counted 11,160 Los Angeles County housing units lost to the Eaton and Palisades fires and attributed the county's 2025 population decline to the fires, to ordinary movement into the Inland Empire and to deportations 3.

The insurance record moved in dated steps. A $1 billion assessment on the FAIR Plan's member insurers was approved in February 2025 12. On March 31, 2025 higher commercial limits for the plan were approved 13. In October 2025 AB 226 allowed the plan to seek bond financing through IBank, the state's infrastructure and economic development bank 1. The dwelling increase approved in April 2026 reaches policies from October 15, 2026 214.

The Los Angeles County section carries the county's population and housing counts, and the insurance section the plan's own record.

Insurance

California's insurer of last resort: its book, its 2025 commercial limits and its 2026 dwelling rates.

The Assembly's count of the plan's book, the Department of Insurance's 2026 dwelling decision and the 2025 commercial change.

The California FAIR Plan Association is the state's insurer of last resort, organised under Insurance Code section 10090 and the sections after it 15. The Assembly Insurance Committee's 2026 update counts 668,609 policies in force and $724 billion of exposure in December 2025 1.

For dwellings, the Department of Insurance approved an average increase of 29.1% against the 35.8% the plan had asked for, effective for new and renewal policies on or after October 15, 2026 2. The department's list of approved filings shows the filing closed as approved on April 6, 2026 14.

For commercial property, Division I limits of $20 million per building and $100 million per location were approved on March 31, 2025, to be available within 120 days 13. Among the department's 2026 approvals up to August 31, 2026, none is a commercial FAIR Plan rate filing 14. No source gathered for this page names a carrier that withdrew from California in 2025 or 2026.

The plan's own finances changed in the same period. AB 226, by Calderon and Alvarez and signed in October 2025, lets it seek bond financing and credit through IBank, with the Insurance Commissioner's approval 1. The February 2025 assessment of $1 billion was charged to the insurers that make up the plan's membership, under an order of February 11, 2025 12.

Hazards

Los Angeles County's expected annual losses by hazard, and the federal declarations of 2025 and 2026.

No state-level ranking from FEMA's National Risk Index was gathered for California, so this page reads the index for Los Angeles County, through an aggregator that cites version 1.20 of December 2025 16. There, expected annual loss across all hazards is $7.60 billion, of which earthquake accounts for $4.57 billion, riverine flooding for $2.81 billion and wildfire for $155 million 16. No statewide measure of seismic or flood exposure was found, so those county figures are the quantified hazard record here.

The federal record for the period opens with DR-4856 and the SBA declaration that went with it; the deadline for economic injury loan applications under that declaration was October 8, 2025 11. On May 25, 2026 an emergency declaration covered a chemical release in Orange County, for an incident that began on May 21 17. For wildfire mapping, CAL FIRE draws Fire Hazard Severity Zones in Moderate, High and Very High classes, in both State and Local Responsibility Areas 18.

Population

The Census Bureau and the Department of Finance both record a California decline, on different dates and methods.

Census counts on July 1 and the Department of Finance on January 1, each with its own migration method.

For July 1, 2025 the Census Bureau's Vintage 2025 estimate is 39,355,309 6; its release gives 39,364,774 for the July before and names California among the five states that lost population over that year 19. The estimates base for April 1, 2020 is 39,555,703, and the July 2025 figure sits 0.5% below it 6.

The Department of Finance counts on January 1 and uses a revised method for migration from abroad 3. Its May 2026 release gives 39,592,978 residents on January 1, 2026, down 0.14% from 39,646,907, the first annual loss after three consecutive years of gains 3. The Department traces the turn to net legal immigration from abroad, 248,400 people in 2024 and 126,400 in 2025, and says the state would otherwise have seen a slight increase of 66,000 3.

The two agencies' migration counts cover different years. Census puts net international migration at 109,278 for July 2024 to June 2025, the third highest of any state 19. The Department's E-2 series gives 126,000 from abroad for the year to July 1, 2025, and a net domestic outflow of 216,000 20.

Inside the state total, the Department counted gains in only 17 counties and placed most of the growth in the Central Valley and the Inland Empire 3. It named Placer County the fastest-growing county in the state and Sacramento County the largest numeric gainer among the ten most populous; both sit in the four-county market of the Sacramento feasibility study page.

Grid Capacity

CAISO's Cluster 15 queue, from its 2023 intake to the study that began on June 1, 2025.

The ISO's own briefing and queue notice, with the FERC steps that followed.

The California ISO took 541 interconnection requests totalling 347 GW in the Cluster 15 window of 2023, a queue more than three times the capacity in state plans, its July 2025 board briefing says 23. After screening, and 255 resubmissions totalling 118 GW, 145 projects with 68 GW went into the Cluster 15 study, which began on June 1, 2025 2324. About 185 GW stayed active from Cluster 14 and the clusters before it 24.

The briefing sets that queue beside the CPUC's plan for up to 7,000 MW of new nameplate capacity a year 23. FERC approved the ISO's Order 2023 compliance in May 2025 23, and accepted its Interconnection Process Enhancements 5.0 in part, effective August 5, 2026, with the Cluster 16 window open from October 1 to 15, 2026 25. No statewide record of water allocation limits or of sewer moratoria in 2025 and 2026 was found.

Taxes and Labor

California's sales tax, its manufacturing equipment exemption and its 2026 wage floors.

California's statewide sales tax applies at a base rate with district taxes on top 26. Qualifying manufacturing and research and development equipment carries a partial exemption from it under Revenue and Taxation Code section 6377.1, which CDTFA's guide sets out with the reduced rate and the years it runs 26. A contractor building under a construction contract for a qualified manufacturer can claim the same exemption on form CDTFA-230-MC 27; how construction materials are taxed in general was not found.

From January 1, 2026 the state minimum wage is $16.90 an hour for every employer size, up from $16.50, and the salary threshold for exempt employees is $70,304 a year 28. Fast food employers, from April 1, 2024, and health care facility employers, from October 16, 2024, pay higher sector minimums 29. On public works over $1,000 the Department of Industrial Relations sets prevailing wage rates, and its notice of December 17, 2025 says none may fall below the new state minimum 30.

The Tax Foundation's 2026 table gives California an effective property tax rate of 0.70% on owner-occupied housing, 32nd among the states, a measure that leaves business property out 31. It is the only property tax rate on this page: no effective rate for California commercial property was found, nor an index of building costs in California cities against the nation, nor ranges of impact fees.

Rules by Asset Class

California's rules for family child care, quota liquor licences and underground fuel tanks.

Child care and senior care

A small family child care home may care for up to 8 children and a large one for up to 14 children, and a tenant operator needs the owner's written consent above 6 or 12 children, under Health and Safety Code sections 1597.44(d) and 1597.465(d) 32. Before a licence issues, the Community Care Licensing Division runs an orientation, takes the application and inspects the premises, licensing guides report 33. A staffing ratio for child care centers from a primary source was not found. Nor were the scope of any Certificate of Need review or the licence categories and thresholds for assisted living and memory care, in a state whose residents aged 65 and over number 6,158,371 in the 2020 to 2024 American Community Survey 22.

Liquor licences

On-sale and off-sale general licences are capped by each county's population under Business and Professions Code sections 23816 and 23817, which makes the general licence types quota licences that ABC issues new by priority drawing 34. The state fee and the private transfer prices that licence guides quote carry no date, and ABC's county quota reports are the record a restaurant or bar file reads.

Lodging, car washes and campgrounds

No state statute on short-term rentals, no statewide hotel occupancy tax, no water rule for car washes and no state licensing rule for campgrounds or RV parks was found. The lodging tax rates on this page are the city rates in the San Diego and Silicon Valley sections.

Underground fuel tanks

Single-walled underground storage tanks and nonexempt single-walled piping had to be permanently closed by December 31, 2025, under Health and Safety Code section 25292.05, and a system that was not closed cannot operate 8. The UST Cleanup Fund, financed by a storage fee of $0.02 per gallon, reimbursed $144,282,333 and closed 545 claims in fiscal 2024-25 9. At the State Water Board's meeting of May 6, 2025 the fund had 3,362 active claims and an extension through 2035 35, and a performance audit followed in May 2026, with an implementation plan in July 2026 36. County Business Patterns counts 11,228 gas station and convenience establishments in California in 2023 37.

SBA in California

Six SBA district offices divide California's counties between them.

The Los Angeles office serves Los Angeles, Santa Barbara and Ventura counties 45. The Orange County/Inland Empire office in Santa Ana serves Orange and San Bernardino counties and Riverside County west of the San Jacinto Mountains, and its Palm Desert branch serves Riverside County east of them 7. San Diego's office serves San Diego and Imperial counties 46. Fresno's serves Kern, Monterey and Tulare among its counties 47; San Francisco's serves Alameda, Marin and Santa Clara among others 48; and Sacramento's serves El Dorado, Placer, Sacramento and Yolo among others 49.

California files reach SBA under SOP 50 10 8.1, which governs applications received by SBA on or after October 1, 2026 and replaced SOP 50 10 8, in effect from June 1, 2025 50. SBA's authority to ask a borrower for one is 13 CFR 120.160(b): SBA may require professional appraisals, a survey, or a feasibility study 51, and the practice page on SBA 7(a) and 504 lending covers how each program's tests work.

Los Angeles

Los Angeles County lost homes and residents in 2025, and its largest city still added housing.

This section reads Los Angeles County alone. The Department of Finance counted 9,900,914 residents there on January 1, 2025 and 9,837,286 a year later, and 3,726,653 and 3,742,631 housing units on the same dates, after 11,160 units were lost to the Eaton and Palisades fires 3. The City of Los Angeles held 3,806,201 residents on January 1, 2026, and in 2025 it added a net 11,260 housing units, 7,061 of them multifamily 3.

The Los Angeles-Long Beach-Glendale division added 33,900 nonfarm jobs in the year to August 2026, reaching 4,603,400, by EDD's count 53. EDD's release for April 2026 had recorded a loss of 4,000 jobs in a month in motion pictures and sound recording, with the information sector down in eight of the twelve months before it 54.

Hotel-Online's 2026 outlook puts Los Angeles hotels at 71.6% occupancy and a $196 average daily rate for the twelve months to November 2025 55. In storage, RentCafe's August 2026 report counts 234,074 square feet due in the City of Los Angeles in 2026, after about 740,000 square feet delivered in 2025 56. Kidder Mathews reads the Los Angeles industrial market in the second quarter of 2026 at 6.0% direct vacancy, 6.9% total vacancy and 8.2% availability 57.

The City of Los Angeles applies its transient occupancy tax to hotels, short-term rentals, RV parks and campgrounds for short stays, and an operator must obtain a registration certificate from the city 58.

Inland Empire

Riverside and San Bernardino counties, where warehouses leased at a record pace in mid-2026.

Colliers counted a record 16.7 million square feet of gross industrial leasing in the Inland Empire in the second quarter of 2026, with vacancy down 30 basis points to 7.8%, the first quarterly improvement in more than a year, and 3.1 million square feet of net absorption 59. StorageCafe's 2026 supply report expects 728,000 square feet of new storage space to open across the Riverside-San Bernardino-Ontario metro during 2026 60, and CBRE's apartment figures show deliveries falling to 28 units in the second quarter of 2026, from 260 in the first and more than 5,000 over the year before, with occupancy at 96.0% and an average rent of $2,350 61.

Brokers read the same quarter differently: Kidder Mathews puts Inland Empire direct industrial vacancy at 7.6% 62, against Colliers' 7.8% 59.

Riverside County grew from 2,497,829 residents on January 1, 2025 to 2,506,824 a year later, and San Bernardino County from 2,215,490 to 2,216,685; housing units rose from 893,267 to 904,277 in Riverside County and from 758,120 to 764,801 in San Bernardino County 3. Menifee reached 117,847 residents after a gain of 2,034, and Fontana added 806 single-family homes 3. Riverside County permitted 9,194 residential units in calendar 2025 63; a calendar 2025 total for San Bernardino County was not found.

The Riverside-San Bernardino-Ontario metro added 12,700 nonfarm jobs in the year to August 2026; education and health services gained 17,900 and construction lost 4,600 64. Riverside County's 2023 list of major employers puts the County of Riverside first, at 25,366 employees, and Amazon second, at 14,317, followed by March Air Reserve Base, Nestle USA at Mira Loma and UC Riverside 65. No matching list for San Bernardino County was found.

San Diego

The city's net housing gain was 8,635 units, 6,179 of them multifamily 3. The San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad metro added 13,400 nonfarm jobs in the year to May 2026, to 1,584,900 69.

Hotel Guru's April 2026 update puts San Diego occupancy at 81.37% in March 2026, the average daily rate at $210.76 and revenue per available room at $171.50, up 18.09% on a year earlier 67. Over the twelve months to late 2025 Hotel-Online gives 72.3% occupancy 55. In August 2025 the San Diego Tourism Authority's weekly report, built on STR data, named the new Gaylord hotel in Chula Vista among the supply weighing on county occupancy, in a week of 73.9% occupancy, 5% lower than a year before, with revenue per available room of $163, down 7.9% 70. Hotel Guru had reported 2,700 rooms in nine hotels scheduled to open in San Diego during 2025 71.

Kidder Mathews reads San Diego's second-quarter 2026 industrial market at 7.4% total vacancy and 11.0% availability 72. The City of San Diego's transient occupancy tax on stays under 30 days is 10.5%, under Municipal Code section 35.0103, by a secondary lookup site 73.

San Francisco

San Francisco, Alameda, Contra Costa, San Mateo and Marin counties: residents, homes and the 2026 apartment quarter.

Between January 1, 2025 and January 1, 2026 San Francisco County rose from 844,394 residents to 845,658, while Alameda went from 1,667,901 to 1,666,136, Contra Costa from 1,167,506 to 1,163,891, San Mateo from 748,412 to 748,182 and Marin from 253,171 to 250,823 3. Housing units rose from 422,007 to 423,517 in San Francisco and from 652,678 to 656,619 in Alameda County 3.

CBRE's second-quarter 2026 figures put Bay Area apartment vacancy at 2.8% and San Francisco's at 2.4%, with rents up 7.7% over the year across the Bay Area and 11.4% in San Francisco and the Peninsula 74. In that quarter 884 units were delivered and 5,104 absorbed 74. For laboratory buildings, Cushman & Wakefield's Silicon Valley MarketBeats puts Bay Area life sciences vacancy at 28.5% in the first quarter of 2026 75.

In San Francisco, short-term rental hosts collect and remit the city's transient occupancy tax and hold a Certificate of Authority 76.

Silicon Valley

Central Valley

Fresno and Kern counties: level populations, and Bakersfield industrial space with one preleased project under way.

What is the wage floor in a 2026 California feasibility study?

$16.90 an hour for employers of every size since January 1, 2026, against $16.50 before it, with an exempt salary threshold of $70,304 a year 28. $725,928,829 of a $1,181,997,613 allocation by December 31, 2025, the most deployed by any jurisdiction in Treasury's quarterly report 5.
