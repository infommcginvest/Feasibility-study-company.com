Greater Boston

Greater Boston, read on its five Massachusetts counties: Essex, Middlesex, Norfolk, Plymouth and Suffolk.

The Census Bureau's Boston-Cambridge-Newton metro also takes in Rockingham and Strafford counties in New Hampshire.

Matthews Real Estate Investment Services counted 14,418 apartment units under construction across Greater Boston in its September 2026 report 2. Its June report recorded the other end of the pipeline: starts in the second quarter fell to 177 units, the lowest since 2016 1. The two figures answer different questions for an apartment loan. The units already rising are the competition a new building meets during its own lease-up; the starts figure bears on what comes after them, and it belongs to one broker and one quarter, which is how a study should quote it.

Rents come from the same broker. Greater Boston's asking rent stood at $2,990 a month in Matthews' May 2026 report, and Intown Boston's at $3,840 7. Matthews places new apartment building in four clusters: Somerville and Charlestown; Everett, Malden and Medford; Chelmsford and Tyngsborough; and Quincy, Milton and Randolph 1. The largest submarket pipeline in its June report was Cambridge and Somerville, with 3,427 units under construction 1. That is a Greater Boston submarket in the broker's map, not a Cambridge city count, so it is read here rather than in the Cambridge section.

Retail and industrial space have their own quarterly readings. Matthews' second-quarter retail report counts 587,000 square feet of retail space under construction, and the same report prints Greater Boston's retail vacancy once as 2.6% and once as 2.8% 8; a study citing it says which of the two it took. Cushman & Wakefield's industrial MarketBeat put second-quarter vacancy at 12.4% 9. A second broker's industrial reading for that quarter reached the record only through a news relay that carries no date, so it is not printed here. An industrial study keeps its comparison buildings on one broker's series from start to finish.

Population and permits are Census counts and cover three of the five counties. Suffolk County counted 791,891 residents on July 1, 2025, below its April 2020 base of 800,934 10. Middlesex County counted 1,669,979 11. In 2025 Suffolk County authorized 2,338 housing units, Middlesex County 2,341 and Norfolk County 1,194 1213. A Vintage 2025 count for Norfolk County was not found, and nothing dated is on record for Plymouth County; Essex County's own figures sit in the Lowell section, where that county is read with Lawrence.

Payrolls are the figure to handle with care here. BLS publishes the Boston-Cambridge-Newton metro's nonfarm count for the Massachusetts and New Hampshire area as one series, so this page prints no Greater Boston payroll figure. A study that needs one quotes the BLS metro series under its two-state name, or works from Massachusetts county counts.

The register table gives the 2020 to 2024 American Community Survey's median gross rent for Suffolk County and median household income for Middlesex County. Both are Massachusetts county values; none of the register's New Hampshire rows is used. The multifamily and retail methods behind these readings are set out on the multifamily hub and the retail hub.

Cambridge and Kendall Square

Cambridge city: lab vacancy by submarket, and a population estimate of its own.

Every figure in this section is a Cambridge city figure, from the state data center or from lab brokers; Middlesex County's counts belong to the Greater Boston and Lowell sections.

Lincoln Property Co.'s second-quarter 2026 lab report puts vacancy in Alewife and West Cambridge at 39.5% 3. The same report gives 22.8% for Cambridge as a whole, 13.4% for East Cambridge and 15.5% for Mid-Cambridge 3. Those are several lab markets inside one city, so a lab or lab conversion study names its submarket before it quotes a rate. Cresa's 2026 life sciences report measured East Cambridge's lab inventory at 11,973,499 square feet at the end of 2025, with 15.7% vacant and Class A rent of $105 a square foot 14. Lincoln's 13.4% and Cresa's 15.7% are readings by different brokers for different quarters, not two answers to one question; a study keeps to one broker's series through its comparison.

New lab space is still being delivered. Banker & Tradesman reported preleased build-to-suit buildings for Takeda, Biogen and AstraZeneca under construction in Kendall Square in 2026, alongside Alexandria's petition for more lab space in a Tech Square transfer district and a housing conversion by special permit under the city's Affordable Housing Overlay 15. For a lender a preleased building adds no competing vacancy and an unleased one does, so a study counts the two kinds of pipeline separately.

The UMass Donahue Institute's Vintage 2025 estimates for cities and towns give Cambridge 122,588 residents in July 2025, against 121,837 a year earlier 16. A 2020 base, a 2025 permit count and a payroll series for the city itself were not found, and the Middlesex County figures sometimes offered in their place are Greater Boston's. Apartment demand in the city is read through the Cambridge and Somerville submarket in the Greater Boston section, which is how Matthews reports it.

The register's Cambridge figure is the city's median gross rent from the 2020 to 2024 American Community Survey, a city value like the rest of this section.

Population and Payrolls

Massachusetts residents and jobs in the 2025 and 2026 releases.

The state data center, the Census Bureau and BLS each date their statewide count; the market sections carry the county ones.

The UMass Donahue Institute, the state's data center, put Massachusetts's gain at 15,524 residents between July 1, 2024 and July 1, 2025, in an analysis titled for growth that was slowing but still positive 4. The Census Bureau's Vintage 2025 components, as reproduced from its state file, show how the year's change was made up: a domestic outflow, net domestic migration of -33,340, set against an international inflow of +40,240, both for the twelve months to June 2025 17. CommonWealth Beacon reported in January 2026 that the international rate was the fourth-highest among the states and the District of Columbia 18. For a residential or retail project the county sections on this page say where residents were counted; the statewide components say only where the year's newcomers came from.

BLS counted 3,706.0 thousand nonfarm jobs in Massachusetts in August 2026, preliminary and seasonally adjusted, 8.3 thousand more than a year earlier 19. The state's August data sheet breaks the year down by supersector: private education and health services added 14,400 jobs, manufacturing 4,100 and construction 2,300, while trade, transportation and utilities lost 4,400 and professional and business services 3,200 20. The Joint Economic Committee's Massachusetts update, dated September 18, 2026, shows the same direction for education and health, trade and professional services 21. The summer months ran the other way: the Executive Office of Labor and Workforce Development's release of September 18 recorded payroll losses of 4,000 jobs in July and 7,100 in August, with an unemployment rate of 4.3% and labor force participation of 65.3% 5.

Several other sector rows on the data sheet were read from a PDF text extraction that still awaits a check against the source table, so this page leaves them out. A study that leans on one sector's twelve-month change quotes that row from the data sheet's own table.

Statewide Supply

Statewide supply in Massachusetts: the register's hotel and rent series, and a payer-mix reading for assisted living.

The register supplies the statewide floor. County Business Patterns counts the state's hotel establishments for 2023, and the American Community Survey gives its median gross rent for 2020 to 2024 (table below). Neither is a vacancy rate or a pipeline, and statewide series of that kind for apartments, hotels or industrial space were not found. Apartment pipelines appear on this page for Greater Boston, its Cambridge and Somerville submarket and Essex County, and lab space for Cambridge, each in its own section; the hotel hub sets out how a hotel study assembles its competitive set where no regional series exists.

Senior housing has one statewide reading, and it is a payer mix rather than an occupancy rate. The Executive Office of Aging & Independence told its Assisted Living Residences Commission on February 26, 2025 that about 90% of assisted living residents pay privately 22. A residence underwritten in Massachusetts therefore rests on private-pay households near the site: their incomes, their ages and the residences already serving them. The demand method for a residence is on the assisted living and memory care hub.

Worcester and the I-495 Belt

Worcester County and the city of Worcester: counts since 2020 and industrial leasing along I-495.

Every figure here is for Worcester County or the city.

Worcester County's Vintage 2025 count was 888,502 on July 1, 2025, up from an April 2020 estimates base of 862,093; the city of Worcester's was 213,862, from a base of 206,508 1023. Covered employment in the county stood at 350,633 in March 2026, 0.6% below a year earlier 24. A study for a project that serves households reads the residents; one that depends on local hiring reads the county's covered jobs.

Industrial demand here follows I-495. Cushman & Wakefield's second-quarter MarketBeat records a new-build lease of 616,875 square feet in Hopedale 9. Lornell's market page sorts the city's corridors by use: Class A industrial on Route 146, industrial on Route 20, neighborhood retail on Shrewsbury Street and offices on Green Hill Parkway 25. Each is one property or one broker's map; a study uses them as named comparables, not as a vacancy rate.

A Worcester development panel that Bowditch reported on July 8, 2026 named power and utility capacity as the defining constraints for the next building cycle, and called vacancy in industrial buildings under 100,000 square feet exceptionally low without giving a dated rate 26.

Rural programs touch only the edges of this market. USDA's Economic Research Service maps, of 2000 vintage, show the Worcester urbanized area as ineligible and outer towns as partly eligible; a current tract list was not retrieved, so a USDA file here begins with the agency's own eligibility determination 27. Merrimack Valley

Lowell and Lawrence, read through Essex County's apartments and the district around UMass Lowell.

The register's Lowell market is the two cities. The dated figures below are county figures, for Essex County, where Lawrence lies, and Middlesex County, Lowell's, and they are labelled that way.

Essex County counted 826,653 residents in July 2025 in the UMass Donahue Institute's Vintage 2025 county summary, against 826,170 a year earlier 29, and it issued permits for 942 units of housing in 2025 30. Middlesex County's 2,341 units, given in the Greater Boston section, are Lowell's county count as well. Population, permits and payrolls for the cities of Lowell and Lawrence themselves were not found.

Matthews reports Essex County apartments on their own. In the second quarter of 2026 it gave county vacancy of 4.2%, rent of $2,638 a month and 45 units under construction, and for the trailing twelve months 859 units delivered and 417 units absorbed 31. A study for a new Essex County building sets its lease-up pace against that absorption figure rather than against deliveries, and names the quarter it used. Matthews also lists Chelmsford and Tyngsborough among the places where Greater Boston's apartment building concentrates 1.

The dated pipeline is the district around UMass Lowell. The university launched its Designing the Future campaign on October 17, 2025 with a goal of $250 million and nearly $166 million in commitments, some of it earmarked for LINC, a mixed-use district of 1.2 million square feet 32. WGBH reported in March 2024 that LINC would cost about $800 million, with GMH Communities and Wexford, carry about 500 housing units, with completion targeted for 2027 33. Within it, Hoodline reported on April 1, 2026 that Draper's IMPACT microelectronics center had won approval, the City Council having approved a land-disposition agreement with Wexford; Hoodline put the center at about $100 million and about 75,000 square feet with more than 150 jobs 34.

No register figure is shown for the Lowell market: the register's values there are for the two cities together, and this section's dated evidence is county evidence.

Entry Rules

Massachusetts entry rules by asset class: surgery, assisted living, liquor, child care and short-term rentals.

Ambulatory surgery and other health care capacity

The Department of Public Health's Determination of Need program reviews substantial capital spending, new services and equipment, changes in service and ownership transfers at health care facilities, and its July 2025 application instructions apply it to freestanding ambulatory surgery whether or not the project passes the expenditure minimum 35. The program's January 15, 2025 decision on an Atrius Health surgery center in Waltham shows what an approval can carry: six operating rooms, approved with a Community Health Initiative contribution of $1,038,886.05 36. A surgery center pro forma here holds that contribution as a project cost and the decision date as a schedule milestone; the medical office and ambulatory surgery hub covers the market side.

Assisted living

An assisted living residence needs certification from the Executive Office of Aging & Independence to operate or to advertise 37. Chapter 197 of the Acts of 2024 authorized Basic Health Services, and the final amendments the administration announced on July 10, 2026 require a residence that offers them to keep licensed nursing on site for at least 16 hours a day 38. For a residence planning Basic Health Services the nursing hours enter the operating budget from its first year. A separate memory care category, with its own unit threshold, was not found in the state's rules.

Liquor licenses

Massachusetts caps on-premises and package licenses through a statutory quota tied to each municipality's population, and the statute does not lower a quota when population falls; Boston sits outside it under caps of its own 39. Transfer prices between license holders were found only on an undated aggregator and are not printed here. A local-option statute in force since July 1, 2025 lets an adopting municipality allow wine-and-malt licensees to convert to all-alcohol licenses without buying on the open market, as Start Business by State describes it 40. A restaurant study names the town, whether it has adopted that option and the license the operator will hold; the restaurant hub covers the sales side.

Child care

The Department of Early Education and Care licenses three classes under its regulations: family child care in a private residence, small group and school age, and large group and school age 41. Its ratio and group-size limits sit in the same chapter, which the record dates only as current, so a center's staffing plan reads them on the date the study is written. The child care hub sets out the enrollment side.

Short-term rentals

State law taxes short-term rentals under the room occupancy excise and requires registration with the Department of Revenue, but it leaves operators to local regulation: each city or town may govern them by bylaw or ordinance 42. The Cape Cod section gives a Nantucket court ruling on rentals in residential districts.

Interconnection

Executive Order 658 and the utilities' data-center queues, from September 2026.

On September 8, 2026 the Governor's Executive Order 658 asked the Department of Public Utilities to have the distribution companies take measures to remove speculative data-center projects from their interconnection queues. The department's directive to Unitil, Eversource and National Grid followed: list every data-center project above 1 MW in the queue and verify the legitimacy of those above 25 MW 43. For a data-center proposal the directive is a test it meets before any market question: its queue position has to survive the utility's review, and a study dates its schedule from the utility's answer.

Smaller generators follow a separate track. The Department of Energy Resources publishes the utilities' monthly interconnection data, through March 2026 in the latest file on record 44, and the Technical Standards Review Group's December 11, 2025 materials apply FERC Transitional Cluster Study cases to every distributed energy project in the 2026 Affected System Operator study, with the opt-in closing at the end of 2025 45. A project that adds rooftop solar or batteries to cut its operating costs carries that study's timing in its schedule and does not book the savings before the connection date.

Springfield, Massachusetts

Springfield, Massachusetts: Hampden County, with Hampshire and Franklin counties in the register's market.

Hampden County's dated counts and BLS's Springfield metro series carry this section.

Hampden County counted 464,338 residents in July 2025, below its April 2020 base of 465,818, in the state data center's Vintage 2025 county table 29. The section reads Springfield, Massachusetts; the Springfields of Illinois and Missouri are sections of those state pages. The register's Springfield market adds Hampshire and Franklin counties; this page holds no dated Vintage 2025 count for either, and Franklin County appears again in the Berkshires section, where the research files it.

Two federal payroll series describe the market, and they point different ways. BLS's Springfield metro series stood at 208.2 thousand nonfarm jobs for August 2026, preliminary, 1.1% more than a year earlier 46, while covered employment in Hampden County was 197,784 in March 2026, 0.9% lower than a year before 24. The two differ in method, and the research advises reconciling them before relying on either; a Springfield study that sizes demand on jobs says which one it uses and why.

Leisure and hospitality is the nearest hotel measure on record: the metro's jobs in that sector ran from 17.6 thousand in February 2026 to 21.0 thousand in July, preliminary 46. A room count, occupancy series or pipeline for the market was not found, so a hotel study here buys its competitive data. The convention center financing fee of 2.75% applies to hotel stays in Springfield, West Springfield and Chicopee, as in Boston, Worcester and Cambridge, under the Department of Revenue's rates as updated on December 30, 2025 47.

The city's opportunity zone prospectus, an undated document, lists Baystate Health, MassMutual, Sisters of Providence (Mercy), MGM Springfield and Smith & Wesson as the largest employers and confirms that opportunity zones exist in the city 48; headcounts from it are not printed because it carries no date. Western Mass EDC lists the University of Massachusetts Amherst, in Hampshire County, among the region's largest employers 49. USDA's 2000-vintage maps show the Springfield urbanized area as ineligible and hill towns as partly eligible 27.

The register row is the market's SBA 7(a) loan volume for FY2016 to FY2025, across the three counties of the register's market.

Berkshires and Western Massachusetts

Berkshire County and the Pittsfield metro: residents by year, payrolls by season.

The register reads this market as Berkshire County alone; the research adds Franklin County, which the register places in the Springfield market.

The UMass Donahue Institute's Vintage 2025 summary counts 128,224 Berkshire County residents in July 2025, 129,049 a year earlier and 129,026 at the 2020 Census 29. A project whose customers live in the county reads those three counts together; a lodging or second-home project reads the visitors, whom no county count measures.

BLS's Pittsfield metro shows the season. Nonfarm jobs stood at 57.1 thousand in February 2026 and 59.8 thousand in July, preliminary 50. Leisure and hospitality held 9.7 thousand jobs in July, 2.0% fewer than a year before, beside 16.1 thousand in education and health and 5.6 thousand in professional and business services 50. A twelve-month change in total payrolls for the metro was not found. Covered employment in Berkshire County was 55,935 in March 2026 24, a winter quarter, so a hotel or restaurant study sets its staffing and demand assumptions by month rather than from an annual average.

The Berkshire Eagle reported the county's progress toward the state housing goal from the state's tracker, but the article carries no date, so its unit count is not printed 51; a 2025 permit count for the county was not found. Hotel supply was not found either, and the leisure payroll above is the closest dated measure.

Franklin County, which the research groups with Berkshire County as western Massachusetts, has one USDA eligibility reading, from a page that carries no date: it describes nearly all of the county as eligible 52. In Berkshire County, USDA's 2000-vintage maps show the Pittsfield urbanized area as ineligible and rural towns as eligible 27. Rockland Trust reports that SBA's Massachusetts office named it the most active lender to rural counties in FY2024 28. A hill-town project meets the program tests described on the USDA Rural Development practice page.

Costs and Insurance

Massachusetts operating costs with a date on them: lodging taxes, wages, the tank fund and the FAIR Plan.

Lodging taxes

The Department of Revenue's rate page, updated December 30, 2025, sets the state room occupancy tax at 5.7%, allows a local option of up to 6% (6.5% in Boston) and adds the 2.75% convention center financing fee in Boston, Cambridge, Worcester, Springfield, Chicopee and West Springfield 47. A combined rate for a given town was not found as a published figure, so the lodging budget of a hotel or short-term rental assembles it from the rates the subject's town has adopted. On the Cape, a further water fund charge applies in some towns (Cape Cod section).

Property tax

Municipalities set separate commercial rates. Massachusetts Almanac's table of FY2026 certified rates, as of January 2026, gives the highest commercial rate in the state as Holyoke's $38.15 per $1,000 of value 53. A study prices the tax line from the subject town's certified rate for the year of the pro forma, not from a statewide figure.

Minimum and living wages

Massachusetts sets its minimum wage at $15.00 an hour for 2026 and its tipped service rate at $6.75, unchanged since January 1, 2023, with no local minimums and no inflation indexing, as MassLegalResources summarized the statute on August 22, 2026 54. Boston's living wage for city-funded contracts and financial-assistance arrangements is $19.36 an hour from July 1, 2026 55. A prevailing wage rule for state-assisted projects was not found.

The petroleum cleanup fund

The Department of Revenue's tank reimbursement rules were revised in 2025 56. The c.21J Petroleum Product Cleanup Fund's status report of February 28, 2025 set the FY2025 claims appropriation at $8,490,000 and net funding for claims at $6,990,000, with a further $3.3 million in payments expected later in the fiscal year 57. A fuel station study reads that report for the money set aside for claims in the year; whether a given release is reimbursed turns on the reimbursement rules; the gas station and convenience store hub covers the trade side.

Property insurance

The Massachusetts Property Insurance Underwriting Association, the FAIR Plan, offers homeowners, dwelling fire and commercial property coverage at rates the Division of Insurance approves, and a new FAIR Plan policy on a coastal flood-zone property (a Special Flood Hazard Area in a Coastal Zone Management community) needs a flood policy of its own 58. From February 1, 2025 a new policy insures the dwelling (Coverage A) to 90% of replacement cost; the floor had been 80% 59. Enrollment reached more than 173,000 policies in fiscal 2024, up from 158,660 in fiscal 2023; CommonWealth Beacon, citing the Insurance Commissioner's 2024 annual report, called it the plan's first annual rise since 2017 and its biggest one-year gain since 2007, and reported that the plan had not raised rates since 2006 60. Approved commercial rate changes and carrier withdrawals for 2025 and 2026 were not found; the FAIR Plan's own conditions above are the dated part of the Massachusetts insurance record.

Cape Cod and the Islands

Barnstable, Dukes and Nantucket counties, where septic systems and rental rulings reach the site.

The register's Cape Cod market is the three counties; Nantucket's Vintage 2025 count was not found.

Barnstable County held 233,539 residents at July 1, 2025 and 229,064 at the April 2020 base, and 36.4% of them were 65 or older 61. Dukes County counted 21,219 in July 2025 against 21,354 a year earlier in the UMass Donahue Institute's summary 29. The age profile is the demand base for health care, senior housing and services on the Cape; the seasonal visitor is the base for lodging and restaurants, and no county count measures that visitor.

The two federal payroll series disagree here too. Covered employment in Barnstable County was 84,915 in March 2026, 1.9% below a year earlier 24, while BLS's monthly metro survey showed the Barnstable Town metro up 5.1% from a year earlier in March 2026 and 5.9% in January 62. The research advises reconciling the two before relying on either; a study names the series it used.

Wastewater is the constraint the Cape record turns on. Barnstable County's Title 5 factsheet describes most Cape homes as served by septic systems 63, and Barnstable's comprehensive wastewater plan, with the Hyannis treatment plant upgrade and sewer areas in Centerville and along Phinney's Lane, is the town's answer 64. That plan draws part of its funding from the hotel rooms, meals and short-term rental taxes collected in the town 65. For a restaurant, inn or care facility, the study confirms with the town whether and when the parcel will be sewered before it projects covers or beds.

The lodging tax stack differs by town. Barnstable's short-term rental page adds a Cape Cod and Islands Water Protection Fund charge to the state and town taxes 66. Who pays that charge is read two ways: in Barnstable's reading every Cape town belongs to the fund and the islands do not, while the Cape Cod Chronicle reports the charge applying in Dukes and Nantucket counties as well 67; a study confirms it with the town where the subject sits. The Chronicle also reports a Land Court ruling of June 2025 that short-term rentals are not an allowed primary use in Nantucket's residential districts, now under appeal, and a local ban on corporate ownership of short-term rentals 67.

Insurance is the other site-level question. The port's demand case is offshore wind, and its record in 2025 and 2026 is a record of federal orders. The February 2026 blizzard, its counties and the federal answer, with the last confirmed declaration before it.

The storm of February 22 and 23, 2026 brought snowfall rates of up to four inches an hour and cut power to nearly 300,000 customers, the Governor's office reported 71; a letter from Senator Markey's office, reported by New Bedford Guide, put snowfall above 41 inches in parts of southeastern Massachusetts 72. Massachusetts Questions

Massachusetts questions from lenders and sponsors.

Which Greater Boston submarket holds the largest apartment pipeline?

Cambridge and Somerville, on Matthews' map: its June 2026 report put 3,427 units under construction there, the largest submarket pipeline in the region, and placed new building in four clusters, Somerville with Charlestown, Everett with Malden and Medford, Chelmsford with Tyngsborough, and Quincy with Milton and Randolph 1. That submarket is drawn on the broker's map and is not a count for the city of Cambridge. Essex County is reported on its own: 45 units were under construction there in the second quarter of 2026, and over the trailing twelve months it saw 859 units delivered against 417 absorbed 31. A lease-up schedule is tested against the buildings in the subject's own cluster, not against the regional total.

Does one vacancy rate describe Cambridge's lab market?

No. Lincoln Property Co.'s second-quarter 2026 report gives 39.5% for Alewife and West Cambridge, 22.8% citywide, 13.4% in East Cambridge and 15.5% in Mid-Cambridge 3. Cresa's 2026 report measured East Cambridge differently: 11,973,499 square feet of East Cambridge lab space at the close of 2025, 15.7% of it vacant, and $105 a square foot of Class A rent 14. The two East Cambridge readings, Lincoln's and Cresa's, come from different brokers and different quarters, so a lab or conversion study names its submarket, picks one broker's series and stays with it through the comparison. The build-to-suit buildings under way for Takeda, Biogen and AstraZeneca are preleased and add no competing vacancy; an unleased building does 15.

What does a Massachusetts assisted living residence need before it offers Basic Health Services?

Certification by the state's Executive Office of Aging & Independence, needed to operate or advertise 37. Those services were created by Chapter 197 of the Acts of 2024, and the administration's final amendments of July 10, 2026 require a residence offering them to keep a licensed nurse on site for at least 16 hours of every day 38, a staffing line that enters the operating budget from year one. Private payers make up about 90% of residents, the aging office told its commission on February 26, 2025 22, so the demand case is built from the incomes and ages of private-pay households around the site.

What did Determination of Need attach to the Waltham surgery center it approved in January 2025?

A Community Health Initiative contribution of $1,038,886.05, on the decision of January 15, 2025 that approved an Atrius Health ambulatory surgery center with six operating rooms 36. The program sits in the Department of Public Health and reviews large capital outlays, new services and equipment, service changes and ownership transfers; its July 2025 application instructions bring freestanding ambulatory surgery under review whether or not the project clears the expenditure minimum 35. A surgery center pro forma in Massachusetts therefore carries the contribution among its project costs, treats the decision date as a milestone in its schedule, and reads the Waltham decision for the conditions a new center can expect.

What does a new Massachusetts FAIR Plan policy on a coastal property have to carry?

Flood cover of its own: a new FAIR Plan policy written on a property in a Special Flood Hazard Area within a Coastal Zone Management community carries a separate flood policy 58. Since February 1, 2025 a new policy has also had to insure the dwelling to 90% of its replacement cost, where 80% had been enough 59. Enrollment rose from 158,660 policies in fiscal 2023 to more than 173,000 in fiscal 2024, the first annual rise since 2017, CommonWealth Beacon reported from the Insurance Commissioner's annual report 60. The same outlet put 2023 nonrenewals at one policy in 16 for Barnstable County and one in 9 for Dukes County 60, so a Cape project confirms its cover with the carrier before fixing its budget.

Is Eastern Bank or Rockland Trust the top SBA lender in Massachusetts?

Each, by its own release, on a different measure. Eastern Bank says SBA placed it first in Massachusetts by 7(a) loan count for a 17th consecutive year; it made 290 loans in FY2025, approximately $43.5 million in all 6, and a year earlier it reported 305 7(a) loans and 23 loans under the 504 program as the top banking 504 lender 79. Rockland Trust reports the most 7(a) dollars in FY2024 and the most activity in rural counties 28, and the leading 504 third-party lender position for FY2023, at $21.8 million 80. SBA's own statewide totals and a ranking below first place were not found; neither release ranks lenders for a single market, and the register's ten-year totals on this page come from SBA's loan-level files.

What ties Cape Cod's lodging taxes to its sewers?

Barnstable's wastewater plan. Most Cape homes are on septic systems, by Barnstable County's Title 5 factsheet 63, and the town's plan, which upgrades the Hyannis treatment plant and sewers Centerville and Phinney's Lane 64, is funded partly from the room, meals and short-term rental taxes the town collects 65. Barnstable's short-term rental page also lists a Water Protection Fund charge for the Cape Cod and Islands 66, which the Cape Cod Chronicle reports reaching Dukes and Nantucket counties too, where the town's reading leaves the islands out 67. So an inn, restaurant or care facility on the Cape asks the town whether and when its parcel will be sewered, and which taxes it will collect, before forecasting covers or beds.
