Health Payrolls

Education and health added New Jersey jobs in a year when the private payroll shrank.

The Department of Labor and Workforce Development's tables of September 17, 2026, preliminary for August 2026.

The statewide payroll moved in two directions at once. On the seasonally adjusted series, education and health services employed 824,300 people in August 2026, the one private supersector above its August 2025 level in the table below, while total private employment fell by 13,100 over the twelve months 1. Government was the only other supersector to grow 1. A study whose demand rests on a hospital, a clinic or a care residence can point to a state payroll that is still adding people in its own sector; a study for anything else cannot borrow that growth.

The unadjusted series splits the health side further, and every line of it grew over the year: hospitals, ambulatory health care and nursing and residential care 1. Care Licensing

New Jersey opens long-term care beds to applicants only when the Department of Health calls for them.

On paper the Certificate of Need statute reaches far: it requires a certificate to build or expand a health care facility or to start a new service, including construction above $2 million by a licensed facility 6. In practice its exemptions are broad, and the Institute for Justice reads the program as aimed mainly at long-term care 7.

For long-term care beds the gate is a call. The Department of Health accepts a full-review application for general or specialized long-term care beds only in answer to a call it publishes in the New Jersey Register, under N.J.A.C. 8:33H 8. Full reviews go to the State Health Planning Board, while listed categories move on an expedited track decided by a Deputy Commissioner 9. A nursing home study therefore begins with the call itself: whether one is open, for which county and bed type, and on what timetable. A bed count in a pro forma that answers no call has no licensing path.

Assisted living residences are licensed by the same department, but the category definitions and the bed or unit thresholds are not in any source this page holds, and a memory care or assisted living file obtains them from the Department itself 9.

Staffing carries its own floor. From January 1, 2026 the minimum wage for long-term care direct care staff is $18.92 an hour, against $15.92 for most employers 10. A care residence prices its aides from the higher of the two.

Liquor Licenses

A law signed in January 2024 obliges New Jersey's idle liquor licenses to be used or sold.

Retail liquor licenses in New Jersey are capped by a population quota written into the statute, N.J.S.A. 33:1-12.14, as a law firm's guide sets it out 14. Where a town's quota is full, a new restaurant or hotel bar can only buy a license that already exists, and in January 2023 Restaurant Business reported that such a permit can cost upwards of $1 million 15.

The supply of licenses for sale is itself on the record. In August 2024 the NJEDA, planning a program to help small businesses acquire them, counted 1,379 inactive licenses in the state's system 2. P.L. 2023, c. 290, signed in January 2024, requires inactive licenses to be used or sold, and on April 23, 2025 the State League of Municipalities reported that the Division of Alcoholic Beverage Control's quartile list showed 1,165 inactive licenses available for transfer 16. The two counts come from different lists on different dates, and neither carries a price.

For a hotel or restaurant loan the license is bought like land, it sits among the project's costs, and a lender reads whether a contract for it is already signed. The current transfer price for any town is not in a source this page can cite; the 2023 figure is a newsroom's report of what a permit can cost, not a quote. The study takes the price from a signed agreement or a broker's dated offer in the project's own municipality, and dates it.

Electricity

New Jersey's utility board projected residential bills 17.23% to 20.20% higher from June 1, 2025.

The board's order of June 30, 2026 traces that increase largely to PJM's capacity auction of July 2024.

The state regulator dated the shock itself. In its June 30, 2026 order on the 2026 Residential Universal Bill Credit, the Board of Public Utilities records projected average monthly residential bill increases of 17.23% to 20.20% from June 1, 2025, depending on the utility's service territory, and says they largely stemmed from the PJM base residual auction of July 2024 17. The same order sets the next step beside it: the basic generation service auction certified on February 12, 2026 projected changes of -1.8% to +1.6% from June 1, 2026 17. PJM's following auction, for the 2026/27 delivery year, cleared at its price cap in July 2025, a result for PJM's whole market rather than a New Jersey price 18.

Both ranges are projections of average monthly bills by territory, the first for residential customers, and neither is a commercial tariff. An operating budget for a hotel, a cold store or a care residence takes its own utility's commercial rate and dates the quote; one written before June 2025 carries an electricity line priced before the increase.

The state set its own data center terms in 2026. The statewide data center plan was announced on May 27, 2026, and its guardrails, labor standards with prevailing wage guidance among them, were fully in force on August 27, 2026 19. On that same day P.L. 2026, c. 77 cut the Next New Jersey Program's AI tax credit authorization from $500 million to $250 million and closed it to new applicants 20. The manufacturing arm of the program had made its first award on June 18, 2026: $37.5 million in tax credits for Starman New Photonics' $150 million optical transceiver project in Warren Township, Somerset County, with 250 jobs 21.

Middlesex County

Middlesex County: Turnpike warehouses at Exit 8A, and a payroll division whose estimate fell.

One county, on its own figures. It belongs to the New York metro, and none of that metro's New York counties is counted here.

At New Jersey Turnpike Exit 8A, across Monroe, Cranbury and South Brunswick, NAI James E. Hanson put industrial vacancy at 7.5% and the asking rent at $15.68 per square foot for 2Q 2026, with Exits 10 and 12 at 6.4% 12. The same report records two big-box leases, 600,000 square feet to DSV at Monroe and 800,000 to GoFo at Exits 10 and 12 12, and KBC Advisors had Central New Jersey vacancy down 60 basis points over the quarter 40. A warehouse study at the interchanges reads its own submarket; the Northern New Jersey figure in the supply section is a regional average, not this one.

BLS reads the county's jobs inside the Lakewood-New Brunswick Metro Division, which is wider than Middlesex: 1,189.8 thousand jobs on the August 2026 preliminary count, down from 1,195.8 thousand, a fall of 6.0 thousand that BLS reports as not significant 27. A division total cannot show which county lost the jobs, and no county series is on record here.

The Census Bureau estimates 883,335 Middlesex residents for July 1, 2025, up 2.3% from the April 2020 base of 863,225 41. Houses outnumbered apartments in its 2025 permits, 551 units of one- and two-family housing against 287 units of multifamily 3. USDA counts the county inside its service area, though its eligibility notice took Princeton Meadows off the list 26.

Newark and Essex County

Newark and Essex County, New Jersey: airport and port works.

Essex County alone. Its metro, New York-Newark-Jersey City, crosses into New York, and no New York county is in these figures.

Essex County, home to New Jersey's Newark, authorized 5,474 housing units in 2025, 2,628 of them multifamily and 2,087 in mixed-use buildings 3. Its population, 862,775 in the April 2020 base, had reached 896,379 by July 2025, up 3.9% 22. In July 2026 New York YIMBY counted about 1,200 residential units across six projects in Downtown, the Ironbound and University Heights 42.

The Port Authority's proposed capital plan for 2026 to 2035, a record $45 billion in its own description, funds a new Terminal B and the AirTrain at Newark Liberty International Airport, where Terminal A, a $2.7 billion project, is open 43. On US Route 1 in Newark, Goodman acquired a 1.7 million square foot property from Anheuser-Busch for $360 million in the first half of 2026, and the Ports submarket of Bayonne, Jersey City and Port Newark stood at 6.6% vacancy in the second quarter 12.

The payroll series BLS publishes here is the Newark Metro Division, which reaches beyond Essex: 1,039.6 thousand in the August 2026 first estimate and 1,036.1 thousand twelve months before, a change BLS flags as not significant 27.

A room night in Newark carries a local hotel tax of 6% beside the reduced State Occupancy Fee, by the Division of Taxation's TB-81R 45. NJDEP issued draft combined sewer overflow permits to Newark, East Newark and the Passaic Valley Sewerage Commission on March 10, 2025, and Newark elected sewer separation 46. In June 2026 SBA opened economic injury loans for businesses hurt by the fire in Belleville, also in Essex County 47. USDA lists no eligible area in the county 39, so a Newark project's federal channels are SBA, HUD and the agencies rather than Rural Development.

Jersey City and Hudson County

Jersey City and Hudson County: connection fees reset in May 2026, and the apartment pipeline the city counts.

Hudson County alone.

Costs and Insurance

New Jersey's cost lines: the homeowner tax rate, the FAIR Plan's commercial cover, room fees by city and the tank fund's wait.

NJBIA, reporting the Tax Foundation in March 2025, ranks New Jersey's effective rate of property tax on owner-occupied housing the highest of any state, at 2.23% on 2023 survey data 82. A second, secondary site states a different rate on 2024 data that could not be checked against the Tax Foundation's own table 83. Neither is a commercial rate, and no published commercial effective rate for the state is on record, so the tax line in a study comes from the parcel's own assessment and its municipality's rate.

The state's FAIR Plan is run by the New Jersey Insurance Underwriting Association, which writes cover for most commercial buildings and business property, but its base policy carries neither theft nor liability coverage 84. The rate filings that drew attention in 2025 were for homeowners lines 85, and none of them prices a commercial building.

The minimum wage for most employers rose to $15.92 an hour on January 1, 2026; seasonal and small employers pay $15.23 and farms $14.20, and the long-term care rate is in the care section 10. Small and seasonal employers reach parity by 2028 and farms by 2030, and the rates are indexed to inflation each year 10, so a five-year pro forma carries a floor that rises.

Room charges depend on the city. The State Occupancy Fee runs at a reduced rate in Atlantic City, Elizabeth, Newark and Jersey City and at a separate rate in Wildwood, North Wildwood and Wildwood Crest 86, and those seven cities may not add the municipal occupancy tax that other towns can adopt by ordinance 87. Where a town has adopted it, the Department of Community Affairs' Local Finance Notice 2026-09 gives its ceiling as 3% and sets out how it reaches short-term rentals 54. The city layers that the Division of Taxation dates are in the Newark, Jersey City and Atlantic City sections.

For a fuel site, the Petroleum Underground Storage Tank Remediation, Upgrade and Closure Fund, run by NJDEP with NJEDA, offers grants and loans, but NJDEP's own fact sheet warns of a long wait for review and points leaking tanks to the Hazardous Discharge Site Remediation Fund 88. The fund's rules carried a chapter expiration date of May 8, 2025 89, and whether they were readopted is not on record here. A lender counting on the fund asks NJDEP where the application stands in its queue.

New Jersey Questions

What lenders ask about New Jersey projects.

How large is New Jersey's nursing and residential care payroll, and what is its wage floor?

Nursing and residential care employed 103,000 people in August 2026 in the Department of Labor and Workforce Development's unadjusted table, 3,000 more than a year before 1. Hospitals and ambulatory health care have their own rows in that table, and all three grew over the year 1; on the seasonally adjusted series the whole education and health services supersector stood at 824,300 1. The wage side comes from a different document. From January 1, 2026 long-term care direct care staff earn at least $18.92 an hour, above the general minimum 10, so a care residence prices its aides from that higher floor and takes its demand from the care row rather than from the hospital count.

When does New Jersey's Department of Health accept an application for new long-term care beds?

Not at any time. The Department takes an application for new general or specialized long-term care beds, on full review, once it has published a call for them in the state's Register, under the rules at N.J.A.C. 8:33H 8. The State Health Planning Board hears the full reviews, and a Deputy Commissioner decides the categories that run on the expedited track 9. A study for new beds therefore names the call it answers, its county and its bed type. Where no call is open, the beds have no route to approval, and the pro forma has nothing to stand on until one is. The same department licenses assisted living residences, but no source on this page gives their bed or unit thresholds 9.

Has New Jersey's 2024 liquor license law put more licenses up for sale?

The record does not show it. P.L. 2023, c. 290, signed in January 2024, obliges an inactive license to be used or sold 16. Two counts exist: NJEDA found 1,379 inactive licenses in the state's system in August 2024 2, and on April 23, 2025 the League of Municipalities reported 1,165 on the quartile list the Division of Alcoholic Beverage Control keeps 16. The lists differ and so do the dates, so the second count is not a fall from the first, and neither carries a price. The quota itself is statutory 14, and a January 2023 report put a permit in a full town at upwards of $1 million 15. A restaurant study prices its license from a signed contract or a dated offer in its own municipality.

What raised New Jersey's residential electric bills from June 1, 2025?

Largely PJM's base residual auction of July 2024, by the Board of Public Utilities' own account. Its order of June 30, 2026 on the Residential Universal Bill Credit puts the projected rise in the average monthly residential bill at 17.23% to 20.20% from June 1, 2025, by utility service territory 17. The same order gives the next step: the February 12, 2026 certification of the basic generation service auction points to -1.8% to +1.6% from June 1, 2026 17. A hotel, cold store or care residence budgets from its own utility's commercial tariff, dates the quote, and treats a budget written before June 2025 as priced before the increase.

Which health systems lead Monmouth County's 2025 employer list?

Hackensack Meridian Health, first with 12,794 employees, and CentraState Healthcare, third with 2,646, on the county Division of Economic Development's ranked 2025 list of its ten largest non-government employers 4. Saker ShopRites sits second with 10,300. Four of the seven places this page prints are health care: Visiting Nurse Association Health Group, with 2,217, and RWJB Monmouth Medical Center, with 1,920, are the other two 4. The order is not strictly by headcount: Commvault is fourth with 2,882 staff, more than CentraState above it, so a study quotes the number beside the rank 4. Ocean County has no such list here; for a care home there, the register's count of residents aged 65 and over is the first demand line.

Where can a New Jersey project use USDA Rural Development?

Only in the areas USDA lists. Its eligibility packet, revised in December 2019, names whole towns in Atlantic County and most of Cape May County, nine townships in Burlington County and parts of Gloucester County, and lists no eligible area in Hudson, Essex, Bergen or Union counties 39. A later notice moved some places off the list, Ocean City and Long Beach Island among them, and added parts of Bayville 26. The state office is in Mount Laurel 80.

What changed for New Jersey data center projects on August 27, 2026?

Two things, on one day. The guardrails of the statewide data center plan announced on May 27, 2026 came fully into force, labor standards that include prevailing wage guidance among them, as NJEDA's municipal resource hub sets them out 19. The same day P.L. 2026, c. 77 cut the authorization for the Next New Jersey AI tax credit from $500 million to $250 million and shut it to new applicants 20. A hotel, a warehouse or a clinic meets the guardrails only if it seeks an NJEDA incentive; the program's manufacturing arm had made its first award on June 18, 2026, in Warren Township 21.
