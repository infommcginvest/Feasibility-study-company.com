Hotels

Seattle's hotel year: the July 2025 peak beside July 2019.

Seattle is the one Washington market with a published hotel record. Visit Seattle's summer report, built on STR data, puts citywide occupancy at 89.2% in July 2025, the year's peak month, against 89.4% in July 2019, and occupancy in downtown hotels at 85.8% in August 2025 1. Those are summer months, not annual figures, and a hotel study that leans on them says so. The downtown reading is for August and the citywide peak for July, so the pair is not a comparison of downtown against the city in one month.

Kidder Mathews' first-quarter 2025 hotel report gives occupancy of 70.3% over the twelve months to March 2025, an average daily rate of $182 for the quarter and revenue per available room of $128 5. The Puget Sound Business Journal, as the Downtown Seattle Association republished it on May 4, 2025, put the March 2025 average daily rate at $196.15, 1.4% lower than a year earlier, in a report on softer demand 6. Kidder's rate is for a quarter and the Journal's for a single month, and they come from different publishers, so they are given side by side rather than read as one series.

On supply, CBRE's forecast of May 29, 2025, published under a headline about business travel and limited supply growth, expected Seattle's hotel room supply to grow 0.6% in 2025 9. No list of named hotel openings or of rooms under construction was found.

Housing

King County's permits for 2024 and 2025, its average rents, and apartment vacancy in the metro.

King County permitted 8,505 residential units in 2025, 2,510 units of them single-family and 5,995 units multifamily, after 10,564 units in 2024, a fall of 19% in WCRER's annual count of the Census Bureau's permit survey published in May 2026 2. Multifamily units outnumbered single-family units in King County's 2025 permits, 5,995 units against 2,510 units 2. The 2025 row for Pierce County was not extracted from the same report.

WCRER's fourth-quarter 2025 apartment report puts King County's average rent for all unit types at $2,095, after $2,126 in the third quarter, and apartment vacancy across the Puget Sound region at 6.0% 3. Kidder Mathews' second-quarter 2026 reading of Seattle metro multifamily vacancy is 6.7%; its first-quarter reading and the one a year earlier were both 7.0% 7. WCRER's region and quarter differ from Kidder's, so the two vacancy figures are each given with their own area and date.

The same Kidder report names the quarter's completions: The Elowen, 324 units, finished in May 2026, and Kendrick Landing in Pacific, 245 units, delivered in April 2026 7. It also records the sale of Elara at the Market in Belltown, 150 units bought by Seattle Social Housing for $60,900,000, or $406,000 a unit 7. One sale prices one building; it is a comparable for a study of a similar property, not a reading of the market. A count of apartment units under construction was not found, so an apartment study builds its own list of projects under way near the subject.

Across the three counties of the Census Bureau's Seattle-Tacoma-Bellevue metro, King, Pierce and Snohomish, the 2020 to 2024 American Community Survey finds 1,608,596 households, of which 643,789 are renter-occupied, and a median owner-occupied home value of $717,500 10.

Other Supply

Storage, retail, senior housing, RV parks and fuel in King and Pierce counties: the sources the record points to.

Outside hotels, apartments and industrial space, the Seattle record holds names of sources rather than figures. For self-storage space per resident and the storage pipeline, the nearest source the research names is Yardi Matrix, a subscription service. For RV parks and campgrounds and for gas stations and convenience stores, it points to the Census Bureau's County Business Patterns under NAICS 721211 and 457110. No retail vacancy for Seattle, King County or Pierce County was found. A storage, retail, RV or fuel study in either county therefore brings its own dated count of competitors, and the lender reads it as the study's own census rather than a published market figure.

Population

King and Pierce counties in OFM's April estimates for 2020, 2025 and 2026.

OFM's April 1 estimates, published on June 29, 2026, count 2,269,675 residents in King County in 2020, 2,411,700 in 2025 and 2,424,700 in 2026, and 920,393 in Pierce County in 2020, 959,900 in 2025 and 967,000 in 2026 11. They are the base a demand model for either county starts from, each on its own April date, and they are OFM's figures, not the Census Bureau's July estimates, which run on a different date and method.

The research gives no sourced population for the City of Seattle alone, so a trade area inside the city is built from census tracts rather than from a city total. Snohomish County, the metro's third county, is read on the Washington page's Everett section with its own OFM and permit figures.

For senior housing, the 2020 to 2024 survey counts 583,198 residents aged 65 and over across King, Pierce and Snohomish counties 10. No senior housing occupancy reading was found for any of the three; the research names NIC MAP, a subscription service, as the nearest source, and a study that needs occupancy buys it.

Jobs and Industry

King County payrolls in August 2026, and the industrial projects brokers name in Southend and SoDo.

ESD's King County profile counts 1,480,300 nonfarm jobs for August 2026, up 6,000, or 0.4%, from August 2025, with county unemployment at 4.7% 12. The Spokane page carries the same ESD profile series for the Spokane metro, for July 2026, so the two markets can be read on one publisher's method, a month apart.

No primary list ranks King County's five largest employers by 2025 headcount, and the research did not retrieve workforce figures for Joint Base Lewis-McChord or the University of Washington. The statewide employment counts for Amazon, Boeing and Microsoft are on the Washington page.

Kidder Mathews' second-quarter 2026 industrial report reads Seattle-area vacancy at 9.5%, up from 8.9% at the end of 2025 13. Its pipeline table names the Port of Seattle's Des Moines Creek West project in Southend, 405,680 square feet due in the third quarter of 2026, and an Amtrak maintenance facility in SoDo of 100,000 square feet due at the end of 2026, whose cost was not published 13. Southend and the close-in SoDo district are the industrial nodes the record names, with Pacific for apartments; no retail, hospitality or storage corridor and no WSDOT traffic count for a Seattle road was found.

In the 2020 to 2024 survey, median household income across King, Pierce and Snohomish counties is $115,177 and income per person $63,994 10.

Lenders

SBA lending for King County projects in fiscal 2024 and 2025, and across the three-county metro since 2016.

SBA's 7(a) and 504 programs approved 771 loans for $403.5 million for King County projects in fiscal 2025, up from 657 loans for $380.9 million in fiscal 2024, by 425 Business's report of the Seattle District's release on November 20, 2025 4. The district's office in Seattle serves the county; the district's own total also takes in counties outside King and outside Washington, as the Washington page sets out.

Over a longer run and a wider area, SBA's loan-level FOIA files show projects in King, Pierce and Snohomish counties drawing 7,878 loans under 7(a) in fiscal years 2016 to 2025, worth $4,465.0 million, and 570 loans under 504, whose debentures came to $655.9 million; loans later cancelled are not in those counts 14. The 425 Business figures are King County's alone for two fiscal years; the register's are the three counties' for ten.

No ranking of the most active SBA lenders or development companies in King or Pierce County was found; the statewide 7(a) lender table for calendar 2025 is on the Washington page. The rule that lets SBA ask for a study is 13 CFR 120.160(b): SBA may require professional appraisals, a survey, or a feasibility study 15; the 7(a) and 504 tests themselves are on the SBA lending page. Opportunity Zone, HUBZone and USDA rural status were not settled for any part of the two counties, so each is read for the parcel on the CDFI Fund's list, SBA's HUBZone map and USDA's eligibility map.

Local Rules

Seattle's data center moratorium and lodging tax, and the local rules the record does not hold.

Cascade PBS reported in August 2026 that, along with Seattle, ten other cities or counties had passed moratoriums on building data centers 8. The moratorium's terms and its end date were not in the research.

Seattle's lodging taxes differ from the rest of the state's. HB 2559's bill report for the 2026 session allows a special lodging tax of up to 4% in Seattle, against up to 2% elsewhere, and caps the combined lodging rate at 12% outside Seattle only 16. The research found two combined Seattle rates, in the Department of Revenue's older tax reference 17 and in a 2026 secondary source, and they do not agree.

Impact fees, zoning changes in 2025 and 2026, a short-term rental ordinance and news of water or sewer capacity limits were not found for Seattle or for King or Pierce County. The state's short-term rental law and Certificate of Need list, which do apply here, are on the Washington page.

Storms and Insurance

King and Pierce counties in the December 2025 storm requests, and what the record says about cover.

Both counties of this page are in the record of the December 2025 atmospheric-river storms. FEMA declared emergency EM-3629 for Washington on December 12, 2025 18, and the Governor's Individual Assistance request of January 21, 2026 named King and Pierce among the ten counties it covered, with Snohomish 19. On April 11, 2026 a major disaster was declared for the storms of December 5 to 19 20.

The record holds no county-level count of insurer non-renewals or of commercial premium changes for King or Pierce County; the statewide homeowner count and the FAIR Plan's terms are on the Washington page. A commercial file here prices property cover from a quote for the building itself.

Worked Cases

The Washington coffee shop case, and a marina case set in Gig Harbor.

The Washington coffee shop case is an SBA 7(a) study of a planned specialty coffee store with two drive-thru lanes, which its page places in a fast-growing suburban commuter trade area of a major Washington metro; the page sets a Puget Sound commuter corridor apart from Spokane and sends the credit through SBA's Seattle District Office. It does not name a county, so none of this page's King or Pierce figures is applied to it here. The Gig Harbor marina case works a marina under SBA 504, and no figure on this page is a Gig Harbor figure. The Washington page lists both cases with the rest of the state's record.

Verify

Checks a King or Pierce County file needs before credit.

  • Hotel demand by month. The subject's own STR set for the months its demand depends on, read against the summer figures above.
  • Hotel pipeline. Competing rooms under construction or planned near the subject, property by property.
  • Seattle lodging rate. The combined rate the Department of Revenue gives for the filing date.
  • Which vacancy reading. For an apartment file, the reading the study relies on, Kidder's metro quarter or WCRER's Puget Sound quarter, named with its date.
  • Employer headcount. A dated local count for any one employer a project's demand rests on.
  • Pierce County permits. For a Pierce County site, the county's 2025 permit count from WCRER.
  • Data center rules. The terms and end date of Seattle's moratorium, for any project with a heavy power load.
  • Storm history. Whether the parcel was damaged in the December 2025 storms, and what the insurer's quote says about it.
  • Industrial deliveries. The delivery dates of Des Moines Creek West and the SoDo Amtrak facility, for a warehouse or flex file that competes with them.
Seattle Questions

Seattle feasibility study questions.

What were Seattle hotel rates in early 2025?

Kidder Mathews put the average daily rate at $182 for the first quarter of 2025, with revenue per available room of $128 and twelve-month occupancy to March of 70.3% 5. The Puget Sound Business Journal put the March 2025 rate at $196.15, 1.4% below a year earlier 6.

Does King County permit more apartments than houses?

In 2025, yes: 5,995 of the 8,505 units WCRER counts were multifamily and 2,510 single-family, though the total fell from 10,564 in 2024 2. Pierce County's 2025 row was not extracted from the same report, so a Pierce County file takes it from WCRER directly.

What does an apartment in King County rent for?

WCRER's all-unit King County average was $2,095 for the closing quarter of 2025, after $2,126 a quarter earlier 3. Kidder Mathews put Seattle metro multifamily vacancy at 6.7% in the second quarter of 2026 7.

Which SBA figures on this page are King County's alone?

The 771 loans worth $403.5 million approved under 7(a) and 504 for King County projects in fiscal 2025, and the 657 loans worth $380.9 million in fiscal 2024, both from the district release 425 Business reported on November 20, 2025 4. The ten-year counts for King, Pierce and Snohomish together come from SBA's FOIA files, and the Seattle District's own total reaches beyond King County and beyond the state line.

Has Seattle restricted new data centers?

Yes. Cascade PBS's August 2026 reporting lists Seattle among the cities and counties with a moratorium on building data centers, ten others besides it 8.

What are OFM's April 2026 counts for King and Pierce counties?

2,424,700 residents in King County and 967,000 in Pierce County on April 1, 2026, from 2,411,700 and 959,900 a year earlier, in the estimates OFM published on June 29, 2026 11. They are OFM's April figures; the Census Bureau's July estimates run on another date and method.

Two industrial projects in Southend and SoDo are due in the second half of 2026.

A warehouse or flex study in King County sets its lease-up against those two deliveries, Des Moines Creek West at the Port of Seattle and the Amtrak facility, and against Kidder Mathews' vacancy for the quarter it is written in.
