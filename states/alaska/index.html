Oil and the Treasury

North Slope barrels, the Permanent Fund draw and the state forecast an Alaska project inherits.

The studies Feasibility Study Company prepares in Alaska are independent and lender-grade, written for projects that an EB-5 offering, a bank, an SBA 7(a) or 504 lender or a USDA Rural Development program is asked to finance, and each is prepared for the owner or developer who proposes the project and for the lender reviewing the request. An Alaska feasibility study consultant starts with the Department of Revenue's oil forecast. North Slope crude and the Permanent Fund feed the state's unrestricted general fund, and a hotel, clinic, apartment building or supply yard whose customers draw public paychecks or work on state contracts depends on that fund whether or not it ever sells to the oil industry.

The Spring 2026 Revenue Forecast, under a cover letter dated March 13, 2026, puts the average Alaska North Slope price at $75.26 per barrel for fiscal 2026 and production at an average of 459.2 thousand barrels per day for fiscal 2026 and 518.5 thousand barrels per day for fiscal 2027 1. New production is starting: Santos announced first oil from Pikka on May 17, 2026, as Alaska Business reported 2. The Energy Information Administration, working on a different basis in November 2025, forecast Alaska crude production of 477,000 barrels per day in calendar 2026, the most since 2018 14. The state figure is a fiscal-year forecast and EIA's a calendar-year one, published in different seasons; a study quotes each with its own basis and date and does not merge them.

The same forecast carries the treasury. Unrestricted general fund revenue is forecast at 6,491.9 million dollars for fiscal 2026 and 6,725.3 million dollars for fiscal 2027, and the transfer from the Permanent Fund into the general fund, the percent-of-market-value draw, at 3,798.9 million dollars for fiscal 2026 1. The Fund itself was valued at $87.6 billion on January 31, 2026 1. Mining adds its own line, with unrestricted mining revenue forecast at 102.0 million dollars for fiscal 2027 1.

Alaska North Slope crude averaged $69.48 per barrel in February 2026, as Alaska Public Media reported in March 2026 from the Department of Revenue, in an article on forecasters' expectation that disruption from the war with Iran would linger for months 15. A forecast written in one season can be overtaken in the next, and the department publishes a new one each spring and fall, so a study that leans on state spending names the edition it used and the price assumption inside it.

The practical consequence for a borrower is narrow and specific. A Juneau apartment project leased to public employees, an Anchorage hotel that books state and industry travel, or a Kenai Peninsula fabrication shop serving North Slope contractors carries some exposure to this forecast. The study identifies which line of the forecast the project's demand rests on, prints it with its date, and tests the project at a weaker reading of that line.

Payrolls and Output

Two September 2026 job counts, a quarter of openings and closings, and output in current and chained dollars.

BLS counted 340.7 thousand seasonally adjusted nonfarm jobs in Alaska in September 2026, preliminary, and printed a change of +1.2% over twelve months 16. The Department of Labor and Workforce Development's own release for the same month reports jobs up 0.1% from September 2025 17. The two are different series from different publishers, each with its own basis, and a study that needs a statewide job count picks one, names it and does not average or reconcile them.

The department's Alaska Economic Trends for August 2026 carries an article on the federal jobs Alaska lost in 2025 18.

Business Employment Dynamics adds the churn behind the totals. From September to December 2025, Alaska's private sector recorded 30,293 gross job gains at opening and expanding establishments and 23,914 gross job losses at closing and contracting ones, seasonally adjusted, for a net change of +6,379 19.

Output is reported in two measures. In BEA's annual figures, as FRED carries them, Alaska's all-industry GDP was 73,634.8 million dollars in 2025 in current dollars 20 and 56,335.0 million chained 2017 dollars in real terms 21. The quarterly real series, at a seasonally adjusted annual rate, reads 56,342.4 million chained 2017 dollars for the second quarter of 2025 and 56,159.6 million chained 2017 dollars for the second quarter of 2026 22. A study prints both quarters as BEA published them and states which one its argument uses.

People

The state demographer's July 1, 2025 estimate, and the Census Bureau's separate one.

The state demographer estimated 738,737 Alaska residents on July 1, 2025, up +1,649 (+0.2%) from a year earlier 4. Births minus deaths added +3,389 and net migration ran the other way: 1,740 more people left Alaska than moved in 4. The base for that change was the department's revised estimate for July 1, 2024, 737,088, as Alaska Public Media reported in relaying the Alaska Beacon 23.

The Census Bureau publishes its own estimate. Its resident population for Alaska in 2025, as FRED carries it, is 737.270 thousand persons 25. The Census and state figures are separate estimates, and this page uses the state demographer's for the statewide count and the boroughs because the borough table read here is the department's. A study states which estimate it uses and keeps to it.

For demand, the statewide gain says less than its location does. The department's 2025 estimates are published by borough and census area 26, and the largest gain went to one borough, the Matanuska-Susitna, whose figure is printed in its section below. A housing, retail or child care study outside the Mat-Su therefore starts from its own borough's estimate and change, and it does not borrow growth from the state total. Natural increase matters for one product in particular: births drive demand for child care and pediatric care before they drive demand for housing.

Air, Sea and Visitors

Record months and seasons at the airports and cruise docks, and the statewide series that do not exist.

Nearly 460,000 passengers passed through the Anchorage and Fairbanks airports combined in July 2026, the busiest month on record, as Alaska Public Media reported from the Alaska International Airport System 12. The Anchorage Economic Development Corporation's 2026 forecast, as Alaska Business reported it, counted 3.82 million tonnes of cargo at Ted Stevens Anchorage International Airport in 2025, a record, and record cargo at the Don Young Port of Alaska in the same year 3.

Cruise traffic sets the summer for Southcentral and Southeast Alaska. Visit Anchorage gives cruise passenger capacity in Southcentral of 492,000 in 2025 and 526,000 projected for 2026 27, a regional figure rather than Anchorage's alone. Juneau's counts are in the Juneau section. The state's Spring 2026 forecast also assumed a record cruise year for 2026 among its assumptions 1.

The statewide supply series a lender usually expects are thin. Statewide figures for hotel occupancy, room rates and RevPAR were not found in a dated release. No broker report on office, industrial, retail or apartment space was found for any Alaska market. The department's rental survey has no statewide row, and the page that carries its area rows prints no survey date, so its figures are not used on this page. The department's statewide housing tables, including sales prices, new units and foreclosures, were read from pages that carry no publication date and are not printed either.

What that leaves a study to do is plain. Lodging performance comes from a paid report or from the operator's own records; apartment rents and vacancy from the dated edition of the rental survey or a rent roll; and commercial rents from leases and broker opinions gathered for the subject. The airport and cruise records above set the season and the visitor count, and the study's own survey sets the price.

Hazards

Typhoon Halong's remnants brought two federal declarations, and fire seasons brought management grants in 2025 and 2026.

Alaska's recent federal disaster record opens on the western coast. FEMA declared DR-4893 on October 22, 2025 for severe storms, flooding and the remnants of Typhoon Halong between October 8 and October 13, 2025, and declared DR-4912 on May 29, 2026 for the same storms 10. A winter storm from December 6 to December 9, 2025 led to DR-4904 on April 7, 2026 10. After DR-4893 the Department of Labor and Workforce Development expanded Disaster Unemployment Assistance for qualified individuals in Western Alaska, in a release of February 12, 2026 11.

Wildfire is the other recurring entry. FEMA approved fire management assistance for the Bear Creek and Himalaya Road fires in June 2025, for the Nenana Ridge Complex in July 2025 and for the Mukluk Fire on August 18, 2026 10. Three major disaster declarations issued in January 2025 covered storms and flooding from August and October 2024 10.

Energy is a supply question in Southcentral and the Interior, not only a price. ENSTAR, the gas utility, has said it could fall short of gas this winter, and in October 2026 the state favored ENSTAR's proposal in a contest between two plans to store gas in the Kenai Loop field 29. Insurance after the 2025 storms began with the regulator. Rate filings and earthquake coverage take-up with a recent date were not found, so a study carries a quoted premium for the subject rather than a market rate.

No construction cost index for Anchorage, Fairbanks or any other Alaska market was found from Rider Levett Bucknall, Cumming or Turner, and no wage determination was read. A budget is therefore supported by a contractor's bid or an independent cost estimate, with freight and the building season stated as their own lines.

Rules by Asset Class

Boroughs, a municipality and city-boroughs write most of the rules; the 2026 changes a study cites by name.

Alaska governs land through boroughs, a consolidated municipality in Anchorage and city-boroughs such as Juneau, and the state publishes its local statistics by borough and census area 26. Where no borough government exists, the census area is a statistical unit rather than a government, so the study establishes whether a city or the state holds land-use authority over the parcel.

Multifamily and housing

In Anchorage the mayor's housing strategy has been followed by residential permitting that outpaces recent years, and the Municipality says it has shortened both its residential and its commercial zoning reviews, in a release of July 22, 2026 32. The Fairbanks North Star Borough approved a property tax exemption for qualifying multifamily developments in early 2026, open to applications through 2028, as KUAC reported 33. An apartment pro forma in either place states whether the project uses the faster review or the exemption, and the tax line follows from that answer.

Hotels, tours and visitor retail

Anchorage collects a hotel bed tax and car and RV rental taxes, whose 2025 collections are printed in the Anchorage section 27. In Juneau the 2026 season was the first under voluntary daily cruise passenger limits 8, which change the arithmetic of a shop or tour business from a seasonal total to a capped day. A hotel or retail study in either city states the tax or the limit in force on the date of the study.

Data centers, power and industrial land

Alaska Public Media reported in October 2026 that data centers have been proposed in Alaska, with a North Slope data center and power plant drawing public comment in July 2026, and that Interior Secretary Burgum spoke for them in Fairbanks 30. Near Houston in the Mat-Su, residents worried that a proposed transfer of state land could open the door to data centers 34. A proposal tied to the Alaska LNG project would reduce the oil and gas property tax; Anchorage Daily News reported it in January 2026 as a proposal, not law 35. None of these is a rule a study can cite as settled; each is a pending decision the study names with its date.

Senior housing and care

Touchmark broke ground in late September 2026 on a retirement campus near O'Malley Road and Lake Otis Parkway in Anchorage, with condominiums, villas, assisted living and memory care, and completion expected in 2029, as Alaska Business reported 36. That is one property's schedule, not a market count; licensed bed counts for senior housing in Alaska were not found, and a study sources them from the state licensing records for the borough.

Anchorage

The Municipality of Anchorage: record air cargo, payroll counts from two BLS programs and a mayor's housing strategy.

The state demographer's estimate for the Municipality of Anchorage was 289,221 residents on July 1, 2025 26. Two BLS programs measure its jobs, and they answer different questions. The monthly survey for the Anchorage metro area, a wider area than the Municipality, counted 183.4 thousand nonfarm jobs, not seasonally adjusted, and a civilian labor force of 200.5 thousand in September 2026, both preliminary 41. The quarterly county release counts 145,080 jobs in the Municipality itself in March 2026 and 8,754 establishments 5. A study uses the metro count for a regional demand argument and the Municipality's for a payroll or wage input, and it labels each.

Freight and travel are Anchorage's distinct records. AEDC's 2026 forecast gave Ted Stevens Anchorage International Airport a record 3.82 million tonnes of cargo in 2025 3, and BLS's occupational survey counts 17,980 transportation and material moving jobs in the Anchorage metro area in May 2025, with a mean hourly wage across all occupations of $36.14 13. A warehouse, logistics or airport-hotel study cites the cargo record for demand and the occupational count for the labor it will hire.

Visitors leave a dated record in the Municipality's taxes. Visit Anchorage reports more than $57.5 million in municipal tax collections from travel and tourism in 2025, including the hotel bed tax and car and RV rental taxes, and cruise passenger capacity in Southcentral of 492,000 in 2025 with 526,000 projected for 2026 27. Hotel occupancy and average rate for 2025 or 2026 were not found; Visit Anchorage's annual report page prints neither. New rooms are coming in: in 2026 the Aspen Suites Hotel Anchorage Downtown opened, a dual-branded Candlewood Suites and Holiday Inn Express is open, and a Courtyard by Marriott is expected, as Visit Anchorage's 2026 roundup lists them 42. A hotel study counts those openings in its competitive set by name and sources their performance from a paid report or the operators.

The local economy carries mixed signals in 2026. AEDC's report of August 5, 2026, as the Anchorage Daily News covered it, put consumer confidence at 40.5 out of 100 in July 2026 and the average Anchorage home price at $481,000, and named military investment at Joint Base Elmendorf-Richardson as an opportunity alongside the headwinds 37. The Municipality's housing strategy, with faster zoning reviews and permitting ahead of recent years, and its estimate of the homes the military community at JBER will need, are in the July 22, 2026 release 32. An apartment or for-sale housing study in Anchorage sets the subject's price against the AEDC figure and its absorption against the permit record and the JBER schedule, each with its date.

Office, industrial and retail vacancy, rent and cap rates for 2025 or 2026 from a broker were not found for Anchorage, nor were Alaska DOT traffic counts or a short-term rental ordinance. A commercial study gathers lease comparables for the subject and cites the brokers by name and quarter.

Mat-Su

The Matanuska-Susitna Borough: the largest population gain in Alaska, a rail grant for Port MacKenzie and requests for larger spaces.

The Matanuska-Susitna Borough, home to Wasilla and Palmer, had the largest population gain among Alaska's boroughs and census areas from 2024 to 2025, +1,696 residents 4. BLS's quarterly county release, which spells the borough "Mantanuska-Susitna", counts 29,137 jobs in March 2026, an average weekly wage of $1,154 in the first quarter of 2026 and 2,962 establishments 5. AEDC's August 2026 report put the Mat-Su's average home price at about $474,200, as the Anchorage Daily News reported it 37.

Those figures frame the borough's case: the Mat-Su's average weekly wage of $1,154 5, and AEDC's average home price of about $474,200 beside Anchorage's $481,000 37. A study for housing, child care, a grocery or a clinic in Wasilla or Palmer builds demand from the borough's population gain, and if its customers earn their pay in Anchorage it documents that with its own data rather than assuming it. The Mat-Su wage of $1,154 applies to jobs located in the borough, not to every household living there.

Commercial space is the gap. A Wasilla broker told Alaska Business in April 2026 that small offices, mixed shop and office space and large leases were all in demand, and the article printed no vacancy or lease-rate figures 43. Building permit counts for 2025 or 2026 were not found, and neither were hotel performance figures or traffic counts. A retail or flex-industrial study in the Mat-Su builds its supply survey from the ground up and dates each listing it uses.

Two decisions in October 2026 bear on industrial land. A federal grant announced on October 19, 2026 revives the stalled rail link from Port MacKenzie to the Alaska Railroad near Houston, with completion no earlier than 2029, as Alaska Public Media reported 40. Near the same town, residents worried that a proposed transfer of state land could open the door to data centers 34. A port-related or industrial project in the western Mat-Su states both as pending, with dates, and does not count the rail link as built.

Fairbanks

Fairbanks North Star Borough: the Interior's payroll, a military housing need and a power grid under strain in October 2026.

The Fairbanks North Star Borough held 97,083 residents on July 1, 2025 in the state demographer's estimate 26. BLS counts 38.2 thousand nonfarm jobs in the Fairbanks-College metro area in September 2026, preliminary 44, and its occupational survey a mean hourly wage across all occupations of $35.32 in May 2025 28. The quarterly county release counts 35,985 jobs in the borough in March 2026 and 2,497 establishments in that first quarter 5.

The military and the university shape housing demand. KUAC reported in April 2026 that more service members and their families are expected to arrive over the next couple of years, and that the borough approved a multifamily property tax exemption early in 2026, with applications open through 2028 33. The same report argued that a high vacancy reading in Fairbanks does not mean renting there is easy 33. In June 2026 the University of Alaska Fairbanks agreed to house Eielson Air Force Base personnel in Stevens Hall and Nerland Hall, for one year and renewable 45. An apartment study in Fairbanks segments its supply by quality and age before it reads vacancy, and it dates the military arrivals it relies on to the published schedule.

Power is the Interior's newest constraint. Golden Valley Electric Association issued its first-ever energy emergency alert in October 2026, and Interior Secretary Burgum spoke in Fairbanks in favor of data centers, both in an Alaska Public Media report of October 15, 2026 30. A large-load project, whether a data center, a cold-storage building or a processing plant, needs the utility's written answer on capacity before its schedule is credible.

Broker reports by asset class, hotel occupancy and room tax receipts, building permit counts for the city or borough and current F-35 personnel figures at Eielson were not found with a date. Wildfire is a site question as well: the fire management declarations of 2025 and 2026 are listed in the hazards section, and a study checks the state's fire records for the parcel itself.

Juneau

The City and Borough of Juneau: a capital's payroll and a cruise record set under the first daily limits.

The City and Borough of Juneau held 31,182 residents on July 1, 2025 26. The quarterly county release counts 16,413 jobs in March 2026, an average weekly wage of $1,449 in the first quarter of 2026 and 1,185 establishments 5. Juneau is the state capital, so the state forecast in the first section reaches its payroll directly.

Summer runs on ships. Large-ship cruise passengers in Juneau reached 1,733,016 in the 2026 season, which ended on October 6, a fourth consecutive record, as CruiseMapper reported from Juneau's tourism manager 6. The previous record, 1,688,738 in 2025, comes from the City and Borough's Docks and Harbors as the Juneau Independent reported it 7. The 2026 season was the first under voluntary daily passenger limits, with a lower cap on Saturdays, as KTOO reported when the first ship arrived on April 27, 2026 8.

For a retail shop, tour operator, restaurant or hotel in downtown Juneau, the limits change how demand is modeled. The season total is a record, but the busiest days are now capped, so a study builds its revenue from the number of ship days, the capped daily volume and the capture rate the business can reach on those days, and it uses the season total only as a check.

Several standard series are missing for Juneau. The department's labor force page lists the area without an unemployment rate, and building permits, hotel occupancy, airport passenger statistics and the sales tax rate, with the date it took effect, were not found. A Juneau study gathers those from the City and Borough and from the operator, and it states each source's date.

Kenai Peninsula

Kenai Peninsula Borough: a payroll and wage from the county release, and the gas storage choice at Kenai Loop.

The Kenai Peninsula Borough held 61,327 residents on July 1, 2025 26. The quarterly county release puts the borough's March 2026 employment at 20,214 and its first-quarter 2026 average weekly wage at $1,297 5. The Peninsula Clarion's January 2025 report of the previous year's estimates was headlined on the borough's continued growth 24, and the 2025 estimate above is the current one.

Energy decisions land on the peninsula. In October 2026 the state favored ENSTAR in a contest between two proposals to store natural gas in the Kenai Loop field, as Alaska Public Media reported, and ENSTAR has said it could be short of gas this winter 29. Alaska Questions

Questions an Alaska project raises before its lender reads the study.

Why does a study for a project in Anchorage, Juneau or Fairbanks read the state oil forecast?

Because oil and the Permanent Fund pay for the state's general fund, and state spending reaches payrolls and contracts. The Department of Revenue's Spring 2026 forecast puts unrestricted general fund revenue at 6,491.9 million dollars for fiscal 2026 and 6,725.3 million dollars for fiscal 2027, with a transfer of 3,798.9 million dollars from the Permanent Fund in fiscal 2026 1.

What should a first message to Feasibility Study Company about an Alaska project say?

What the project is, which borough or census area holds the site, and where the capital is expected from: a bank, EB-5 investors, USDA Rural Development or an SBA 7(a) or 504 lender. The studies are independent and lender-grade for each of those programs, and the reply covers the analytical framework, the deliverable the chosen program looks for and what follows. Naming the borough matters in Alaska because the wage record is kept at its lines: BLS's quarterly release gives the Matanuska-Susitna Borough an average weekly wage of $1,154 in the first quarter of 2026 and the City and Borough of Juneau $1,449 5.

What cruise count does a market study for a Juneau shop, tour or hotel start from?

1,733,016 large-ship passengers in the season that ended on October 6, 2026, a fourth consecutive record, as CruiseMapper reported from Juneau's tourism manager 6. The 2025 record of 1,688,738 comes from the City and Borough's Docks and Harbors as the Juneau Independent reported it 7. 2026 was also the first season of voluntary daily passenger limits 8, so a shop, tour or lodging study in Juneau models the busiest days, not only the season total.

Did births or migration move Alaska's 2025 population estimate?

Births. The Department of Labor and Workforce Development's estimate for July 1, 2025 is 738,737 residents, up +1,649 (+0.2%) over the year, with births minus deaths at +3,389 while 1,740 more people left Alaska than moved in 4. Among boroughs and census areas the largest gain, +1,696, was the Matanuska-Susitna Borough's 4. A child care or pediatric project reads the births line; a housing study reads its own borough's estimate rather than the state total.

How much SSBCI money is allocated in Alaska, and to which programs?

Two allocations on the Alaska Small Business Development Center's page: $54M for the state's loan guarantee program, part of a 10-year program, and $83.1M for the Alaska SSBCI Tribal Consortium, whose program began on August 1, 2024 9.

What did Typhoon Halong's remnants leave in FEMA's record for western Alaska?

Two major disaster declarations for one incident period: DR-4893, declared on October 22, 2025, and DR-4912, declared on May 29, 2026, both for the severe storms, flooding and remnants of Typhoon Halong of October 8 to October 13, 2025 10. A December 2025 winter storm brought DR-4904 on April 7, 2026 10, and after DR-4893 the Department of Labor and Workforce Development expanded Disaster Unemployment Assistance in Western Alaska 11. A coastal or river site there documents whether the Halong storms reached it rather than assuming an answer.

Which airport records does an SBA feasibility study for an Anchorage hotel or warehouse cite?

The passenger month and the cargo year. Nearly 460,000 passengers passed through the Anchorage and Fairbanks airports combined in July 2026, their busiest month on record, as Alaska Public Media reported from the Alaska International Airport System 12. Cargo is Anchorage's own record: AEDC's 2026 forecast counted 3.82 million tonnes at Ted Stevens Anchorage International Airport in 2025 3, and BLS's occupational survey gives the Anchorage metro area 17,980 transportation and material moving jobs in May 2025 13. A hotel study reads the passenger month; a warehouse or logistics study reads the cargo record and the occupational count.
