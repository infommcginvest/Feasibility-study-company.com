Michigan Feasibility Studies

Michigan gives a feasibility study little to borrow from the state line: the +27,922 residents it gained in the year to July 2025 were fewer than the +30,706 who arrived from abroad on net 1, and its payroll gain came with losses in professional and business services, construction and trade 3. Demand is therefore sized on the subject county's own series first, and an employer's announced hiring enters only as a separate, dated case that is rerun whenever the figure is amended. Michigan's licensing and wage rules are then priced into the file, by license band and by county, before the market work is read.

The Michigan Thesis

A state that barely grows, carried by arrivals from abroad and by a few large plants.

Michigan's job gain was small and unevenly shared. Across the twelve months to August 2026 the state added +8,000 nonfarm jobs; among the sectors its release itemizes, government grew by +15,600 and private education and health services by +7,800 3, while professional and business services lost -6,400, construction -4,000 and trade, transportation and utilities -3,800 3. Other sectors are not itemized. August itself brought a loss of -2,200, the first monthly decline after five months of gains, leaving 4,496,100 payroll jobs, seasonally adjusted 3. A revenue line tied to one sector's hiring needs that sector's record in the subject county, not the statewide total.

Population growth is just as narrow. Michigan's 10,127,884 residents on July 1, 2025 were +27,922 more than a year before 1. Net international migration on its own, +30,706, exceeded that gain; the domestic balance of +1,796 was the first positive one since at least 1990, and deaths have exceeded births for five years running 12.

The roles were reversed a year earlier: on the revised count for the twelve months to June 2024, international migration brought 50,486 people while the domestic balance was -8,065 1. Each vintage revises the one before, so a Michigan population line is only as reliable as the vintage date printed beside it, and a project that needs newcomers, not the households already in place, leans on the most changeable part of the count.

The rest of Michigan's growth is lumpy: a battery plant in Delta Township, a data center campus in Saline Township, casting and machinery investment around Saginaw, each a construction schedule and a hiring promise as much as a demand driver.

Reading the Michigan evidence

  • Where Michigan sources disagree (the payroll and household surveys, the reported Stargate costs, the Delta Township headcounts), this page prints at least two readings and names the conflict instead of choosing one.
  • The six Detroit counties are written up separately on the Detroit market page; Grand Rapids, Ann Arbor, Lansing, Kalamazoo, Flint and Saginaw, Traverse City and the Upper Peninsula are read in sections further down this one.

Statewide, deaths outnumbered births from July 2024 to June 2025 1. The research holds neither series by county, so a childcare or school study should take births for its own trade area from county records before it projects enrollment from total population.

Payrolls, which count positions at the employer's address, rose over the year 3, yet the Local Area Unemployment Statistics program, which counts employed people at their homes, shows the typical region losing ground: the median twelve-month change across the state's 18 labor market regions was -6.5% to August 2026 4. Where a payroll series exists for the trade area it should lead, with the study explaining why the home-based count differs.

Pooled over 2020 to 2024, the Census Bureau's five-year survey puts Michigan at 4,076,369 households with a median income of $72,875 84. Renting households paid $1,129 at the median, gross of utilities, and 1,876,266 Michiganders were 65 or older 84. Five years of pooled responses describe the structure of a trade area well and its trend poorly; a ratio that divides one of them by a single-year 2025 estimate describes no real year. Income per head on the federal accounts basis, $63,690 for 2024 85, counts transfer payments and investment income as well as wages, which suits a spending-power model and misleads in a rent-burden test.

Capital Access

Michigan's SBA record, its loss rates and the state money that can sit under a loan.

The federal loan file is the firmest Michigan lending record; single-year totals and lender rankings reach this page secondhand.

For Michigan borrowers in the seasoned cohorts of FY2011 to FY2020, SBA charged off 4.9% of 7(a) loans by count and 1.0% of 504 loans 86. The file does not give reasons. It does place a 7(a) request in the book with the higher loss rate, and in such a request the projections, and the study under them, carry most of the argument. Over approvals dated October 2015 to September 2025, with later cancellations struck, the same file holds 20,775 Michigan 7(a) loans and 1,496 504 loans 86.

Huntington's own release credits the bank with 1,385 Michigan 7(a) loans for $191,740,500 in FY2024 and the next-largest lender with 85 loans for $55,340,300 8. A study that needs a share should compute one from SBA's FY2025 year-end lender reports on data.sba.gov and print the period beside it. District totals are secondhand as well: lenders quoting SBA put the district's FY2025 approvals at 2,867 loans worth $1.3 billion across all programs 56, against 2,921 loans in FY2024 7, and no SBA table for either year was retrieved.

What the Michigan Strategic Fund adds

SSBCI, the federal credit initiative for small businesses, approved Michigan for up to $236,990,950 in its second funding round 12, and roughly $72 million, the first tranche, arrived in August 2022 13. Of the allocation, $75 million went to the Small Business Venture Capital Program 13, and in June 2025 up to $9.8 million of SSBCI venture capital was committed to the New Community Transformation Fund II 16. Lending support runs through Michigan Strategic Fund programs that MEDC administers: the Capital Access Program, the Collateral Support Program, the Loan Participation Program and the Loan Guarantee Program 1415.

Two of them reach into a loan file directly. Under the Capital Access Program the lender and borrower pay a premium into a pooled reserve 14. Under the Collateral Support Program the state can pledge cash collateral 15, enough to close a collateral shortfall on a project built on heavy equipment or leasehold improvements. Where either program is in the structure, the state's share shows in sources and uses as an enhancement, while the coverage ratio is computed on every dollar the borrower repays, the supported share included.

SBA in Michigan

Huntington's two FY2025 awards, a Grandville CDC and the seasonal files SBA sees from the lakeshore.

The banks and development companies SBA's Michigan District honored, how its two offices divide the markets, and what a seasonal northern project meets on 504 equity and the 7(a) fee.

Huntington took both Lender of the Year and Rural Lender of the Year in the Michigan District's FY2025 awards, announced on June 9, 2026 6. Independent Bank and United, a credit union, were among that year's Top 10 honorees 5; the FY2024 list had also carried Chase, Comerica, Fifth Third, PNC, Horizon Bank and Lake Michigan Credit Union, the last named Credit Union Lender of the Year 7. On the debenture side, Great Lakes Commercial Finance of Grandville was the district's CDC Lender of the Year for FY2025 9, with the Michigan Certified Development Corporation in East Lansing and the Oakland County Business Finance Corporation also writing 504 loans 988. No ranked table of Michigan CDCs and no FY2025 split of district volume between 7(a) and 504 turned up in the research.

SBA reads Michigan files from its Michigan Avenue office in Detroit and a Grand Rapids virtual office whose county list runs from Berrien up the lakeshore to Grand Traverse and Leelanau 10. Mapped onto the market sections below, Kalamazoo County and the Grand Rapids market's Barry, Ionia, Kent and Ottawa counties fall on the Grand Rapids list, while Montcalm appears on both offices' lists; the ten Traverse City counties are split between the two offices, and Washtenaw, the Lansing and Flint and Saginaw counties and every Upper Peninsula county sit on Detroit's 10.

13 CFR 120.160(b) leaves a feasibility study to SBA's discretion 17, and our note on the clause sets out its wording. The northern and lakeshore sections below are where Michigan's seasonal, single-use property sits: marinas, resort hotels and car washes, each named as special purpose property in SBA's 504 rules 18. A 504 project that pairs special purpose property with a business of two years or less carries an owner contribution of 20% of project cost 18; for a marina or resort that earns most of its year in a few summer months, the study's monthly cash flow has to show debt service met after a short or wet season with that contribution already in. The remainder of SOP 50 10 8.1 is summarized on our SBA practice page.

USDA Rural Development

Business and Industry guarantees in Michigan.

The state office sits in East Lansing 11. No FY2025 B&I obligation total for Michigan turned up, and a USDA release citing a total Michigan investment gave no fiscal year; a lender who wants to know how often the program closes here should ask the state office for its obligation report.

USDA writes its own study standard into the program rule, with five components that differ from an SBA market read; a research note applies all five to a single rural project, the deadline calendar tracks fiscal-year timing, and the deliverable itself is laid out on our USDA practice page. Rural eligibility turns on the site's place and population, so a Michigan study should print the eligibility lookup for its address instead of assuming that a northern location qualifies. SBA's own rural test is county-level, and the FY2027 fee notice waives the upfront fee for rural businesses on 7(a) loans up to $700,000 19, so a northern study records its county's result on that test too.

HUD, EB-5 and Conventional

Agency, EB-5 and bank capital: what the Michigan record holds and lacks.

The research names no HUD multifamily office for Michigan applications and found no Michigan totals for HUD multifamily or residential care insurance, so a Michigan HUD file should name its office and application path at the outset; the market study requirements follow from them.

An EB-5 raise in Michigan gets no head start from the research, which holds no Michigan targeted employment area designations and no regional center inventory. The study has to build the job count and the area test for its own site; what a reviewer checks in that count is set out in how EB-5 jobs are counted.

No ranking of Michigan's conventional commercial lenders was found; the banks on the SBA honor rolls above are the nearest named list. The Michigan Strategic Fund also approves conduit bond financing for private projects, including, in September 2026, wastewater and facility work for a camp operator 62. What institutional lenders test is set out under conventional and institutional lending.

Rules by Asset Class

The Michigan rules that set capacity, cost and opening date.

Each rule below fixes a capacity, a cost or an opening date for a Michigan project before demand is weighed.

Health care: certificates above a capital threshold

Under the Certificate of Need program run by MDHHS, a covered capital expenditure in a clinical service area triggers review above $4,272,500, effective January 1, 2026 20, across 14 clinical service categories 21. The standards moved in 2025 and 2026: psychiatric bed standards reached final action on September 18, 2025, CT and long-term-care bed standards went to public hearing on April 30, 2026, and surgical services were under workgroup review in 2026 22. For a medical office or surgery center, the program caps competing supply and adds a permit step, so a study should state whether the project needs a certificate, cite the standard in force on its filing date and show the opening date with and without approval.

Senior housing: no assisted living license, three capacity bands

Michigan has no assisted living license: a building that provides personal care needs an Adult Foster Care or Home for the Aged license, and memory care carries none of its own 2523. The license follows capacity: an adult foster care small group home holds 12 or fewer residents, a large group home 13 to 20, and a Home for the Aged 21 or more 23. Since February 11, 2018, a Home for the Aged may serve residents from age 55, down from 60 24. The bands come from a 2015 federal compendium, and the research did not re-confirm that the current rule still sets them, so the study should confirm them with LARA. The band decides the competitive set: a memory care or assisted living building competes with licensed homes in its own band, and the lower age threshold widens the pool to residents in their late fifties.

Childcare: new home rules and center ratios

MiLEAP's new rule set for family and group child care homes, dated May 26, 2026, caps a family home at 6 unrelated children, or 7 on approval, and a group home at 12, or 14 on approval 26. Draft rules circulated in 2025 set minimum child-use space at 400 sq ft for a family home and 600 sq ft for a group home 28. Center rules were updated on May 7, 2025, and a center must submit a floor plan of all child-use areas 27.

Restaurants and bars: a quota state with priced licenses

Michigan allots on-premises liquor licenses by population, at 1 per 1,500 residents, and specially designated distributor licenses at 1 per 3,000 2930. The quota is recalculated after each census, and an escrowed license can be transferred within its county 30. The state fee for a Class C license is $600 a year, but the license itself is bought in a transfer market; a secondary 2026 guide to Michigan licensing, writing statewide, puts prices at about $25,000 in rural areas and $150,000+ in metro Detroit, Grand Rapids and Ann Arbor 31. In 1999 the legislature's analysts reported average prices of $40,000 to $50,000, with some up to $300,000 29. Because the price moves with county and year, a restaurant study's uses should carry it as a dated broker quote.

Fuel: the tank fund and its fee

Michigan charges an environmental fee of 7/8 cent per gallon on refined petroleum, and the Refined Petroleum Fund took in $31,649,932 in FY2024 32. The Michigan Underground Storage Tank Authority, inside EGLE, runs a cleanup fund for releases discovered after December 30, 2014; its predecessor was declared insolvent in 1995 32. A study for a fuel and convenience site should state whether a known release predates the fund's coverage.

Short-term rentals and lodging tax

No statewide law overrides local short-term rental ordinances or creates a registry, so local rules govern (see Traverse City below); House bills that would let voters approve a local excise tax were introduced in late October 2025, as proposals rather than law 35. No typical combined hotel tax rate for Michigan was found, so a hotel study should get its municipality's rate and any convention or tourism assessment from the local authority.

Costs, Taxes and Insurance

Property tax, the FAIR plan and the price of new power.

Each Michigan cost measure in the research describes a class of property or a single utility docket, never a parcel.

The owner-occupied effective property tax rate in Michigan was 1.19% in 2024, down from 1.23% in 2023, on an ACS-based measure published by the Tax Foundation 36. That is a residential measure. The research found no statewide effective rate for commercial property. Its nearest evidence ranks cities, not the state: the Lincoln Institute's study of taxes paid in 2022, as its announcement summarizes it, put Detroit and Chicago highest among the largest cities of each state on a benchmark commercial property 37.

The research documents Michigan's property insurance market only at the structural level. The Michigan Basic Property Insurance Association, which serves as the FAIR plan, was organized under Act 262 of 1968 and writes homeowners, dwelling and commercial property; its member insurers face pro rata assessments for its deficits 39. Its commercial rating used a loss cost factor of 2.82, with limits of $1 million for a single interest and $1.5 million for multiple interests, as reported in 2024 38. Homeowners' premiums in Michigan rose 29% from 2021 to 2024 according to consumer advocates 40. That is a residential series. The research located no approved commercial rate change for 2025 or 2026 and no carrier withdrawal, so the insurance line comes from a quote.

Michigan's power cost question is being argued in a single docket. DTE projected a 2026 system peak of about 10.71 GW 41, and on December 18, 2025 the Michigan Public Service Commission conditionally approved DTE's special contracts for the Stargate campus, provided that other customers bear none of their cost 42. The Attorney General appealed that approval on April 17, 2026 41, while sales tax exemptions for data centers have applied since early 2025 43. Until the appeal is decided the no-cost-shift condition is not final, and a study in DTE territory that budgets new service at today's rates should name that assumption.

Labor on public work is priced under the Prevailing Wages on State Projects Act, restored by Public Act 10 of 2023 from February 13, 2024 and extended by Public Act 110 of 2024 from April 2, 2025; projects under federal Davis-Bacon rules are exempt from the state act 44. Neither a construction cost index for Michigan cities nor impact fee ranges turned up in the research.

Hazards

Ice and the gaps in the hazard record.

The hazard event a 2026 reviewer will remember is the ice storm of March 28 to 30, 2025. The federal declaration, DR-4880, came on July 22, 2025, and designated 13 counties for Public Assistance 45: Alcona, Alpena, Antrim, Charlevoix, Cheboygan, Crawford, Emmet, Kalkaska, Mackinac, Montmorency, Oscoda, Otsego and Presque Isle, with the Little Traverse Bay Bands of Odawa Indians. In March 2026 the declaration was widened to Category F, which covers public utilities including nonprofit electric cooperatives 46. A hotel, campground, marina or senior care study in those counties should ask how the operator kept running through the outage, whether the site has backup power, and what the business interruption cover pays.

For a lakeshore or riverside site the study starts from the parcel: the flood panel that covers it and an insurer's premium quote for the building. Nothing statewide in the research can stand in for either, since past the ice storm it holds no federal declaration for 2025 or 2026 and no Michigan hazard score, neither a National Risk Index ranking nor a flood, wind or seismic exposure measure.

Between the Markets

Michigan outside the eight markets: Jackson, Midland and the townships.

Two statewide releases name Michigan places outside the eight markets.

Jackson posted 12-month payroll growth of +1.2% to August 2026, among the largest of any Michigan metro 3, yet the research names none of the employers behind it; a Jackson study should find them before it counts on the trend continuing.

Midland points the other way. Its count of employed residents dropped -7.6% between August 2025 and August 2026, a fall matched only by Lansing 4, while statewide payrolls rose across the same span 3. A Midland County study has to reconcile the two series before either enters its demand case.

Ann Arbor

Ann Arbor: the university, a hyperscale campus and state-backed housing.

Washtenaw County alone makes up the Ann Arbor metro. Most of its published evidence describes three projects rather than the market around them.

The University of Michigan anchors the county. Michigan Medicine's own headcount and the next four employers do not appear in the research, so current enrollment and employment should come from the university itself.

What evidence there is centers on Stargate Michigan, the data center campus of Related Digital, Oracle and OpenAI in Saline Township. Its full build-out load is 1,383 MW 5841, on a site of 250 acres 59. The campus has 3 buildings of 550,000 sq ft each, the first nearly complete in June 2026 61. Its cost is reported at least two ways, $16 billion by one outlet 59 and about $10 billion by another, which also puts bond financing at $14 billion 61; the developer reported >$5 billion already invested in construction by June 1, 2026 60. The township board first voted against the rezoning, and the project went ahead after a lawsuit settlement 59.

Verification

What a Michigan study has to bring that this page cannot.

Records a Michigan credit file can obtain and this page could not, from the population vintage to the parcel's eligibility lookups.

  1. The population vintage and its components

    The vintage date of every estimate used, with international and domestic migration shown apart.

  2. A county payroll series

    Where only the household survey covers the trade area, as in Lansing, Ann Arbor and Kalamazoo, county payroll data from the state's labor market office should stand behind any employment claim.

  3. The employer's own headcount

    Announced, current and contracted employment differ, as in Delta Township. Build demand on the current, dated figure.

  4. The license tier or certificate

    Senior care, childcare and health care projects should state the license or Certificate of Need they need, the capacity band they fall in, and the date the rule in force was confirmed with LARA, MiLEAP or MDHHS.

  5. The liquor license price

    Price the license from a same-county broker quote with its date, never from a statewide range.

  6. A supply census for the trade area

    Competitors named one by one, with capacity and the date each was checked, from LARA or MiLEAP lists where a license applies and from a site visit where none does.

  7. The tax line after the sale

    The research found no statewide commercial effective tax rate. Get the parcel's bill from the assessor, restate it for the post-sale assessment, and show any abatement with the year it ends.

  8. Service terms and a premium quote for the site

    Delta Township is the one Michigan site with a utility record in the research. Elsewhere the electric and water providers' written terms for the subject, dated, go in the file (in DTE territory with the appeal's status noted), beside a carrier's quote for the building.

  9. Program eligibility by address

    USDA rural status, Opportunity Zone and HUBZone location, an EB-5 targeted employment area and SBA's rural fee waiver were settled for no Michigan market in the research; the study records each lookup for the subject's address with the date it ran.

Michigan Questions

Michigan feasibility study questions.

Does a statewide payroll gain support a Michigan project's revenue ramp?

Not unless the subject's sector is one of the rows that rose. Over the year to August 2026 government added +15,600 jobs and professional and business services shed -6,400 3, and the release itemizes only some sectors, so the headline hides which payrolls a tenant or customer draws on. A ramp is accepted only when the revenue line has been matched to a sector row pulled at county level, and the projection should also be shown with that sector held flat, since August also brought the first monthly loss after five months of gains 3.

Which license should a Michigan memory care loan be conditioned on?

The one for the capacity band the building will actually fill, confirmed with LARA before the loan is sized, because Michigan issues no assisted living or memory care license of its own 2523. The 21-resident floor for a Home for the Aged comes from a 2015 federal compendium whose currency the research left unconfirmed 23, so the file quotes LARA's present threshold and the day it was confirmed. The lender then asks whether the bed count sits safely inside its band or one resident above the next, since crossing the line changes the staffing model and the homes the study counts.

Does the January 2027 wage step belong in a Michigan restaurant's first-year projection?

In the first projection year or the second, wherever the opening date places it, with coverage shown at that wage rather than averaged across the ramp. The scheduled minimum of $15.00 from January 1, 2027 33 lands while sales are still building; the question is whether the file clears the test with menu prices held before any pricing recovery is counted. The license is the other dated cost: a secondary 2026 guide, writing about the state as a whole, quotes $150,000+ in the Ann Arbor, Grand Rapids and metro Detroit markets 31, and the lender decides whether to fund at that figure or to require a county broker's quote first.

Should a Michigan surgery center loan close before the Certificate of Need is issued?

The construction draw should wait; the question is what share of the loan sits exposed before MDHHS acts. Covered clinical spending above $4,272,500 has needed review since January 1, 2026 20, and the surgical services standard was before a workgroup during 2026 22, so a certificate applied for on today's standard may be decided on a revised one. The file should state which standard was in force when it filed, carry two opening dates, one for each outcome, size the interest reserve on the later one, and make the certificate a condition precedent to any construction advance.

Does Michigan's collateral support change what a feasibility study has to prove?

Only the weakness the bank is covering. The Collateral Support Program can pledge cash collateral 15, and the Capital Access Program matches a pooled reserve 14; both sit on the lender's side of the ledger, so coverage is tested on every dollar of the loan with no part netted out. What the programs add is a question of their own: why the collateral is short, a specialized asset or improvements with no resale market, since that fact also decides what the lender recovers if the projections miss.

Which of the Stargate job figures may a Washtenaw County file cite, and for how long?

Each for the period it describes, and none past it. The developer's own figures, issued at the June 1, 2026 construction celebration, put union construction jobs at 2,500+ and permanent onsite jobs at 450+ 60. The decision splits by lease length: a hotel or extended-stay property may carry the crews only as a bridge with an end date taken from the build schedule, while an apartment building on twelve-month leases is sized on the 450+ alone. The research records no decision on the Attorney General's appeal against the DTE approval 41, so a projection that leans on the campus opening date prints that status with the date it was checked.
