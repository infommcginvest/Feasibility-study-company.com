Private School Feasibility and Market Studies

A private school feasibility study answers two lender questions about one K-12 school: can it fill its grades at the tuition families actually pay after aid, and do the borrower's tax status, campus and enrollment contracts fit the SBA lender, bond underwriter, bank or USDA office that will finance it. The private school market study inside it builds enrollment from the school-age population and the NCES roster and reconciles it to the school's own admissions funnel, because no published capture rate or stabilization benchmark exists for the sector: NCES counted 30,553 private K-12 schools enrolling 5,096,365 students in 2023-24 1, and 12,035 of them, 39.4% of the total, had fewer than 50 students 2. This page covers K-12 enrollment, tuition and campus debt under the SBA 7(a) and 504 rules, the USDA Community Facilities program and the tax-exempt bond market as they stood in October 2026; licensed preschool and early-childhood programs, including the preschool years of a Montessori school, belong to the childcare and daycare hub.

The Private School Thesis

Underwrite the enrollment line, the borrower's tax status and the building's second use.

A private K-12 school borrows against a campus and repays from families who sign one-year enrollment contracts. NCES counted 30,553 private schools enrolling 5,096,365 students in the 2023-24 school year 1, against 29,727 schools and 4,731,303 students in 2021-22 4, so the survey count rose between the two surveys, although the rise may include schools newly found for the survey frame rather than new openings (see the supply section). The average private school enrolled 167 students in 2023-24, and 12,035 schools, 39.4% of the total, had fewer than 50 2. A lender to a school of that size is lending on one admissions cycle at a time.

Schools of that size fail in a recognizable way, and the 2025 to 2026 record shows it. Manhattan Country School filed for Chapter 11 in May 2025 8, the Academy at Penguin Hall filed for Chapter 11 and closed in June 2025 9, Midtown International School in Atlanta announced on March 30, 2026 that it would close four days later 10, and Roycemore School in Evanston missed a bond payment in July 2026 and said it would close 11. Applications thinned, aid requests grew, covenants slipped, and the closure left families' prepaid tuition exposed.

Neither of the two numbers a lender would reach for is published. No federal program, trade body or rating agency publishes a capture rate, fair share or seats-per-capita benchmark for private schools, so enrollment is built from the school-age population up and reconciled to the school's own admissions funnel. No broker publishes a school cap rate either, and the best public price evidence for a campus whose school has closed is a Massachusetts city's appraisal file on school and church buildings sold for school use 6, set out in the valuation section.

The file turns first on who borrows. SBA lends only to businesses organized for profit, and its Inspector General faulted SBA in March 2026 for a screening framework that did not check that status 5. Many religious and independent schools are nonprofits, so the charter picks the lender among the capital routes, and the lender picks the format of the study. The page covers K-12 enrollment, tuition and campus debt; preschool and early-childhood programs sit on the childcare and daycare hub. Seven school types, independent day schools first, are treated below, and the page ends with the worked case.

State agencies publish more recent figures. The rest of the budget is unpublished. A study therefore lists each line with its own evidence: the land contract or appraisal, a dated bid, a furniture and technology schedule, itemized soft costs and a contingency.

A school earns enrolled students times the tuition families actually pay after aid, plus fees, auxiliary programs and gifts. Its costs start with the faculty, which is hired for a full year before the school knows whether the fall class arrived. Private schools employed 517,780 full-time-equivalent teachers in 2023-24 1, against 482,571 in 2021-22 4, and the pupil-to-teacher ratio across all private schools was 9.8 2. Each school type has its own ratio, as the Catholic and special-education sections show, so payroll should come from the school's own staffing plan.

Independent schools' per-student medians and margin evidence are in the independent day school section. For the rest of the sector no published source gives an operating expense ratio, an EBITDA margin for a for-profit K-12 operator, a management fee norm or franchise financial performance. The study has to establish the expense budget from the school's staffing plan, salary scale, benefits load, occupancy cost and insurance, shown per enrolled student.

The calendar fixes revenue timing. The average private school year was 178.8 days in 2023-24 2, and enrollment is contracted once a year, so tuition arrives in deposits and installments while payroll runs monthly. A school that collects tuition in advance holds families' money for months, which is where two of the failures below begin.

Scale sets the fixed-cost burden. The average elementary and middle school enrolled 113 students in 2023-24, against 252 students for secondary schools and 235 students for combined schools 2. A small elementary school carries a head of school, admissions and a business office on very few tuition payers. The building's running cost follows its size and age. Education buildings averaged 31,100 square feet and 62.7 thousand Btu per square foot a year in the federal 2018 survey 18, while the 2018 energy code's prototype primary and secondary schools model at 43.6 and 36.6 thousand Btu 19. The utility line should say which of the two benchmarks it assumes for the subject's building, and why.

The industry code needs a word of caution before any of these numbers is used. NAICS 611110 is elementary and secondary schools, and the site's register grades it a partial fit for this hub: the code covers elementary and secondary schools whatever their ownership, so the private slice needs an ownership filter, and specialty education sits in other codes, NAICS 6116 (other schools and instruction) and 6117 (educational support services). The Census count of 23,201 establishments in 2023 20 is read with that boundary. It is not the same list NCES surveys, and the supply section explains why the two counts differ. SBA loans coded to 611110 carry the same boundary, and they also reach only schools organized for profit 5, which leaves out most of the sector.

Within that boundary the program is small and steady. SBA approved 546 7(a) loans in that code for $519.8 million in the ten fiscal years to 2025, and 279 504 loans for $387.9 million 21. In the last five full years, fiscal 2021 to 2025, SBA approved 189 of the 504 loans and 259 of the 7(a) loans 21.

The seasoned cohorts give the loss record. Loans approved in fiscal 2011 to 2020 charged off at 2.5% by count and 0.4% by dollars under 7(a), and at 0.5% by count under 504 21. The 504 rate rests on a single charged-off loan in a small cohort, so it says little about the next default. Our note on SBA default economics explains why a count rate and a dollar rate can tell different stories, and why neither is a forecast.

The agency's own controls are the other half of the picture. SBA's Inspector General reported in March 2026 that for 73,302 7(a) loans approved between August 1, 2023 and December 31, 2024 there was limited assurance borrowers met eligibility rules, about $32 billion in questioned costs, and that SBA lacked sufficient documentation for sampled loans worth about $60.7 million 5. Its screening had not tested whether a borrower was organized for profit, and eligibility decisions returned to delegated lenders on May 31, 2025 22. A school study should state the borrower's tax status and ownership on its first page.

Eligibility comes first

SBA lends to businesses organized for profit, and its Inspector General's March 2026 report listed that test first among the screens SBA's framework had skipped 5. A school's charter and tax filings settle the point before anything else. The religious question no longer stops a for-profit faith-based school: SBA deleted the rule that made businesses principally engaged in teaching or indoctrinating religion ineligible, effective August 1, 2022, and later SOP editions dropped the religious-use-of-proceeds prohibition and its certification form, while the for-profit requirement stayed 26. Since March 1, 2026 every direct and indirect owner must be a U.S. citizen or national, by a lender's reading of SBA policy 27.

Equity and the special-purpose question

For a for-profit school opening its first grades, the 7(a) test looks forward: the borrower brings 10% of total project cost, and the enrollment plan has to show 1.15x coverage within two years 25, which for a school is two admissions cycles. A buyer of an operating school is tested on the past instead, at 1.25x on historical EBITDA over total post-transaction debt service, on the last fiscal year-end or the average of the last two 25, and that history is the seller's re-enrollment by grade. Either way the real estate can run up to 25 years. A campus bought or built under 504 carries a contribution set by two facts, the age of the business and the class of the building: 10% as the standard tier, 15% if one of the two applies and 20% if both do 61. No SBA document found names schools as special-purpose property, and the lender-published lists the research reviewed leave them out 28, so the CDC classifies the building. Pairing the two programs, a school can reach $10 million in total, when the 7(a) loan is approved first and the 504 loan second; Policy Notice 5000-879058, effective July 4, 2026 25.

USDA Community Facilities for rural nonprofits

USDA's Community Facilities program lists private schools among eligible educational services, but only where the borrower is a public body, a community-based nonprofit or a federally recognized tribe, the place falls under the program's rural population limit, and the project is not a private commercial undertaking 23. The program's federal listing gives its scale: direct loans were $720,604,476 in fiscal 2024 and an estimated $851,352,038 in fiscal 2025, and guarantees were $44,954,000 and an estimated $89,339,000, with $650 million of guarantees planned for fiscal 2026 17. The guaranteed program sets its own maximum loan and guarantee share, on a program page the research cites without a date 29. USDA's recent school loans have gone to charter schools: $7 million to a North Carolina charter school's expansion in March 2026 and $15 million for another's new K-12 building in April 2026 3031. No Community Facilities loan to a private religious or independent school appeared in the evidence, and no USDA Business and Industry loan to any school was found. The lender's handbook lists a financial feasibility report among the documents, if USDA requires one 24; our USDA Rural Development practice area sets out how those reports are built.

Bonds and banks

Established nonprofit schools borrow mostly through tax-exempt revenue bonds that a city or state authority issues on the school's behalf, with the school repaying. Roycemore School's unrated Series 2021 bonds, issued through the City of Evanston, are the current example of how that structure fails: its coverage and days-cash tests were below the bond terms at June 30, 2025 32, and the school missed its July 15, 2026 payment 11. No published source gives 2026 loan-to-value, coverage, spread or recourse terms for bank loans on school real estate, and no HUD program fit was found. Our conventional and institutional lending practice area covers the bank route in general terms.

At the parcel the binding rules are zoning and parking. Where a school is a conditional or special use, a public hearing sits between the purchase contract and the first day of class. Parking ratios are set code by code: Boise, for example, sets one ratio for elementary and junior high schools and a different per-classroom ratio for high schools 34. The building stock adds its own conditions. In the federal 2018 survey, 56% of education buildings were under 10,000 square feet 18, and many schools start in converted churches, offices or retail space. No published source gives environmental benchmarks for private schools on tanks, stormwater, wastewater or noise, or quantifies what delays school permits, so the study establishes them from the site: the Phase I assessment, the building's age and the local permit path. USDA requires an environmental review of every facility its Community Facilities program finances 24.

The clearest value evidence for a school building is a city's appraisal file. Newburyport, Massachusetts, weighing the sale of a former school in March 2025, relied on two comparables: a school-to-school sale in Maynard at $1,702,000 for 35,640 square feet, or $48 a square foot, and a former church in Chelsea bought for public educational use at $147 a square foot for 17,719 square feet 6. Those are sale prices for buildings in school use, and they are the collateral evidence; the construction budgets in the development section are not. A lender's collateral case for a school is the price another school or a church would pay.

No broker or appraiser publishes cap rates for private K-12 schools by quality tier, and no source says how much of a school's value belongs to the campus and how much to the operating school. Listing sites show asking cap rates on some "school" properties, but those are asking prices on listings that are mostly childcare and pre-K buildings 35, which belong on the childcare hub rather than in a K-12 appraisal. Platform transactions are priced as businesses: the consortium that bought Nord Anglia Education, an international school group, paid a $14.5 billion enterprise valuation in March 2025 36, a figure that says nothing about a single campus.

A school lender's appraisal has to answer the question the Massachusetts comparables raise: what another school would pay for this campus if the school closed. On an SBA purchase the valuation also limits the loan, since total acquisition debt, including seller debt not on full standby, is capped at the business valuation; any excess must be equity 25. Where the CDC or lender classes the campus as special-purpose property, meaning a limited-market property with a unique physical design, special construction materials, or a layout that restricts its utility to the use for which it was built, the SOP asks for a going-concern appraisal by an appraiser with recent work on equivalent property 25. Our note on special purpose property risk covers how that collateral behaves after a default.

The early-childhood schools averaged 18 students 2, and their economics belong with childcare rather than with a K-12 campus. A competitor census that does not sort by grade span overstates the competition.

For-profit platforms are few, and none publishes school-level economics. Spring Education Group, the largest operator the research names, runs schools in several states under several brands, many of them preschools 38, and its Stratford School brand opened a second high school in Milpitas, California in 2025 37. No published source ranks the ten largest U.S. K-12 operators, and no K-12 franchise system was identified, so the franchise share is not a measure that exists.

The study has to build demand from the trade area and show its arithmetic, in five steps.

  1. Count the children. School-age population by single year of age in the trade area, projected by grade.
  2. Apply the private share. The local share already in private school, from the NCES roster against the same population.
  3. Narrow to the families who can pay. Household income at the planned tuition, adjusted for state choice programs.
  4. Subtract the competition. Every competing private school's enrollment by grade span, from the federal public-use file released in September 2026 41, plus charter schools and any announced openings.
  5. Reconcile to the funnel. The school's inquiries, applications, admissions, yield and re-enrollment by grade, which show whether the model's share is reachable.

Locale matters more than the national total. Private schools enrolled 2,299,043 students in cities and 1,910,970 in suburbs in 2023-24, against 587,788 in rural areas and 298,563 in towns 2. Rural schools draw on thin populations, and that is where the USDA channel applies.

Choice programs are now a first-order demand input. EdChoice counted 1,300,905 students using private school choice programs in July 2025, up from about 1,038,500 students a year earlier 3, and a 2026 EdChoice page counts 774,299 education savings account participants and 433,230 voucher participants 39. A study should use the state's own election, not either tally.

The population underneath choice funding is growing slowly: national growth slowed to 0.5% in the year to July 2025 as net international migration declined 40. Where the school-age count is flat, a new school's enrollment comes from existing schools.

The funnel is the test that matters most, because an entry-grade miss persists. No national attrition or stabilization benchmark is published for the sector, so the study uses the school's own history and, where the school is new, the record of comparable schools nearby. Our note on why the trade area is the study sets out how the trade area is drawn, and the note on absorption forensics covers how a claimed ramp is checked.

Subtypes

Seven school types, seven study scopes.

All seven types are treated in full on this page. For six of them the federal evidence is a census of schools; microschools rest on advocacy surveys.

The school types follow the NCES axes where they can and depart from them where lenders do. Affiliation separates Catholic and parochial schools from Christian and other faith-based schools. Program emphasis separates Montessori and Waldorf schools and therapeutic and special-education schools. Boarding schools and microschools are not federal categories, but each changes the study enough to need its own scope. Independent day schools, the tuition-led nonsectarian schools whose association publishes the sector's only per-student finances, come first.

A school paid mostly by public placements carries a government receivable, and the agency's rate decisions and payment timing become the credit question.

How lenders underwrite it. No published lender treatment specific to these schools was found, and residential or health licensing for therapeutic programs was not sourced. A for-profit operator can use SBA, subject to the eligibility screens the Inspector General has pressed on 5. A residential program is a licensed facility as well as a school, and the study should list each license with its renewal date.

Where it fails review. Special-education files fail when enrollment is projected without the placing agencies' commitments, when revenue is shown at contract rates without payment history, when staffing assumes hiring that the local labor market cannot supply, and when a residential license or accreditation is assumed rather than documented.

Subtype: Microschools

Microschools: a fast-growing format measured only by its advocates.

Every national figure on microschools comes from an advocacy body's surveys, reported through trade press. None is a federal count, and lending to the format is not documented.

What a microschool is. A microschool is a very small school, often a few classrooms in a rented space, a church hall or a home, and its legal status varies. The National Microschooling Center's 2024 sector analysis found that 55% of microschools served children under state homeschool rules, 37% operated as licensed private schools and 6% were public charters; its May 2025 edition put the homeschool share at 53% 53. A microschool in homeschool mode is not a private school in the state's records and may not appear in the NCES survey at all, while a licensed one is counted with the small schools in the structure section.

How big the format is. K-12 Dive, citing the center's 2026 analysis, reported an estimated 75,000 microschools serving about 1.5 million students 54. Those are advocacy estimates built from a survey of about a thousand operating, planned or closed microschools, not federal counts, and they cannot size a local market. The same reporting gives a median enrollment of 20 students for nonpublic microschools 54.

How it earns and where it operates. In the 2026 survey 63% of microschools relied on tuition and half received money from state school-choice programs; 38% operated in commercial space, 22% in houses of worship and 17% in private homes; and 67% were not accredited, though 87% wanted to be 54. On tenure, 20% had operated six years or more, 45% three to five years and 16% under one year 55. Microschools that grew reported early enrollment gains in a 2025 survey 56, but those are self-reported by operators who grew, not a ramp benchmark. No source publishes revenue per student, margin, a stabilization period or a failure rate.

How lenders underwrite it. No published lender treatment for microschools was found. A for-profit microschool can apply under SBA 7(a), usually to finance a business in leased space, with equipment and working capital as collateral. The study has to establish the legal status first, because a homeschool-mode program, a licensed private school and a charter face different rules and exits, and then document the lease, the state program approvals and enrollment by month.

Where it fails review. Microschool files fail when national advocacy estimates stand in for local demand, when state-program money is projected without the approval behind it, when a lease shorter than the loan secures the operation, and when the founder is the whole school.

Review Failures

How private school feasibility studies fail review.

The closures and bond defaults of 2025 and 2026, and the Inspector General's March 2026 finding on SBA's screening, are the record behind this list.

  1. The borrower's tax status left for later

    SBA lends only to businesses organized for profit, and the Inspector General found SBA's screening had not tested that point on the 2023 to 2024 loans it reviewed 5. A school file that does not settle the borrower's status, ownership and any church or nonprofit control on its first page invites the question at the worst time.

  2. A ramp to a full building with no funnel behind it

    No published source gives months to stabilization for a private school. A projection that fills the building in three years without inquiries, applications, admission yield and re-enrollment by grade has no evidence under it, and an entry-grade miss carries through every later grade.

  3. Published tuition instead of net tuition

    Revenue has to be built on tuition after aid, sibling discounts and remission, not on the posted price. The Atlanta closure of 2026 followed fewer applicants and more requests for aid 10, which is how a discount grows while headcount holds.

  4. Choice-program money treated as permanent

    State programs set awards, eligibility and payment timing each legislative session, and the federal credit depends on each state opting in 33. A school whose growth rests on program students should show its coverage without them.

  5. A public-school cost or a stale quote as the budget

    No private-school cost benchmark exists, and construction costs kept rising through the third quarter of 2026 16. The budget rests on bids that carry their dates, and the public-school figures serve only to test them.

  6. Collateral valued at what the building cost

    The Massachusetts appraisal record prices school-use buildings by their sales 6, not by what they cost to build. A loan sized on cost rather than on what another school or congregation would pay has less collateral than it shows.

  7. Prepaid tuition and covenant headroom ignored

    The Academy at Penguin Hall filed for Chapter 11 on June 11, 2025 and closed on June 30, with parents alleging prepaid tuition was not refunded 9, and Roycemore was below its bond covenants at June 30, 2025 32 before it missed its July 2026 payment 11. A study should show where tuition deposits are held and how much a bad admissions year moves the covenants.

  8. A national count used as a competitor census

    The Census and NCES counts differ by design, and a state ratio says nothing about the trade area. The competitor list has to name each school with its grade span, affiliation and enrollment from the federal roster 41.

Private School Questions

Private school feasibility and market study questions.

When does a lender ask for a private school feasibility study?

No program makes one automatic. SBA's regulation is permissive: 13 CFR 120.160(b): SBA may require professional appraisals, a survey, or a feasibility study, while the USDA Community Facilities handbook names a financial feasibility report only as a document the agency may call for 24. The request follows a new school, a campus purchase or a grade expansion, and a bonded school is tested on the coverage and days-cash covenants in its indenture 32. Our note on the SBA feasibility myth covers the SBA rule.

Which lender fits a private school: SBA, USDA, a bond issuer or a bank?

The borrower's tax status decides it before the building does. SBA 7(a) and 504 reach only businesses organized for profit, the screen SBA's Inspector General found untested in March 2026 5; a for-profit religious school has been eligible since SBA deleted its religious-teaching exclusion effective August 1, 2022 26. A nonprofit school borrows through tax-exempt revenue bonds issued on its behalf, through a bank, or, as a community-based nonprofit in a rural area of 20,000 people or fewer, through USDA Community Facilities 57.

How many private schools are there in the United States?

Two federal counts disagree, and a study prints both. NCES counted 30,553 private K-12 schools in its 2023-24 survey 1, assembled from the lists that states and associations supply plus an area search 2. County Business Patterns counts establishments that the Census business register codes to NAICS 611110, a class that spans every ownership and excludes preschools, shown with its boundary in the supply section 220. Catholic schools carry the same split between the federal survey and the church's own census 244; neither pair is averaged.

What does it cost to build a private school?

No published source gives a private K-12 school's construction cost per square foot or per seat for 2025 or 2026. The nearest benchmarks are public schools: a cost database's 2024 metro model costs, Maryland's 2025 state allowance and North Carolina's reported 2025 costs 71214, and the national cost index rose through the third quarter of 2026 16. Public projects carry prevailing-wage rules a private builder may not face, so the study prices from the school's own dated bids and labels the public figures as checks.

Does SBA treat a school building as special-purpose property under 504?

Not by any SBA text the research found, and the lender-published lists of special-purpose types leave schools out 28. The CDC decides it building by building, and the answer sets the borrower's 504 contribution: 10% as the standard tier, 20% where the business is two years old or less and the property is special-purpose, and 15% where only one applies 61. The Massachusetts appraisal file shows school and church buildings selling for school use, and an appraiser's range for school or religious use 6. A written classification from the CDC belongs in the file before the equity is set.

Can school choice funding carry a private school's enrollment projection?

As one revenue line, shown with and without it. The July 2025 EdChoice count put 1,300,905 students in private school choice programs, against about 1,038,500 a year earlier 3, and from January 1, 2027 a federal tax credit for donors to scholarship-granting organizations applies in the states that opt in 33. Legislatures reset award amounts, eligibility and payment timing every session, and an opting-in state may impose its own conditions on participating schools 33. A defensible study shows coverage with and without program students.

What enrollment evidence does a private school market study need?

The funnel and re-enrollment by grade, not the September headcount. The sector publishes no national benchmark for attrition or stabilization; the one sector-wide demand signal is the Catholic association's finding that 39.3% of its schools had a waiting list in 2024-25 44. The competitor roster comes from the federal public-use file of September 2026, naming every school with grade span, affiliation and enrollment 41. A lender should see inquiries, applications, admissions and yield across several admission cycles, and the deposits already collected for next year.

Why did private schools close or default in 2025 and 2026?

The cases on record are small, tuition-dependent schools with facility debt. Riverside Military Academy defaulted on its campus bonds in March 2025 51; Chapter 11 filings came from Manhattan Country School in May 2025 8 and from the Academy at Penguin Hall, which closed in June 2025 9; Midtown International School announced its closure on March 30, 2026 after fewer applicants and more aid requests 10; and Roycemore School missed a bond payment in July 2026 11. The study tests for a shrinking applicant pool, a growing aid discount and thin covenants.
