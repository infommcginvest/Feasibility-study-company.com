A fuel and convenience store is an operating business on special purpose land, and the study has to prove the business. In 2025 the average inside-store transaction lost 7 cents after expenses while fuel margins averaged over 40 cents a gallon 54, and second-quarter 2026 margins were inflated by an oil shock 23. We test each site on its own traffic, named competitors and tank record, price margin through the cycle, and size the loan to the SBA, USDA or bank rules in force in October 2026.

The Fuel and Convenience Thesis

Underwrite the operator and the margin cycle, not the canopy.

On a loan application a gas station looks like real estate: land, a building, tanks in the ground and a canopy at the road. A credit officer who underwrites it that way misreads the risk. The collateral is special purpose property that holds its value mainly as the business it was built for, the income comes from two product lines with opposite economics, and the larger of the two by revenue is the smaller by profit. In 2025 fuel produced 65.0% of industry sales dollars but only 38.8% of gross profit dollars 3. Inside the store, the average transaction lost 7 cents after all expenses, against a 1-cent profit the year before, even though the average basket grew 5. What carried the year was the fuel margin, which averaged over 40 cents a gallon 4.

That margin is the first thing a 2026 study has to take apart. At the listed chains it widened to between 35.1 and 52.6 cents a gallon in the second quarter of 2026, from 29.2 to 44.9 cents a year earlier, as crude climbed after the conflict that began in late February 23. A pro forma that carries those two quarters forward over a ten- or twenty-five-year amortization capitalizes a supply shock. The gallons themselves are not growing either. U.S. gasoline consumption fell 1% in 2025, and the Energy Information Administration expects it to keep falling through 2027 as the fleet becomes more efficient 54. A new store wins its gallons by taking them from an existing one.

The second thing is the gate list. Every SBA gas station loan begins with a Phase I environmental site assessment, whatever its size 3436, because the country still had 53,777 tank releases awaiting cleanup at the end of fiscal 2025 42. Towns in Michigan, Ohio and Georgia, and a council member in Houston, have moved to pause new stations or push them away from homes 59616263. Neither gate is a market question, and either can stop a loan the market would support.

A useful feasibility study for a fuel site therefore does four things. It measures demand as a share of a flat fuel pool, in a trade area drawn on the road network, against competitors it names. It prices fuel margin through a cycle rather than from the latest quarters. It builds inside sales from the transactions and categories the site can actually serve, and treats foodservice as a capital and labor decision. And it puts the environmental, entitlement and fuel supply documents on the lender's checklist with their dates. This page sets out the national evidence for each step as of October 2026. The six subtype pages carry the format-specific evidence, and the five worked cases apply the method to real towns in Texas, Florida, Alabama, Georgia and Ohio.

The only all-in development cost a public operator has disclosed for a new fuel store comes from the registration statement Yesway filed in March 2026 before its listing. It puts a self-funded new-to-industry store at $10.0 to $12.0 million, counting land, soft costs, site work, tanks and fuel equipment, the building, store equipment and signage 7. That number describes one product: a 5,800-square-foot store on 3.7 acres with 27 dispensers, built in the rural Southwest and Midwest, set against the 3,400-square-foot, 1.1-acre, 10-dispenser store that makes up the operator's older fleet 7. It is far larger than the single store most SBA borrowers build. A study that quotes it as the cost of a typical station overstates the budget for a small format and understates it for a large one.

The same filing shows how chains pay for that product. Under a build-to-suit program the operator funds about a quarter of land and construction, $2.5 to $3.0 million a store, and net-lease investors fund the rest 7. An SBA borrower has no such partner, which is why the equity tiers in the capital section bind harder on a fuel site than on most owner-occupied real estate. The filing also shows that land is not always bought: the operator owns about 65% of its store real estate 7. No source in our evidence gives land or equipment as a share of total project cost, so a study has to build both from the subject's own land price and bids rather than from a ratio.

Building permits fill in part of the picture and mislead when read alone. A Wawa filed in Fort Wayne, Indiana in October 2026 carries a construction estimate of $1,350,000 for 6,374 square feet 14. A Sheetz in Findlay, Ohio was permitted at $3.7 million for 6,139 square feet with the canopy and fuel system included 15. Two older Wawa permits, $3.1 million in Elizabeth City, North Carolina in 2023 16 and $915,400 in Jacksonville, Florida in 2022 17, predate the tariff changes of 2025. Permit valuations usually cover vertical construction and leave out land, tanks, dispensers and soft costs, so the spread between these four says more about what each permit counted than about regional cost. A contractor's published pricing is the other public reference: $150 to $350 per square foot for building and canopy, and $2.2 to $2.8 million all-in for a 3,000-square-foot store with four dispensers 18. It is marketing material, and the range is wide enough that only a bid for the subject settles it.

The direction of cost is clearer than its level. Producer prices for inputs to new nonresidential construction rose 7.1% in the year to June 2026, with steel mill products up 16.9%, aluminum mill shapes up 52.4% and copper and brass up 26.0% 19. The contractors' association ties the metals moves to tariffs as high as 50 percent 19: steel and aluminum duties doubled from 25% to 50% on June 4, 2025, and a 50% duty on copper-containing products followed on August 7, 2025 20. A second series, compiled by another contractor group from the same federal data, showed inputs up 9.6% in the year to May 2026 and 2.6% in that month alone 21, and construction machinery and equipment prices were up 5.6% at the end of 2025, the largest rise since December 2023 22. A fuel site is metal-heavy. Canopy steel, tanks, piping, dispensers and electrical gear all sit in those indexes, so a contractor quote dated before mid-2025 should be repriced before it enters the sources and uses.

Operator evidence brackets the choice between building and buying. Casey's operating chief told investors in June 2026 that an acquired store costs about $1 million less than a new build while meeting the same return requirement 10, and the chain set a fiscal 2026 property and equipment budget of about $600 million against an 80-store target, split evenly between new builds and purchases of retailers that run one to three stores 11. Murphy USA's 2024 capital spending was $503 million, in a year when it opened 32 new stores and completed 47 raze-and-rebuilds 13. Yesway's own 2026 new-store budget is reported two ways, $40 to $50 million for six to eight stores by the trade association 8 and $50 to $70 million by an investor-call aggregator 9, and neither source reconciles them. When a public company's budget is reported inconsistently, a study should not borrow a chain's per-store cost at all.

What the lender should see instead is specific to the subject: the land contract or an appraisal of the land, a general contractor's bid dated after the 2025 tariff changes with tanks, dispensers, canopy and site work broken out, an environmental contingency sized to the site's history, and soft costs itemized. Where any of those is missing when the study is written, the study says so and the gap becomes a lender condition rather than an estimate.

The State of the Industry data NACS released in April 2026 is the broadest public benchmark: in-store sales of $341.2 billion in 2025, up 1.7%, and fuel sales of $476.3 billion, down 5.4% as pump prices fell 3. Gallons through convenience stores rose 0.5% 3, so the decline in fuel revenue was price, not volume. The full survey behind that release, which carries per-store income statements and expense ratios, is sold commercially. Its public summary gives no operating expense ratio, NOI margin or payroll share for an average store, and we found no free substitute. That gap matters because those are the ratios a reviewer compares a pro forma against, so our studies take them from operator filings and label the result as such.

Fuel margin: three measures, one cycle

Three published margin measures are in circulation, and they are not interchangeable. NACS reports a gross gasoline margin of 39.7 cents a gallon in 2024, 11.9% of a $3.33 average price, and a four-year average of 38.0 cents 6; its research director told the 2026 summit that margins averaged over 40 cents in 2025 4. A bank compilation of OPIS data puts the five-year national average at 39.3 cents for gasoline and 54.3 cents for diesel 23. And the listed chains report their own margins every quarter: in the second quarter of 2026, Casey's earned 47.8 cents, Couche-Tard's U.S. business 52.6, CrossAmerica 51.3, Murphy USA 35.1 and Yesway 52.6 23. A year earlier the same companies ranged from 29.2 to 44.9 cents 23. The jump arrived with crude at $97.26 a barrel on September 9, 2026 and a New York Harbor gasoline crack spread running about $1 a gallon above 2025 since May 23.

For underwriting the rule follows directly. The base case uses a through-cycle margin near the multi-year averages, adjusted for the subject's brand, price position and competitors. The downside case uses the low end of the prior year's operator range. Any margin above the averages is upside, not debt capacity. Diesel deserves its own line because its multi-year margin runs well above gasoline, but the diesel buyer is more price-sensitive and more contract-driven, as the travel center section shows.

Volume: the average hides the format

The average store sells about 4,000 gallons of gasoline a day 6, but the spread by format is wide. Yesway's new large-format stores averaged 3.1 million gallons a year against 1.0 million at its legacy stores, and earned $1.4 million of store contribution against $0.4 million 7. Same-store gallons in the second quarter of 2026 ran from minus 8.1% at CrossAmerica to plus 1.4% at Yesway 23. A subject's gallons should be built from its own trade area and competitors, as the demand section describes, and then checked against the format it most resembles rather than against a national average.

Inside sales: where the profit and the risk sit

Foodservice made up 28.5% of in-store sales and 38.9% of in-store gross profit in 2025 3, which makes it the category that most separates a strong store from a weak one. The basket economics turned in 2025, however. Transactions per store fell 2.7% to 45,160 a month 3, industry transactions fell 3.0% with fuel transactions at their lowest since 2021 4, and the average in-store transaction lost 7 cents after all expenses despite a 24-cent rise in basket value 5. A study that applies an industry-average inside margin to a projected transaction count, without carrying card fees, shrink and the labor a food program needs, will show a profitable inside operation the industry itself did not have that year.

Labor and operating expense

Labor is the largest cost an operator controls. The industry averaged 19.9 employees per store at $15.04 an hour in 2025 3. Yesway runs about 2.6 employees per shift across its fleet, and its salaries and benefits came to 7.5% of revenue 7. That ratio moves with the fuel price, because fuel dominates revenue, so a study should build labor from staffed hours, shifts and local wages rather than as a share of sales. Direct store operating expenses rose 4.2% in 2025, the slowest increase since the pandemic, but card fees reached a record $21.3 billion and total expenses are up 23.3% since 2021 34. Card fees need their own line priced on the fuel price forecast, because they scale with the pump price rather than the gallons. Murphy USA's store operating expense, excluding payment fees and rent, ran $36,200 to $36,600 per store per month 12, a reasonable floor for a lean, high-volume format. Third-party management fee norms and franchise Item 19 figures were not found in any source we accept, so a study should take the subject's actual agreements rather than assume a rate.

Lenders ask a fair question about this asset: how have SBA loans to gas stations performed? As of October 2026 no one has published the answer in a form we can cite. SBA reports program totals, not loan counts, volumes or charge-offs by industry code, and our evidence review found no SBA, Inspector General or GAO publication of a gas station charge-off or default rate. Industry rates that circulate online come from consulting websites rather than from SBA, and we do not repeat them. This page therefore prints no gas station loss rate, and no all-industry rate is offered in its place.

The data to answer the question does exist. SBA's 7(a) and 504 loan-level files are public, refreshed every quarter, current to June 30, 2026, and each loan carries its NAICS code 28. Two coding details decide whether a tabulation is right. Gasoline stations with convenience stores appear under 447110 and its successor code 457110, and other gasoline stations under 447190 and its successor 457120, so a query has to take both codes across the approval years it covers. Convenience retailers without fuel sit in a separate retail code altogether. A sound tabulation then separates what program totals blur: approval cohort, loan size, 7(a) against 504, start-up against acquisition, and state. It reports charge-offs on a dollar basis and a count basis separately, and shows how many loans in each cohort are still too young to have defaulted. Our notes on SBA default economics and special purpose property risk explain why the measurement basis matters and how special purpose collateral changes recovery after a default.

What can be cited is program-wide and says nothing specific about fuel. SBA reported a record fiscal 2025: 84,400 7(a) and 504 loans for $44.8 billion, of which 77,600 were 7(a) loans for $37 billion and 6,750 were 504 loans for $7.8 billion 24. The same release said the agency had reduced its workforce by 43% and tightened underwriting standards 24. Fiscal 2026 is running lower. A data vendor counts 40,824 7(a) approvals for $21.8 billion in the first nine months, against 61,270 for $27.6 billion in the same months a year earlier 26. The average 7(a) loan fell from $538,903 in fiscal 2022 to $443,097 in fiscal 2024 25. Those trends describe the channel a gas station borrower enters, not the risk of a gas station loan.

Oversight findings are also program-wide. The Inspector General found 5,044 7(a) loans approved with unresolved hold codes, of which SBA had resolved 3,015 and planned to review 2,029 29, and the auditor of SBA's fiscal 2025 statements disclaimed an opinion on the balance sheet, citing control weaknesses that included loan guaranty estimates 30. None of this is about gas stations. It matters to a gas station file because it explains why lenders in 2026 document more and why a reviewer is more likely to ask where each number came from. A study that publishes its inputs and their sources answers that question before it is asked, and where a lender needs a performance view, the loan-level tabulation belongs in the credit memorandum as a dated exhibit.

The governing text is SOP 50 10 8.1 with Technical Policy Updates, effective October 1, 2026 31. It names gas stations among its examples of special purpose property, which it defines as a limited-market property with a unique physical design, special construction materials, or a layout that restricts its utility to the use for which it was built 31. On the study itself the regulation is unchanged: 13 CFR 120.160(b): SBA may require professional appraisals, a survey, or a feasibility study 32. Lenders ask for one on a fuel site when there is no operating history to underwrite, and the program decides what it has to show. Our SBA 7(a) and 504 practice area covers the program rules in full.

7(a) for a new store

A new store financed with 7(a) needs 10% of total project cost as equity, and its projections must reach 1.15x coverage within two years 31. The loan can reach $5 million, with real estate amortized over up to 25 years. Because 7(a) finances land, building, fuel equipment and working capital in one loan, it is the usual route for a single-store operator. The test year carries the file: coverage in year two depends on how fast gallons and inside sales ramp, and no published benchmark gives a ramp for new convenience stores, so the ramp has to be argued from the subject's competitors and the operator's own record.

7(a) for an acquisition

Buying an operating station is a change of ownership, and the rules are stricter. Equity is 10% of total project cost and cannot be waived, and coverage of 1.25x is measured on historical EBITDA over total post-transaction debt service, on the last fiscal year-end or the average of the last two 31. For most businesses projections are reviewed but cannot meet the floor, except an owner-occupied Special Purpose Property acquisition whose price the appraised value fully supports, where the floor may be met on projections within two years 31. That exception reaches gas stations directly. A buyer whose price the appraisal fully supports can show coverage on projections, which puts the study and the appraisal at the center of the file. The quality of earnings review that larger purchases need does not apply to Owner Buyout, ESOP & Cooperative, and any acquisition of an owner-occupied Special Purpose Property. The whole loan can run 25 years when 85% or more of the use of project cost is real estate and the station is integral to the business; otherwise the business portion amortizes over 10 years 31. The Opelika acquisition case works the historical test through.

504 and the combined limit

Under 504 a bank first lien and a Certified Development Company debenture finance fixed assets. The borrower contributes 10% in the standard case, 15% for a new business or a special purpose property, and 20% for both 31. A gas station is special purpose, so no fuel project sits in the bottom tier, and a new operator building a new station sits in the top one. The structure follows the contribution: Third Party Lender 50%, debenture 40%, borrower 10%; debenture 35% at a 15% contribution and 30% at 20%. Coverage is 1.15x, and the debenture is capped at $5 million. Since July 4, 2026 a borrower who takes a 7(a) loan first and a 504 loan second can reach $10 million combined 2731, which brings larger interchange formats within reach of the programs.

Fees and fuel supply agreements

For loans approved from October 1, 2026 the upfront 7(a) guaranty fee is 0% on loans of $700,000 or less to businesses in a rural area, meaning a county that the U.S. Census Bureau has defined as at least 30 percent rural 33. Few new fuel stores fit under that loan size, but the purchase of an older small-town store can, and a study for one should state the county's rural share. Separately, the SOP treats agreements covered by the Petroleum Marketing Practices Act, including distributor, fuel supply, dealer and jobber agreements, as franchises 34, so the fuel supply contract goes through the same eligibility review as a store franchise. Our note on the c-store franchise category covers how that review reads the agreement.

USDA, banks and net-lease capital

USDA's Business and Industry guarantee is the rural alternative, and its rule differs in kind: for a guaranteed loan over $1,000,000 to a new business, the regulation requires an independent feasibility study 47. The program page lists an 85% guarantee below $5 million and 80% at or above, a 3% initial fee, a 0.55% annual retention fee and terms up to 40 years 45. Congressional testimony in September 2025 described the fiscal 2025 guarantee as 80% 46, so the percentage should be confirmed for the fiscal year of closing. We found no named B&I loan to a convenience store or fuel station. Our USDA Rural Development practice area sets out the five study components the rule defines.

Conventional terms are the least documented. No lender survey or bank disclosure in our evidence gives 2026 loan-to-value, coverage or pricing for fuel sites, and broker marketing is not market evidence. Chains finance new stores another way. Yesway's build-to-suit partners funded 29 projects between December 2023 and December 2025 at an average cap rate of 7.86% 7. That capital is priced on a chain's credit and is not available to an independent, which is the subject of most SBA files.

The most consistent reason a fuel loan stalls is environmental. Under SOP 50 10 8 every loan to a NAICS 457 business begins with a Phase I environmental site assessment regardless of the loan amount, and the assessment must satisfy the separate gas station appendix, including documented tank and equipment testing 3437. A lesser screen, such as an environmental questionnaire or a records search with risk assessment, will not do for a gas station at any size 35. Tank and line testing must show no deficiencies 38. Where the Phase I recommends more work, a Phase II follows, prepared by a licensed professional engineer or geologist, and a contaminated site needs a remediation cost estimate 3435. Anyone holding environmental indemnification rights must waive and release SBA and the lender through SBA's indemnification agreement 35.

The 2025 SOP widened the net. Environmental investigation now covers all commercial real estate collateral, secondary collateral included; reports must be dated within a year of the SBA loan number; and lenders with delegated authority lost the option of sending complex environmental files to general processing 3637. An environmental firm reads SOP 50 10 8.1 as leaving the environmental chapter unchanged in substance 39. Older SBA guidance asked for proof of $1 million of errors and omissions cover from the environmental professional 44; whether that figure carries into the current SOP was not verified, so the lender should check its own environmental policy.

The national tank record explains the caution. EPA counts approximately 534,000 active underground storage tanks 40, and its fiscal 2025 report shows 4,307 newly confirmed releases, 5,920 cleanups completed and 53,777 releases still awaiting cleanup at year-end 42. More than 581,000 releases have been confirmed since the program began 41. At mid-year only 61.0% of facilities met the technical compliance measure, against 87.7% for operator training and 90.0% for financial responsibility 43. In other words, the paperwork is usually in order while the equipment often is not, and the Phase I and the testing records are where that difference surfaces. State cleanup funds raise and spend approximately $1 billion a year 41, but what a fund will pay for a given release depends on the state's rules, which the environmental professional should read for the subject.

Environmental rules also change the value of existing stores. Yesway agreed to sell 29 Iowa and Kansas stores for $17.5 million plus inventory, citing impending capital spending that new Iowa regulations would require and that it judged uneconomic 7. For an acquisition the lesson is direct: the study should read tank ages, the testing and compliance history and any state upgrade mandate, and carry the capital they imply in the pro forma rather than leave it for the Phase I to discover. The feasibility consultant does not perform the Phase I or Phase II. The study schedules them, budgets a contingency, and states what the loan looks like if remediation turns out to be needed.

The second tier is the operator sale-leaseback, where the buyer underwrites the operating company rather than a rated parent: Yesway's build-to-suit program averaged 7.86% across its 29 projects 7. The third tier, the independent owner-operated station held in fee simple, is the collateral behind most SBA loans, and no source publishes a cap rate for it. Nor did we find a price per site for single-store sales or a market norm for splitting value between the real estate and the business. The nearest U.S. transaction evidence is a portfolio, 29 Iowa and Kansas stores sold for $17.5 million plus inventory 7, a price shaped by the regulatory capital those stores faced.

Operating companies trade on cash flow. Listed convenience operators traded at a median 9.3 times trailing EBITDA on September 10, 2026, below their ten-year median of 10.2 times, with a wide spread from Casey's at 16.0 times to ARKO at 5.2 times 23. A single store carries none of the scale, growth or diversification that sets those multiples, so they serve as a ceiling for reasonableness rather than a comparable.

For an SBA loan the appraisal does the valuation work. Special purpose property calls for a going-concern appraisal by a Certified General appraiser with recent going-concern experience on equivalent property, and the appraiser must allocate value among land, building, equipment and intangibles 34. Those terms reach us through a secondary quotation of SOP 50 10 8, so the engagement letter should cite the current SOP. On an acquisition, total acquisition debt, including seller debt not on full standby, is capped at the business valuation; any excess must be equity 31, and where the business purchase price is at or below $350,000 the lender may value the business itself. The feasibility study supplies what the appraisal capitalizes: a supportable stabilized income built from the subject's market, with the margin cycle priced in. Rates set the backdrop. The 10-year Treasury yield stood at 4.96% on September 11, 2026 and passed 5% three days later, its highest level since October 2023 23.

No federal or lender standard says how many people a gas station needs. Unlike hotels or senior housing, fuel retail has no codified demand ratio, and we found no regional per-capita, fair-share or capture benchmark from a primary source. What exists are national trade figures and the method practitioners build on them. NACS counts one store for every 2,257 residents on a 343 million population base, or 4.43 stores per 10,000 people 1. That ratio is a check on a finished analysis, not a way to produce one. A trade area with fewer residents per store may be saturated, or it may sit on a corridor that imports its customers, and only the traffic and competitor work can tell which. Our note on gas station demand per capita discusses the ratio and its limits.

The method, step by step

  1. Draw the trade area on the road network. Drive times, interchange geometry, medians and turning movements decide which cars can reach the pumps; a circle on a map counts customers who cannot turn in. Our note on why the trade area is the study sets out the approach.
  2. Size the fuel pool. Start from the vehicles based and commuting in the trade area and from the state transportation department's count stations on the roads that serve the site, naming each station and its count year. In 2025 the average vehicle used 457 gallons, bought 8.4 gallons per fill and came to a station 54.4 times 6, which turns a vehicle count into fill-ups and gallons.
  3. Census the competitors. List every fuel retailer in the trade area by name, fueling positions, brand, food program and hours, and include big-box fuel: 6,862 hypermarket and club sites sell about twice the volume per site of traditional retailers 6. Announced and permitted stores belong in the census too.
  4. Assign a fair share and a capture rate. Fair share follows fueling positions, adjusted for access, visibility, price position and brand; capture of pass-by traffic follows the count stations and the site's turning movements.
  5. Build inside sales from transactions. Estimate the fuel customers who come inside, transactions per day and the basket by category, and size foodservice to the kitchen and labor the operator will actually fund.
  6. Cross-check. Compare the result with residents per store, with the volumes of the format the subject most resembles, and with the operator's own record.

A pool that shrinks slowly

U.S. motor gasoline consumption averaged 8.9 million barrels a day in 2025, down 1% from 2024 and 4% from 2019, and EIA forecasts further declines in 2026 and 2027 54. Its forecast assumes fleet fuel economy improves about 1% a year while employment grows 0.3% and the working-age population 0.1% a year 54. EIA pairs a 1.2% rise in vehicle miles in 2025, as reported in federal highway data, with a 1.9% gain in implied fleet fuel economy 54; the highway agency's own year-end trend report gives a different cumulative mileage figure, so a study should cite one series and say which. For a new store the conclusion is plain. Gallons come from share, not from market growth, and a pro forma that grows per-store gallons faster than the trade area adds vehicles needs a stated reason.

Price and season

Price shapes the pro forma more than it shapes demand. EIA's October 2026 outlook puts the 2025 retail gasoline price at $3.10 a gallon and forecasts $3.91 for 2026 and $3.56 for 2027 55. Higher prices raise card fees and squeeze the inside basket, which is why the fuel price forecast belongs in the expense lines as well as the revenue line. Seasonality is modest at the national level: demand ran from 8.483 million barrels a day in January 2025 to a peak of 9.262 million in June, and August demand was 8.8% above January 6. A tourist corridor, a lake town or a harvest-season farm market swings far more, and the study should show monthly volume when the trade area is seasonal. No source publishes a monthly index for inside sales, so inside seasonality has to come from the operator or from comparable stores.

Bigger stores, more gallons each

Chains that build large stores are raising per-store volume in a flat market. Casey's average gallons sold per store rose from 1,102 in fiscal 2024 to 1,217 in fiscal 2026, in the units its 10-K table defines 56. That is share moving toward bigger, better-located stores, and it is the same competition a new independent store faces. Where a trade area lacks a modern store, a new one can take share quickly; our note on underserved gas station markets explains how to find those places and how to tell them from places that are simply small.

Subtypes

Six formats, six study scopes.

Each format has its own page with its own evidence. Truck stops and travel centers are a separate channel with their own hub.

Fuel retail is segmented along three lines at once, and the subtype pages follow them. One is format: a fuel store, a store without fuel, or a store built around food. Another is the supply contract, branded or unbranded, which applies to every fuel format. The third is location, and an interchange site can be any of the formats. The federal industry codes cut the market differently again, separating gasoline stations with convenience stores, other gasoline stations and convenience retailers without fuel, while NATSO treats travel plazas as a channel of their own. A study should say which of these lines its subject sits on, because each changes the evidence a reviewer expects.

Branded Fuel

A major-oil brand on the canopy brings a fuel supply agreement, image requirements and card programs, and SBA reviews that agreement as a franchise 34. No national count of branded sites exists, so the study proves the brand's value at the subject from competitors' prices and volumes. The page covers franchise disclosure costs and branded margins.

Unbranded and Independent

Independents set their own price and buy fuel on the rack or through a jobber. Companies with ten or fewer stores own 63.0% of U.S. stores 1, so this is the typical SBA borrower and the typical acquisition. The page covers dealer and consignment margins and the change-of-ownership test.

Convenience Without Fuel

Foodservice and QSR

Truck Stops and Travel Centers

Travel centers: a separate channel with its own hub and its own cases.

Diesel volume, truck parking and fleet contracts drive a travel center. The evidence is thinner than for convenience stores because the large operators are private or sit inside larger companies.

Travel centers serve professional drivers first, with diesel lanes, truck parking, showers and scales, and the store and restaurants attached. NATSO's members operate more than 5,000 travel plazas and truck stops along the Interstate system 70, and travel centers and truck stops provide more than ninety percent of publicly available truck parking 71. The channel is concentrated. Pilot ran 675 travel centers and 82 fuel-only locations at the end of 2025 69, and Love's reached 680 locations in 42 states by August 2026 68. Love's planned 20 new stores, 35 remodels and $700 million of spending for 2026, adding 1,500 truck parking spaces to a network of more than 52,000 and opening 23 RV stops, after 18 new locations in 2025 67.

Scale does not guarantee margin. Pilot sold about 10.9 billion gallons in 2025 69, but its revenue fell to $42.2 billion from $46.9 billion and its pre-tax earnings fell 69%, to $190 million from $614 million, by a trade-press reading of Berkshire Hathaway's annual report 72. Its ten largest diesel customers bought about 10% of its diesel gallons 69, a reminder that fleet contracts, not pass-by traffic, set much of a travel center's volume and price. ARKO's proprietary cardlock diesel margin rose to 49.0 cents a gallon in 2025 from 46.0 cents 73. EIA's October outlook puts retail diesel at $6.29 a gallon in September 2026 and forecasts $5.19 for 2026 and $4.49 for 2027 55, a price path that will move fleet fueling decisions over the first years of any new loan.

The gaps are larger here than for convenience stores. Unit development cost and acreage for travel centers were not found, and no named lender to a travel center appeared in our evidence. The same special purpose rules and equity tiers apply as for any fuel site. Our truck stop and travel center hub sets out the diesel and parking method, and two worked cases apply it: the Terrell truck stop on I-20 east of Dallas under SBA, and the Hankamer travel center on I-10 between Houston and Beaumont under USDA Business and Industry, which shows the rural guarantee on a diesel-led site.

Review Failures

How gas station and c-store studies fail review.

No source counts gas station studies rejected at review, so we cite none. These are the patterns the 2025 to 2026 evidence exposes, each tied to its mechanism.

  1. Capitalizing the 2026 fuel margin

    Listed operators earned 35.1 to 52.6 cents a gallon in the second quarter of 2026 on a supply shock 23. A study that sizes debt on those quarters lends against a price event. Coverage should hold at a through-cycle margin and be shown at the prior-year low.

  2. Gallons read off a traffic count

    A count is not a customer. Volume needs a fair share among named competitors, a capture rate tied to access and turning movements, and a check against the format's volumes. A broker's quoted daily traffic, with no count station or year, is not evidence.

  3. Inside profit the industry did not earn

    The average inside transaction lost money after expenses in 2025 5. A study that applies a mature foodservice margin in year one, or leaves card fees and shrink out of the inside expense lines, overstates the store.

  4. A cost budget built from a permit or a stale quote

    Permits omit tanks, dispensers, land and soft costs, and metals prices moved sharply after the 2025 tariff increases 1920. Sources and uses need a dated bid with fuel equipment broken out and an environmental contingency.

  5. The environmental gate treated as a closing item

    A Phase I is mandatory on every SBA gas station loan regardless of amount 34, and a recognized environmental condition can add a Phase II, a remediation estimate and months. A study that ignores tank age, testing history and state upgrade rules leaves the lender an unpriced risk.

  6. Entitlement assumed

    Moratoria, residential buffers, specific use permits and water-source protection zones decide whether fuel can be sold on a parcel 5962. A study that does not name the district and the permit path has not shown the project can open.

  7. An acquisition sold on the buyer's plan

    For most changes of ownership the coverage floor is tested on the seller's historical EBITDA 31; the special purpose exception needs an appraisal that fully supports the price. A study that leads with the buyer's growth plan instead of the seller's verified history misreads the test.

  8. The wrong value tier

    A credit-tenant net-lease cap rate is not the value of an independent store 48. Capitalizing an independent's income at a chain's lease rate overstates the collateral, and the going-concern appraisal will not support it.

Worked Cases

Five fuel and convenience cases, and what each one tests.

Each case is a representative worked example on a real town: the market evidence is public and dated, the site and sponsor are hypothetical, and the model and its inputs are published.

The five gas station cases were chosen so that each one tests a different part of the method on this page. The Lockhart, Texas case puts a new store on the US 183 corridor in Caldwell County, a county seat on the southern edge of the Austin metro, and shows how a study handles a growing town where a pending entrance-corridor overlay could add a specific use permit to the fuel use; the Texas market page gives the state context. The Lake Wales, Florida case sits on the US 27 corridor in Polk County, where a large travel stop already serves the highway, and shows how a competitor census separates the diesel and car markets before sizing a store under SBA 7(a).

The Opelika, Alabama case is the acquisition: an existing store at an I-85 exit, financed under the change-of-ownership rules at 1.25x on historical EBITDA, and it shows what a study can and cannot add when the test runs on the seller's history. The Cartersville, Georgia case places a new store one turn off the I-75 Exit 296 roundabouts, without interstate frontage, where the interchange itself is already served by diesel-led operators, and shows how a study narrows the concept to the car and commuter trade the site can win. The Versailles, Ohio case tests a new store in a Darke County village with a small trade area and a large employer, where the fuel component needs a conditional use permit and the village restricts underground petroleum storage in its drinking water protection zone.

Read together, the five show the same lesson from different directions: the market evidence sets the loan only after the site's gates are cleared, and each case ends with the lender conditions that its missing evidence produced. Gas Station Questions

Gas station and c-store feasibility questions.

Will an SBA lender ask for a feasibility study on a gas station?

Often, but the regulation leaves it to discretion: under 13 CFR 120.160(b) SBA may require an appraisal, a survey or a feasibility study 32. Lenders use that discretion on new builds, raze-and-rebuilds and format changes, where there is no operating history to underwrite. What the SOP makes mandatory on every gas station loan, at any amount, is a Phase I environmental site assessment that meets the gas station appendix 3437. The two documents answer different questions and come from different professionals.

What fuel margin should a 2026 gas station pro forma use?

Not the margin of the last two quarters. Listed operators earned 35.1 to 52.6 cents a gallon in the second quarter of 2026, against 29.2 to 44.9 cents a year earlier 23, and the jump followed a crude oil shock. A defensible base case sits near the multi-year averages, 39.3 cents for gasoline over five years on OPIS data 23 and 38.0 cents over the four years to 2024 on NACS data 6, adjusted for the subject's brand and competitors, with coverage also shown at the prior-year low.

How many residents does a gas station need to be feasible?

No federal or lender standard exists. Nationally there is one convenience store for every 2,257 residents 1, but density runs from 5.20 stores per 10,000 people in Texas to 3.09 in California 257, and an interchange site draws drivers who live elsewhere. A study uses residents per store only to check a demand estimate built from traffic counts, vehicles, named competitors and capture.

Is a gas station special purpose property for SBA 504?

Yes. Gas stations are on SBA's list of special purpose property examples 31, so a 504 borrower contributes at least 15%, rising to 20% when the business is also new. The classification also brings a going-concern appraisal that allocates value among land, building, equipment and intangibles 34.

How is an SBA loan to buy an existing station tested?

As a change of ownership. The buyer injects 10% of total project cost, and coverage of 1.25x is measured on the seller's historical EBITDA 31. For most businesses projections cannot meet that floor, but an owner-occupied special purpose acquisition whose price the appraisal fully supports may meet it on projections 31. The study tests whether the seller's history survives a new operator and new debt.

Why does the tank record matter before the market study?

Because it can end the loan first. EPA counted 53,777 tank releases still awaiting cleanup at the end of fiscal 2025, and only 61.0% of facilities met the technical compliance measure at mid-year 4243. A site with an open release, failed tightness tests or tanks facing a state upgrade mandate carries cost and delay that no market strength offsets, which is why the study schedules the Phase I and budgets for a Phase II.

Will fuel economy shrink gallons over a 25-year loan?

EIA expects U.S. gasoline consumption to keep falling in 2026 and 2027, mainly because fleet fuel economy improves about 1% a year while travel grows slowly 54. That argues for flat or declining gallons per vehicle in a pro forma, with growth coming only from share. Charging is a separate decision with its own subtype page, and we found no SBA or USDA underwriting rule written for it.

Can USDA Business and Industry finance a rural gas station?

It can, and it is the main rural alternative to SBA. The program page lists an 85% guarantee on loans under $5 million, a 3% initial fee, a 0.55% annual fee and terms up to 40 years 45, though September 2025 testimony gave the fiscal 2025 guarantee as 80% 46. Unlike SBA, USDA's rule requires an independent feasibility study for a guaranteed loan over $1,000,000 to a new business 47. We found no published B&I loan to a fuel station, so lender appetite should be confirmed early.
