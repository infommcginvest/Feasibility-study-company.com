The Fuel and Convenience Thesis

Underwrite the operator and the margin cycle, not the canopy.

On a loan application a gas station looks like real estate: land, a building, tanks in the ground and a canopy at the road. Underwritten that way, its risk is misread. The collateral is special purpose property that holds its value mainly as the business it was built for, and the income comes from two product lines with opposite economics. In 2025 fuel produced 65.0% of industry sales dollars but only 38.8% of gross profit dollars 3. Inside the store, the average transaction lost 7 cents after all expenses, against a 1-cent profit the year before, even though the basket grew 5. What carried the year was a fuel margin that averaged over 40 cents a gallon 4.

That margin is the first thing a 2026 study has to take apart. At the listed chains it widened to between 35.1 and 52.6 cents a gallon in the second quarter of 2026, from 29.2 to 44.9 cents a year earlier, as crude climbed after the conflict that began in late February 23. A pro forma that carries those quarters across a long amortization capitalizes a supply shock. The gallons are not growing either: U.S. gasoline consumption fell 1% in 2025, and the Energy Information Administration expects further declines through 2027 as the fleet becomes more efficient 49. A new store wins its gallons from an existing one.

The second thing is the gate list. Every SBA gas station loan begins with a Phase I environmental site assessment, whatever its size 31, because 53,777 tank releases still awaited cleanup at the end of fiscal 2025 37. Towns in Michigan, Ohio and Georgia, and a council member in Houston, have moved to pause new stations or push them away from homes 54565758. Neither gate is a market question, and either can stop a loan the market would support.

A useful study therefore measures demand as a share of a flat fuel pool against competitors it names, prices margin through a cycle, builds inside sales from what the site can actually serve, and puts the environmental, entitlement and fuel supply documents on the lender's checklist with their dates. This page sets out the national evidence for each step as of October 2026, covers the store formats, and ends with five worked cases in Texas, Florida, Alabama, Georgia and Ohio.

The only all-in cost a public operator has disclosed for a new fuel store is in Yesway's March 2026 registration statement: $10.0 to $12.0 million for a self-funded new-to-industry store, counting land, soft costs, site work, tanks and fuel equipment, the building, store equipment and signage 7. It describes one product, a 5,800-square-foot store on 3.7 acres with 27 dispensers, set against the 3,400-square-foot, 1.1-acre, 10-dispenser store in the operator's older fleet 7. That is far larger than the single store most SBA borrowers build. Under the same company's build-to-suit program the operator funds about a quarter, $2.5 to $3.0 million a store, and net-lease investors fund the rest 7; an SBA borrower has no such partner. The operator owns about 65% of its store real estate 7, and no source gives land or equipment as a share of project cost, so a study builds both from the subject's own land price and bids.

Building permits fill in part of the picture. A Wawa filed in Fort Wayne, Indiana in October 2026 carries a construction estimate of $1,350,000 for 6,374 square feet 14, and a Sheetz in Findlay, Ohio was permitted at $3.7 million for 6,139 square feet with the canopy and fuel system included 15. Two older Wawa permits, $3.1 million in Elizabeth City, North Carolina in 2023 16 and $915,400 in Jacksonville, Florida in 2022 17, predate the 2025 tariffs. Permit valuations usually leave out land, tanks, dispensers and soft costs, so their spread says more about what each counted than about regional cost. One contractor publishes $150 to $350 per square foot for building and canopy and $2.2 to $2.8 million all-in for a 3,000-square-foot store with four dispensers 18; it is marketing material, and only a bid for the subject settles it.

The direction of cost is clearer than its level. Producer prices for inputs to new nonresidential construction rose 7.1% in the year to June 2026, with steel mill products up 16.9%, aluminum mill shapes up 52.4% and copper and brass up 26.0% 19. Steel and aluminum duties doubled from 25% to 50% on June 4, 2025, and a 50% duty on copper-containing products followed on August 7, 2025 20. A second series showed inputs up 9.6% in the year to May 2026 21, and construction machinery prices were up 5.6% at the end of 2025 22. Canopy steel, tanks, piping, dispensers and electrical gear all sit in those indexes, so a quote dated before mid-2025 should be repriced.

Operators bracket the choice between building and buying. Casey's operating chief told investors in June 2026 that an acquired store costs about $1 million less than a new build at the same return requirement 10, and its fiscal 2026 budget of about $600 million backed an 80-store target split evenly between new builds and purchases of one-to-three-store retailers 11. Yesway's 2026 new-store budget is reported as $40 to $50 million for six to eight stores by the trade association 8 and $50 to $70 million by an investor-call aggregator 9, unreconciled, so a study should not borrow a chain's per-store cost. The lender should see the land contract or an appraisal, a contractor's bid dated after the 2025 tariff changes with tanks, dispensers, canopy and site work broken out, an environmental contingency and itemized soft costs; anything missing becomes a lender condition.

Fuel margin: three measures, one cycle

NACS reports a gross gasoline margin of 39.7 cents a gallon in 2024, 11.9% of a $3.33 average price, and a four-year average of 38.0 cents 6; its research director said margins averaged over 40 cents in 2025 4. OPIS data compiled by a bank give five-year averages of 39.3 cents for gasoline and 54.3 cents for diesel 23. The chains' own second-quarter 2026 margins ran from 35.1 cents at Murphy USA to 52.6 cents at Couche-Tard's U.S. business and at Yesway, against 29.2 to 44.9 cents a year earlier 23, with crude at $97.26 a barrel on September 9, 2026 and the New York Harbor gasoline crack spread about $1 a gallon above 2025 since May 23. The base case belongs near the multi-year averages, adjusted for the subject's brand and competitors. The downside uses the prior year's low. Margin above the averages is upside, not debt capacity.

Volume and inside sales

The average store sells about 4,000 gallons of gasoline a day 6, but format decides the spread: Yesway's new large stores averaged 3.1 million gallons a year against 1.0 million at its legacy stores, and earned $1.4 million of store contribution against $0.4 million 7. Same-store gallons in the second quarter of 2026 ran from minus 8.1% at CrossAmerica to plus 1.4% at Yesway 23. Gallons come from the subject's trade area, as the demand section describes, checked against the format it most resembles. Inside, foodservice was 28.5% of in-store sales and 38.9% of in-store gross profit in 2025 3, yet transactions per store fell 2.7% 3, fuel transactions reached their lowest since 2021 4, and the average inside transaction lost 7 cents after expenses despite a 24-cent rise in basket value 5. An industry-average inside margin applied to projected transactions, without card fees, shrink and food labor, shows a profit the industry did not earn that year.

Labor and operating expense

The industry averaged 19.9 employees per store at $15.04 an hour in 2025 3. Yesway runs about 2.6 employees per shift, and its salaries and benefits came to 7.5% of revenue 7, a ratio that swings with the pump price, so labor should be built from staffed hours and local wages. Card fees reached a record $21.3 billion and total expenses are up 23.3% since 2021 34; card fees scale with the pump price and need their own line. Murphy USA's store operating expense excluding payment fees and rent, $36,200 to $36,600 per store per month 12, is a floor for a lean, high-volume format. Third-party management fee norms and franchise Item 19 figures were not found, so the study takes the subject's actual agreements.

Lenders ask how SBA loans to gas stations have performed. As of October 2026 no published series answers the question. SBA reports program totals, not counts, volumes or charge-offs by industry code, and our evidence review found no SBA, Inspector General or GAO publication of a gas station charge-off or default rate. Industry rates that circulate online come from consulting websites, and we do not repeat them. This page prints no gas station loss rate and offers no all-industry rate in its place.

The data exists. SBA's 7(a) and 504 loan-level files are public, refreshed every quarter, current to June 30, 2026, and carry each loan's NAICS code 28. A query has to take both the older codes and their successors, 447110 and 457110 for gasoline stations with convenience stores and 447190 and 457120 for other gasoline stations, across the approval years it covers; convenience retailers without fuel sit in a separate retail code. A sound tabulation separates approval cohort, loan size, program, start-up against acquisition, and state, reports charge-offs by dollars and by count, and shows how many loans are still too young to have defaulted. Our notes on SBA default economics and special purpose property risk explain why the measurement basis matters and how special purpose collateral changes recovery after a default.

What can be cited is program-wide. SBA reported a record fiscal 2025 of 84,400 7(a) and 504 loans for $44.8 billion, while saying it had reduced its workforce by 43% and tightened underwriting 24. A data vendor counts 40,824 7(a) approvals for $21.8 billion in the first nine months of fiscal 2026, against 61,270 for $27.6 billion a year earlier 26, and the average 7(a) loan fell from $538,903 in fiscal 2022 to $443,097 in fiscal 2024 25. The Inspector General found 5,044 7(a) loans approved with unresolved hold codes 29, and the auditor of SBA's fiscal 2025 statements disclaimed an opinion on the balance sheet, citing weaknesses that included loan guaranty estimates 30. None of this is about fuel. It explains why reviewers ask where every number came from, and why a performance view belongs in the credit memorandum as a dated tabulation of the loan-level file.

The governing text is SOP 50 10 8.1 with Technical Policy Updates, effective October 1, 2026 31. It names gas stations among its examples of special purpose property, defined as a limited-market property with a unique physical design, special construction materials, or a layout that restricts its utility to the use for which it was built 31. On the study the regulation is unchanged: 13 CFR 120.160(b): SBA may require professional appraisals, a survey, or a feasibility study 32. Lenders ask for one on a fuel site when there is no operating history to underwrite. Our SBA 7(a) and 504 practice area covers the program rules in full.

7(a) for a new store

A new store financed with 7(a) needs 10% of total project cost as equity, and its projections must reach 1.15x coverage within two years 31. The loan can reach $5 million, with real estate amortized over up to 25 years. Year two carries the file: no published benchmark gives a ramp for new convenience stores, so the ramp is argued from the subject's competitors and the operator's own record.

7(a) for an acquisition

Equity is 10% of total project cost and cannot be waived, and coverage of 1.25x is measured on historical EBITDA over total post-transaction debt service, on the last fiscal year-end or the average of the last two 31. For most businesses projections are reviewed but cannot meet the floor, except an owner-occupied Special Purpose Property acquisition whose price the appraised value fully supports, where the floor may be met on projections within two years 31. That exception reaches gas stations directly and puts the appraisal and the study at the center of the file. Quality of earnings work is not required for Owner Buyout, ESOP & Cooperative, and any acquisition of an owner-occupied Special Purpose Property. The whole loan can run 25 years when 85% or more of the use of project cost is real estate and the station is integral to the business; otherwise the business portion amortizes over 10 years 31. The Opelika acquisition case works the historical test through.

504 and the combined limit

The 504 borrower contributes 10% in the standard case, 15% for a new business or a special purpose property, and 20% for both 31, so no fuel project sits in the bottom tier and a new operator building a station sits in the top one. The structure follows the contribution: Third Party Lender 50%, debenture 40%, borrower 10%; debenture 35% at a 15% contribution and 30% at 20%. Coverage is 1.15x, the debenture is capped at $5 million, and since July 4, 2026 a 7(a) loan followed by a 504 loan can reach $10 million combined 2731, which brings larger interchange formats within reach.

Fees, USDA and banks

For loans approved from October 1, 2026 the upfront 7(a) guaranty fee is 0% on loans of $700,000 or less to businesses in a rural area, meaning a county that the U.S. Census Bureau has defined as at least 30 percent rural 33. The purchase of an older small-town store can fit under that size, and its study should state the county's rural share. USDA's Business and Industry guarantee is the rural alternative, and its rule differs in kind: for a guaranteed loan over $1,000,000 to a new business it requires an independent feasibility study 42. The program page lists an 85% guarantee below $5 million and 80% at or above, a 3% initial fee, a 0.55% annual retention fee and terms up to 40 years 40, while September 2025 testimony gave the fiscal 2025 guarantee as 80% 41, so the percentage should be confirmed for the year of closing. Our USDA Rural Development practice area sets out the study components the rule defines. Conventional terms are the least documented: no lender survey or bank disclosure gives 2026 loan-to-value, coverage or pricing for fuel sites.

The most consistent reason a fuel loan stalls is environmental. Under SOP 50 10 8.1 every loan to a NAICS 457 business begins with a Phase I environmental site assessment regardless of the loan amount, and the assessment must satisfy the separate gas station appendix, including documented tank and equipment testing 31. An environmental questionnaire or a records search with risk assessment will not do for a gas station at any size 31, and tank and line testing must show no deficiencies 31. Where the Phase I recommends more work, a Phase II follows from a licensed professional engineer or geologist, a contaminated site needs a remediation cost estimate 31, and anyone holding environmental indemnification rights must waive and release SBA and the lender through SBA's indemnification agreement 31.

Timing matters as much as scope: under the current SOP an environmental investigation report must be dated within one year of the date SBA issues the loan number, so a report ordered early for a slow deal can expire before closing 31. An environmental firm reads SOP 50 10 8.1 as leaving the environmental chapter unchanged in substance from the 2025 edition 34.

The national tank record explains the caution. EPA counts approximately 534,000 active underground storage tanks 35, and its fiscal 2025 report shows 4,307 newly confirmed releases, 5,920 cleanups completed and 53,777 releases awaiting cleanup at year-end 37. At mid-year only 61.0% of facilities met the technical compliance measure, against 87.7% for operator training and 90.0% for financial responsibility 38: the paperwork is usually in order while the equipment often is not, and the Phase I and the testing records are where that difference surfaces.

Environmental rules also change the value of existing stores. Yesway agreed to sell 29 Iowa and Kansas stores for $17.5 million plus inventory, citing impending capital spending that new Iowa regulations would require and that it judged uneconomic 7. For an acquisition the study should read tank ages, the testing history and any state upgrade mandate, and carry the capital they imply in the pro forma. The feasibility consultant does not perform the Phase I or Phase II; the study schedules them, budgets a contingency, and states what the loan looks like if remediation is needed.

Value evidence for fuel sites comes in three tiers that should never be mixed. The first is the credit-tenant net lease, a long corporate lease priced on the tenant's balance sheet. Asking cap rates for 15-year leases in the first quarter of 2026 ran 4.90% to 5.20% for Wawa and 5.35% to 5.65% for Circle K, the 7-Eleven range had tightened to 4.80% to 5.15% by the third quarter, and a 7-Eleven lease with five years left asked 5.90% to 6.20% 4344. A listing aggregator found a median Wawa asking rate of 5.00% across 59 listings in October 2026 46, against 6.80% for single-tenant net lease as a whole in the first quarter 45. These rates say what an investor pays for a chain's promise to pay rent, not what a store is worth to the person running it.

The second tier is the operator sale-leaseback, where the buyer underwrites the operating company rather than a rated parent: Yesway's build-to-suit program averaged 7.86% across 29 projects 7. The third tier, the independent owner-operated station held in fee simple, secures most SBA loans, and no source publishes a cap rate for it, a price per site for single-store sales or a norm for splitting value between real estate and business. The nearest U.S. transaction evidence is a portfolio of 29 Iowa and Kansas stores sold for $17.5 million plus inventory 7, a price shaped by the regulatory capital those stores faced.

Operating companies trade on cash flow. Listed convenience operators traded at a median 9.3 times trailing EBITDA on September 10, 2026, below their ten-year median of 10.2 times, with a spread from Casey's at 16.0 times to ARKO at 5.2 times 23. Couche-Tard's agreement to buy Poland's Żabka, announced on July 31, 2026, was priced at about 9.3 times trailing adjusted EBITDA on an enterprise value near $11.0 billion 23. A single store carries none of the scale or growth behind those multiples, so they are a ceiling for reasonableness, not a comparable.

For an SBA loan the appraisal does the valuation work. Special purpose property calls for a going-concern appraisal by a Certified General appraiser with recent going-concern experience on equivalent property, allocating value among land, building, equipment and intangibles 31. SOP 50 10 8.1 asks for an appraiser with at least four going-concern appraisals of equivalent special use property in the past 36 months, which the engagement letter should require by name 31. On an acquisition, total acquisition debt, including seller debt not on full standby, is capped at the business valuation; any excess must be equity 31, and where the business purchase price is at or below $350,000 the lender may value the business itself. The study supplies the stabilized income the appraisal capitalizes. No federal or lender standard says how many people a gas station needs. Fuel retail has no codified demand ratio, and we found no regional per-capita, fair-share or capture benchmark from a primary source. NACS counts one store for every 2,257 residents on a 343 million population base, or 4.43 stores per 10,000 people 1. That ratio checks a finished analysis rather than producing one: a trade area with fewer residents per store may be saturated or may sit on a corridor that imports its customers. Our note on gas station demand per capita discusses the ratio's limits.

The method, step by step

  1. Draw the trade area on the road network. Drive times, interchange geometry, medians and turning movements decide which cars can reach the pumps; a circle on a map counts customers who cannot turn in. Our note on why the trade area is the study sets out the approach.
  2. Size the fuel pool. Start from the vehicles based and commuting in the trade area and from the state transportation department's count stations on the roads that serve the site, naming each station and its count year. In 2025 the average vehicle used 457 gallons, bought 8.4 gallons per fill and came to a station 54.4 times 6, which turns a vehicle count into fill-ups.
  3. Census the competitors. List every fuel retailer by name, fueling positions, brand, food program and hours, including big-box fuel: 6,862 hypermarket and club sites sell about twice the volume per site of traditional retailers 6. Announced and permitted stores belong in the census too.
  4. Assign a fair share and a capture rate from fueling positions, adjusted for access, visibility, price position and brand.
  5. Build inside sales from transactions. Estimate the fuel customers who come inside, transactions per day and the basket by category, and size foodservice to the kitchen and labor the operator will fund.
  6. Cross-check the result against residents per store, the volumes of the format the subject most resembles, and the operator's own record.

A pool that shrinks slowly

U.S. motor gasoline consumption averaged 8.9 million barrels a day in 2025, down 1% from 2024 and 4% from 2019, and EIA forecasts further declines in 2026 and 2027, assuming fleet fuel economy improves about 1% a year while employment grows 0.3% and the working-age population 0.1% a year 49. EIA pairs a 1.2% rise in vehicle miles in 2025 with a 1.9% gain in implied fuel economy 49, while the highway agency's own year-end count shows miles up 0.9%, or 29.8 billion vehicle-miles 72; a study should cite one series and say which. Either way gallons come from share, not market growth, and a pro forma that grows per-store gallons faster than the trade area adds vehicles needs a stated reason.

Price and season

Price shapes the pro forma more than demand. EIA's October 2026 outlook puts the 2025 retail gasoline price at $3.10 a gallon, reports $4.35 for September 2026, and forecasts $3.91 for 2026 and $3.56 for 2027 50. Higher prices raise card fees and squeeze the inside basket, so the study should state the price its revenue line assumes. National seasonality is modest: demand ran from 8.483 million barrels a day in January 2025 to a peak of 9.262 million in June, and August was 8.8% above January 6. A tourist corridor, a lake town or a harvest-season farm market swings far more, and the study should then show monthly volume. No source publishes a monthly index for inside sales.

Bigger stores, more gallons each

Chains that build large stores are raising per-store volume in a flat market: Casey's average gallons sold per store rose from 1,102 in fiscal 2024 to 1,217 in fiscal 2026, in the units its 10-K table defines 51. That is share moving toward bigger, better-located stores, the same competition a new independent store faces. Where a trade area lacks a modern store, a new one can take share quickly; our note on underserved gas station markets explains how to find those places and tell them from places that are simply small.

How lenders underwrite it. Our note on the c-store franchise category covers how the franchise review reads the contract.

Where it fails review. Branded files come back for four reasons: a base case priced at the peak margin year; a supply agreement that ends or can be reassigned before the loan matures; a supplier that keeps environmental indemnity rights nobody waived through SBA's indemnification agreement 31; and loan sizing that leans on a value including brand goodwill. The study answers each by showing coverage at the lower margin year and at the net margin, naming the supplier, its owner and the contract's end date, reading the contract for indemnity rights before closing, and showing coverage on the tangible assets alone.

Truck Stops and Travel Centers

Travel centers: a separate channel with its own hub and its own cases.

Diesel volume, truck parking and fleet contracts drive a travel center. The evidence is thinner than for convenience stores because the large operators are private or sit inside larger companies.

Travel centers serve professional drivers first, with diesel lanes, truck parking, showers and scales, and the store and restaurants attached. NATSO's members operate more than 5,000 travel plazas and truck stops along the Interstate system 65, and travel centers and truck stops provide more than ninety percent of publicly available truck parking 66. The channel is concentrated. Pilot ran 675 travel centers and 82 fuel-only locations at the end of 2025 64, and Love's reached 680 locations in 42 states by August 2026 63. Love's planned 20 new stores, 35 remodels and $700 million of spending for 2026, adding 1,500 truck parking spaces to a network of more than 52,000 and opening 23 RV stops, after 18 new locations in 2025 62.

Scale does not guarantee margin. Pilot sold about 10.9 billion gallons in 2025 64, but its revenue fell to $42.2 billion from $46.9 billion and its pre-tax earnings fell 69%, to $190 million from $614 million, by a trade-press reading of Berkshire Hathaway's annual report 67. Its ten largest diesel customers bought about 10% of its diesel gallons 64, a reminder that fleet contracts set much of a travel center's volume and price. ARKO's proprietary cardlock diesel margin rose to 49.0 cents a gallon in 2025 from 46.0 cents 68. EIA's October outlook puts retail diesel at $6.29 a gallon in September 2026 and forecasts $5.19 for 2026 and $4.49 for 2027 50, a path that will move fleet fueling decisions in the first years of any new loan.

Unit development cost and acreage for travel centers were not found, and no named lender to a travel center appeared in our evidence; the same special purpose rules and equity tiers apply as for any fuel site. Our truck stop and travel center hub sets out the diesel and parking method, and two worked cases apply it: the Terrell truck stop on I-20 east of Dallas under SBA, and the Hankamer travel center on I-10 between Houston and Beaumont under USDA Business and Industry, which shows the rural guarantee on a diesel-led site.

Review Failures

How gas station and c-store studies fail review.

No source counts gas station studies rejected at review, so we cite none. These are the patterns the 2025 to 2026 evidence exposes, each tied to its mechanism.

  1. Capitalizing the 2026 fuel margin

    Listed operators earned 35.1 to 52.6 cents a gallon in the second quarter of 2026 on a supply shock 23. A study that sizes debt on those quarters lends against a price event. Coverage should hold at a through-cycle margin and be shown at the prior-year low.

  2. Gallons read off a traffic count

    A count is not a customer. Volume needs a fair share among named competitors, a capture rate tied to access and turning movements, and a check against the format's volumes. A broker's quoted daily traffic, with no count station or year, is not evidence.

  3. Inside profit the industry did not earn

    The average inside transaction lost money after expenses in 2025 5. A study that applies a mature foodservice margin in year one, or leaves card fees and shrink out of the inside expense lines, overstates the store.

  4. A cost budget built from a permit or a stale quote

    Permits omit tanks, dispensers, land and soft costs, and metals prices moved sharply after the 2025 tariff increases 1920. Sources and uses need a dated bid with fuel equipment broken out and an environmental contingency.

  5. The environmental gate treated as a closing item

    A Phase I is mandatory on every SBA gas station loan regardless of amount 31, and a recognized environmental condition can add a Phase II, a remediation estimate and months. Ignoring tank age, testing history and state upgrade rules leaves the lender an unpriced risk.

  6. Entitlement assumed

    Moratoria, residential buffers, specific use permits and water-source protection zones decide whether fuel can be sold on a parcel 5457. A study that does not name the district and the permit path has not shown the project can open.

  7. An acquisition sold on the buyer's plan

    For most changes of ownership the coverage floor is tested on the seller's historical EBITDA 31; the special purpose exception needs an appraisal that fully supports the price. Leading with the buyer's growth plan instead of the seller's verified history misreads the test.

  8. The wrong value tier

    A credit-tenant net-lease cap rate is not the value of an independent store 43. Capitalizing an independent's income at a chain's lease rate overstates the collateral, and the going-concern appraisal will not support it.

Gas Station Questions

Gas station and c-store feasibility questions.

Will an SBA lender ask for a feasibility study on a gas station?

Often, but the regulation leaves it to discretion: under 13 CFR 120.160(b) SBA may require an appraisal, a survey or a feasibility study 32. Lenders use that discretion on new builds, raze-and-rebuilds and format changes, where there is no operating history to underwrite. What the SOP makes mandatory on every gas station loan, at any amount, is a Phase I environmental site assessment that meets the gas station appendix 31. The two documents answer different questions and come from different professionals.

What fuel margin should a 2026 gas station pro forma use?

Not the margin of the last two quarters. Listed operators earned 35.1 to 52.6 cents a gallon in the second quarter of 2026, against 29.2 to 44.9 cents a year earlier 23, and the jump followed a crude oil shock. A defensible base case sits near the multi-year averages, 39.3 cents for gasoline over five years on OPIS data 23 and 38.0 cents over the four years to 2024 on NACS data 6, adjusted for the subject's brand and competitors, with coverage also shown at the prior-year low.

How many residents does a gas station need to be feasible?

No federal or lender standard exists. Nationally there is one convenience store for every 2,257 residents 1, but density runs from 5.20 stores per 10,000 people in Texas to 3.09 in California 252, and an interchange site draws drivers who live elsewhere. A study uses residents per store only to check a demand estimate built from traffic counts, vehicles, named competitors and capture.

Is a gas station special purpose property for SBA 504?

Yes. Gas stations are on SBA's list of special purpose property examples 31, so a 504 borrower contributes at least 15%, rising to 20% when the business is also new. The classification also brings a going-concern appraisal that allocates value among land, building, equipment and intangibles 31.

How is an SBA loan to buy an existing station tested?

As a change of ownership. The buyer injects 10% of total project cost, and coverage of 1.25x is measured on the seller's historical EBITDA 31. For most businesses projections cannot meet that floor, but an owner-occupied special purpose acquisition whose price the appraisal fully supports may meet it on projections 31. The study tests whether the seller's history survives a new operator and new debt.

Why does the tank record matter before the market study?

Because it can end the loan first. EPA counted 53,777 tank releases still awaiting cleanup at the end of fiscal 2025, and only 61.0% of facilities met the technical compliance measure at mid-year 3738. A site with an open release, failed tightness tests or tanks facing a state upgrade mandate carries cost and delay that no market strength offsets, which is why the study schedules the Phase I and budgets for a Phase II.

Will fuel economy shrink gallons over a 25-year loan?

EIA expects U.S. gasoline consumption to keep falling in 2026 and 2027, mainly because fleet fuel economy improves about 1% a year while travel grows slowly 49. That argues for flat or declining gallons per vehicle in a pro forma, with growth coming only from share. Charging is a separate decision with its own subtype page, and we found no SBA or USDA underwriting rule written for it.

Can USDA Business and Industry finance a rural gas station?

It can, and it is the main rural alternative to SBA. The program page lists an 85% guarantee on loans under $5 million, a 3% initial fee, a 0.55% annual fee and terms up to 40 years 40, though September 2025 testimony gave the fiscal 2025 guarantee as 80% 41. Unlike SBA, USDA's rule requires an independent feasibility study for a guaranteed loan over $1,000,000 to a new business 42. We found no published B&I loan to a fuel station, so lender appetite should be confirmed early.
