The Multifamily Thesis

A lender on an apartment building is underwriting a lease-up and a refinance.

An apartment building of five or more units has no operating business behind it in the sense an SBA lender means. The borrower is a landlord, the income is rent from households who sign leases of a year or less, and the loan is repaid from net operating income and, at maturity, from a refinance or a sale. Federal small business programs treat that as passive real estate and do not lend on it 68; the capital section sets out the SBA rule once, with the edition now in force. The debt for this asset therefore comes from Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac, HUD-insured lenders, banks, insurers and debt funds. In 2025 a total of 2,530 lenders originated $381.8 billion of multifamily mortgages 5.

The question a 2026 study has to answer is narrower than "is there demand for apartments". National demand exists. What a credit officer needs to know is how fast this building fills, at what rent after concessions, with what expense load, and whether the stabilized income supports the permanent loan that is supposed to take out the construction lender. Completions of units in buildings of five or more units fell to an annual rate of 302,000 in August 2026, down 35.7% from the rate a year earlier 1, so the delivery wave is behind most markets. Credit has not followed: Trepp's multifamily CMBS delinquency rate reached 7.69% in July 2026 4, and Freddie Mac's multifamily serious delinquency rate stood at 0.64% in August 2026 20. Those loans were made on rent and exit assumptions that a study should have tested.

This page sets out the national evidence a lender-grade apartment study rests on as of October 2026: development cost per unit from public filings, operating benchmarks, loan performance, the agency and bank capital routes, valuation, industry structure, the demand method and supply. Five building and program types are treated in full in the subtypes section, and six worked cases apply the method in Arizona, Florida, Georgia, New York, North Carolina and Texas. Single-family rental communities, manufactured housing communities and mixed-use projects have their own hubs, linked below.

The filings state totals and home counts. We do not divide one by the other: the filings do not state building type, several projects include commercial space or structured parking, and some totals are estimates that later moved.

The cleanest public cost evidence for 2025 comes from a city. Detroit's housing department compared four recent multifamily projects and found costs between $278,000 and $331,000 per unit, with a composite 47-unit project at $14,310,000 13. The study is useful because it is itemized and recent, and limited because it describes small urban infill in one Midwestern city, financed with public gap money, not the suburban garden community most agency files carry.

The real estate investment trusts fill in the Sun Belt. Camden reported three communities under construction at the end of 2025 totalling 1,162 homes, with $492.0 million of estimated cost and $278.2 million already incurred 22. That gap between estimate and spend is the number a construction lender cares about, because cost to complete is where overruns live. Camden's own Charlotte estimate moved within one quarter, from $163.0 million at September 30, 2025 21 to $157.0 million at December 31 for the same 420 homes 22. A sponsor's budget is a forecast, and a study should show its date, its contingency and the contractor's form of contract. Camden's other filings put a 393-home Nashville community at $184.0 million 23 and Raleigh and Charlotte communities of 369 and 349 homes at $138.0 million and $154.0 million 24, while a 498-home Nashville project of unstated type carries $300.0 million 21. MAA's pipeline held 8 projects and 2,522 units at year-end 2025 25.

Coastal and urban product costs more, and the filings show it without saying why. AvalonBay's pipeline of 24 communities and 8,572 homes was budgeted at $3,307 million 26; two of its communities in Maryland and Florida, with 612 homes and commercial space, cost $287.0 million combined 26. Equity Residential started a 450-unit New York joint venture at $201.2 million in 2025 28. RAND's comparison of projects built between 2015 and 2024 found California costs more than twice those in Texas, with municipal fees of $12,000 a unit in Colorado and under $1,000 in Texas 30. A cost per unit borrowed from another state or another building form is not evidence for the subject.

What is not published

No public source gives the land share of total development cost, the share of furniture, fixtures and equipment, or a 2025 to 2026 split between hard, soft and land cost. Camden's balance sheet carries land at $1,784,349 thousand 23, which describes a portfolio, not a project. The only published split is the 2022 developer survey that attributed 40.6% of multifamily development cost to regulation 29, an older figure that mixes fees, delay and design mandates. Equipment lead times were not found either. The study therefore takes land from the purchase contract or appraisal, hard cost from a dated bid, and soft cost from an itemized schedule.

Tariffs and the dated quote

Material prices moved after early 2025. Duties on Canadian softwood lumber rose from 14.5% to 35%, a further 10% Section 232 tariff applies to timber and lumber, and steel and aluminum carry a 50% tariff 15. Framing lumber, rebar, structural steel for podiums and aluminum storefront all sit inside an apartment budget, so a guaranteed maximum price signed before mid-2025, or an estimate built from square-foot rules, should be repriced before a lender relies on it.

Revenue, margin and the gaps

No primary national source publishes 2025 revenue per unit, an operating expense ratio, an NOI margin, payroll as a share of expense, or a norm for third-party management fees. We state those as gaps and do not fill them with consultant estimates. The nearest public evidence is fragmentary. Camden collected $388,773 thousand of property revenue in the first quarter of 2026 across 58,811 homes 23, a large, mostly Sun Belt, Class A portfolio. New York City's rent board found net operating income of $626 a month per rent-stabilized unit in 2023 33, before the 2026 freeze discussed under mid- and high-rise. For new product, the Census absorption survey reported a median asking rent of $2,034 a month for apartments completed in the fourth quarter of 2025 2.

Asking rent is not collected rent. RealPage counted concessions on 15.4% of stabilized units in August 2026 34, and lease-up buildings usually offer more than stabilized ones. The study should set rent from named comparables, show the concession each one offers, and carry effective rent into the pro forma. Our note on effective rent against asking rent sets out the arithmetic.

Loan Performance

How apartment loans perform: the SBA file, the agencies and CMBS.

SBA does not lend on apartment buildings, yet its loan file holds loans coded to the residential lessor class. The performance that matters for this asset is in agency books, CMBS and bank filings.

Readers sometimes expect an SBA loss rate for apartments. There is no clean one, because the asset is ineligible, but the loan-level file is not empty either. SBA's 7(a) file lists 205 loans for $121.6 million under the residential lessor class over the ten fiscal years to 2025, and the 504 file lists 11 loans. The class is NAICS 531110, lessors of residential buildings and dwellings. It holds apartment owners, but also landlords of houses, duplexes and other residential buildings, and borrowers whose code was entered loosely, so these loans cannot be read as SBA financing for apartment buildings. Among seasoned 7(a) cohorts in the class, the charge-off rate by count is 3.9%, on a small base.

The stress sits where leverage met floating or resetting rates. In CMBS, the multifamily delinquency rate rose from 6.86% in August 2025, then a nine-year high, to 6.94% in January 2026 and 7.69% in July 2026 36374, and matured loans that could not refinance made up 66% of newly delinquent balances in July 4. Special servicing of multifamily CMBS had already reached 8.61% in August 2025 36. The agency books are far cleaner and moved in different directions. Fannie Mae's serious delinquency was 0.78% at March 31, 2026, and the company warned it could rise later in the year 38; instead it fell to 0.60% at June 30 after a portfolio modification and foreclosures, though the company still expects more delinquencies 41. Freddie Mac's rate rose, reaching 0.64% in August 2026, against 0.48% a year earlier and a prior August peak of 0.35% in 2011 20.

The bank evidence is concentrated in New York City. Flagstar reported $8.8 billion of loans on buildings at least half rent-regulated in the first quarter of 2026, of which $4.3 billion were criticized or classified 39, and in July it put about $11 billion of multifamily loans, at a weighted average coupon of about 3.90%, as resetting or maturing between June 30, 2026 and December 31, 2027 40. Low coupons written years ago are the problem: a building that covered its debt at the old rate may not cover a new loan at today's rate, whatever its occupancy. That is why a refinance study tests coverage at the takeout lender's current rate and sizing, as our note on coverage-constrained takeout works through.

No SBA Inspector General finding on residential rental loans was found, and no USDA multifamily finding either. HUD's own Inspector General found in 2024 that HUD did not issue notices within 15 days to 45% of sampled properties that failed to remedy physical deficiencies 42, which is why inspection history belongs in the file for any HUD-assisted or HUD-insured acquisition.

The agencies dominate the permanent market and set the terms everyone else prices against. FHFA raised the 2026 multifamily purchase cap to $88 billion for each of Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac, from $73 billion, and kept workforce-housing loans outside the caps 43. Within the caps, at least 50% of volume must be mission-driven, which favors income-restricted units. MBA counted $381.8 billion of multifamily originations in 2025 5, so agency appetite and pricing move the whole market.

For a smaller building the Fannie Mae Small Loan program is the most common agency route.

HUD's 221(d)(4) program is the long-dated construction-to-permanent alternative. Mortgagee Letter 2026-1 sets market-rate leverage at 87% of cost and coverage at 1.15x 16, HUD cut the mortgage insurance premium to 0.25% upfront and annually for applications from October 1, 2025, from as much as 95 basis points before 1744. The cost is time: HUD's market study and underwriting run longer than a bank's. HUD's MAP Guide calls for a market study on new construction and substantial rehabilitation and says none is required for a 223(f) refinance 9.

Construction lenders do not publish their terms. We found no 2026 survey of bank loan-to-cost, spread or recourse for apartment construction, so a study cannot cite a market norm and should instead show the takeout: stabilized NOI at the permanent lender's vacancy and expense assumptions, divided by the coverage floor, sized at the current rate. If that loan does not repay the construction loan, the gap is equity the sponsor needs on day one. The conventional and institutional practice covers bank, life company and agency studies, and the EB-5 practice covers projects that raise part of their equity from immigrant investors and must count jobs.

The SBA question

Lenders still ask whether SBA can finance housing. SOP 50 10 8, the June 2025 edition of SBA's lending rules, lists apartment buildings as ineligible for 7(a) and 504 6, the regulation excludes passive businesses owned by developers and landlords who do not use the assets 7, and USDA's Business and Industry guarantee excludes rental housing 8. A lender should confirm the same SBA wording in the edition now in force, SOP 50 10 8.1. The only SBA route to residential space is a building the business itself occupies: an existing building where the business uses at least 51% of the space, or new construction where it uses 60% 6. That owner-occupancy route, and the income-share tests for projects whose income comes from more than one use, are covered on the mixed-use hub; an apartment building with incidental commercial space, financed as multifamily, stays on this page. The regulation that lets SBA ask for a study, 13 CFR 120.160(b): SBA may require professional appraisals, a survey, or a feasibility study 45, does not reach an apartment loan at all; our note on the SBA feasibility study myth covers what it does say.

The market study for an apartment project rests on four measures. The capture rate is the share of age, size and income-qualified renter households in the primary market area that the subject must lease to reach stabilized occupancy. The penetration rate is the share that all existing and proposed comparable units must capture together. The absorption period runs from the first certificate of occupancy to the signed lease on the last unit needed for stabilization, and the trade association's convention assumes three to six months of pre-marketing before it 64. Market occupancy closes the loop. HUD's own research office notes the absence of a fully developed national practice standard for market analysis 18, so thresholds are set program by program.

State housing finance agencies publish the most explicit thresholds. Illinois updated its standards on August 3, 2026, treating capture of 5% for family projects and 7% for elderly projects as historically acceptable and expecting overall affordable penetration under 25% 10. Those are one state's rules for tax-credit housing, not a national norm, but they show what a reviewer reads: capture by income band, not a single blended figure. Market-rate agency lenders apply the same logic less formally, through rent comparables and absorption evidence.

Building the count

Demand starts with households, not population. The 2024 American Community Survey counted 46,101,640 renter-occupied units nationally and 28,579,477 units in buildings of five or more 60, out of 146,740,964 housing units 60. A study repeats that count for the primary market area: renter households by income band and age, the share in multifamily buildings, household growth from the census estimates, and the turnover that frees units each year. It then subtracts the competing supply, every lease-up and under-construction project in the trade area, and checks the result against absorption the comparables actually achieved. Our note on why the trade area is the study covers the boundary, which moves the capture rate more than any other choice.

Absorption is the test

The Census Survey of Market Absorption is the public record of how fast new apartments lease. Of apartments completed in the fourth quarter of 2025, 49% were leased within three months and 90% within twelve 2, against 61% and 96% for 2014 completions 11, and the three-month figure fell to 41% for the first quarter of 2026 12. The survey covered 77,380 completed units in the fourth quarter of 2025 2. Subtypes

Five apartment formats treated here, three neighbors with their own hubs.

Lenders segment apartments by building form, by rent tier and by specialty product. Each changes the study's demand pool, its cost evidence and its capital route.

An apartment study has to say which segment the subject belongs to before it can pick its comparables. Building form separates garden-style communities from mid-rise and high-rise buildings. Rent tier separates market-rate units from workforce housing with moderate income limits and tax-credit housing with deep ones. Specialty product separates student housing, leased by the bed on an academic cycle. All five are treated in full below, each with its own evidence.

Three neighbors have their own hubs. Single-family and townhome rental communities built for rent are financed by single-family rental lenders and studied against house rents, on the build-to-rent hub. Manufactured housing communities lease pads, not units, under tenant site lease protections, and are covered on the manufactured housing hub. Projects whose income comes from more than one use, and the income-share and occupancy tests that sort them, including SBA's owner-occupancy route, sit on the mixed-use hub; an apartment building whose commercial space stays within the agencies' limits on commercial income, which the mixed-use hub sets out, stays here. Age-restricted independent apartments use the elderly thresholds described under demand; licensed care belongs to the assisted living hub.

Subtype: Garden-Style

Garden-style communities: the agency staple, with lease-up as the weak point.

Two and three-story walk-up buildings on a suburban site, surface parking and a clubhouse. No public source separates garden product, so its evidence is the national lease-up record.

How it earns. A garden community earns rent from a large number of mid-sized units on cheap land, with surface parking and wood-frame construction keeping cost per unit below urban product. No public source isolates garden-style rent, revenue per unit or margin, and the Census absorption survey has no garden category. The format is nonetheless where most new suburban supply has gone, and multifamily construction for rent remains heavy: 95% of multifamily starts in the fourth quarter of 2025 were built for rent 62. The REIT Sun Belt pipelines in the development section are largely suburban, but their filings do not state building type, so we cite them as apartment costs and not as garden costs.

What it costs. Garden product's advantage is site cost and simple structure, and its exposure is the same tariff-driven lumber and framing costs as any wood building. No source gives garden acreage, density, parking ratios or staffing norms; state agency site standards list parking ratio, units per acre and access as review items without setting numbers 10. The study takes those from the subject's zoning and site plan.

How lenders underwrite it. Garden communities are the core of agency lending. A bank construction loan is sized to the expected Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac takeout, and HUD 221(d)(4) offers 87% of cost at 1.15x coverage for sponsors who can wait 16. The coverage test at the takeout rate, with the concession burden of a competitive suburban submarket, usually binds before loan to value.

Where it fails review. Garden files fail on lease-up. Only 49% of apartments completed in the fourth quarter of 2025 were leased within three months 2, and new garden communities in the same submarket deliver in waves. A study that assumes the pre-2020 pace, ignores competing lease-ups within a few miles, or carries asking rent without concessions overstates the first two years and the takeout. The second failure is Class C competition in the South, where older properties cut rents sharply in 2026 67 and pull price-sensitive renters away from new product.

Which cases show it. The Florida case tests a market-rate garden community against its competitive pipeline, and the Georgia case a garden or mid-rise community in a metro-Atlanta suburb.

Subtype: Workforce Housing

Workforce housing: a financing category defined by income limits, not a building type.

There is no single federal definition. Two federal regimes now reward income restrictions between the tax-credit band and the market: FHFA's cap exemption and HUD's Middle Income option.

How it earns. Workforce units rent at levels set by a state or local program for households earning roughly between the tax-credit limit and the market. HUD describes the usual band as 60% to 120% of area median income, reaching 150% in some high-cost areas 16. The building may look like any market-rate garden or mid-rise property; what changes is a recorded restriction on who may rent and at what price. No public source publishes cost per unit, revenue per unit or margin for workforce properties, and we found no 2025 count of workforce loan volume.

What it costs. Because the product is a covenant, not a format, cost follows the building form, and the evidence in the development section applies. The covenant's cost is in the rent: units held at or below an income band give up the premium a market-rate lease-up might capture.

How lenders underwrite it. The restriction buys cheaper or larger debt. Under HUD Mortgagee Letter 2026-1 a property with at least 50% of units restricted at or below 120% of area median income, under a recorded restriction of at least 10 years, qualifies for the Middle Income option at 90% of cost and 1.11x coverage, against 87% and 1.15x at market rate, with a 7% vacancy factor in underwriting 16. FHFA's 2026 definitions count a loan as 50% mission-driven when fewer than half of its units are income-restricted and 100% when at least half are 6980. Workforce-preservation loans sit outside the agencies' 2026 caps when affordability is preserved for at least 10 years, and for those loans the definitions give full mission credit when at least 20% of units are restricted 4369; the income thresholds are 80% of area median income generally and 100% in cost-burdened areas 69. The study must therefore show demand at the restricted rents, not at market rents.

Where it fails review. We found no published review-failure evidence specific to workforce housing. The mechanisms follow from the rules. A restriction that is promised but not recorded, or that runs shorter than the program requires, loses the leverage or the cap exemption. Restricted rents set from area median income without checking them against market rents can leave restricted units priced above what the market charges, in which case the restriction costs nothing and demand is no deeper than for market-rate units. And a pro forma that ignores the HUD vacancy factor will not size the way HUD sizes it.

Which cases show it. None of the six worked cases uses a workforce restriction; the case list shows market-rate agency structures that a restriction would modify.

Review Failures

How apartment feasibility studies fail review.

Nobody publishes how many apartment studies agencies or lenders send back, and we invent no count. Each item below pairs a weakness the 2025 to 2026 data reveal with the way it distorts the loan.

  1. Lease-up at the old pace

    Three-month absorption of new apartments fell to 41% in the first quarter of 2026 12. A study that schedules lease-up at the 2014 or 2021 pace shortens the construction loan's interest carry and overstates the takeout. The schedule should come from the comparables' own lease-ups.

  2. Asking rent in place of effective rent

    Concessions ran on 15.4% of stabilized units in August 2026 and are deeper in lease-up 34. A pro forma built on asking rents overstates income in exactly the years the construction lender is exposed.

  3. Expenses grown at inflation

    Tax-credit properties saw operating expenses rise 10.5% in 2024 and insurance far faster 7476. Insurance, taxes after reassessment and payroll need their own lines and their own growth rates.

  4. A takeout sized on loan to value

    A study that shows only value at stabilization, not the loan that coverage supports, hides the equity gap at refinance.

  5. Competing supply left out

    Starts in early 2026 ran ahead of 2025 1, and lease-ups in the same submarket compete for the same renters. A trade area drawn without every delivered, under-construction and permitted project understates penetration.

  6. A cost per unit borrowed from somewhere else

    Published costs range from a Detroit infill average to a coastal tower at $573,000 per unit 1314. Only a dated bid for the subject, repriced after the 2025 tariffs 15, supports the budget.

  7. Rent regulation treated as a footnote

    New York City froze stabilized renewals in June 2026 68. A regulated building needs separate projections for stabilized and free-market units under the guideline orders.

  8. The wrong capital program assumed

    Apartment loans are tested under agency, HUD or bank rules, as the capital section explains. A study written to an SBA test, or one that assumes HUD terms without HUD's market study standard 9, is reviewed against the wrong rule.

Multifamily Questions

Apartment feasibility and market study questions.

Can an apartment building be financed with an SBA 7(a) or 504 loan?

No. SBA's rules treat an apartment building as ineligible passive real estate, and USDA's Business and Industry guarantee excludes rental housing 68; the capital section of this page sets out the rule, the edition now in force and the narrow route where an owner's business occupies the building. Where SBA does lend, 13 CFR 120.160(b): SBA may require professional appraisals, a survey, or a feasibility study 45, as our SBA feasibility myth note explains. Apartment debt comes from Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac, HUD-insured lenders and banks, so the study is written to their standards.

When does an agency or lender require an apartment market study?

HUD's MAP Guide calls for one on new construction and substantial rehabilitation, and says none is required for a 223(f) refinance 9. State housing finance agencies require one for tax-credit applications, with their own format, age limits and an identity-of-interest certification for the analyst 10. Agency and bank lenders on market-rate projects usually ask for one before a construction loan, because the permanent takeout depends on stabilized rent. The reviewer sets the format and the age limit, so the file should name the program before the analyst starts.

How fast should a 2026 apartment lease-up be scheduled?

From the comparables, not from a national average. Census absorption data show only 49% of apartments completed in late 2025 were leased within three months, against 61% for 2014 completions 211, and the rate dropped to 41% for early 2026 completions 12. Supply has not stopped either: 128,000 units in buildings of five or more were authorized but not started in August 2026, and starts in early 2026 ran ahead of 2025 1. A study should take the monthly pace from recent lease-ups in the trade area, net of concessions, and show the construction loan's interest carry at a slower pace.

What does a new apartment unit cost to build in 2026?

No national series answers that. Detroit's recent projects cost $278,000 to $331,000 per unit 13, and the only per-unit cost a 2025 REIT filing states is $573,000 for a California mid-rise 14. REIT pipelines elsewhere publish totals without building type, and the totals move: Camden's Charlotte estimate went from $163.0 million to $157.0 million within one quarter for the same 420 homes 2122. A lender should rely on a dated bid for the subject, repriced for the 2025 lumber and steel tariffs 15, with its contingency and the contractor's form of contract stated.

What operating expense per unit should a 2026 apartment pro forma use?

Build it line by line, then check it against the last national benchmark. The Income/Expense IQ survey closed at the end of 2025; its final edition put total operating expense at $8,657 per unit in 2024 3, up from $8,419.88 in 2023 32, with administrative and payroll at $2,323 per unit 3. Taxes after reassessment, insurance quotes, payroll by position and utility responsibility each need their own line and growth rate, and a budget well below the national figure needs an explanation. Carry effective rent too: RealPage counted concessions on 15.4% of stabilized units in August 2026 34.

How do HUD 221(d)(4) and a Fannie Mae Small Loan compare?

They serve different stages. HUD 221(d)(4) finances construction and the permanent loan together at 87% of cost and 1.15x coverage for market-rate projects 16, with a 0.25% mortgage insurance premium 17, but its market study and underwriting take longer to close. The Fannie Mae Small Loan refinances or buys a stabilized property.

Is there a standard acceptable capture rate for apartments?

Not nationally. HUD's research office notes there is no fully developed national practice standard for market studies 18. Illinois treats 5% capture for family tax-credit projects and 7% for elderly projects as historically acceptable, with penetration under 25% 10, and other states set their own grids. Market-rate lenders read capture alongside absorption and rent comparables rather than as a pass mark. A study should show capture by income band, not one blended figure, and compare it with the lease-up the comparables actually achieved.

How is an apartment acquisition or refinance tested?

On today's debt, not the old coupon. The study restates trailing income at current taxes, insurance and concessions, then sizes the loan at the takeout lender's terms. The average price per unit was $220,684 in the fourth quarter of 2025, against about $151,000 for 2015 to 2019 49, and the apartment price index fell 18.9% over the three years to September 2025 52. A building bought at the peak may not refinance at the same loan amount; the study shows that gap as equity.
