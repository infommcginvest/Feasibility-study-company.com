Feasibility Study Company prepares auto service and collision feasibility studies for owners, developers and their lenders. A study joins a dated market read, a count of the shops already working the trade area, a priced build and a coverage test that shows how much debt a shop can carry. This page runs that work on one hypothetical project: a combined collision and mechanical repair shop that an established operator builds and owns in Rapid City, South Dakota, in place of the building it rents today, financed with an SBA 504 loan. Owners who search for an auto service feasibility study consultant, or for an auto service feasibility study company they can hand to a bank, will find on this page what such a study contains. Ask us to run the same work on your site.

The subject is generic: a one-story steel building of about {{case:v.in_bldg_sf}} square feet on about {{case:v.in_site_acres}} acres along the Interstate 90 corridor in Rapid City, in Pennington County, with a paint area, a spray booth and a short bank of mechanical bays. No parcel, owner, brand, insurer program or listing is meant. Every market figure is public and dated. Every model figure is computed by the case engine from the inputs listed near the end, and each input carries a source or the word assumption. The shop belongs to our auto service and collision practice. The city sits in South Dakota, and the program rules are laid out on our SBA 7(a) and 504 page.

One limit governs how to read the page. The research for this state holds labor, wage, permit, airport, visitor and traffic records for Rapid City and the Black Hills, and federal business counts for the repair class, but no repair order count, shop revenue, labor rate or insurer data, and it names no shop. Those inputs are labelled assumptions, and the determination below is only as firm as they are.

{{case:glance}}

The demand answer is yes. The state labor agency tables 79,900 residents for Rapid City in the Census estimate for 2024, inside a metropolitan area of 156,000 [^2]. Pennington County had 62,445 covered workers in 2025 and the metropolitan area 74,312 [^4,3]. Unemployment in September 2026 was 1.7% in the city and 2.0% across the metropolitan area [^7,9]. A repair shop's customers are employed and insured. The county is also a gateway for visitors: South Dakota counted 14.97 million visitors in 2025 [^18], and the state's count stations logged 448,166 vehicles entering Sturgis over the ten days of the August 2026 rally [^16].

The competitive answer is harder, and it comes from federal business counts rather than from any named rival. The register of County Business Patterns holds at least 122 establishments in the auto service and collision class in Pennington County in 2023, which is 10.53 per 10,000 residents against 8.42 for South Dakota as a whole [^35]. The county is more densely supplied with repair establishments than the state, so a new building has to win customers from shops that are already there. The research names no shop, so no row of the competitor section is a business. The supply table counts the field, and a named census is a lender condition.

The financing answer follows from the model. Under the sponsor's {{case:v.equity_proposed_pct}} equity proposal, which is the standard 504 tier for an operator past its second year, the project costs {{case:v.tpc_proposed}} and Year 2 coverage is {{case:v.dscr_y2_proposed}} against a floor of 1.15x. The equity tier is met and the coverage test is not. Restructured, the sponsor brings {{case:v.equity}}, {{case:v.equity_pct}} of project cost, the bank lends {{case:v.tpl}} and the debenture is {{case:v.debenture}}. That structure meets the Year 2 test at the floor and reaches {{case:v.dscr_y3}} at stabilization. The binding constraint in this case is coverage, not the equity tier, and the break-even section shows how thin the Year 3 cushion is.

People and jobs

Employment is counted more cleanly than population. Pennington County averaged 62,445 covered workers in 2025, half a percent above the 62,109 of 2024, while neighboring Meade County had 9,178 and Lawrence County 13,694 [^4]. The metropolitan area, as the state labor agency defines it, averaged 74,312 covered workers across 7,928 establishments, and 3,328 of those workers were federal government employees [^3]. The research does not say where those federal workers are stationed, so the lender should ask the operator to map its own customer list against the base and the other public employers.

The monthly labor figures say the market is tight. The city's labor force was 41,200 in September 2026, with 40,517 employed, and a year earlier the rate had been 1.8% [^7]. Pennington County's labor force was 59,596, with 58,596 employed [^8]. A shop in that market competes for technicians as well as for customers, and the model's hiring plan reflects it.

Pay and the cost of technicians

Wages bear on a repair shop twice, as a cost and as buying power. The metropolitan area's average annual pay was $59,377 in 2025, and the 10,953 leisure and hospitality workers who serve visitors earned $27,622 [^3]. Pennington County averaged $59,768, Meade County $59,355 and Lawrence County $50,950 [^5]. Statewide, pay averaged $60,577 in 2025 and construction $71,093 [^6]. A skilled body or paint technician earns more than the county average, which is why the model carries {{case:v.in_avg_pay}} a year per employee before payroll costs and does not use the county figure itself.

Visitors, the rally and the traffic they bring

The Black Hills draw drivers from every state. South Dakota's Department of Tourism counted 14.97 million visitors in 2025, who spent $5.16 billion, supported 59,145 jobs and generated $406.1 million of state and local tax revenue, and tourism supplied 16.5% of the state's sales tax collections [^18]. Leisure and hospitality employed 55.7 thousand people statewide in September 2026 [^24], and Deadwood gaming sent $2,315,908.04 of excess proceeds to the state general fund in the fiscal year to June 2025 [^30].

The August motorcycle rally is the heaviest single week. The state's count sites logged 448,166 vehicles entering Sturgis over the ten days of the 2026 rally, 10.0% below the 2021 to 2025 five-year average of 498,042 [^16]. The first three days brought 168,482 vehicles, down 5.8% from the year before, and the first Saturday was the busiest day at 58,519 vehicles, 2.5% above the five-year average for that day [^15]. The Department of Revenue estimated $1,404,408 of tax collections from the rally, 11% below the $1,581,736 of 2025, with $473,668 of it from 161 vendors in Rapid City, Custer, Hill City and Keystone [^17]. A rally week fills the roads with out-of-state vehicles whose owners may need quick service, but the research gives no repair count for that week, so the model does not count on it.

Growth and construction

The city's 2025 building valuation topped $390.9 million across 2,428 permits, behind the 2023 record of $463.5 million and ahead of 2024's $383.6 million [^1]. Single-family permits rose to 186 from 119 [^1]. Building activity is steady, not surging, and the contractors who build run trucks and vans that need repair.

Rapid City Regional Airport boarded a record 452,195 passengers in 2025, against 427,325 in 2024, with 905,211 passengers in all [^13]. In the first quarter of 2025 it boarded 76,958, up 8.8% from 70,763 a year earlier [^14]. Air travel is not repair demand, but visitors who arrive by air and rent a vehicle feed rental fleets, a steady source of collision work.

Housing, wealth and the cost of staying put

The median listing price in the Rapid City housing market was $417,450 in September 2026, against $449,900 in May [^19]. The Federal Housing Finance Agency index for the metropolitan area was 489.32 in the second quarter of 2026, up from 480.78 a year earlier [^20]. The metropolitan area produced $9,425.612 million of gross domestic product in 2023, the last year before the series was discontinued [^21]. That is context for repair demand and not a forecast of it.

The state around the city

South Dakota had 935.094 thousand residents on July 1, 2025 [^22]. Its civilian labor force was 484,700 in September 2026, with 475,100 employed [^9], and nonfarm employment averaged 469,400 in 2025 [^10]. State gross domestic product was $82,001.9 million in 2025, or $59,865.2 million in chained 2017 dollars [^25,26]. The state labor agency projects 562,027 wage and salary jobs in 2034 and 63,399 average annual openings across all occupations from 2024 to 2034, a count that includes workers leaving the labor force and changing occupations [^11,12]. Openings of that size across every trade suggest technicians will stay scarce.

What the counts say

Pennington County holds at least 122 repair-class establishments with at least 677 paid employees, and the metropolitan area at least 155 with at least 825 [^35]. The county's density of at least 10.53 per 10,000 residents is above the 8.42 of the state and the 9.94 of the metropolitan area [^35]. A county with more repair establishments per resident than its state is not an empty field. It may serve visitors and rural customers from the surrounding Black Hills as well as its own residents, which would lift the count, and the rate divides by the Census population estimate for July 2023, so the metropolitan area's growth since then lowers the density somewhat [^35,2].

The employee counts describe a field of small businesses. Dividing the county's paid employees by its establishments gives an average of roughly five or six per establishment, and the state's gives about the same [^35]. The class mixes independent mechanical shops, tire stores, glass shops and larger collision centers and cannot be split further. The subject, with {{case:v.in_fte}} full-time equivalents in the stabilized year, is well above the average, so it needs a steady flow of insurer-referred work that the small shops do not need. {{case:loan}}

An SBA 504 project is split three ways. The rules in force from October 1, 2026 (SOP 50 10 8.1 with Technical Policy Updates) [^38] put the split at Third Party Lender 50%, debenture 40%, borrower 10%; debenture 35% at a 15% contribution and 30% at 20% [^38]. The standard contribution is 10% of project cost. The model applies the higher tier of 15% to a new business, which it defines as one with two years or less in operation, or to a special purpose property [^38]. The subject operator is past that point, so the standard tier applies to the business.

The property is the second question. The SOP defines a special purpose property as a limited-market property with a unique physical design, special construction materials, or a layout that restricts its utility to the use for which it was built [^38]. Its list of examples includes service centers with pits and in-ground lifts [^38]. A collision and mechanical shop with above-ground lifts and an ordinary steel frame is a general commercial building by that test, and the CDC and the lender decide that on the plans, which makes the property type a lender condition. If they decided otherwise, the tier would rise and the structure would need to be rerun.

The debenture at both structures sits well under the $5 million limit. The sponsor's {{case:v.equity_proposed_pct}} proposal meets the equity tier of {{case:v.equity_req_pct}} and fails the coverage floor, which is why the engine does not stop at the tier. It raises the contribution to the smallest share at which Year 2 coverage meets the floor, {{case:v.equity_pct}}. The 504 upfront fee on the debenture is 0.50% under the fiscal year 2027 schedule [^39], added to project cost.

The debenture rate is {{case:v.in_debenture_rate}}, a planning rate, since the research for this state holds no debenture pricing and the rate is set when the debenture is sold. The bank rate is an assumption of {{case:v.rate}}, a quarter point over Prime of 7.00% [^40], and SBA caps the third-party spread at 6% over New York Prime [^38]. The bank's term and amortization are assumed at 25 years, matching the debenture, and the bank may set a shorter maturity that would raise debt service.

Two more program tests belong in the file. The operating business must occupy the building: the SOP's figure is 60% for a newly constructed property and 51% for an existing one [^38]. A single-tenant repair shop that owns its building meets the test on its face. And a 504 loan is tied to jobs, at $95,000 of debenture per job for a project that is not a small manufacturer or an energy project [^38]. The CDC will test the staffing plan, {{case:v.in_fte}} full-time equivalents in the stabilized year, against the debenture. Because the headcount is itself an assumption, that arithmetic is a lender condition.

{{case:inputs}}

Every input in this model is an assumption. The research for this state is rich in labor, visitor and traffic records and holds no repair shop unit economics, so the assumptions carry the model, and several cite a research figure only to show why a number is placed where it is. The most important to verify are collision order volume, the value of a collision order, building cost and staffing, in that order.

  1. The relocation ramp is slower than modelled

    By Year 3 the model needs {{case:v.in_col_ros}} collision orders a year from customers who today use the shops the register counts, and the register cannot say how many of them do collision work [^35]. A late ramp fails the Year 2 test, and the sensitivity table shows how little room there is.

  2. Insurers steer work elsewhere

    Collision volume depends on insurer referral programs, and the research holds no insurer data. A program that cuts what it pays a shop cuts coverage with it, which is why the order value is the input the sensitivity table varies.

  3. Technicians are scarce and expensive

    City unemployment was 1.7% in September 2026 and the metropolitan rate 2.0% [^7,9]. Statewide projections count 63,399 average annual openings across all occupations [^12], and a shop that pays the county average of $59,768 [^5] will lose people to employers that pay more. A shop that cannot staff its stalls does not reach its utilization.

  4. The field is dense

    Pennington County has at least 122 repair-class establishments and 10.53 per 10,000 residents, against 8.42 for the state [^35]. A well-capitalized neighbor can match price on the work the new building most needs, and the research cannot name that neighbor.

  5. Construction and equipment cost more than assumed

    Every dollar of overrun adds debt the shop can only just carry. A city with more than $390.9 million of 2025 permit valuation [^1] means contractors have other work and may bid high.

  6. The rally and the tourist season soften

    The 2026 rally count of 448,166 vehicles was 10.0% below its five-year average of 498,042 [^16], and rally tax collections fell 11% [^17]. The model does not depend on rally traffic, but fewer visitors would reduce walk-in work on the corridor.

  7. Farm income

    Corn production was forecast at 881,840 thousand bushels for the 2026 crop, against 1,085,850 thousand in 2025, and soybeans at 226,800 thousand against 237,820 thousand [^28]. Rural households that bring vehicles and farm trucks into the city spend less on repairs when farm income drops, and weather is the one risk the model cannot price.

  8. Rates differ from the model

    The debenture rate is set when the debenture is sold and the bank rate at pricing. Both are planning assumptions, and Prime stood at 7.00% on the October 9, 2026 release [^40]. A higher bank rate raises debt service immediately.

  9. The local economy cools

    Statewide nonfarm employment averaged 469,400 in 2025 [^10], and manufacturing employed 43.8 thousand people in September 2026 [^23]. A shop financed on a Year 3 ramp is exposed if hiring stalls. A weaker local economy would push some owners toward more repair and less replacement, but it would also slow the commercial and insured work the shop depends on.
