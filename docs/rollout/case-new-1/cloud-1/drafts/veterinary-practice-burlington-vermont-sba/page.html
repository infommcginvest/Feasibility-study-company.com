Owners who search for a veterinary practice feasibility study consultant or a veterinary practice feasibility study company usually want one answer before a lender asks for it: can the hospital carry the debt, and what will the bank and the development company need to see. Feasibility Study Company prepares veterinary practice feasibility studies for owners, developers and their lenders. A study joins a dated market read, a count of the competing supply, a priced project and a coverage test that shows how much debt the income supports. This page runs that work on one hypothetical project: a veterinarian who is starting a practice buys an older commercial building, converts it into a six-exam-room small-animal hospital and finances the purchase, the conversion and the equipment with an SBA 504 loan. Ask us to run the same work on your hospital.

The subject is generic: an existing one-story building of about {{case:v.in_bldg_sf}} square feet on a lot of about one acre, in the Burlington-South Burlington area of Chittenden County along the Williston Road commercial corridor. The corridor is a case assumption, and no parcel, owner, listing or practice is described. Every market figure on the page is public and dated. Every model figure is computed by the case engine from the inputs listed near the end, and each input carries either a source or the word assumption. The project belongs to our veterinary practice work, the town sits in Vermont, and the program rules are laid out on our SBA 7(a) and 504 page.

{{case:glance}}

The conclusion has three parts, and the third matters most. The first is that the county is the largest and best-paid market in a small state. Its covered wages sit above the state's, its homes were listed at $614,900 in July 2025 against $525,000 for Vermont [^16], and the register counts a population of 169,758 and 71,456 households with a median household income of $96,759 [^26]. A household that can pay for a full-service hospital is the customer this project needs.

The second is that growth is not what carries the case. Chittenden covered employment was 100,700 in March 2026, down 1.8% over the year, and the state's was 304,200, down 1.0% [^2]. Vermont's labor force was 337,142 in September 2026 against 347,040 a year earlier, both not seasonally adjusted, and the Burlington metro area's was about 126.2 thousand in September 2026 against 130,114 in September 2025 [^1,4]. A hospital that opens in a shrinking labor market cannot count on a rising tide of new households. It has to win clients from the 28 veterinary establishments the register already lists in the county [^27].

The third part is labor. Chittenden County's unemployment rate was 1.9% in March 2026 and 2.3% in June, each the lowest or tied for the lowest of Vermont's 14 counties [^8,9]. Every veterinarian, credentialed technician and kennel attendant this hospital hires has to be drawn from an employed workforce or recruited from outside a state where the Department of Labor reported that updated Census data show the population down by about 1,900 residents [^10], and where the labor force fell by more than 7,800 people in the year to February 2026 [^11]. The county average weekly wage was $1,521 in the first quarter of 2026, up 3.4% over the year, against $1,322 for the state [^2], and a median home listed for $577,925 in September 2026 [^15]. A recruit must be able to live within reach of the hospital on the wage it pays. That is why the model charges the owner a market salary and sizes a working-capital reserve to the first year's loss. The research holds no veterinarian wage, no technician wage and no count of veterinarians in Vermont, so those facts are lender conditions and not numbers on this page.

The determination follows from those three parts. A start-up veterinary hospital financed under the 504 rules passes the stabilized-year test comfortably and fails the Year 2 test as the sponsor proposes it. Restructured, the loan meets the test only with much more of the owner's money in the project. The ramp is the problem, not the market.

A small-animal hospital sells to households, so the demand read starts with how many there are and what they earn. The register's five-year Census estimates put Chittenden County at the counts below [^26]. They are the only household measures in the evidence: the research behind this page found no pet ownership rate, no count of pet-owning households and no veterinary spending figure for Vermont, and each of those is a lender condition.

The labor market that supplies both clients and staff

The same wages that let a household pay a full-service fee make a technician expensive to hire. Chittenden County's covered employment was 100,700 in March 2026, down 1.8% over the year, and its average weekly wage was $1,521, up 3.4% [^2]. The state's covered employment was 304,200, down 1.0%, with an average weekly wage of $1,322, up 3.0%, and 33,374 establishments in the unemployment insurance system [^2]. Franklin County, on the northern edge of the metro area, counted 15,967 covered jobs and averaged $1,255 a week [^2]. All of these are first-quarter 2026 figures from a preliminary file.

The Department of Labor's September 2026 report put Vermont's seasonally adjusted labor force at 337,877, with 329,128 employed and 8,749 unemployed [^6], and its comparison column carries 347,408 for September 2025 [^7]. BLS's table of metro areas has the unadjusted state labor force at 337,142 for September 2026, a preliminary figure, against 347,040 a year earlier [^4]. The same table gives the Burlington-South Burlington labor force as 130,114 in September 2025 and 126,589 in August 2026 [^4], and BLS's metro page shows about 126.2 thousand in September 2026 with 125.2 thousand nonfarm jobs [^1]. State nonfarm payrolls on a seasonally adjusted basis were 311.2 thousand in September 2025 and 310.8 thousand in August 2026 [^5]. The labor force fell by more than payroll jobs did, which is how a workforce becomes scarce.

The sectors that matter to a hospital's hiring are health care, professional services and the businesses that compete for the same administrative staff. In September 2026 the state had 65.5 thousand jobs in education and health services, 56.8 thousand in government, 27.9 thousand in manufacturing and 31.4 thousand in professional and business services, all seasonally adjusted and preliminary [^3]. Private employers added 16,739 jobs and lost 19,658 between June and September 2025 [^3]. That churn is large against the size of the workforce, and it means a hospital that offers a stable schedule can recruit, but it also means its trained technicians can be recruited away. The one wage the research holds for the metro area is the mean hourly wage for all occupations, $35.75 for May 2025 [^3]. It is not a technician wage and the study does not treat it as one.

Housing, which decides who can be hired

A county with a 1.9% unemployment rate in March 2026 [^8] asks a new employer to recruit people who then have to find somewhere to live. The median listing price was $577,925 in September 2026 [^15]. The Federal Housing Finance Agency's all-transactions index for the county stood at 322.44 for 2025 with the year 2000 equal to 100, and the metro index at 422.61 in the second quarter of 2026 with the first quarter of 1995 equal to 100 [^14,13]. The county authorized 1,026 new private housing structures in 2025 [^12]. Supply is arriving, but a veterinary technician's wage does not buy a median home, and the hospital's staffing plan has to assume that some of its people commute from Franklin County and beyond.

What the county's other drivers do and do not mean for a hospital

Chittenden County accounted for 24.5% of Vermont's visitor spending in 2024, more than $1 billion [^17]. The study does not turn visitor spending into veterinary demand. Visitors may bring pets, but the research holds no figure that links the two, and a hospital whose model rests on tourists has not been underwritten. The state's agricultural economy points the same way. Vermont produced 2,513,000,000 pounds of milk in 2025 [^25], which makes livestock the larger veterinary story in the state, but that story is large-animal practice, and a small-animal hospital in Williston Road retail space does not serve it. A lender reading the state's headline numbers should not read them into this subject.

A competitor census for a veterinary hospital would name each general-practice and emergency clinic in the trade area, with its own website or the county assessor's record, its doctors and its hours. The research behind this page names none. Its Chittenden County rows are Labor Department tables, Census and Federal Reserve series, and press reports about housing and the economy, and not one of them lists a veterinary hospital. This study will not invent a competitor, will not describe a clinic that no document names and will not dress the subject up as one. The census is therefore a lender condition: a named competitor census with each competitor's own website or assessor record, delivered before commitment.

What the supply table says

Veterinary services are a small industry here. The county holds 28 employer establishments and 534 paid employees, and the metro area adds a handful of both beyond the county line [^27]. The county's rate of 1.65 establishments per 10,000 residents sits a little above the metro area's 1.54 [^27], and the metro area also takes in Franklin and Grand Isle counties, where the rate is lower. A lender can read the count two ways. It says the field is not crowded by the standard of the county's household count in the demand section. It also says each existing hospital has had years to build its client base, and a newcomer has to win clients from one of a few dozen practices that clients already know.

The count is also a ceiling on what the register can tell a lender. It lists employer establishments only, so a mobile veterinarian working alone appears nowhere, and it does not say which clinics take new patients, which have an emergency service or which belong to a corporate group. A hospital with three doctors and a hospital with one count the same. The census the lender condition asks for fills those gaps. Until it exists the study treats the field as unmeasured and builds the ramp from the owner's own client base and not from any share of a market.

The subject site is generic, so this section describes no parcel. It lists what the public record says about the setting and sends the rest to the lender conditions. The research found no Burlington or South Burlington zoning ordinance, no zoning district for the corridor, no flood zone determination, no utility provider or tap fee schedule and no traffic count for Williston Road or any other street, and it does not guess at them.

Use, zoning and the state permit question

A veterinary hospital is a different use from the retail or office building it replaces. It brings animal wards, noise, medical waste, controlled substances and an exhaust and odor system. Whether the corridor's zoning allows the use as of right or by conditional approval, what the parking ratio is and whether a conversion triggers state land use review are all matters for the municipality and the state, and none is in the research. A zoning verification letter and a written determination from the town's planning staff are lender conditions, and the contract for the building should be contingent on them.

Floods and drainage

The federal disaster record shows that Chittenden County has been inside Vermont's recent flood and storm declarations more than once. FEMA's notice for the July 2023 flooding listed Chittenden among nine counties designated for Individual Assistance [^22]. Its notice for the January 2024 winter storm named Chittenden among five counties designated for Public Assistance [^23], and its notice for the July 2024 storms named Chittenden among seven counties designated for both Individual and Public Assistance [^24]. The state's recovery grants followed: the Agency of Commerce and Community Development approved $49,991,445 in its November 2025 CDBG-DR awards for housing, infrastructure, mitigation and planning [^21].

None of that places the subject in a floodplain. It does show that a hospital with kennel wards, a pharmacy and medical records has a flood question to answer before closing. A flood zone determination and a drainage review are lender conditions, and flood insurance, if required, sits inside the insurance allowance until a quote replaces it.

Utilities, traffic and access

An animal hospital runs its ventilation around the clock and cannot lose power to its refrigerated drugs and its anesthesia and imaging equipment. The study does not know which utilities serve the corridor, what capacity charges apply to a conversion or whether three-phase power is at the lot, and the research found no traffic count for Williston Road. The model's site-work allowance is a placeholder for these answers, and an access and signage review is a lender condition.

Revenue is the number of patient visits times what each visit earns. Visits reach {{case:v.in_visits}} in the stabilized year (Year 3), and the full ramp is in the inputs table. The hospital earns a net professional fee of {{case:v.in_fee_per_visit}} a visit and sells {{case:v.in_retail_per_visit}} of pharmacy, diet and retail goods per visit, both held flat across the five years. At stabilization the hospital takes in {{case:v.rev_y3}} a year, and in the test year, Year 2, {{case:v.rev_y2}}.

These are planning figures. The research holds no Vermont visit count, no fee schedule and no revenue per veterinarian, and the study will not borrow another state's number or a national average and call it local. A hospital's records are the right evidence, and the lender conditions ask for the trailing records of two comparable hospitals and for the owner's own production history if the owner has one. If the owner is leaving an employer, the lender should ask what client base the owner can credibly bring, and under what restriction on soliciting.

The ramp is the part of revenue a lender should read twice. Year 1 revenue is {{case:v.rev_y1}}, and revenue in Year 2 is {{case:v.rev_y2}}. A new hospital in a county that already has dozens of clinics earns its clients one at a time, through referrals and a reputation that takes months to form. The model assumes each doctor is about as busy in Year 2 as in Year 3, so what grows is the number of doctors. If the first associate is hired late, or the first year is slower than the ramp, the Year 2 test is where the project fails.

Retail sales are the line most exposed to online pharmacies, and the research does not measure that leakage. Fees are held flat on purpose, so the model does not depend on a price increase to meet its floor.

The pro forma carries three layers of cost. Drugs, supplies and outside laboratory work are charged as a share of professional fees ({{case:v.in_supplies_pct}}), and the goods sold at retail are charged at a cost of {{case:v.in_product_cost_pct}} of their sales. Payroll is the largest operating cost: {{case:v.in_dvm_fte}} veterinarian full-time equivalents in Year 3 at {{case:v.in_dvm_pay}} each, {{case:v.in_tech_fte}} technicians and assistants at {{case:v.in_tech_wage}}, {{case:v.in_support_fte}} client service and kennel staff at {{case:v.in_support_wage}} and a manager at {{case:v.in_manager_pay}}, all before payroll costs of {{case:v.in_burden}}. The owner is paid a market salary inside the veterinarian line, so the earnings shown are not flattered by unpaid labor.

The wage inputs are assumptions with a local frame. The county's average weekly wage rose 3.4% in the year to the first quarter of 2026, and the state's rose 3.0% [^2]; the model grows wages and costs at {{case:v.in_inflation}} a year. The metro area's mean hourly wage for all occupations was $35.75 for May 2025 [^3], and a technician paid less than that mean has a reason to leave for an employer that pays more. {{case:sources}}

Under the 504 program a bank takes the first lien and a certified development company funds the second through an SBA-guaranteed debenture. The standard split is Third Party Lender 50%, debenture 40%, borrower 10%; debenture 35% at a 15% contribution and 30% at 20% [^28]. The owner in this case is starting a veterinary business, and a business of two years or less is a new business under the program, so the equity tier is 15% and not the standard 10% [^28]. The tier rises to 20% for a new business that also occupies a special purpose property [^28].

The model treats the hospital as a general-purpose building and not a special purpose one. Special purpose status is a classification that the development company makes, and the definition is a limited-market property with a unique physical design, special construction materials, or a layout that restricts its utility to the use for which it was built [^28]. A converted retail or office building does not have a layout that restricts it to hospital use, but floor drains, kennel wards and shielded rooms move a conversion toward that line. The classification is a lender condition. If the development company read the hospital as special purpose, the tier would be the higher one and the Year 2 test would still decide the equity, because the restructured contribution here already exceeds that tier.

The sponsor proposes {{case:v.equity_proposed_pct}} equity. That is below the 15% tier for a new business, so the proposal fails the equity test before coverage is looked at. It also fails coverage: Year 2 net operating income covers the {{case:v.ds_proposed}} of annual debt service only {{case:v.dscr_y2_proposed}} against a floor of 1.15x [^28]. The debenture of {{case:v.debenture_proposed}} would be within the program limit of $5 million [^28]. The engine raises the borrower's contribution until Year 2 coverage reaches the floor. At {{case:v.equity_pct}} equity the bank lends {{case:v.tpl}}, the debenture is {{case:v.debenture}} and annual debt service is {{case:v.ds}}.

{{case:loan}}

Owner occupancy and the rest of the program

The operating borrower must occupy at least 51% of an existing building and 60% of a new one [^28]. The hospital occupies the whole building in this case, so the test is met on the plan as drawn. The upfront fee on the debenture is 0.50% for loans approved in fiscal year 2027, and the model adds it to project cost [^29].

The program offers debenture maturities of 10, 20 or 25 years [^28]. The 25-year term used here suits a loan whose largest asset category is real estate, which here is the building and the conversion, but equipment is treated separately under the program's maturity rules, and the lender and the development company may split the equipment into a shorter debenture or finance it another way. The page keeps one 25-year debenture and lists the equipment financing as a lender condition, because a shorter term on the equipment would raise debt service.

Why not a 7(a) loan

A 7(a) loan would suit a project that is mostly business and equipment, as when the owner buys an existing practice and leases its premises. This project has most of its cost in real estate, so the 504 structure is the better fit. An owner who buys an established practice would face a different test, with historical earnings in place of a projection.

The state and federal funding backdrop

A Vermont borrower has more than one public lender to compare. The Vermont Economic Development Authority invested $13,883,650 across 32 projects in ten counties in the third quarter of its fiscal year, January to March 2026 [^18]. The governor announced $57.9 million of State Small Business Credit Initiative funding in July 2022, with part of it a loan participation program that VEDA administers [^20]. A closing date that depends on federal processing needs slack in the purchase contract. The lender conditions ask for the contract to say so.

{{case:inputs}}

  1. The second year is the test

    The proposal fails coverage in Year 2 at {{case:v.dscr_y2_proposed}} and passes in Year 3 at {{case:v.dscr_y3_proposed}}. A slower first-associate hire, a bad winter or a competitor's price cut pushes the weak year into the second year of repayment, which is where a lender looks.

  2. Doctors and technicians cannot be hired

    Chittenden County's unemployment rate was 1.9% in March 2026 [^8], and Vermont's labor force fell from 347,040 to 337,142 between September 2025 and September 2026 [^4]. The model adds doctors on a schedule, and a hospital that cannot hire on that schedule cannot produce the visits. The research holds no veterinarian or technician count for Vermont.

  3. Wages outrun the model

    The county's average weekly wage rose 3.4% in a year [^2], and the model grows costs at {{case:v.in_inflation}} while holding fees flat. If a technician must be paid closer to the metro area's mean hourly wage of $35.75 [^3] than the model assumes, payroll rises faster than revenue.

  4. The price carries the deal

    The purchase price is a round planning figure with no commercial sale behind it. Every extra dollar of price is a dollar of equity under the restructured loan, and equity is already large.

  5. The volume has no local evidence

    No visit count, fee schedule or pet ownership rate is in the research, and a new hospital's share of the county's visits is unknown until the competitor census exists.

  6. The conversion hides cost

    An older building can hide bad slabs, drains and hazardous materials. The contingency is an allowance and not a finding.

  7. Flood and storm exposure

    Chittenden County was named in FEMA declarations for the July 2023 flooding, the January 2024 winter storm and the July 2024 storms [^22,23,24]. A hospital with live animals has to protect them, its drugs and its records.

  8. Federal processing time

    SBA estimated that $1,586,902 a week of Vermont loan proceeds was blocked during the October 2025 shutdown [^19]. A purchase contract without slack for a federal delay can lapse.

  9. The owner is the practice

    A start-up practice depends on one veterinarian's health, license and reputation. Key-person life and disability insurance, and a plan for covering the owner's absence, are lender conditions.
