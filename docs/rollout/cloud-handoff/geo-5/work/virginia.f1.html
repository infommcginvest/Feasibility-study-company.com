Feasibility Study Company prepares independent feasibility studies for projects in Virginia, written for the owner or developer and for the lender who has to approve the loan: an SBA 7(a) or 504 lender, USDA Rural Development, a bank, or the sponsor of an EB-5 offering. Someone looking for a Virginia feasibility study consultant usually needs one thing from the document, a demand and cost case that a credit officer can trace back to dated public sources. This page lays out those sources for Virginia and says where they stop.

Electricity is where the Virginia record begins. EIA's profile of the state for 2024 counts 138,044,772 MWh of retail sales, an all-sector average price of 10.62 cents per kWh and 29,147 MW of net summer generating capacity [^2]. Data centers are why that profile reaches the state budget: their sales and use tax exemption survived the 2026 session, and a new tax on the electricity data center operators use began on July 1, 2026 [^1]. What the exemption costs the treasury is reported two ways for one fiscal year, and the power section prints both estimates beside each other without choosing between them.

Payroll is the second thread. BLS counted 4,250.6 thousand seasonally adjusted nonfarm jobs in Virginia for September 2026, a preliminary figure [^13], and Virginia Works data that ARLnow reported on January 8, 2026 showed 22,400 fewer federal jobs in November 2025 than a year before [^3]. Most of the federal workforce that lives in Virginia lives in the counties across the Potomac from Washington [^7], and those counties are covered on their own page, Northern Virginia feasibility studies. Elsewhere the cuts show up inside public payrolls that BLS counts for Richmond and Hampton Roads, which mix federal, state and local employers in one line.

The third thread is the markets. Eight are read on this page: Richmond, Hampton Roads, Roanoke, Charlottesville, Lynchburg, Blacksburg-Christiansburg, Harrisonburg and Winchester. Each has a BLS payroll count for September 2026, a quarterly employment count for its central city or county, Realtor.com listing prices and an FHFA price index; Richmond, Hampton Roads and Roanoke add broker readings, and two of the eight cross a state line. The market overview sets out the boundaries, and each market section names the jurisdictions its figures cover.

EIA's Virginia profile for 2024, released on November 10, 2025, is the statewide power record: retail sales of 138,044,772 MWh, an average retail price of 10.62 cents per kWh across all sectors, and net summer generating capacity of 29,147 MW [^2]. The average blends households, businesses and industry. A commercial or industrial price for Virginia was not in the research, so a study prices electricity from the serving utility's tariff for the project's rate class and uses the statewide average only to check that the result is in proportion.

Two published estimates put a cost on the sales and use tax exemption that data centers claim, and both are for fiscal 2023. Holland & Knight, in its alert of August 20, 2026, cites the Joint Legislative Audit and Review Commission's December 2024 report for $928.6 million of sales taxes the exemption saved data centers that year [^1]. The Virginian-Pilot, in a report Governing carried on November 26, 2025, printed $685 million as the amount for fiscal 2023 [^6]. Both outlets were reporting on the commission's work, and they print different amounts. This page gives each with its source and does not reconcile them; a study that cites the cost names which estimate it relies on.

The 2026 change runs two ways at once. The exemption stays, and data center operators now owe an electricity consumption tax of $0.011 per kWh from July 1, 2026 to June 30, 2028, with annual collections capped at $600 million, as Holland & Knight summarized the budget language [^1]. To qualify for the exemption a data center still meets the thresholds of Va. Code section 58.1-609.3(18) and (19): $150 million of investment and 50 new jobs paying at least 1.5 times the prevailing average wage, with lower thresholds in distressed localities, and the alert gives June 30, 2035 as the exemption's end date, with later extensions available [^1].

For any other kind of project, the ledger matters in two ways. A site in a county where data centers cluster competes with them for land, for construction trades and for the utility's ability to serve a new load, and the evidence on that last point is the utility's written statement for the parcel, not a statewide figure. And the tax treatment is specific to the use: the exemption and the consumption tax reach only operators that qualify as data centers, so a warehouse, clinic or hotel in the same county finds nothing in this record that changes its own tax line.

The research did not reach the commission's report itself, a count of data centers in the state, or a figure for the capacity utilities have committed to them. Loudoun County publishes data center figures on a page that carries no release date, so they are not printed here.

The Weldon Cooper Center at the University of Virginia estimates 8.88 million Virginia residents on July 1, 2025, in the release it published on February 24, 2026 [^5]. The Census Bureau's Vintage 2025 state page could not be read for this research, and Virginia's net domestic migration for that vintage was not found, so a study whose demand rests on households takes its county's components of change from the Census table itself.

BLS counted 4,250.6 thousand seasonally adjusted nonfarm jobs in Virginia for September 2026 and a civilian labor force of 4,461.2 thousand, both preliminary [^13]. The labor force counts residents who are working or looking for work, and the payroll count counts jobs at establishments inside the state, so a Virginian who commutes to an office in the District of Columbia appears in the first figure and not the second. FFXnow, reporting state data released on April 16, 2026, gave 173,934 unemployed Virginians for January 2026, not seasonally adjusted and preliminary [^14].

The federal cut is the change in the 2025 payroll that the record documents most directly. Virginia Works data released on January 7, 2026, as ARLnow reported it the next day, showed 22,400 fewer federal jobs in Virginia in November 2025 than in November 2024; October 2025 was skipped because of the federal shutdown [^3]. VPM News reported in January 2026 that more than half of the federal civilian workers who live in Virginia live in Northern Virginia [^7], whose payroll and office record is on the Northern Virginia page. In the eight markets below, the nearest measure is BLS's government count for Richmond and for Hampton Roads. It combines federal, state and local employers, so a study whose demand rests on federal spending says so and names the agency or installation it depends on.

Covered employment adds wages and establishments. In the first quarter of 2026 the average weekly wage across all covered employment in Virginia was $1,665, and the state counted 324,758 covered establishments, in the BLS county release of September 2, 2026 [^4]. The flows beneath those counts come from Business Employment Dynamics: in the second quarter of 2025 private employers in Virginia gained 197,863 jobs at opening and expanding establishments and lost 196,049 at closing and contracting ones, and the release puts 43,606 of the gains at establishments that opened and 33,616 of the losses at establishments that closed [^15].

For a business that has to win customers, the churn says more than the level. A restaurant, shop or service business is underwritten in a state where openings and closings run side by side every quarter, and the subject's own trade area decides which side of that churn it is likely to sit on. The market sections give the central city's or county's employment count for March 2026 for exactly that reason.

Output is relayed from BEA through FRED. Virginia's nominal GDP for 2025 was 807,925.0 million dollars [^16], and real GDP for the second quarter of 2026 ran at 639,147.7 million chained 2017 dollars, a seasonally adjusted annual rate [^17]. One is in current dollars for a year and the other in constant dollars for a quarter, and they are not set against each other here.

Construction labor has one dated count. The occupational survey for the Virginia Beach-Chesapeake-Norfolk metro found 41,190 jobs in construction and extraction occupations in May 2025, and a mean wage of $30.83 an hour across all occupations [^9]; that metro includes three North Carolina counties, so the count is not Virginia's alone. Building costs themselves are priced from contractor bids, and these counts describe the labor pool those bids draw on.

The state portion of the sales and use tax was 4.3% as The Virginian-Pilot printed it in the report Governing carried on November 26, 2025; local additions were not printed there, and the Department of Taxation's site could not be reached for this research [^6]. A budget for furniture, fixtures and equipment uses the combined rate for the site's locality, confirmed with the department on the date of purchase. Electricity enters the operating budget at the serving utility's tariff, as the power section sets out.

Insurance is the gap. A homeowners premium or a total of approved rate filings from the Bureau of Insurance was not found, because the regulator's site did not resolve for this research and the press coverage that was found dealt with auto rates. For a commercial building the evidence is a dated quote for the parcel and, on the coast, the flood zone on the parcel's map panel. Published construction cost indices for Virginia metros and a statewide lodging tax rate with an effective date were not found either, and a study that needs them sources them from the contractor and from the locality's tax office.

Data centers

The exemption thresholds and the consumption tax in the power section are the most specific state rules in this record [^1]. A data center study states which threshold applies to the site, the standard one or the lower one for a distressed locality, and carries the consumption tax as an operating cost for the months between July 1, 2026 and June 30, 2028 that fall inside its projection.

Lodging and short-term rentals

A statewide short-term rental statute for 2024 to 2026 could not be confirmed: bills were filed, but none was read at its final status in this research, and the ordinances of Virginia Beach and Norfolk were not reached. A rental or small-lodging plan in Virginia therefore cites the ordinance of the city or county that holds the parcel, with the date the study read it, and a hotel plan adds the locality's transient occupancy tax, which was not found in a statewide source.

Apartments and housing

No statewide zoning reform with bill numbers was verified for this page. Housing evidence is the permit and listing series in the supply section and the city series in the market sections, and an apartment study reads the zoning on its own parcel, whether the use is permitted by right or needs a rezoning, before it sizes demand.

The capital source decides what the study has to prove. SBA's rule is 13 CFR 120.160(b): SBA may require professional appraisals, a survey, or a feasibility study [^76], so a study enters an SBA file at the request of the lender or the agency, typically where the cash flow has not yet been earned: a start-up, an expansion into a new line or a special-purpose property. The rules in force are SOP 50 10 8.1 with Technical Policy Updates, effective October 1, 2026 [^77]. Standard 7(a) debt service coverage is 1.15x, and a standard 504 project puts in 10% equity, rising to 15% where the business is new or the property is special-purpose [^77]. How those tests apply to a project is explained on the SBA 7(a) and 504 lending page.

SBA's results for its Virginia district offices in fiscal 2025 were not found, because the office page returned an error, and neither were the Virginia Small Business Financing Authority's program totals or the state's SSBCI deployment to date. A Virginia loan file therefore cites SBA's own loan-level data for the subject's county, not a re-total from a third party.

The USDA Rural Development page sets out the program tests.

Banks lend on most stabilized property, and their tests are described on the conventional and institutional lending page. Whichever lender or investor is involved, a Virginia feasibility study company has one job, which Feasibility Study Company does for each of these programs: a study independent of sponsor and lender, written to the program's requirements and traceable, figure by figure, to dated sources like those on this page.
