D4 (pre-count method). A Sonnet reader per state writes a fact sheet (`~/Downloads/fsc-rollout/drafts/geo-2/facts/<state>.json`, outside the repo) listing every dated, sourced figure with its geography. `drafts/geo-2/tools/precount.py` then counts, per geography, the figures a page could count: detectable by pagecheck's figure rules, not a register series, not already on any served page by value (the eight GEO-2 state pages being rebuilt are left out of that index), once per page, state figures shared with a metro moved to the metro, and figures shared by two metros counted for neither. A page is written when research reaches 20 and research plus at most 10 register figures reaches 30 (Amendment 1 A6). The rest fold.

D5 (pre-count result: 2 metro pages, 82 folds). The pre-count is collision-aware: a research figure whose value already sits on a live page (by pagecheck's value and rounding rules) cannot count at any gate, so it does not count here. A trace of where the collisions sit (`drafts/geo-2/tools/where.py`) shows they are mostly small percentages and round counts already on the live research notes, hubs, Texas pages and cases (for example +4.5% on 19 pages, 8% on 40), not on pages GEO-2 replaces. On that count only Boston (24 research, 34 with register) and Montgomery County, Maryland (24, 34) stand on their own evidence. The nearest misses are Portland ME 19, New York City 17, Prince George's County 17, West Virginia's Eastern Panhandle 17, Buffalo 16 and Baltimore 15. The lane note says not to stretch, and ruling 6 decides by the count, so they fold. Every fold keeps its slug as a section id on the state page (FOLDED.md). The state pages carry the folded markets' evidence, which also lifts the state pages whose own statewide count is low (Massachusetts 15 and Delaware 12 statewide, each with 30 or more more in their folded sections).

D8 (no hand-computed figures). The first writer brief let a page show a difference of two cited figures when both were cited. D10 (Vintage 2025 population). The reader brief flagged Census population estimates as register series, so most writers left every population count off their pages and described growth in words. Ruling 10 says the opposite for a newer vintage: where the research holds Vintage 2025 and the register only Vintage 2023, the research figure stands and the register's PEP is not shown. So a Vintage 2025 count from the research, with its Census source, is a usable research figure. The pages as drafted are not wrong (no PEP from the register appears on any of them, and the growth statements carry their citations); writers still drafting were told, and a page may add its Vintage 2025 counts in a later tranche without breaking the one-vintage rule.

D11 (stat band on phones). The house stat band is two columns at 390px; a stat number of seven or more wide characters (6,206,700, $255,197,631, $466.35) widened the layout viewport past 390 (gate 13), on six drafts. No figure may be rounded to fit (D8), so the finisher picks counted figures that are naturally short (six visible characters at most, commas and points counting half). The look gate checks every page at 390 for any element right of the viewport, not only page scrollWidth.
