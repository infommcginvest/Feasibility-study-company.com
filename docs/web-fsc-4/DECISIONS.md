## Dashes (ruling 12)

24. **Method.** `docs/web-fsc-4/tools/dashes.py` finds every dash in the eight listed encodings in every served file outside the hold (HTML, XML, CSS, Markdown, Python, JSON) and decides by rule where the context settles it: an en dash becomes a hyphen with its spacing kept (1,901 numeric ranges, the rest compounds such as Dallas-Fort Worth); an em dash in a title, heading, og/twitter title or JSON-LD name or headline becomes a colon; a pair in one sentence becomes commas, or parentheses when the aside has its own comma; a dash that is a whole table cell becomes a hyphen; a dash in a comment, stylesheet or script becomes a hyphen; a dash after a bold label becomes a colon. The 1,148 left were grouped into 920 sentences and decided by four Sonnet reviewers (cases A to H, cases I to Z, states, everything else) plus six by the main session for the sentences the Florida recompute had rewritten; the decision files are in `docs/web-fsc-4/dashes/`.
25. **Fidelity.** On the sweep commit, all 150 changed served files have the identical word sequence before and after, counted over the whole file (text, attributes and JSON-LD), so the sweep changed punctuation only. No full stop was chosen, so no capital letter changed either.
26. **Sentence splitting.** The splitter breaks at "U.S.", "St." and "No.", so a few paired dashes reached the reviewers as two single dashes; they chose commas, colons or semicolons that read correctly for each half rather than parentheses.
