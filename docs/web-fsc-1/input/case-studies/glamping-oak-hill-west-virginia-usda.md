# Glamping Resort Feasibility Study Near Oak Hill, West Virginia: 26 Units at New River Gorge on a USDA B&I Loan

{{byline}}

> **Representative worked example.** The site, sponsor and project are hypothetical. The market evidence is real, public and dated October 2026. This page shows how the market analysis is built and how it sets the loan. It is not a client engagement.

New River Gorge has the best demand story of any national park unit in the eastern outdoor market. Visits set a record in 2025 and are up nearly two-thirds since 2019. A lender looking at a glamping resort ten minutes from the park could easily stop there. This case does not, because the local short-term rental market shows the other half of the picture: listings are growing faster than visitors, and nightly rates are flat to falling.

## At a glance

| Item | Finding |
|---|---|
| Subject | 26 units on about 30 acres in unincorporated Fayette County, WV, outside Oak Hill: 12 safari tents (May to October), 8 insulated domes with hot tubs, 6 four-season cabins with hot tubs |
| Program | USDA Business and Industry guaranteed loan |
| Park demand | 1,958,440 recreation visits in 2025, a record, up 8.09% |
| Local rental market | Oak Hill ADR $224 to $238; occupancy 39.6% to 53%; supply up 47.8% |
| Rates the study underwrites | Domes $255, cabins $235, safari tents $165 |
| Stabilized revenue | $797,481 |
| Stabilized net operating income | $254,514 |
| Total project cost | $4,440,088 ($170,773 per unit) |
| Coverage at 25% equity | 0.77x |
| B&I loan the market supports at 1.25x | $2,060,000 |
| Sponsor equity at that loan | $2,380,088 (53.6%) |
| Market conclusion | Demand is not the risk. Dilution and winter are. The market supports the product at local rates, not national glamping rates, and that sets the loan near 46% of cost. |

## The market conclusion

Park demand has grown every year since redesignation, and the gap in local supply is real: purpose-built domes, safari tents and hot-tub cabins are thin within 25 miles, while the two big adventure resorts sell lodging as a companion to rafting and zip lines. That gap is the subject's opening. But per-unit rental revenue in Oak Hill is falling as new listings capture the growth, and the low season averages 28% occupancy. Underwritten at local rates, the resort earns $254,514 of net operating income. At the program's 25% equity tier that covers debt only 0.77 times. Sized to 1.25x, the market supports a $2.06 million B&I loan, and the sponsor carries just over half the cost.

The park's July average is about seven times its January average. Autumn adds two peaks of its own: the Gauley release weekends and Bridge Day, which in 2026 fall on the same October weekend. That is why the resort carries safari tents only from May to October and puts its winter revenue on domes and cabins with private hot tubs. A county-level visitor-spending figure for 2025 circulates on aggregator sites but could not be confirmed on the state's own tables, so this case does not use it.

| Fayetteville, AirDNA | 812 (up 201.9%) | 51% | $233 (down 11.8%) | $118 (down 6.8%) | $37.3K | The listing jump likely reflects a redrawn market boundary [13] |
| Beckley, AirDNA and AirROI | 56 and 31 | 61% and 41.5% | $115 and $134 | | | Lower-rate hotel market nearby [14] |

National glamping surveys put the average daily rate at $251 (2025 report) and $304 (2026 report), and the 2026 edition restated 2025 at $239 [15]. Those are operator surveys with self-selected respondents. Locally, the best-documented hot-tub cabin in Oak Hill lists from $289 a night, and the main adventure resort's hot-tub cabins start at $249. The study prices domes at $255 and cabins at $235, inside the local band, and treats the national figures as upside. Camping demand nationally supports the format: KOA counts 52.16 million camping households in 2025, glamping at 29% of camping, and 54% of campers planning to glamp in 2026 [16].

## Competitive supply within 25 miles

| Property (location) | Units | Published 2026 rates | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| ACE Adventure Resort (Oak Hill) | 1,500 acres; cabin tents, cedar cabins, hot-tub cabins, log homes, bunkhouses, camping | Cabin tent $79 (no AC, shared baths); cedar cabin $179; hot-tub cabin $249; log homes $549 to $729 | Waterpark, rafting, restaurant [17] |
| Adventures on the Gorge (Lansing) | Operator profile: 103 cabins, 4 vacation homes, 10 RV sites, 4 new glamping tents | Room snapshots $127 to $375 | Three restaurants, rafting, zip lines [18] |
| The Outpost (Fayetteville) | Cabins, bunk cabins, RV hookups, tent platforms, vintage campers | Not published | Elevated cabins under construction [19] |
| New River Cabins and Tiny Houses (Fayetteville) | 8 cabins, 4 tiny houses | $119 to $161 | Tiny houses close for winter [20] |
| Oak Hill hot-tub cabin (Glamping Hub listing) | 1 cabin, sleeps 6 | From $289 | [21] |
| Dome resort, Alderson (outside the 25-mile ring) | 6 domes on 65 acres | Not published | Nearest dedicated dome product found [22] |

Supply is shaped like a barbell. At one end are large adventure campuses whose lodging supports activity sales, with entry rates from $79 to $249. At the other are hundreds of scattered whole-home rentals. Design-led glamping with domes, private hot tubs and safari tents is largely absent within 25 miles, and that is the subject's main argument. The main threat is that the adventure resorts, which already hold the land and the guests, add glamping themselves. One has already listed four new glamping tents.

## What the market supports: the revenue build

| Unit type | Units | ADR | Occupancy | Occupied nights | Lodging revenue |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Insulated domes with hot tubs (year-round) | 8 | $255 | 45% | 1,314 | $335,070 |
| Four-season cabins with hot tubs | 6 | $235 | 45% | 986 | $231,592 |
| Safari tents (May to October, 184 nights) | 12 | $165 | 52% | 1,148 | $189,446 |
| **Total lodging** | 26 | | | 3,448 | **$756,109** |
| Firewood, packages and other fees ($12 per occupied night) | | | | | $41,372 |
| **Total revenue** | | | | | **$797,481** |

Year-round units are underwritten at 45% annual occupancy, between AirROI's 39.6% and AirDNA's 53%, with the winter trough already inside that average. Tents are underwritten at 52% over their six-month season. Operating expenses total $542,966: payroll of $220,291 for a general manager, guest services, housekeeping and maintenance with a 20% burden; booking channels and card processing at 9% of lodging; utilities of $36,000; insurance of $40,000; property tax of $30,000 (a model assumption, since the county's total Class III levy was not found); and a 4% replacement reserve, set high because tent and dome covers wear quickly. Net operating income is $254,514, a 31.9% margin. The downside case cuts every rate 10% and occupancy 5 points, to $644,418 of revenue and $143,607 of net operating income. That mirrors the documented supply growth and Fayetteville's 11.8% ADR decline.

| Structure | B&I loan | Annual debt service (8.75%, 25 years) | Coverage: study case | Coverage: downside |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Program equity tier of 25% | $3,330,066 | $328,535 | 0.77x | 0.44x |
| Sized to 1.25x on the study case | $2,060,000 | $203,234 | 1.25x | 0.71x |

At the 25% tier that 7 CFR 5001.105(d) sets for a new business with construction, the resort cannot cover its debt. Sized to the market, the loan is $2.06 million and the sponsor's equity is $2,380,088, or 53.6% of cost. The downside row shows why the funded reserve stays: a 10% rate cut on top of five lost occupancy points takes coverage to 0.71x. No outdoor-hospitality sale with a disclosed price per unit or cap rate was found in West Virginia, Virginia, Kentucky or Pennsylvania for 2023 to 2026, so collateral will rest on cost and income approaches, with tents and domes discounted as equipment.

Two levers move the answer. A smaller common building (the 1,800 sq ft welcome building and bathhouse is $486,000 of mostly non-revenue cost) and a packaged partnership with a rafting outfitter, which sells the October peak without competing with the adventure resorts' own lodging.

## Conditions a lender should set before closing

1. NPS monthly visitation for 2025 and 2026 pulled directly from the park's statistics system, current state traffic counts for I-64, I-77 and US 19, and a licensed 4- and 6-hour drive-time demographic study.
2. A paid comparable set (AirDNA or Key Data) for hot-tub cabins and glamping units within 25 miles, by month, to settle the 13-point occupancy gap between vendors.
3. A zoning officer's letter confirming the parcel's district under the county's Unified Development Code, and the status of the county's RV park regulations (Section 7001).
4. Plan review under 64CSR18 at least 45 days before construction. The campground permit expires each June 30, is not transferable on a change of ownership, and caps density at 25 sites per acre with 1,200 sq ft per site [23]. A ruling from WV DEP on whether the wastewater design flow triggers a state permit.
5. The county's 2026 Class III levy, utility tariffs and an insurance quote.
6. A parcel-level result on the USDA Eligibility Map. Unincorporated Fayette County near Oak Hill is far from any city of 50,000 [24].

## Questions sponsors ask about this market

**How many people visit New River Gorge?** 1,958,440 recreation visits in 2025, a record and up 8.09% on 2024. July averages about 280,000 visits and January about 40,000.

**What do glamping units rent for near New River Gorge?** Local hot-tub cabins list from about $249 to $289 a night. Oak Hill's short-term rental market averages $224 to $238. National glamping surveys report $239 to $304, but those figures run above what the local market shows.

**Is the area oversupplied?** Not with design-led glamping, which is thin within 25 miles. General short-term rental supply in Oak Hill grew 47.8% in a year while revenue per listing fell 8.6%, so new units are splitting the demand growth.

**What does the county charge guests in tax?** A 6% county hotel occupancy tax on stays in unincorporated Fayette County, remitted by the 15th of each month, plus state sales tax. Both are passed through to guests [25].
